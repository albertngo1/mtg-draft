Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

The 17Lands numbers on these tiles are the spine of this document — with one large caveat specific to this set, below. What the three expert guides in draft-guides/ add is everything a win-rate column structurally cannot measure: when to commit to a lane, how to sequence around counterspells, how many sources a splash needs, and how the soup deck actually wins. That is what this brief collects.

Sources: Limited Resources (six episodes ending in the #857 Sunset Show), NumotTheNummy (33 VODs across the full format — the deepest single-set coverage in the repo), Lords of Limited (ten episodes ending in a 50-takes retrospective). No Limited Level-Ups guide exists for this set.

⚠ Read the win-rate column carefully here. SOS is a soup format: a top archetype is a greedy multicolour pile, and 17Lands splits that deck's record across dozens of invisible three-and-four colour builds while showing clean two-colour decks in full. The one measurable trace: colorless cards post the highest game-weighted GIH WR of any group in the set at 56.6% — above every actual colour — because the soup staples (Potioner's Trove, Sundering Archaic, Strixhaven Skycoach, Fields of Strife, Together as One) are colorless. That is a proxy, not proof, but it points where all three hosts point.

Calibrate to the baseline: the sample is 29.2M games across 327 measured cards, and mono-colour game-weighted GIH WR spans only 55.75% to 56.49% — the tightest colour spread in the reference. Individual colour differences are near noise. IWD separates far more cleanly.

What the data says

(The full archetype table with metagame share renders directly above this brief.)

The two white colleges are a tier of their own — the gap from 2nd to 3rd is 2.3pp, enormous for a five-archetype format. Limited Resources' settled advice, that if you only care about winning and the table is open you should be in white-black or white-red, is the plain right answer.

Prismari is the trap. Last of the five and the second most-drafted at 23.8% — a quarter of the field in the worst college. If you want a two-colour deck, this is the one to leave.

Colour order: W 56.49 > R 56.04 > U 55.98 > B 55.77 > G 55.75 — a 0.74pp spread, so no colour is unplayable and none is a lock.

The soup payoffs, and note how late two of them go: Together as One (65.6%, IWD +16.0 — the best card in the set) · Mathemagics (61.9%, +12.7) · Snarl Song (61.7%, +11.2 — the best uncommon) · Arcane Omens (59.8%, +9.0, and it still wheels to ALSA 5.5) · Exhibition Tidecaller (60.3%, +9.6) · Sundering Archaic (58.8%, +6.9) · Divergent Equation (58.0%, +6.9) · Wisdom of Ages (57.7%, +7.1).

Potioner's Trove is the keystone and the field still passes it — 57.4%, IWD +6.1, 324k games, ALSA 4.6. A top-seven common available at pick five.

Best commons: Elite Interceptor (59.2%) and Pursue the Past (59.1%, and it wheels to ALSA 5.8) are effectively tied for first, then Rubble Rouser (57.8%), Unsubtle Mockery (57.5%, IWD +3.9), Last Gasp (57.4%), Cost of Brilliance (57.4%), Render Speechless (57.4%), Potioner's Trove (57.4%).

The draft plan

  1. Default to a white college — Silverquill or Lorehold — and let pack one pick the other half. Open a strong white card and see whether black or red is flowing. The gap between the two white pairs is 0.4pp, so just take the open one.
  2. Never force soup. It needs a payoff first. All three sources say this independently, and it is the most-agreed claim across them. Commit only once you have at least one over-the-top payoff. "Bad soup" — fixing with no payoff, or payoffs with no early interaction — loses to everything.
  3. Take Potioner's Trove and Arcane Omens above where the field takes them. Both are top-tier and both wheel.
  4. Be on the play, and expect long games. Games run long enough that decking is a real win condition and life gain is everywhere, but spending mana every turn still beats the extra card — especially with a five-mana hand-emptier legal in every deck.
  5. Prefer the card that does two things. The reason a soup deck out-cards a grindy deck is that its spells are removal and draw at once: Stress Dream (60.1%, IWD +8.6), Traumatic Critique (60.3%, +9.8), Stock Up (60.0%, +8.8).

What the numbers can't tell you

  • Don't play around specific answers; do sequence around counter mana. There are too many possible instants per colour to play around any one — make the straightforward play, but split your turn so a counterspell only stops half of it.
  • Sandbag your prepared-spell finishers. The soup decks have to tap low to deploy their payoffs; that is the window.
  • Time the mill finisher for a tapped-out opponent, and count their mana first. Decking is a real plan here, not a novelty.
  • Don't run fragile creatures in a spell deck. Removal is cheap, plentiful and good in every colour and rarity — a two-toughness body that isn't doing anything dies for free.
  • Avoid two-toughness creatures that can't grow, especially at three-plus mana. They fold to the cheap red removal and let the slow decks do their thing.
  • Don't fall out of your role. Aggro must be ahead; soup needs parity; being behind in either is where games are lost fast.
  • Quandrix is interaction-starved, not power-starved. Numot's refinement is the useful one: don't force GU tempo — base it blue-red and splash green, or accept you have no removal beyond counters.

Deckbuilding doctrine

  • 17 lands standard. 18 in a shaky soup base or with rummage/pitch outlets. 16 only for tight low-curve aggro.
  • Splash benchmark: roughly 7–8 sources per main colour, and a single-basic splash is justified by about five Converge cards. Two colours wants 2–3 on-colour tapped duals; multicolour wants 4–6.
  • The fixing-when-poor heuristic runs backwards from instinct: if your mana is already fine, take power; the off-colour fixer pays off most exactly when your mana is bad.
  • Great Hall of the Biblioplex beats Terramorphic Expanse for Converge — 58.8% with IWD +5.9 versus 56.3% and +2.6. Terramorphic yields one colour and eats a basic; the true five-colour land keeps every colour live.
  • Silverquill needs its own targets: six-plus two-drop creatures and twelve-plus creatures total, or your targeted spells have nothing to point at.
  • Card filtering beats raw card advantage, because hitting land drops matters enormously and there is always somewhere to sink mana.

Where the field misprices cards

  • Cards that wheel and shouldn't (ALSA 5.5+): Arcane Omens (59.8% at 5.5, IWD +9.0 — by far the biggest gap in the set), Pursue the Past (59.1% at 5.8), Cost of Brilliance (57.4% at 6.0), Run Behind (57.1% at 5.9, +4.9), Archaic's Agony (56.1% at 6.2, +5.4), Embrace the Paradox (56.3% at 6.2, +5.2), Quick Study (56.1% at 6.2, +5.1), Masterful Flourish (56.1% at 7.2), Essence Scatter (57.0% at 5.1, +5.4).
  • Cards taken early that shouldn't be (negative IWD by pick 5): Comforting Counsel (45.2%, −6.5), Armageddon (51.5%, −3.0), Leech Collector (51.8%, −2.4), Nita, Forum Conciliator (51.4%, −2.3), Forum Necroscribe (51.3%, −1.9), Abigale, Poet Laureate (54.6%, −1.6), Joined Researchers (54.9%, −1.5), Summoned Dromedary (54.2%, −1.2), Eternal Student (54.2%, −1.1).
  • The whole UR aggro creature suite is below baseline, which is why Prismari finishes last: Deluge Virtuoso 51.9% · Spectacular Skywhale 52.2% · Muse Seeker 52.6% · Tackle Artist 52.7% · Elemental Mascot 54.6% · Expressive Firedancer 55.3%. The last two are the ones Numot said to take every copy of.
  • Daydream (48.8%, IWD −3.9) is the worst card in the set relative to its reputation.
  • Diary of Dreams (51.7%, IWD +0.4) and Root Manipulation (52.7% at ALSA 6.1) are both below-average uncommons despite being pitched as engines.
  • Render Speechless is available, not impactful — 57.4% GIH but IWD +0.5 at ALSA 6.6. You can always have one; having it changes little. Imperious Inkmage (57.2%, IWD −0.2 at 6.6) and Killian's Confidence (56.5%, −0.1) share the shape: Silverquill's commons make the deck without winning the game you drew them in.
  • Rubble Rouser is the Lorehold engine (57.8%, IWD +2.4 at ALSA 5.1); Garrison Excavator is the part you can miss (56.7%, IWD +0.4).
  • Grave Researcher (55.5% while being taken at pick 2.1) is a rare you're first-picking for a below-average result.
  • The bonus sheet is bimodal — check the tile. One card per pack, ranging from Emeritus of Ideation (64.5%, IWD +14.5) down to Archmage Emeritus (50.0%, −1.5). Several bonus-sheet cards have samples in the low thousands rather than hundreds of thousands, so their numbers carry far wider error bars.

Open questions the data can't settle

  • How good is soup, really? All three sources call it the best thing to be doing and all three argue 17Lands structurally cannot see it. They are describing a real limitation, not excusing a bad take — but note the colour spread is only 0.74pp, so even a large correction doesn't move much. The practical synthesis: soup is a real ceiling that needs payoffs first; the white colleges are the reliable floor.
  • "Don't pick commons picks 1–8." One Lords of Limited host holds this hard; the other explicitly won't co-sign it. The data can't settle a draft-strategy claim, but note the format's best commons (Elite Interceptor 59.2%, Pursue the Past 59.1%, Potioner's Trove 57.4%) beat a great many uncommons — so the hard version of the rule costs real equity.
  • Lorehold versus Silverquill. 0.4pp apart, and Lorehold has the larger metagame share. Genuinely too close to call; take the open one.
