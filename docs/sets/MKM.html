Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

The 17Lands numbers on these tiles are the spine of this document. What the three expert guides in draft-guides/ add is everything a win-rate column structurally cannot measure: how to sequence a turn, which removal spell actually answers what, how many lands to run, and why a card plays better or worse than its rate. That is what this brief collects.

Sources: Limited Resources (six episodes ending in the #743 Sunset Show), NumotTheNummy (21 VODs across the full format), Lords of Limited (six episodes ending in the 2024-03-25 retrospective). No Limited Level-Ups guide exists for this set. All three had played the format out when they recorded their retrospectives, so their gameplay reads are well-tested — but the data below is the finished 2024 sample and settles anything they disagree about.

Calibrate to the baseline: MKM win rates run tight. Game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR spans only 54.3% to 56.1% — a 1.8pp spread across all five colours — so small GIH differences between commons are mostly noise. Read IWD alongside GIH: in this format the two disagree unusually often, and the gap is informative (see "Where the field misprices cards").

What the data says

(The full archetype table with metagame share renders directly above this brief.)

Boros is the best deck and the most-drafted deck at once, 1.3pp clear of second while a fifth of the field is in it — a contested lane that wins anyway. Every source called it. White's four pairs finish 1st, 2nd, 4th and 5th, so being predisposed to white is a real heuristic, not a slogan.

Colour order: G 56.08 ≈ W 56.00 > R 55.37 > U 54.92 > B 54.31. Green and white are tied at the top; black is last but only 0.6pp behind blue, so "avoid black" overstates it. What black is short of is a good archetype: its three pairs finish 8th, 9th and 10th.

Where to sit when white is gone: Simic (56.07%) is the best non-white pair and under-drafted at 9.8%. That is the open seat. Golgari and Dimir are the traps — 8.4% and 4.8% of the field for 53.57% and 52.65% returns.

The rares decide games. Aurelia's Vindicator 68.6% · Izoni, Center of the Web 65.3% · Vein Ripper 63.6% (IWD +12.9) · Cryptic Coat 63.2% (+10.2) · Doppelgang 62.4% (+9.3) · Teysa, Opulent Oligarch 61.5% (+10.0). Black is the worst colour and holds two of the six best cards — which is exactly why drafting black for a bomb works while drafting black speculatively does not.

The draft plan

  1. Be predisposed to white; look for reasons to stay rather than exit. Four pairs in the top five.
  2. Have a turn-two play, and win the die roll. The most-confirmed claim across all three guides and nothing in the data argues with it. Not affecting the board on two of your first three turns loses to most decks, not just to aggro.
  3. Take cheap instant interaction. Roughly three efficient removal spells minimum — it is the scarcest late-pack resource. Benchmarks: Long Goodbye (56.8%, IWD +4.2, ALSA 3.0), Extract a Confession (56.1%, +3.0) — better than Murder (55.0%, +2.5), as all three guides say — and Bite Down on Crime (57.2%, +2.6).
  4. Take the bomb rare when you open it, especially in black. Good drafters avoid black, so black bombs flow late. Committing to black for a bomb is the play; committing speculatively is not.
  5. The engine of the best deck is its commons, not its signposts. Boros runs on Novice Inspector (59.5%, IWD +2.7, ALSA 2.8 — the best common in the set), Makeshift Binding (58.5%), Person of Interest (58.2%), Dog Walker (57.8%) and Case of the Gateway Express (57.7%). Its gold signpost Meddling Youths sits at 54.8% with IWD −1.9.

What the numbers can't tell you

  • Re-evaluate every removal spell through ward 2. A face-down disguise creature is a ward-2 2/2, so a one-mana burn spell is often a three-mana kill. Removal that sidesteps ward is worth more than its rate suggests: fights (Bite Down on Crime), edicts (Extract a Confession), and −X/−X (Slice from the Shadows) don't pay the tax the same way.
  • Fire instant-speed removal in response to the face-down play, not after it flips. This is the single most repeated self-diagnosed punt in Numot's VODs.
  • Check Watchdog before you plan a flip — it stops you turning creatures face up, and it is easy to forget even when you know the text.
  • Hold priority for artifact and clue triggers. Activate a sacrifice outlet before a token auto-sacrifices; crack a Clue before its draw resolves when you want to chain off an empty hand. Arena's auto-pass throws this value away silently.
  • Don't trust the auto-tapper when you have flip costs, activated abilities, or a trick to hold up — it has cost lines outright.
  • Combat tricks beat removal more often than usual, because the threat of a face-down flip makes defenders play scared. Two of the three guides land on this independently.
  • Just commit your board. Every wrath in the set is rare or mythic and Clue card advantage means both players rebuild, so over-committing is rarely the losing line without a specific read.
  • Don't sandbag a bomb. Casting the game-winner now beats holding it for a perfect spot.
  • Evaluate cards by how many synergy boxes they check — surveil/self-mill, creatures leaving the graveyard, collect-evidence enabling, investigate. A card that does three of them beats a card with a better rate that does one.

Deckbuilding doctrine

  • 17 lands default. 16 only with an ultra-low curve and no real top end; 18 for slower value and control builds.
  • Cap tap-lands at about two in anything aggressive. A tapped turn two is real lost equity in the fastest window of the game.
  • Play the mediocre two-drop anyway. Good two-drops are scarce and four-drops are everywhere; a turn-two play you don't love beats no turn-two play. Just don't take them early.
  • Collect Evidence is free — don't warp picks for it. The cards are priced to cast without it and the graveyard fills from ordinary trading.
  • A single-pip splash wants about four sources, and green does the fixing heavy lifting.
  • Escape Tunnel is the fixing worth having — 55.8% at ALSA 4.1 and the most-played nonbasic in the set — but note its IWD of +0.2: it enables the deck, it doesn't win the game.

Where the field misprices cards

  • Cards taken early that shouldn't be (negative IWD by pick 5): Connecting the Dots (47.4%, IWD −7.3) and Assemble the Players (47.9%, −7.0) are the two worst early picks in the set by a distance — one is a rare draw engine that reads well, the other a top-of-library payoff. Then Case of the Pilfered Proof (48.2%, −6.0), Officious Interrogation (49.0%, −3.2), Delney, Streetwise Lookout (51.7%, −2.8 while being first-picked at ALSA 1.7), Mentor of the Meek (52.0%, −2.7), Meddling Youths (54.8%, −1.9), Suspicious Detonation (51.1%, −1.9 — aim for zero).
  • Cards that wheel and shouldn't (ALSA 5.5+): Reasonable Doubt (56.3% at 6.3, IWD +1.6), Fanatical Strength (56.1% at 6.4), Festerleech (55.4% at 5.8, +1.7), Rubblebelt Maverick (55.6% at 5.5, +1.6), Unauthorized Exit (55.7% at 6.2), Deduce (55.4% at 5.7), Vitu-Ghazi Inspector (55.5% at 5.7). Cheap blue interaction is the recurring bargain.
  • Watch for the high-GIH / negative-IWD shape — it means "good decks run this," not "this wins games." Seasoned Consultant (55.1%, IWD −1.9) and Museum Nightwatch (55.3%, −1.6) are the format's filler two-drops: play them, don't pick them early. On the Job (55.7%, IWD −1.8, wheeling to ALSA 5.7) is the same shape despite being framed as Boros' finisher.
  • The reverse shape — low GIH, positive IWD — means "works when drawn, wrong deck." Insidious Roots (51.6%, IWD +2.4) and Chalk Outline (51.9%, +2.0) are the graveyard build-arounds: real cards, but the decks assembled around them were too slow for the format.
  • Crimestopper Sprite is a bust (54.1%, IWD +0.3); Forensic Researcher (55.0%, +1.2) is the better card at a similar price.
  • Public Thoroughfare (52.3%, IWD −0.6) is close to unplayable. The other fixing is fine.
  • Jaded Analyst is not a defensive all-star — 52.3% with IWD −1.8 at ALSA 6.6.
  • Three cards that wheel because the field is right about them, despite reputations as late-format overperformers: Red Herring (55.4%, IWD −1.1), Felonious Rage (55.6%, −1.1 at ALSA 6.6), Goblin Maskmaker (54.3%, −1.5 at 6.5). Respectable GIH, negative IWD — the aggro decks that ran them were good, the cards weren't.
  • The Special Guests bonus sheet carries no expert notes — Enlisted Wurm (60.6%), Smuggler's Copter (63.4%), Crashing Footfalls (62.7%). Their samples are a few thousand games rather than a few hundred thousand, so read those tiles with much wider error bars.

Open questions the data can't settle

  • Dog Walker's exact ceiling. Consensus best hybrid common; one host flags it as over-rated by the community. The numbers split it — 57.8% at ALSA 3.5 makes it the 4th-best common, but its IWD of +0.6 is modest. A card that makes decks rather than wins games: very high pick in Boros, ordinary elsewhere.
  • Pauper or Prince. One retrospective settles on Pauper — disguise blunts bombs enough that an all-common low-curve Boros deck beats a good rare. The other calls it a deep, fair rare pool where rares define games. Both are supported: the top rares clear the top commons by 5–9pp, and the best archetype needs none of them.
