BLB is a finished format (Aug-Sep 2024) — the 17Lands GIH WR is mature and full-format, and the expert notes are end-of-format retrospectives (LoL's '50 Takes' finale, LR's Sunset Show, Kenji's last BLB VODs), so this reference is settled, not provisional. Big caveat: it's a typal/synergy format, so GIH WR is archetype-conditional — a tribal 'false friend' (Carrot Cake is great in GW Rabbits, bad in RW Mice) reads average overall but swings hard by deck. BLB is a typal/synergy format — each color pair is an animal tribe with a narrow home. Think "BG Food/Forage card," not "green card." The flattest format in this reference: the top five pairs sit inside 0.9pp, so draft the open seat rather than a ranking. Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

The 17Lands numbers on these tiles are the spine of this document. What the three expert guides in draft-guides/ add is everything a win-rate column structurally cannot measure: how to enable a mechanic, how to sequence combat, what to mulligan, and how many copies of a card to run. That is what this brief collects.

Sources: Limited Resources (six episodes ending in the #767 Sunset Show), Lords of Limited (six episodes ending in a 50-takes retrospective), NumotTheNummy (four end-of-format VODs — the thinnest sample here, so weight anything only he says accordingly). No Limited Level-Ups guide exists for this set. All three recorded their settled reads after playing the format out.

Calibrate to the baseline: BLB win rates run high and tight. Game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR spans 54.1% to 56.9%, and the four non-red colours sit inside 1.3pp of each other. A 56% card is average. Read IWD — in a typal format it separates "a card good decks happen to run" from "a card that wins the game you drew it in."

What the data says

(The full archetype table with metagame share renders directly above this brief.)

This is the flattest format in the reference — the top five pairs sit inside 0.9pp. Which is exactly Limited Resources' settled position that the format self-corrected and any pair can win if it's open. Draft the open seat; don't rank pairs out of contention.

The real separation is at the bottom: the two blue pairs finish 9th and 10th, and together account for only 8.4% of the two-colour metagame. The field is correct about them.

Colour order: B 56.89 > G 56.51 > W 56.05 > U 55.61 > R 54.13. Black edges green at the top — inside the noise, but the "green is the best colour, not close" framing overstates it. Red last by 1.5pp is the real gap, and it shows up in the archetypes too: Rakdos wins because black is carrying it.

The best commons and uncommons: Savor (59.3%, IWD +4.9) is the best common in the set and gets only passing mention in every guide. Then Carrot Cake (58.3%), Cache Grab (57.7%), Scales of Shale (57.7% — a black common that wheels to ALSA 6.0), Intrepid Rabbit (57.9%), Bakersbane Duo (57.3%). At uncommon: Vinereap Mentor (61.7%, IWD +7.9), Hunter's Talent (61.4%, +7.7), Thought-Stalker Warlock (60.8%, +6.3), Shoreline Looter (60.4%, +8.2), Darkstar Augur (60.4%, +6.4), Patchwork Banner (59.0%, +4.6 — a genuine any-deck early pick at ALSA 3.1).

The draft plan

  1. Start black or green and let the seat decide the pair. Both colours support several of the better archetypes, and the pair rankings are too flat to commit early on principle.
  2. Take removal early; it is scarce and it does not wheel. Savor (59.3%, IWD +4.9), Nocturnal Hunger (57.0%, +3.0, ALSA 3.4), Take Out the Trash (56.9%, +2.8).
  3. You want a two-drop, and you want to be streamlined. All three sources converge, and Numot's version is bluntest: a turn-two cantrip on the draw instead of a body is close to conceding, and a hand whose first play is a three-drop is a mulligan question. The creature-type gold cards push you onto rails early, so lock the second colour around pick 5–7.
  4. Blue's cards are the cheapest late picks in the format even though blue's decks are the worst. Nine of the twelve strongest cards still available at ALSA 5.5+ are blue. Take them in UG or UB — just don't end up in the two blue pairs at the bottom of the table.
  5. Take the uncommon Talents on sight. They don't come back, and Innkeeper's (62.7%, IWD +10.1) and Hunter's (61.4%, +7.7) are format-warping. The other three are ordinary — Builder's (56.8%), Scavenger's (56.3%), Blacksmith's (54.8%, IWD +0.4).

What the numbers can't tell you

  • Forage off food tokens, not off your graveyard. The predicted tension between forage and threshold effectively never materialises if you sequence correctly, because the food-makers supply the fodder. All three sources agree and it is the single most useful mechanical insight for the set.
  • Play threshold as an aggressive mechanic. Fill the yard through combat trades, not self-mill spells. The surveil-style enablers look like the way in and are not what the deck wants.
  • Pay Intrepid Rabbit's offspring cost almost always. One mana for the second body is the one offspring cost in the set that is close to mandatory; the rest are a bonus you take when convenient.
  • Gift is rarely a decision. You gift when the card makes you. The green fight spell is the exception worth knowing: gifting the fish is often correct.
  • Count food before committing to a race. Food is everywhere, and it is precisely why low-curve aggro without late-game reach struggles — your small creatures stall while they gain life.
  • Sequence instant-speed food and lifegain into combat, not before it. The sacrifice outlets double as combat tricks and force bad blocks.

Deckbuilding doctrine

  • Streamlined two colours. The fixing exists but the format rewards tightness; splashing is a concession, not a plan.
  • 12+ creatures minimum, and watch the two failure modes Numot names on himself: too much top end, and too few two-drops. They compound — a clunky hand plus a missed land drop is how BLB games are lost.
  • One card-draw spell is good; two strands your curve. Drawing your smoothing instead of a body is the specific way that goes wrong.
  • Don't stack expensive reactive spells. Numot blames a loss squarely on doubling up on the four-mana green removal; the card itself is fine (Polliwallop 56.7%) and the stacking is the error.
  • Two of the five Villages are worth playing, three are not. Mudflat (57.5%, IWD +2.3) and Lupinflower (56.9%, +1.1) are good; Oakhollow (53.9%, −1.3), Rockface (54.3%, −0.8) and Lilypad (53.4%) are not. The blanket "don't play the Villages" is over-broad.

Where the field misprices cards

  • Cards that wheel and shouldn't (ALSA 5.5+): Ruthless Negotiation (58.0% at 5.5), Scales of Shale (57.7% at 6.0), Pearl of Wisdom (56.4% at ALSA 6.8, IWD +5.8), Dire Downdraft (56.6% at 6.4), Wishing Well (56.6% at 5.7, +5.5), Tidecaller Mentor (56.5% at 5.9, +5.4), Into the Flood Maw (56.5% at 5.7, +5.1), Lightshell Duo (55.6% at 7.0), Dazzling Denial (55.3% at 6.6). Nearly all of it is cheap blue interaction.
  • Cards taken early that shouldn't be (negative IWD by pick 5): Stormsplitter (42.8%, IWD −7.6 — the worst mythic in the set by a distance), For the Common Good (45.6%, −4.3), Dawn's Truce (48.7%, −2.2), Mouse Trapper (51.8%, −2.7), Sunspine Lynx (51.3%, −2.2 at ALSA 3.4), Seedglaive Mentor (53.3%, −1.4), Mabel's Mettle (53.2%, −1.1), Baylen, the Haymaker (51.4%).
  • Fountainport Bell is not the format-defining artifact it's billed as — 53.9% with IWD of exactly +0.0 at ALSA 6.1. It wheels, and drawing it changes nothing.
  • Tangle Tumbler is not a hidden Selesnya finisher — 53.8% with IWD −0.7 at ALSA 6.2.
  • Shoreline Looter does not wheel (ALSA 3.9, and at 60.4% / IWD +8.2 it is the best blue uncommon) while Lightshell Duo does (ALSA 7.0, the latest of any real playable in the set). If you are reading blue signals, those are the two to calibrate on — and Eddymurk Crab at ALSA 5.0 is not a signal at all, since picks 4–5 is simply where it goes.
  • Carrot Cake (58.3%) edges Intrepid Rabbit (57.9%) for best white common, and Cache Grab (57.7%) edges Bakersbane Duo (57.3%) for best green common. Both are close enough not to change a pick, but the guides name the runner-up in each case.
  • Bumbleflower's Sharepot (52.9% at ALSA 6.5), Heirloom Epic (53.8%, IWD −1.1) and Short Bow (52.6%, −0.8) are all below the bar despite being recommended.
  • Otterball Antics (54.0%), Wildfire Howl (53.6%) and Coruscation Mage (54.4%) are why Izzet is last. There is no hidden Otters deck.
  • Nightwhorl Hermit (50.2% at ALSA 7.8) is near the bottom of the set — not every late blue card is a bargain.

Open questions the data can't settle

  • Builder's Talent. One Lords of Limited host builds around it and never passes it; the other would rather have Pond Prophet. The aggregate (56.8% at ALSA 5.2) favours the sceptic, but it cannot see an assembled Talent deck — that is exactly the kind of claim a per-card win rate is blind to. Take it when it comes; don't plan around it from pick two.
  • How much the pair ranking should influence you at all. Given a 0.9pp spread across the top five, the honest answer is: very little above the seat you're actually being passed. The extremes (Izzet and Azorius at the bottom) are real; the middle is not worth steering by.
