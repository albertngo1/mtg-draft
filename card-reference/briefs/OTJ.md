## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

The 17Lands numbers on these tiles are the spine of this document. What the two expert guides in
`draft-guides/` add is everything a win-rate column structurally cannot measure: when to use a
mechanic, how to sequence a turn, what to prioritise in a pack, and why a card plays better or worse
than its rate. That is what this brief collects.

**Sources:** Lords of Limited (a "Saddle Up" flashback recorded months after the format closed, plus
a mid-format meta episode, two Arena Open day-two drafts and two early-access drafts) and
NumotTheNummy (four end-of-format VODs). No Limited Resources or Limited Level-Ups guide exists for
this set, so **OTJ has the narrowest source base in this reference** — lean on the numbers here more
than usual.

**Calibrate to the baseline:** game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR runs **53.6% to 56.8%**. Green, white
and black sit within 0.6pp at the top; blue and red are genuinely behind. A 56% card is average in
the good colours and good in the weak ones. The sample is **38.6M games across 364 measured cards** —
the largest in the repo.

### What the data says

*(The full archetype table with metagame share renders directly above this brief.)*

**Green-White is the best deck and the most-drafted deck at once** — nearly a quarter of the field is
in it and it still clears second place by 1.3pp. A heavily-contested archetype that wins anyway is
genuinely the best deck, not an unexploited niche. *"Green-White is the deck to beat"* is exactly
right, and you should have a plan for it in every other seat.

**Colour order:** **G 56.78 > W 56.34 > B 56.19 > U 54.45 > R 53.57.** Green's edge is where the
flashback says it is — rate on creatures and the best uncommons. Blue and red are 1.7pp and 2.6pp
behind green, and it shows: **the two blue pairs are the two worst archetypes in the format**, and
red's own guide-nominated best removal (Explosive Derailment 54.7%, Skewer the Critics 53.4%) sits
under the format baseline.

**Green owns the uncommon slot:** Outcaster Greenblade (61.4%, IWD +6.5, ALSA 2.5) and Spinewoods
Armadillo (60.5%, +5.8, ALSA 2.3). **Throw from the Saddle (58.9%, IWD +3.7) is the best common in
the set.** The best white card outside the rares is **Lassoed by the Law (61.1%, IWD +6.1, ALSA
2.5)** — take it over Mystical Tether (56.9%, +2.0), which the guide ranks first.

**The rares decide games**, which is the flashback's central framing and it holds: Bonny Pall,
Clearcutter 66.4% (IWD **+16.1**) · Railway Brawler 65.2% (+12.5) · Overwhelming Forces 65.0% (+13.3)
· Rakdos, the Muscle 64.2% (+13.8) · Roxanne, Starfall Savant 64.2% (+11.1). Numot arrives at the
same conclusion from the other side — Railway Brawler was by far his most-opened and most-impactful
mythic.

### The draft plan

1. **Bombs, then removal.** The pick-priority framing both sources use, and the rare sheet above is
   why. Take the best card you open; keep answers for theirs.
2. **Exile removal over damage removal at close-to-equal rate.** Recursion runs through black and
   green, so a creature that comes back twice beats a spell that killed it once. Black's three are all
   excellent: **Consuming Ashes (57.9%, IWD +5.0)**, **Shoot the Sheriff (57.8%, +5.2)**, **Desert's
   Due (57.7%, +4.4)**.
3. **Be green, or be able to beat green.** If you are on a controlling plan you need real exile
   removal for the big Green-White creatures — bounce and tap effects buy a turn against a deck happy
   to recast.
4. **Plot is a value mechanic, not a tempo one.** Plotting a threat and casting it next turn still
   leaves it unable to attack for another turn. Plot when you have nothing better to do with the mana.
5. **Don't build around crimes.** They are a sub-theme spread across most colours and the payoffs are
   ordinary (Nimble Brigand 54.0%, Raven of Fell Omens 54.3%). Commit crimes when they're free — from
   deserts, fixing, sacrifice outlets you were already playing.

### What the numbers can't tell you

- **Pass with mana up before you plot.** The genuinely good plot line is holding priority so the
  "didn't cast from hand" triggers fire, then casting the plotted card next turn.
- **Play the ramp/fixing two-drop over the defensive one.** Numot is explicit about this and it's the
  right heuristic in a format whose payoffs are this top-heavy.
- **Do the combat math exactly.** He names a loss to over-pumping with a fight spell by exactly one,
  leaving the opponent at 1. Trick density is high enough that precision matters every turn.
- **Don't attack into open mana and a full grip** without a plan for the trick.
- **Deserts are deck-fixing, not picks.** Enough of them makes Desert's Due and the desert-matters
  cards live. Numot's first five-colour deck failed specifically because it was two deserts short —
  that's the useful version of "take the crime lands," not "take them highly."
- **If you can't commit a crime within a turn or two, just play the creature.** Holding a body back
  waiting for a trigger costs more than the trigger is worth.
- **Don't over-commit to a three-of sub-theme without its payoff.** Numot drafted three copies of a
  synergy card and never saw the enabler.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **17 lands standard, 18 with mana sinks or an expensive multicolour top end.**
- **Have a two-drop.** Numot's Boros deck opened with zero and was unplayable until pack three
  rescued it — against green curving out with oversized bodies, no play before turn three loses.
- **Play Booster structure means uncommons matter more than commons and the wheel is unreliable** —
  doubly so here, where the best uncommons (Outcaster Greenblade, Lassoed by the Law, Spinewoods
  Armadillo) all sit under ALSA 2.6 and never come back.

### Where the field misprices cards

- **Cards that wheel and shouldn't** (ALSA 5.5+): **Humiliate (57.8% at ALSA 6.8, IWD +4.6 — the
  single biggest gap in the set)**, Plan the Heist (56.9% at 5.8), Ambush Gigapede (56.8% at 6.4),
  Phantom Interference (56.7% at 6.0, +5.6), Jailbreak Scheme (56.1% at **7.2**), Take the Fall
  (55.8% at 6.9), Slick Sequence (55.6% at 5.9, **IWD +6.6**), Essence Capture (55.7% at 5.7, +5.6).
  Note what this list is: **cheap blue and black interaction.** Blue's *commons* are the real bargain
  in OTJ even though blue's *archetypes* are the worst — those two facts coexist, and only the first
  one should change your picks.
- **Cards taken early that shouldn't be** (negative IWD by pick 5): Grindstone (**42.6%, IWD −6.2**),
  Port Razer (48.6%, −2.5), Molten Duplication (45.4%, −1.8), Fblthp, Lost on the Range (47.6%, −1.1),
  Raucous Entertainer (52.5%, −2.2), Inspiring Vantage (52.6%, −1.9), Grand Abolisher (50.9%, −1.2),
  and **Drover Grizzly (55.1%, IWD −1.1)** — notable because it's a heavily-played green common that
  looks fine and isn't.
- **Razzle-Dazzler at 50.8% and ALSA 7.1** is the clearest confirmation available that a UR aggro
  plan is not there.
- **Bounding Felidar is not a bomb** — 54.8% with IWD +0.2.
- **The crime lands are fine fixing, not high picks.** Conduit Pylons 56.1% (IWD +1.6) and Jagged
  Barrens 55.4% (+2.5) earn a slot; **Mirage Mesa is 54.7% with IWD −0.1** and Bandit's Haul 54.3%.
- **Rush of Dread (60.2%, IWD +8.3)** is the best black rare outside the mythics.
- **The Big Score bonus sheet carries no expert notes**, because set reviews skip reprints. It ranges
  from Oko, Thief of Crowns (64.2%) and Fractured Identity (63.1%) to Grindstone (42.6%) and
  Transmutation Font (50.0%). **Sample sizes there are two orders of magnitude smaller** — a few
  thousand games rather than a few hundred thousand — so read those tiles with much wider error bars.

### Open questions the data can't settle

- **Five-colour good-stuff versus streamlined two colours.** Numot's stated preference is four-to-five
  colour piles built on desert fixing, and he trophied with them. A per-card GIH WR structurally
  cannot credit that deck — its strength is spread across cards that each look ordinary. Treat it as a
  real option the aggregate can't see, and note it needs a genuine desert count to function.
- **Whether Boros is a deck.** Numot calls it consistent in best-of-three and easily mirrored; the
  flashback rates it C+ and blames red. **WR sits 4th at 55.68% on only 5.7% of the metagame** —
  better than the flashback implies, and the low share suggests it's underdrafted rather than bad.
