## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

Distilled from the two expert guides in `draft-guides/` so you don't need a second window. Sources in
priority order: **Lords of Limited's "Saddle Up" flashback** (2025-01-28, recorded months after the
format closed — the most authoritative source here — plus a mid-format meta episode, two Arena Open
day-two drafts and two early-access drafts), **NumotTheNummy** (four end-of-format VODs,
2024-05-22 → 05-25, a thin tier-2 sample). No Limited Resources or Limited Level-Ups guide exists for
this set, so OTJ has the **narrowest source base of any set in this reference** — weight the numbers
on these tiles more heavily than usual. On conflict: the 17Lands data > the flashback > the
early-access reads.

**The data is the finished format — 38.6M games across 364 measured cards**, the largest sample in
the repo. Where a fully-informed retrospective still disagrees with a number that size, the number
wins.

**Calibrate to the baseline:** game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR runs **53.6% to 56.8%**. Green, white
and black sit within 0.6pp of each other at the top; blue and red are genuinely behind. A 56% card is
average in the good colours and good in the bad ones.

### The draft plan in five lines

1. **Green-White is the best deck by a wide margin, and this is the call both sources got right.**
   Gold-card GIH WR by pair: **WG 59.5 · UG 57.9 · RG 57.5 · BG 57.1 · WB 56.8 · WR 56.3 · UB 55.6 ·
   BR 54.9 · UR 54.3 · WU 53.3.** WG clears second place by 1.5pp — the largest top-of-format gap of
   any set here. Lords of Limited's S-tier ranking and *"Green-White is the deck to beat"* framing is
   fully vindicated. ⚠ These pair numbers average only 5–8 gold cards each, so trust the extremes and
   the direction, not the exact middle ordering.
2. **Green is the best colour and red is the worst — both ends confirmed.** **G 56.8 > W 56.3 >
   B 56.2 > U 54.5 > R 53.6.** Green's edge is exactly where the flashback says it is: rate on
   creatures and the best uncommons. **Outcaster Greenblade (61.4%, IWD +6.5, ALSA 2.5)** and
   **Spinewoods Armadillo (60.5%, +5.8, ALSA 2.3)** are the two best green uncommons in the set, and
   **Throw from the Saddle (58.9%, +3.7)** is indeed the best common, exactly as the retrospective
   names it.
3. **Bombs and removal, in that order.** This is the flashback's explicit pick-priority framing and
   the data backs it hard: the top of the rare sheet is enormous — Bonny Pall, Clearcutter (66.4%,
   IWD **+16.1**), Railway Brawler (65.2%, +12.5), Overwhelming Forces (65.0%, +13.3), Rakdos, the
   Muscle (64.2%, +13.8), Roxanne (64.2%, +11.1). Numot independently lands on the same read from the
   other side: Railway Brawler was by far his most-opened and most-impactful mythic.
4. **Exile removal is premium because recursion is everywhere.** Correct, and the ordering inside it
   is worth fixing: the retrospective ranks Mystical Tether ahead of Lassoed by the Law, but
   **Lassoed by the Law (61.1%, IWD +6.1, ALSA 2.5) is far better than Mystical Tether (56.9%, +2.0)**
   — it is the best white card in the set outside the rares. Black's three are all excellent and all
   confirmed: **Consuming Ashes (57.9%, +5.0)**, **Shoot the Sheriff (57.8%, +5.2)**, **Desert's Due
   (57.7%, +4.4)**.
5. **Plot is a value mechanic, not a tempo one.** Both sources agree and neither overstates it: you
   plot when you have nothing better to do with the mana, not to "get ahead." Plotting a threat and
   casting it next turn still leaves it unable to attack for another turn — that is two turns of
   nothing in a midrange-fast format.

### Where the experts were wrong — judged by 38.6M games

- **The blue thesis is the big one, and it is wrong in every direction.** The mid-format episode is
  built around blue being underrated, undervalued and open — that blue control in UB, UW or UR is
  there for the taking once the field abandons it. **Blue is the 4th colour at 54.5%, and WU is the
  worst pair in the format at 53.3%.** The specific cards named as the tell are worse still: **Canyon
  Crab 54.5%** (called the best and most versatile of them), **Emergent Haunting 52.6%**, and
  **Wrangler of the Damned 50.8%** — which is named as the UW *engine* and is one of the weakest
  uncommons in the set. The field was passing these because they are bad, not because the lane was
  open. Only **Slickshot Lockpicker (55.8%, IWD +4.6)** holds up, and its ALSA of 3.8 means it never
  went late anyway.
- **The two pairs the guide declined to discuss are the 2nd and 3rd best in the format.** GR and GU
  are dismissed together as C-tier "off beaten paths," with a note that the evidence is thin. **UG is
  #2 at 57.9% and RG is #3 at 57.5%** — both ahead of every blue and black pair the guide rated A.
  Numot's instinct here is the better one: his second-most-drafted deck was RG stompy around Railway
  Brawler, and he was right to keep going back to it.
- **Conversely, all three A-tier calls below Green-White land in the bottom four.** UB (rated A) is
  7th, BR (rated A) is 8th, UW (rated A) is 10th. The pattern is consistent: the guide over-rated
  every controlling blue/black shell and under-rated every green pair.
- **Grindstone is the worst card in the set, and it is listed as a UB win condition.** **42.6% GIH,
  IWD −6.2, still taken by pick 4.8.** (It is a Big Score bonus-sheet card with a small sample —
  2,829 games — but the direction is not ambiguous.)
- **Bounding Felidar is not a bomb.** The flashback calls it nearly unbeatable once it resolves. It is
  **54.8% with IWD +0.2** — a below-average uncommon that does nothing when you draw it.
- **Razzle-Dazzler aggro being a trap is emphatically confirmed** — **50.8% at ALSA 7.1.** The guide
  calls UR aggro a trap and says to play the pair as a late-game deck instead; the card data agrees
  about the trap, and the pair numbers say the late-game version is not much better (UR 9th).
- **The crime lands are good fixing, not high picks.** *"Always worth a pick"* is fine; *"take them
  highly"* is not. **Conduit Pylons 56.1% (IWD +1.6)** and **Jagged Barrens 55.4% (+2.5)** earn their
  slot, but **Mirage Mesa — singled out as a strong multi-fixer — is 54.7% with IWD −0.1**, and
  **Bandit's Haul is 54.3%**. Numot's version of the claim is the more accurate one: deserts matter
  because they *enable* Desert's Due and the fixing, not because they are worth an early pick.
- **Red's "best" removal is below average, which is the actual story of red.** The retrospective names
  **Explosive Derailment (54.7%, +1.5)** and **Skewer the Critics (53.4%, +0.8)** as red's best. Both
  sit under the format's mono-colour baseline. Red is not a colour with weak commons and strong
  uncommons — it is weak through the middle, and that is why it finishes last.
- **Numot's own self-diagnosis holds up.** His two named errors — holding a creature for a crime
  trigger it may never get, and drafting a three-of sub-theme without its payoff — are both the same
  mistake, and the data supports the correction: the crime payoffs are mostly ordinary
  (**Nimble Brigand 54.0%**, **Raven of Fell Omens 54.3%**), so committing tempo to enable them is a
  losing trade.

### Gameplay rules that actually change results

- **Have a two-drop.** Numot's Boros deck opening with zero two-drops was unplayable until pack three
  rescued it — in a format where green can curve out with genuinely oversized bodies, no play before
  turn three loses on the spot.
- **Play the ramp/fixing two-drop over the defensive one.** Numot is explicit about this and it is
  the correct heuristic in a format whose payoffs are this top-heavy.
- **Do the combat math exactly.** He names a loss to over-pumping with a fight/pump spell by exactly
  one, leaving the opponent at 1. Trick density is high enough that precision matters every turn.
- **Don't attack into open mana and a full grip** without a plan for the trick — a recurring cause of
  swung games in both directions across his VODs.
- **Pass with mana up before you plot.** The one genuinely good plot line is holding priority so the
  "didn't cast from hand" triggers fire, then casting the plotted card next turn.
- **Answer resolved green threats, don't race them.** If you are on a controlling plan you need actual
  exile removal for the big Green-White creatures — bounce and tap effects buy a turn against a deck
  that is happy to recast.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **17 lands standard, 18 with mana sinks or an expensive multicolour top end.**
- **Take the exile removal over the damage-based removal** at close-to-equal rate. Recursion runs
  through black and green, so a creature that comes back twice beats a spell that killed it once.
- **Don't build around crimes.** They are a sub-theme spread across most colours, and the payoffs are
  ordinary. Commit crimes when they are free (deserts, fixing, sacrifice outlets you were playing
  anyway) and take the payoff only when it is also a fine card.
- **Deserts are deck-fixing, not picks.** Enough of them makes Desert's Due and the desert-matters
  cards live; Numot's first five-colour deck failed specifically because it was two deserts short.
- **Play Booster structure means uncommons matter more than commons and the wheel is unreliable** —
  which is doubly true here, where the best uncommons (Outcaster Greenblade, Lassoed by the Law,
  Spinewoods Armadillo) all have ALSA under 2.6 and never come back.

### Traps and sleepers the data settled

- **Worst early picks** (taken by pick 5 on average, negative IWD): Grindstone (**42.6%, IWD −6.2**),
  Port Razer (48.6%, −2.5), Molten Duplication (45.4%, −1.8), Fblthp, Lost on the Range (47.6%, −1.1),
  Raucous Entertainer (52.5%, −2.2), Inspiring Vantage (52.6%, −1.9), Grand Abolisher (50.9%, −1.2),
  and **Drover Grizzly (55.1%, IWD −1.1)** — the last one notable because it is a heavily-played green
  common that looks fine and is not.
- **Cheapest edges — good cards that wheel** (ALSA 5.5+): **Humiliate (57.8% at ALSA 6.8, IWD +4.6 —
  the single biggest gap in the set)**, Plan the Heist (56.9% at 5.8), Ambush Gigapede (56.8% at 6.4),
  Phantom Interference (56.7% at 6.0, +5.6), Jailbreak Scheme (56.1% at **7.2**), Take the Fall
  (55.8% at 6.9), Slick Sequence (55.6% at 5.9, **IWD +6.6**), Essence Capture (55.7% at 5.7, +5.6).
  Note what this list is: **cheap blue and black interaction.** Blue's *commons* are the real bargain
  in this format — which is the defensible core of a blue thesis whose uncommon-level version failed.
- **Rush of Dread (60.2%, IWD +8.3)** confirms Numot's "overperforms" call and is the best black rare
  outside the mythics.
- **The Big Score / OTP bonus sheet carries no expert notes at all**, because set reviews skip a
  bonus sheet of reprints. Some of it is elite (Oko, Thief of Crowns 64.2%; Fractured Identity 63.1%)
  and some is unplayable (Grindstone 42.6%; Transmutation Font 50.0%). **Sample sizes there are two
  orders of magnitude smaller than the main set** — a few thousand games rather than a few hundred
  thousand — so read those tiles with much wider error bars.

### Cross-source disagreements, left unresolved on purpose

- **Five-colour good-stuff versus streamlined two colours.** Numot's stated preference is four-to-five
  colour piles built on desert fixing, and he trophied with them; Lords of Limited never rates a soup
  archetype at all and frames the format as two-colour pairs plus Mardu-ish value. The card data
  cannot adjudicate this — a 17Lands GIH WR is per-card, and a five-colour deck's strength is spread
  across cards that all look ordinary in isolation. Treat Numot's version as a real option that the
  aggregate structurally cannot credit, and note that it needs a genuine desert count to work.
- **Whether Boros aggro is a real deck.** Numot calls RW consistent in best-of-three and easily
  mirrored; the flashback rates it C+ and blames red. **WR sits 6th at 56.3%** — better than the
  flashback implies, worse than a deck you should plan on. Both are partly right.

### Calibration note

This is the thinnest-sourced set in the reference: two channels, one of them a four-VOD sample. It is
also the set where the sources' framing missed hardest — the whole blue-is-open thesis, and the
dismissal of the two green pairs that finished 2nd and 3rd. That combination is worth naming plainly:
on OTJ, read the guide notes on a card tile for *how the card plays*, and read the win rate for
*whether to take it*. Where the flashback is specific about a single card it is usually right (Throw
from the Saddle, the black removal suite, Razzle-Dazzler being a trap). Where it ranks colours and
archetypes, it is not.
