## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

> **Pre-release brief, written 2026-11-11, two days before the Arena release (2026-11-13).** Every
> expert claim below is a prediction or a read from a handful of early-access drafts and Sealed
> pools. Sources, strongest first: Limited Level-Ups' stream after ~20 drafts (11-12) and rares
> review (11-06), Lords of Limited 504 (11-09, early-access drafts plus one paper prerelease), Numot's
> four early-access Sealed pools (11-07 → 11-10), Limited Resources 879 (11-10), Rough Drafts 86
> (11-05), then the pure card-text reviews (LoL Crash Course, LR 878, LLU set review, Draftsim, MTG
> Arena Zone). The 17Lands numbers in this file are the early sample described above (about 8.7k
> games, ±3–4pp per pair), so read them as a direction, not a ranking. Rewrite this brief after
> release week.

### The format in six lines

1. **Medium speed, with a wide range.** LLU (11-12): "not a blistering fast format, but… slightly
   too fast for you to consistently do the cool things." LoL 504 and Rough Drafts 86 say aggressive
   decks are good. Numot's Sealed games felt "grindy". LLU 11-06 saw games end on turn 4–5 and
   others go to decking.
2. **Play to the board, then win on two-for-ones** (LoL 504's top format truth). Cards that don't
   affect the board "have to be very good". A 12-creature deck is a trap.
3. **Power is low and evenly spread.** LLU's set review: "everything's a C". Numot: "incremental
   advantages", no game lost to an insane card in his first two pools. LLU 11-06: no splashable
   bomb every draft, decks win as "the sum of the parts".
4. **Fixing is everywhere, but soup is not the plan** (LoL 504). There is little worth splashing.
5. **Black and white lead the experts' colour order; green is the split.** LoL 504 (one host):
   black, white, red, blue, green. LLU 11-06: green has the most powerful rares. Early 17Lands has
   green commons at the top (see Where the sources disagree).
6. **Removal is valued highly** by every source that played (LoL 504 "excellent", LLU "if you see a
   good removal spell… take it"). Whether there is enough of it is disputed.

### Mechanics

- **Beam Me Up.** Flashback on sorceries, plus you return a creature you control to hand. Sorcery
  speed only, so you can't use it to save a creature from removal (LLU). Best on cheap value spells
  you can cast any time: `Open Communications`, `Pattern Buffer Preservation`, `There's Coffee in
  that Nebula`, `Boarding Action` (LoL 504, Rough Drafts 86). Best creatures to pick up are cheap ETB
  bodies: `Constable Odo`, `Fugitive Listener`, `Federation Probe`, `Emergency Medical Hologram`.
  It resets finality counters (LR 879). How good it is overall is the biggest open dispute (below).
- **Spacecraft and station.** Tap creatures to put charge counters on a ship; it becomes a creature
  at its threshold. Station numbers are lower than Edge of Eternities, about 5–8 (LLU, LoL 504).
  - Three power stations in two or three taps, two power needs three or four, so 3-power creatures
    are a notch above 2-power ones (LoL 504, Rough Drafts 86).
  - Play two or three, not more (LLU, Rough Drafts 86). The good ones are mostly rare or mythic;
    common and uncommon ships "largely underwhelmed" (LoL 504). Heuristic: "If the card says
    U.S.S. on it, you should probably take it" (LLU 11-12).
  - Don't tap out to station into open removal mana; the ship dies and your tapped board eats a
    swing back (Rough Drafts 86). Station a bit at a time so the ship isn't a creature on their turn,
    and finish on your own turn to dodge sorcery removal (LR 879).
  - Spacecraft payoffs (`Sulu`, `Hemmer`, `Rutherford`) are blank without ships (Numot).
- **Face a Dilemma.** Any "choose one" card triggers it, including the modal lands (Numot, LR).
  Payoffs: `Seven of Nine`, `Tell Me What You See`, `Elim Garak`, `Captain Benjamin Sisko`, `Jett
  Reno`. LLU wants about 6–7 modal cards for the payoffs; LR 879 says 3–4 is enough for the rare
  ones. LoL 504: "Dilemma is not a thing."
- **Strange New Worlds.** Costs X less, where X is the number of *differently named* lands you
  control. Any two-colour deck starts at 2 (LR 878). Sources: the ten common Planet gain-lands, the
  five uncommon Anomaly lands (each sacrifices for a New Planet token, so it is two names on its
  own), rare shocklands, `Xenobotanist`, landcyclers fetching an off-colour basic (LR 878, LLU).
  LLU 11-06: counts reach about 6 on turn 6 if you draft the lands. Payoffs are mostly UG spells:
  `Cloak and Phaser` (the best one, LoL and LLU), `Astute Terraformer`, `The Traveler`, `Deep Space
  Scan`, `Seven of Nine, Unassimilated`.
- **Borg and assimilate.** WB's theme is artifact creatures. `Locutus, the One Who Speaks` anthems
  them and counts itself, so alone it is a 2/2 (LLU Sealed guide). Assimilate puts a card onto the
  battlefield as a Borg artifact creature with a +1/+1 counter; it is rare by design (LR 878).
  Build-arounds (`Conquest Mission`, `Organic Avulsion Unit`, `Hive Mind Coprocessor`) want 12–15
  artifacts; LLU 11-06 says WB reaches 14–15 artifact creatures easily.
- **Federation** counts creature *types* among your non-Borg creatures, not creatures. Most
  creatures have two types, so expect about 1.5–2× your creature count (LR 878).
- **"Eight total power" (RW).** Triggers when you attack while your creatures have 8+ total power;
  the attackers don't need 8 power between them. Usually three creatures (LoL 504). LR 878 compares
  it to exalted: build a board, attack with the one creature carrying the bonuses.

### Removal and fixing

- **Removal is sorcery-heavy and clunky.** LLU 11-12: "I always feel like I end up with less removal
  than I want"; the count is similar to FRA's but the spells are clunkier. Catch-alls to take
  early: `Malfunctioning Holodeck` (white's O-Ring, an artifact), `He's Dead, Jim` (LoL, LLU).
- **Red has the cheap burn.** Plenty of it deals 3, so 4 toughness may be the line that matters (LR
  878). `Plasma Cascade` and `Transporter Malfunction` go face too.
- **Artifact removal is maindeckable** given how many ships and artifacts there are, but take it
  around 9th pick (LLU 11-12).
- **1-toughness commons are everywhere** and there is little flying or reach outside spacecraft, so
  cheap evasive flyers connect (LLU set review). LLU 11-06: "flying is very important."
- **Fixing:** a basic-landcycler at common in every colour (`Free Borg Revolutionaries`, `Humpback
  Whales`, `Rogue Artificial Intelligence`, `Horta`, `Cold-Blooded Crew`), ten common Planet
  gain-lands that replace a basic in about half of packs, `Xenobotanist` and `Class-F Shuttlecraft`
  at common, `Warp Core`, the Anomaly lands, and all ten shocklands at rare (LoL 504, LR 878, Rough
  Drafts 86). Count the landcyclers before you pick a land count: Numot ran 16 lands with two
  landcyclers and 14 basics plus duals with four.
- **Anomaly lands cost you colour.** Rough Drafts 86: black and green are the best of the cycle,
  blue (`Gaseous Anomaly`, seven mana to draw two) is overrated, and don't run them next to
  double-pip two-drops like `Locutus`.

### The ten colour pairs (expert reads, pre-release)

Each pair has two signpost uncommons, one gold and one hybrid (LR 878). Early 17Lands win rates
with sample sizes are in the table above this brief.

|---|---|---|---|
| **W/B** | Borg artifact creatures | `Locutus, the One Who Speaks`, `Zero, Incorporeal Sensate` | The consensus best deck. LoL tier 1 ("highly confident"), LLU's most-backed deck and Alex's 11-12 build, LR's deepest strong cards, Rough Drafts "very, very well supported". |
| **W/U** | Spacecraft and artificers | `Hemmer, Fixing What Is Broken`, `Constable Odo` | LoL tier 1, "outstanding". LLU 6th: only 3 common and 5 uncommon ships, "don't load up". LR middle. |

### Draft plan

1. **Take removal and good creatures; draft cards, then decks.** Both LoL hosts lost early drafts by
   latching onto build-arounds (`Collation Trimatrix`, dilemma payoffs) (LoL 504). LLU 11-06: "If I
   see a solid removal spell, I'm going to take that over some other creature."
2. **W/B artifacts is the lane every expert names first.** Take cheap artifact creatures; "if you
   just take some artifacts, you end up seeing some cards that care about it" (LLU 11-12).
3. **Prefer 3-power creatures and cheap flyers.** They station faster and connect (LoL 504, LLU).
   2/1s are a fine attacking stat line because the format has many 3/2s and few cheap x/3s (LoL 504).
4. **Take spacecraft and the U.S.S. rares, but stop at two or three ships** (LLU, Rough Drafts 86).
5. **Draft Strange New Worlds only if it comes to you.** It costs picks on lands and plays
   off-curve (LoL 504). If you are in it, take the Planets and Anomalies; the good builds are
   proactive, "Strange New Worlds beatdown" with `Astute Terraformer` (LoL 504).

### Deckbuilding and Sealed

- **Two colours, light splash at most** (LoL 504). The early 17Lands sample agrees so far: two-colour
  decks 55.6% on 7,233 games, three-colour 51.7% on 1,295.
- **Give two-drops a late-game job.** They should stay relevant or be sacrifice fodder; don't play
  bad cards just to be on board (LoL 504).
- **Aggro decks need a plan for when the opponent blocks.** That is why `Relentless Drednok`,
  `Sickbay` and `Conquest Mission` overperformed in LLU's drafts.
- **Don't cut land drops.** LoL's Sealed flooded with landcyclers, `Warp Core` and ships, but eight
  mana lets you beam up and replay `Constable Odo` in one turn (LoL 504).
- **Sealed (Numot, LLU):** play the bomb's colour pair even if you preferred another; prefer the deck
  with removal over a clean aggro curve; cheap looters are worth slots as discard fodder for `Seven of
  Nine, Unassimilated`. LLU's Sealed guide names the classic decks as G/W counters, U/R spells and B/G
  graveyard, and calls W/B Borg "a pretty strong deck".
- **Saga tech:** `The Measure of a Man` and `The Naked Time` have a downside chapter III. Sacrifice
  them with `Dahar Master` or `Targ Wrangler`, or blink with `Ghost of Illyria` (LLU Sealed guide).

### Traps

- **The Mission cycle, mostly.** LLU's reviews graded them D range, then found the Esper ones good in
  play: `Conquest Mission` was an all-star, `Vengeance Mission` and `Anthropological Mission` "got some
  legs". Green's `Support Mission` and red's `Exploratory Mission` are "mostly bad still" (LLU 11-12).
- **Ramp.** "Ramp is bad because it takes a turn off" (LoL 504). LR 878/879 call the three-mana
  ramp spells too slow (`Final Frontier`, `Hit It!`) and grade `Branching Timeline` F. Numot: `Stable
  Wormhole` does nothing without a mana sink.
- **Strange New Worlds cards in a basics-heavy deck** (Numot), and **spacecraft payoffs with no ships**
  (Numot).
- **Building hard around Beam Me Up** or casting the timing-sensitive ones on the wrong turn (Rough
  Drafts 86).
- **Expensive top end that does little:** `V'Ger, the Intruder` at 7, `Tea, Earl Grey, Hot` (Numot).
  4-mana x/3s with no ETB are marked down (LLU).
- **`Kobayashi Maru Scenario` and `Rules of Acquisition`:** F, "splinter twin this is not" (LR 879).
- **Cards LoL 504 flipped down after play:** `Command Decision` (to sideboard), `Set Phasers to . . .`,
  `Obsidian Order Operative`, `Final Frontier`, `Marooned`, `U.S.S. Voyager, Lone Wanderer`.

### Where the sources disagree

- **Green: last or first?** LoL 504 ranks green 5th of 5 for its lack of removal and puts U/G and R/G
  in its bottom tier. LLU 11-06 has U/G Strange New Worlds as one of the two most common early decks
  and says green has the most powerful rares; LR 878 expects U/G to be the most interesting deck.
  Alex (LLU) also noted "mostly green cards on top of 17Lands so far" and called that sample "even
  more suspect than usual". The current sample still says so: green commons fill most of the top of
  the GIH WR list and B/G and R/G post the best pair win rates, all inside wide error bars.
- **Speed: "aggro is back" vs "grindy".** Rough Drafts 86's thesis is that aggro is back, with real
  one-drops, a W/B anthem and reach. LoL 504 agrees ("aggressive decks are quite good"). Numot's
  Sealed games were "gritty grinding nonsense", and LR 878 hoped for a slow format like Reality
  Fracture. LLU 11-12 sits between: medium-fast, with explosive starts hard to come back from. Sealed
  is slower than draft, which explains part of the gap.
- **Beam Me Up: linchpin or footnote?** LoL 504: "the linchpin mechanic of the format", and the hosts
  happily beamed up five- and six-drops. LR 879 (LSV after early access): "not worth a lot… just
  worse than Flashback"; shade every LR 878 Beam Me Up grade down about half a grade. LLU 11-12: "not
  a central mechanic… it comes up here and there."
- **R/W: tier 1 or last?** LoL 504 moved R/W to tier 1 because 8 power is easy to reach. LLU ranks it
  10th ("the rewards need combat") and LR 878 is skeptical ("anytime I see eight power or greater,
  I'm like, really?"). Rough Drafts 86 is optimistic about all three Mardu pairs.
- **Is there enough removal?** LoL 504: "removal is excellent". LLU 11-12: removal feels scarce, and
  some of the best decks were "no-removal decks, all gas". LR 878: cheap red burn is plentiful.
- **Dilemma.** LoL 504: "not a thing", and `Tell Me What You See` was "pretty bad" played twice in one
  deck. Numot: `Tell Me What You See` "is actually great in my deck, I have so many dilemma cards",
  and his U/B dilemma Sealed deck went about 7-1. LR 879: 3–4 modal cards turn the rare payoffs on.
- **Soup.** LoL 504: "soup off". LLU 11-06 saw U/G often at 3–4 colours, with soup kept in check only
  by low power. Numot lost to five-colour Strange New Worlds decks more than once and played a
  four-colour deck himself (3-3).
- **Spacecraft at common.** LoL 504: common and uncommon ships "largely underwhelmed". LLU Sealed guide:
  "across the board pretty good", and `Suurok-Class Science Vessel` is a good common. Rough Drafts 86
  expects the manalith ship (`Class-F Shuttlecraft`) to be one of the top commons. LoL 504 moved
  `Suurok-Class Science Vessel` down to "only good in W/U with the right sequencing".
- **Sickbay.** C from both LLU hosts pre-play, then "I haven't seen Sickbay be bad ever" (LLU 11-12).
  Numot wonders whether the format is "slow enough" for clunky value cards like it.

### What the early 17Lands sample can and can't tell you

- About **8.7k games**. Only **57 of 336 cards** have a GIH WR (17Lands hides cards under 500 games
  drawn), and 54 of those 57 are commons, each on 500–1,300 games, so about ±3–4pp per card.
- Pair win rates sit on 450–970 games each (about ±3–5pp). The spread between the best and worst
  pair is 13pp, which is far more than a settled format shows; expect it to shrink.
- The players in this sample are early-access drafters: all decks win 55.0% and the 57 rated cards
  average 56.0% GIH WR, so read about 56% as average, not 50%.
- Nothing here outranks an expert read yet. Treat a number that agrees with the experts as mild
  support and one that disagrees as a question to watch.
