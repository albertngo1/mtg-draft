## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

The 17Lands numbers on these tiles are the spine of this document. What the three expert guides in
`draft-guides/` add is everything a win-rate column structurally cannot measure: how hard to commit
to a creature type, what a mechanic is actually for, how to build a convoke curve, and why the format
rewards a different kind of deck than most. That is what this brief collects.

**Sources:** **Rough Drafts** (seven episodes ending in a settled retrospective — the deepest
format-level thinking available for this set), **Lords of Limited** (a 2026-03-23 retrospective),
**NumotTheNummy** (a thin sample). No Limited Resources or Limited Level-Ups guide exists for ECL.

**Calibrate to the baseline:** the colour spread is nearly flat — game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR
runs **54.8% to 56.0%**, a 1.2pp band across all five. Colour-level differences are close to noise;
**archetype and card-level differences are not.** The sample is **22.2M games across 273 measured
cards**.

### What the data says

*(The full archetype table with metagame share renders directly above this brief.)*

**The five supported pairs are the format, and it is not close.** They take the **top five slots and
90.9% of the two-colour metagame**; the five unsupported pairs are **all below 52%** and together
account for 9%. This is the single most decisive archetype split in this reference, and it confirms
the Rough Drafts retrospective's settled read exactly: the official framing of Blight and Vivid as
"decks" for the off-pairs led the community astray. **They are modifiers you layer onto a supported
pair, not places to be.**

**Elves is the best deck and the most-drafted deck at once** — 56.36% on 23.8% of the field, a
contested lane that wins anyway. Then Merfolk (55.86%), Kithkin (55.72%) and Elementals (55.18%)
inside 0.7pp of each other, with Goblins a step behind at 53.06%.

**Colour order:** **G 55.99 ≈ U 55.97 > W 55.53 > B 55.32 > R 54.82.** Green edges it, which matches
Rough Drafts' read that green is the strongest colour on the depth of its commons and uncommons
rather than on the elf payoffs.

**The Eclipsed hybrid cycle is the best uncommon cycle in the set, and this is the format's most
important fact.** All five land in the top fifteen uncommons: **Eclipsed Elf 63.0% (IWD +8.1) ·
Eclipsed Kithkin 62.4% (+7.7) · Eclipsed Merrow 61.8% (+6.8) · Eclipsed Flamekin 59.9% (+6.7) ·
Eclipsed Boggart 58.9% (+6.2)**. These are precisely the *low-commitment* typal payoffs — they ask
for a few creatures of a type, not fifteen — which is why the "divorce the pair from its tribe"
advice below is the right way to draft. The one genuinely **high**-commitment elf payoff, Morcant's
Eyes, is excellent too (60.4%, +4.4) but is the exception rather than the model.

**The best uncommon in the set is Deepchannel Duelist (63.3%, IWD +8.9)** — the Merfolk lord.

**Best commons:** Dawnhand Eulogist (57.9%, and it wheels to ALSA 5.6), Assert Perfection (57.9%,
IWD +3.2), Scarblade Scout (57.4% at ALSA 5.2), Merrow Skyswimmer (57.1%, +2.5), Lys Alana Informant
(56.9%), **Kulrath Zealot (56.7%, IWD +4.0 at ALSA 5.4)**, Mistmeadow Council (56.6%), **Flamekin
Gildweaver (56.5%, +3.7 at ALSA 5.9)**.

### The draft plan

1. **Be in one of the five supported pairs.** Everything else is 3-6pp worse. If you find yourself
   drifting into an unsupported pair, that is a signal you have misread the seat, not that you have
   found something clever.
2. **Take the Eclipsed hybrid for your pair very highly** — they are five of the fifteen best
   uncommons and the cheapest way to make a typal deck work.
3. **Then draft the colours, not the tribe.** You want to *end up* in a supported pair, but very few
   cards demand a high creature-type count. Most payoffs are happy with five to eight of a type. A
   Black-Green deck with some goblin cards in it is fine; a Merfolk deck running a kithkin payoff off
   changelings is fine.
4. **Draft for the density of genuinely impactful cards, not for a smooth pile of playables.** This
   is Rough Drafts' central mid-format realisation and it is the most actionable thing here: ECL has
   an unusual tier spread — many A-level cards, unusually few generically-good B's. The "pack your
   deck with C+/B goodstuff and out-value them" plan that works in most formats **loses** in this one.
   The uncommons and rares carry games; the commons are role players.
5. **Hold removal for the lords and the engines.** With a flat colour spread and top-heavy card
   quality, the games are decided by whether their impactful card resolves and sticks — not by
   trading two damage into a two-drop.

### What the numbers can't tell you

- **Blight and Vivid are orthogonal vectors, not archetypes.** A single Prismabasher (56.5%, IWD
  +4.8) in a BG deck subtly changes what fills the back half of your deck — now the colour-count
  payoffs are live. A fully dedicated Vivid deck is a rarer, different thing. Same for blight: goblins
  can be a hard typal deck **or** a blight-burn grind deck, and those want different cards.
- **Changelings keep you open**, because they satisfy every typal payoff at once. That is what makes
  the low-commitment plan work in practice.
- **Convoke wants a genuinely low curve**, and vigilance on your attackers is load-bearing — attack
  with them, then still convoke your instant-speed spells after combat.
- **Creatures run small**, which is why a four-mana 6/6 plays as a real threat and why removal that
  scales past two damage is at a premium. It is also why Cinder Strike's blight-boosted mode matters:
  killing a four-mana 4/4 for one mana is the line.
- **The lords tax removal beyond their anthem.** Once you have played one, the opponent has to start
  respecting the next — which buys your other threats a turn.
- **High flash density punishes slamming into open mana.** Play around it more than you would in a
  format of this speed otherwise.
- **Two-drops without synergy are dead weight** — in a top-heavy format the curve effectively starts
  on three unless the two-drop is doing something.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **Creature counts run 15–20**, even in the Vivid and Blight builds.
- **Blight is upside only when you build for it.** The cards that ask you to blight are traps in a
  deck with nothing to gain from the counters.
- **Don't chase a contested tribal lane.** Being in a supported pair unlocks the gold lords, the
  Eclipsed cards and the Commands — but a *contested* lane makes your deck genuinely bad, and the
  changelings are what let you pivot cheaply.

### Where the field misprices cards

- **The shocklands are the format's most expensive habit.** Temple Garden (52.7%, IWD −0.7),
  Hallowed Fountain (52.9%, −0.6), Blood Crypt (52.4%, +0.1), Steam Vents (53.1%, +0.0), Overgrown
  Tomb (54.2%, +0.0) — all taken around **pick 3.7–4.2**, all with essentially **zero** draw impact.
  Fixing you don't need, bought with picks you do.
- **Cards taken early that shouldn't be:** **Chomping Changeling (49.3%, IWD −3.3 at ALSA 4.8)** is
  the worst of them, then Collective Inferno (48.6%, −1.9), Hexing Squelcher (50.7%, −0.6), Meanders
  Guide (54.1%, −0.3), Burdened Stoneback (53.0%, −0.2), Spiral into Solitude (54.3%, IWD +0.0).
  **Mischievous Sneakling (52.4%, −0.5)** deserves its own mention — a heavily-played UB common in the
  format's worst colour pair.
- **Cards that wheel and shouldn't** (ALSA 5.5+): **Dawnhand Eulogist (57.9% at 5.6)** is the biggest
  gap in the set, then Flamekin Gildweaver (56.5% at 5.9, IWD +3.7), Midnight Tilling (56.3% at 6.7),
  Temporal Cleansing (56.1% at 6.1), **Kindle the Inner Flame (55.3% at 6.3, IWD +3.3)**, Gravelgill
  Scoundrel (55.8% at 6.3), Flaring Cinder (55.2% at 6.1, +2.1), Rooftop Percher (55.0% at 6.2).
- **Cinder Strike is very good, and the "best common in the format" billing overshoots.** **55.7%
  with IWD +2.7 at ALSA 4.1** puts it around sixteenth among commons. The gap is instructive: in a
  deck built to blight it is the Flame Slash he describes, and in the average deck the additional
  cost is a real constraint. Take it high in a build that can pay; don't take it over the Eclipsed
  uncommon.
- **Thoughtweft Imbuer (58.3%, IWD +4.2) is the real "win a game below rare" card**, and it does not
  need a kithkin deck — changelings and a couple of one-drops are enough.
- **Moon-Vigil Adherents (58.6%, IWD +3.6, ALSA 3.2)** is confirmed as an overperformer in both Elves
  and Kithkin: it grows off every creature you control *and* every creature in your graveyard, and
  once large it dodges essentially all the red removal.
- **Bark of Doran does not hold up** — **49.4% with IWD −1.7 at ALSA 7.2**, on a small sample. The
  high-toughness-creature theory is sound; the card is not.
- **Sun-Dappled Celebrant (52.9%)**, **Evershrike's Gift (53.2%, IWD −0.5)**, **Goldmeadow Nomad
  (52.6%, −1.7)** and **Stalactite Dagger (51.3%, −0.7)** are the convoke-support cards that don't
  earn a slot. **Glister Bairn (52.3%, IWD +2.8)** has the build-around shape: bad on average, real
  when drawn in the right deck.
- **Auntie's Sentence is not being underrated** — 53.4% with IWD +0.4. The hunch that it might be a
  sleeper does not survive; it is exactly the mediocre top-deck it looks like.
- **Brambleback Brute (52.1% at ALSA 7.0)** and **Gutsplitter Gang (51.9% at ALSA 5.4)** are both
  below the bar. **Omni-Changeling (56.0%, IWD +2.5)** is fine rather than a breakout.

### Open questions the data can't settle

- **How much the "many A's, few B's" framing should change your picks.** It is a claim about the
  *shape* of the card pool rather than about any card, and a per-card win rate cannot test it
  directly. The archetype data is at least consistent with it: with the colour spread this flat and
  the supported pairs this far ahead of the unsupported ones, what separates decks is unlikely to be
  raw colour choice, and impactful-card density is a plausible candidate.
- **Whether Red-Green is a real deck or a green-goodstuff accident.** Rough Drafts reports drafting it
  repeatedly and reaching it off green's depth. The data has RG at **51.87% on 2.2% of the field** —
  a small, weak sample. Most likely both are true: it works for a strong drafter reading an open seat,
  and it is not a plan.
- **The format's own verdict.** Rough Drafts rates ECL highly and felt it rotated out early; Lords of
  Limited's retrospective is cooler on it. Not a pick-order input, but worth knowing which lens each
  guide note on a tile was written through.
