## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

Distilled from the three expert guides in `draft-guides/` so you don't need a second window. Sources
in priority order: **Limited Resources #767 Sunset Show** (the settled end-of-format retrospective,
plus five earlier episodes), **Lords of Limited "Saying Goodbye to Bloomburrow"** (2024-09-03, a full
50-takes retrospective, plus five gameplay episodes), **NumotTheNummy** (four end-of-format VODs,
2024-09-07 → 09-10 — a thin tier-2 sample, already recency-skewed). No Limited Level-Ups guide exists
for this set. On conflict: the 17Lands numbers on these tiles > end-of-format retrospectives >
release-week predictions.

**Every source here is a retrospective, and the data is the finished format — 27.6M games across 265
measured cards.** That is an unusually favourable setup: the hosts had already played the whole
format when they recorded, so where they still miss, the miss is real rather than premature. Several
headline retrospective calls do miss.

**Calibrate to the baseline:** BLB win rates run high and tight. Game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR
spans **54.1% to 56.9%**, and the four non-red colours sit inside 1.3pp of each other. A 56% card is
average. Read IWD — in a typal format it separates "a card good decks happen to run" from "a card
that wins the game you drew it in."

### The draft plan in five lines

1. **Start black or green — and black is very slightly ahead, not behind.** Game-weighted mono-colour
   GIH WR: **B 56.9 > G 56.5 > W 56.1 > U 55.6 > R 54.1.** Lords of Limited's retrospective leads with
   *"green is the best color, not close"*; the data has black on top by 0.4pp, which is inside the
   noise but definitely not "green, not close." What both retrospectives get unambiguously right is
   **red last, by 1.5pp** — a real gap in a format this compressed.
2. **Frogs is the best pair, and the two retrospectives split on it.** Gold-card GIH WR by pair:
   **UG 58.8 · BG 57.6 · WG 57.4 · WB 56.4 · BR 55.7 · UB 55.6 · RG 55.3 · WR 54.5 · WU 54.0 ·
   UR 52.7.** Limited Resources' Sunset Show calls Frogs the #1 archetype for strong players — that
   lands. Lords of Limited ranks it third behind BG Squirrels and GW Rabbits. ⚠ **BLB gold cards are
   only 3–4 per pair, so these are small samples**; trust the ordering at the extremes and the
   direction in the middle, not the exact rank.
3. **Blue is the cheapest edge in the format, and this is where the guides went furthest wrong.**
   Look at what wheels: of the twelve strongest cards still available at ALSA 5.5+, **nine are blue** —
   Pearl of Wisdom (56.4% at ALSA 6.8, IWD +5.8), Wishing Well (56.6% at 5.7, +5.5), Into the Flood
   Maw (56.5% at 5.7, +5.1), Dire Downdraft (56.6% at 6.4, +4.5), Spellgyre (55.9% at 5.8), Calamitous
   Tide (55.9% at 6.0), Lightshell Duo (55.6% at **7.0**), Dazzling Denial (55.3% at 6.6), Tidecaller
   Mentor (56.5% at 5.9). Limited Resources' early *"blue is extremely bad, don't draft it"* is the
   single most costly line in either guide, and their own Sunset Show reverses it.
4. **Take removal early; it is scarce and it does not wheel.** Both retrospectives say so and both are
   right. The benchmarks: **Savor (59.3%, IWD +4.9) is the best common in the set**, then Nocturnal
   Hunger (57.0%, +3.0, ALSA 3.4) and Take Out the Trash (56.9%, +2.8). Note that neither guide leads
   with Savor — it is the highest-win-rate common in BLB and gets a passing mention as a food
   sacrifice outlet.
5. **You want a two-drop and you want to be streamlined.** All three sources converge here and nothing
   in the data argues. Numot's version is the bluntest: a turn-two Cache Grab on the draw instead of
   developing is close to conceding, and hands whose first play is a three-drop are a mulligan
   question. The format's creature-type gold cards push you onto rails early, so lock the second
   colour by roughly pick 5–7.

### Where the experts were wrong — judged by 27.6M games

- **Fountainport Bell is the retrospective's flagship reversal and it is wrong.** Lords of Limited
  gave it the "Golden Egg Award," called it the biggest come-up on a card they had seen, and
  described it as near-mandatory in any deck with synergy triggers. It is **53.9% GIH with IWD of
  exactly +0.0 at ALSA 6.1** — a below-average common that wheels, and drawing it changes nothing.
- **Intrepid Rabbit did not take the white common crown.** Lords of Limited's retro named it the top
  white common over Carrot Cake. **Carrot Cake 58.3% beats Intrepid Rabbit 57.9%**, and Limited
  Resources' own upgrade of Carrot Cake (their review had it merely "solid") is the read that holds.
- **Bakersbane Duo is not the best green common.** Both guides treat it as green's headliner. **Cache
  Grab (57.7%, IWD +3.6) edges it (57.3%)**, and Scales of Shale — a *black* common — matches Cache
  Grab at 57.7% while wheeling to ALSA 6.0.
- **Tangle Tumbler is not GW's secret best card.** Lords of Limited calls it "secretly this deck's
  best card once assembled" and Numot builds food lines around it. **53.8% GIH with IWD −0.7 at
  ALSA 6.2.** It is a below-average uncommon that the field correctly passes.
- **The blue-wheeling signals are backwards in both directions.** Lords of Limited says Shoreline
  Looter "often wheels" (it does not — **ALSA 3.9**, and at 60.4% with IWD +8.2 it is the best blue
  uncommon in the set) and that Lightshell Duo *does not* wheel and must be taken on sight (it has
  **the latest ALSA of any real playable in the format at 7.0**). The Eddymurk Crab read is the same
  shape: seeing it at picks 4–5 is presented as proof nobody to your right is in blue, but its ALSA
  is 5.0, so picks 4–5 is simply where it normally goes.
- **Builder's Talent is the weaker half of a genuine host disagreement, and Ben was closer.** Ethan's
  position is build-around-it-and-never-pass-it; Ben's is that he would rather have Pond Prophet.
  **Builder's Talent is 56.8% at ALSA 5.2** — good, but it wheels to pick five, and it is third of
  five Talents. Limited Resources' Sunset line that the Talents are underrated "especially Builder's"
  is not supported. What *is* supported is the claim both retros make that **Innkeeper's (62.7%, IWD
  +10.1) and Hunter's (61.4%, +7.7) were too strong for the format.** Blacksmith's Talent, which
  Lords of Limited calls the RG glue card, is the worst of the five at **54.8%, IWD +0.4**.
- **"Don't play the Villages" is over-broad.** The blanket ban is right about three of them —
  Oakhollow (53.9%, IWD −1.3), Rockface (54.3%, −0.8), Lilypad (53.4%) — but **Mudflat Village
  (57.5%, +2.3) and Lupinflower Village (56.9%, +1.1) are perfectly good**, and Mudflat outperforms
  most commons in the set.
- **Otters are not a secret.** Numot's read that people undervalue UR and you can assemble an insane
  deck there because nobody wants it: **UR is the worst pair at 52.7%**, and its package backs that
  up — Otterball Antics 54.0%, Wildfire Howl 53.6%, Coruscation Mage 54.4%. Lords of Limited's flat
  *"not a good deck"* is the accurate half. (Their nomination of WU Birds as the format's worst
  archetype is one slot off: **WU 54.0% is 9th, UR 52.7% is 10th.**)
- **Bumbleflower's Sharepot is not a maindeck removal upgrade** (52.9% at ALSA 6.5), and **Heirloom
  Epic is not the card-advantage engine Numot credits it as** (53.8%, IWD −1.1). **Short Bow** (52.6%,
  −0.8) rounds out the same group.
- **Limited Resources' Sunset upgrades mostly land.** Thought-Stalker Warlock (60.8%, IWD +6.3),
  Darkstar Augur (60.4%, +6.4), Pond Prophet (58.0%, +5.2), Overprotect (58.2%, +4.2), Scales of Shale
  (57.7%, +3.2), Sunshower Druid (56.3%, +3.8) — every one of those reversals is confirmed. Their
  downgrades land too: Sunspine Lynx (**51.3%, IWD −2.2 at ALSA 3.4** — an early-pick trap),
  Plumecreed Mentor, Wandertale Mentor. On per-card movement this guide is the most reliable of the
  three; on colour and archetype ranking it was the least.
- **Red-White's problem is real and structural.** Lords of Limited's *"valiant fell flat"* verdict
  holds: **WR is 8th at 54.5%**, Heartfire Hero is a merely-fine 56.7%, and the archetype's actual
  power lives in rares — Manifold Mouse (63.1%, IWD +10.0) and Mabel (63.9%, +11.6). Without them it
  has no engine.

### Gameplay rules that actually change results

- **Forage off food tokens, not off your graveyard.** The predicted tension between forage and
  threshold effectively never materialises if you sequence correctly — the food-makers supply the
  fodder. Every source agrees and nothing contradicts it.
- **Play threshold as an aggressive mechanic.** Fill the yard through combat trades, not through
  self-mill spells. The surveil-style enablers look like the way in and are not what the deck wants.
- **Pay Intrepid Rabbit's offspring cost almost always.** One mana for the second body is the one
  offspring cost in the set that is close to mandatory; the rest are a bonus you take when convenient.
- **Gift is not usually a decision.** You gift when the card makes you; do not build around choosing
  to. The green fight spell is the exception worth noting — gifting the fish is often correct.
- **Count food before you commit to a race.** Food is everywhere and it is the reason low-curve
  aggro without late-game reach struggles: your small creatures stall out while they gain life.
- **Sequence your instant-speed food and lifegain into combat**, not before it — the sacrifice
  outlets double as combat tricks and force bad blocks.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **Streamlined two colours.** The fixing exists, but the format rewards tightness over greed;
  splashing is a concession, not a plan.
- **12+ creatures minimum**, and watch the two recurring failure modes Numot names on himself: too
  much top end, and too few two-drops. They compound — a clunky hand plus a missed land drop is how
  BLB games are lost.
- **One card-draw spell is good; two strands your curve.** Drawing your smoothing early instead of
  a body is the specific way this goes wrong.
- **Patchwork Banner is a genuine any-deck early pick** — **59.0% with IWD +4.6 at ALSA 3.1**, a
  top-three uncommon. Take it before you know your creature type and name it later.
- **Do not run two copies of the four-mana green fight spell.** Numot blames a loss squarely on
  doubling up on expensive reactive cards; the data agrees the card is fine (Polliwallop 56.7%) and
  the *stacking* is the error.
- **Take the uncommon Talents on sight.** They do not come back, and the two best are format-warping.

### Traps and sleepers the data settled

- **Worst early picks** (taken by pick 5 on average, negative IWD): Stormsplitter (**42.8%, IWD −7.6**
  — the worst mythic in the set by a distance), For the Common Good (45.6%, −4.3), Dawn's Truce
  (48.7%, −2.2), Mouse Trapper (51.8%, −2.7), Sunspine Lynx (51.3%, −2.2), Seedglaive Mentor (53.3%,
  −1.4), Mabel's Mettle (53.2%, −1.1), Baylen, the Haymaker (51.4%).
- **Cheapest edges — strong cards that wheel** (ALSA 5.5+): Ruthless Negotiation (58.0% at 5.5),
  Scales of Shale (57.7% at 6.0), Pearl of Wisdom (56.4% at **6.8**, IWD +5.8), Dire Downdraft (56.6%
  at 6.4), Tidecaller Mentor (56.5% at 5.9), Into the Flood Maw (56.5% at 5.7), Lightshell Duo (55.6%
  at 7.0). The pattern is the point: **blue, and cheap interaction.**
- **Vinereap Mentor (61.7%, IWD +7.9) is the best uncommon in the set** and the BG signpost — the
  strongest single piece of support for the Squirrels-are-great read, even though UG edges BG at the
  pair level.
- **Fireglass Mentor (58.0%, IWD +2.1) wheels to ALSA 5.0.** Lords of Limited is right that seeing it
  late means the RB lane is open — and right that they under-rated the archetype all format.
- **Nightwhorl Hermit is not a blue card worth wheeling for** — 50.2% at ALSA 7.8, near the bottom of
  the set.

### Cross-source disagreements, left unresolved on purpose

- **Builder's Talent (Ethan vs Ben).** Covered above — the numbers favour Ben, but Ethan's claim is
  about a built deck and the aggregate cannot see that. Treat it as a good card you take when it
  comes, not one you plan around from pick two.
- **Blue-Black's ceiling.** Both Lords of Limited hosts like it; they split on whether it or Rakdos is
  the better non-green black pair. The gold-card numbers put them within 0.1pp (**BR 55.7 vs UB
  55.6**) — genuinely a coin flip, so this one is unresolvable rather than unresolved.
- **How much the pair rankings matter at all.** Limited Resources' Sunset position is that the format
  self-corrected and the spread shrank to a few points, so no pair should be ranked out of contention
  late. Lords of Limited hands down a firm 1-to-10 list. Given how compressed the colour numbers are,
  the Limited Resources framing is the safer one to draft by — but the extremes (UG at the top, UR at
  the bottom) are real and worth respecting.

### Calibration note

Numot rates the format 4/10 and his sample here is four VODs — the thinnest coverage of any source in
this file, and worth weighting accordingly on anything he alone claims. The two podcast
retrospectives had played the entire format when they recorded, which makes their misses more
informative than usual: where a fully-informed retrospective and a 27.6M-game sample disagree, the
disagreement is almost always about a *synergy* card whose deck the host built and the aggregate
never saw. Fountainport Bell, Tangle Tumbler and Builder's Talent are all that shape. Weigh the
number for how good the card is, and the host for how to build the deck that wants it.
