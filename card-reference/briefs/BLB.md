## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

Every number lives on the card tiles below and in the archetype table above. This brief is the other
half: what three expert guides worked out that a win-rate column structurally cannot say — how to
enable a mechanic, how to sequence combat, what to mulligan, and how many copies to run.

**Sources:** Limited Resources (six episodes ending in the #767 Sunset Show), Lords of Limited (six
episodes ending in a 50-takes retrospective), NumotTheNummy (four end-of-format VODs — the thinnest
sample here, so weight anything only he says accordingly). No Limited Level-Ups guide exists for this
set. All three recorded their settled reads after playing the format out.

**One calibration to carry into the tiles:** BLB's win rates run high and tight, so the raw
percentage separates cards less than usual. What separates them is whether drawing the card changed
the game — in a typal format that is the difference between a card good decks happen to run and a
card that wins games.

### What the data says

*(The full archetype table renders directly above this brief.)*

**This is the flattest format in the reference.** The top five pairs are effectively tied, which is
exactly Limited Resources' settled position: the format self-corrected, and any pair can win if it's
open. **Draft the open seat** and don't rank pairs out of contention.

The real separation is at the bottom. **The two blue pairs finish last and next-to-last**, and
between them they take up a small slice of the field. The field is correct about them.

**Colour order: black and green at the top, effectively tied, then white, then blue — and red well
behind.** The "green is the best colour, not close" framing overstates it; black is right there. Red
being last by a real margin is the actual gap, and it shows up in the archetypes too: Rakdos wins
because *black* is carrying it.

**The best commons:** Savor is the best common in the set and gets only passing mention in every
guide. Behind it, Carrot Cake, Cache Grab, Scales of Shale (a black common that wheels), Intrepid
Rabbit and Bakersbane Duo. **At uncommon:** Vinereap Mentor, Hunter's Talent, Thought-Stalker Warlock,
Shoreline Looter, Darkstar Augur, and Patchwork Banner — a genuine any-deck early pick.

### The draft plan

1. **Start black or green and let the seat decide the pair.** Both colours support several of the
   better archetypes, and the pair rankings are too flat to commit early on principle.
2. **Take removal early; it is scarce and it does not wheel.** Savor, Nocturnal Hunger, Take Out the
   Trash.
3. **You want a two-drop, and you want to be streamlined.** All three sources converge, and Numot's
   version is bluntest: a turn-two cantrip on the draw instead of a body is close to conceding, and a
   hand whose first play is a three-drop is a mulligan question. The creature-type gold cards push
   you onto rails early, so lock the second colour around pick 5–7.
4. **Blue's cards are the cheapest late picks in the format even though blue's decks are the worst.**
   Most of the strongest cards still on the table late are blue. Take them in **UG or UB** — just
   don't end up in the two blue pairs at the bottom of the table.
5. **Take the uncommon Talents on sight.** They don't come back, and **Innkeeper's and Hunter's are
   format-warping.** The other three — Builder's, Scavenger's, Blacksmith's — are ordinary.

### What the numbers can't tell you

- **Forage off food tokens, not off your graveyard.** The predicted tension between forage and
  threshold effectively never materialises if you sequence correctly, because the food-makers supply
  the fodder. All three sources agree and it is the single most useful mechanical insight for the set.
- **Play threshold as an aggressive mechanic.** Fill the yard through combat trades, not self-mill
  spells. The surveil-style enablers look like the way in and are not what the deck wants.
- **Pay Intrepid Rabbit's offspring cost almost always.** One mana for the second body is the one
  offspring cost in the set that is close to mandatory; the rest are a bonus you take when convenient.
- **Gift is rarely a decision.** You gift when the card makes you. The green fight spell is the
  exception worth knowing: gifting the fish is often correct.
- **Count food before committing to a race.** Food is everywhere, and it is precisely why low-curve
  aggro without late-game reach struggles — your small creatures stall while they gain life.
- **Sequence instant-speed food and lifegain into combat**, not before it. The sacrifice outlets
  double as combat tricks and force bad blocks.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **Streamlined two colours.** The fixing exists but the format rewards tightness; splashing is a
  concession, not a plan.
- **12+ creatures minimum**, and watch the two failure modes Numot names on himself: too much top end,
  and too few two-drops. They compound — a clunky hand plus a missed land drop is how BLB games are
  lost.
- **One card-draw spell is good; two strands your curve.** Drawing your smoothing instead of a body is
  the specific way that goes wrong.
- **Don't stack expensive reactive spells.** Numot blames a loss squarely on doubling up on the
  four-mana green removal; the card itself is fine and the stacking is the error.
- **Two of the five Villages are worth playing, three are not.** Mudflat and Lupinflower are good;
  Oakhollow, Rockface and Lilypad are not. The blanket "don't play the Villages" is over-broad.

### Where the field misprices cards

- **Wheels, and shouldn't.** Nearly all of it is cheap blue interaction: Ruthless Negotiation, Scales
  of Shale, **Pearl of Wisdom**, Dire Downdraft, Wishing Well, Tidecaller Mentor, Into the Flood Maw,
  **Lightshell Duo** (the latest-going real playable in the set) and Dazzling Denial.
- **Taken early, and shouldn't be.** **Stormsplitter is the worst mythic in the set by a distance.**
  For the Common Good, Dawn's Truce, Mouse Trapper, **Sunspine Lynx**, Seedglaive Mentor, Mabel's
  Mettle and Baylen all go earlier than they earn.
- **Fountainport Bell is not the format-defining artifact it's billed as.** It wheels, and drawing it
  changes nothing at all.
- **Tangle Tumbler is not a hidden Selesnya finisher** either.
- **Calibrating blue signals:** Shoreline Looter is the best blue uncommon and does *not* wheel, while
  Lightshell Duo goes later than anything else playable. Those are the two to read. **Eddymurk Crab is
  not a signal** — mid-pack is simply where it goes.
- **Carrot Cake edges Intrepid Rabbit for best white common**, and **Cache Grab edges Bakersbane Duo
  for best green common.** Both are close enough not to change a pick, but the guides name the
  runner-up in each case.
- **Below the bar despite being recommended:** Bumbleflower's Sharepot, Heirloom Epic, Short Bow.
- **Otterball Antics, Wildfire Howl and Coruscation Mage are why Izzet is last.** There is no hidden
  Otters deck.
- **Nightwhorl Hermit is near the bottom of the set.** Not every late blue card is a bargain.

### Open questions the data can't settle

- **Builder's Talent.** One Lords of Limited host builds around it and never passes it; the other
  would rather have Pond Prophet. The aggregate favours the sceptic, but it cannot see an assembled
  Talent deck — exactly the kind of claim a per-card win rate is blind to. Take it when it comes;
  don't plan around it from pick two.
- **How much the pair ranking should influence you at all.** Given how tightly the top five bunch,
  the honest answer is: very little, above the seat you're actually being passed. The extremes (Izzet
  and Azorius at the bottom) are real; the middle is not worth steering by.
