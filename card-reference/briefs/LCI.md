## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

> **Read the guide notes with one caveat.** LCI's expert notes are parsed from YouTube auto-caption
> transcripts, and LCI is a set whose names caption badly ("Chupacabra Echo" for Chupacabra Echo is
> fine; "Cavernous Ma", "Zoa lava tongue", "Dino tomaton" are not). The fuzzy matcher recovers most of
> them, but a handful of notes on this page sit on the wrong card — the 📘 blurb describes a card that
> isn't the one in the picture. Six were caught while writing these takes (Idol of the Deep King,
> Deepfathom Echo, Grasping Shadows, Pathfinding Axejaw, Helping Hand, Corpses of the Lost) and the 🤖
> take on each says so. **Where a note and the oracle text disagree, the oracle text wins.**

### The draft plan in five lines

1. **Blue is the best colour and the only flexible one.** Game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR: **U 56.99% ·
   W 56.57% · G 56.26% · R 56.08% · B 54.98%.** That is a 2.0pp spread with black alone at the bottom —
   every other colour is inside 0.9pp of each other. Blue is where you start when you don't know yet.
2. **The format rewards a cheap curve with evasion, crafted into a late game.** Cheap evasive creatures
   beat big ground bodies. The top three commons in the set are **Oltec Cloud Guard (59.27%)**,
   **Miner's Guidewing (59.19%)** and **Waterwind Scout (59.05%)** — a 2-drop, a 1-drop flyer and a
   2-drop flyer, all white or blue.
3. **Craft is the axis that defines the format.** A craft card is a cheap play *and* a late-game mana
   sink, so it double-dips on curve. Craft first, flip when convenient: **Clay-Fired Bricks (60.16%)**
   and **Spring-Loaded Sawblades (59.94%)** are top-10 uncommons on their front faces alone.
4. **The named trainwreck is the half-committed white deck.** White cannot win a long game; a white deck
   that isn't hyper-aggressive loses to any medium dino deck with 4- and 5-power creatures. Lords of
   Limited's framing: avoid white, or fully commit to aggro.
5. **Yes, commit — the pillars don't bleed.** Artifact/tempo (WU, UR), dinosaurs (RG, RW) and black
   descend (BG, UB) share almost no cards. Blue lets you stay open through mid-pack 1; every other
   colour commits you once you take its bomb.

### Gameplay rules that actually change results

- **Descend does not count tokens.** Permanent *cards* in the graveyard only. This is the single
  biggest intuition failure in the set — a token-maker is not a descend enabler, and the thresholds
  (4 and 8) are high enough that getting this wrong kills the deck.
- **Explore on the ground is a downgrade, not an upgrade.** You want the +1/+1 counter, not the land;
  on a non-evasive body, missing is devastating. The exemplar: **River Herald's Boon**-style explore
  creatures lose to a plain 3/3. On a one- or two-drop **flyer** the same variance is fine, because the
  ceiling ends games — this is why Spyglass Siren is a top-3 uncommon and the ground explorers are not.
- **Captain Storm, Cosmium Raider must die on sight.** Kenji calls it "one of the most egregious cards
  in the format"; a turn-two Storm with no answer is "a very quick loss." Weight cheap instant-speed
  interaction up in any deck that will face UR.
- **Grade a craft card on its front face.** Tithing Blade is premium because of the **edict**, not
  because you flip it. Once you stop holding craft cards hoping to flip them and just cast them as the
  cheap spell they are, the whole mechanic gets better.
- **Caves are a tool, not a plan.** Don't P1P1 Calamitous Cave-In. Draft normal signals, note caves
  going late, and build the package from pick 6-9 if it's open. 5-7 caves is plenty with Compass Gnome
  and Scampering Surveyor to find them.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **17-18 lands.** 18 for caves/control (mana sinks, multiple cave hits); 17 for aggressive white/red;
  **16** in blue-white flyers (Kenji's build) or in frog-ramp turbo-mill green-black.
- **Curve:** take two-drops aggressively. In a craft format a two-drop is also a four-drop, so cheap
  plays are never dead late. The decks that lose are the ones with no turn-two play.
- **Black removal skeleton, if you are the descend/control deck:** ~3 Dead Weight, 2 Tithing Blade,
  1-2 Join the Dead. Dead Weight covers the one- and two-drops; Tithing Blade covers three- and
  four-drops on the edict; **Join the Dead is the only thing that answers an actual bomb — don't zero
  out on it.** Black cannot kill a big threat with Dead Weight alone.
- **Artifact count matters in UR.** The pirate/artifact deck wants **9+ artifacts** and ways to make
  more. Kenji's rule: don't run land cyclers in it — they dilute the artifact count and don't beat down.
- **Splashing is cheap here.** Hidden/cave lands and Sunken Citadel make a splash for a bomb nearly
  free. A splash for one over-the-top card is correct; a three-colour goodstuff pile is not — the best
  three-colour deck, Jund (BRG), still only managed 51.86%.
- **Colour requirements for WU flyers:** Kenji ran 10-7 or 9-7, wanting at least 8 Plains — the white
  cards are the double-pip ones.

### Where the experts were wrong — judged by the data

Scored against the 17Lands numbers on this same page (27.8M PremierDraft games, 1,029,185 two-colour games).

| Call | Source | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Blue-green is the worst archetype by data" | LoL | **Wrong.** GU finished **8th at 53.12%**, *above* WB (52.87%) and BR (52.48%). GU is thin and low-share (3.9%), not the floor. **Rakdos is the real floor.** |
| Gruul rated **B+** | LoL | **Underrated.** RG finished **3rd at 56.84%**, ahead of two pairs LoL ranked above it. |
| UB is "A / the best descend shell" | LoL | **Overrated.** UB finished **7th at 53.66%** on the largest descend share (8.8%). BG, rated B+, finished 6th (54.42%). Descend asked the most and returned the least. |
| Selesnya — never discussed | LoL | **Missed.** GW finished **5th at 55.45%** on the smallest share in the format (5.5%). The genuinely open seat. |
| "Black is tied for #3 with white" | LoL | **Wrong.** Black is **last at 54.98%**, 1.1pp behind 4th. Green, which LoL called the worst colour, is **3rd at 56.26%**. |
| "Blue-white is the best deck" | LoL | **Right.** WU **1st at 57.33%**, and the most-drafted pair (17.6%). |
| Orzhov is the biggest trap archetype | LoL | **Right.** WB **9th at 52.87%** — mediocre white aggro stapled to mediocre black descend. |
| "Deep-Cavern Bat is the #3-or-better uncommon in the set" | LoL | **Right, exactly.** It is **#3 at 61.88%**, behind only Zoetic Glyph (63.00%) and Spyglass Siren (62.62%), and leads all three on IWD at **+9.6**. |
| Take Spyglass Siren over Petrify P1P1 | Numot | **Right.** 62.62% vs 57.86%. |
| "Staunch Crewmate over the 6/6 rare — it's cheaper in this more aggressive format" | Numot | **Right.** Crewmate is a 60.41% uncommon; the expensive ground rares he passed on are nowhere near it. |
| "Burning Sun's Cavalry is unplayable filler" (early access) | LoL, self-corrected | The late correction won — LoL flagged this themselves by 2023-12-04. |

The pattern: **LoL's archetype calls at the top were right and its calls at the bottom were wrong.** It
correctly identified the best deck and the trap deck, and mis-ranked the entire middle-to-bottom of the
format — including inverting green and black as colours.

### Traps and sleepers the data settled

**Overdrafted (early ALSA, mediocre win rate):**

| Card | ALSA | GIH | IWD | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Treasure Map** | **2.0** | 51.54% | **−2.2** | The most overdrafted card in the set. Taken in the top two picks, performs like a filler common. Negative IWD means you were better off not drawing it. |
| **Get Lost** | 2.7 | 50.96% | −2.1 | A Constructed staple that reads like premium removal and isn't — the Map tokens hand the opponent real value in a format where artifacts matter. |
| **Gishath, Sun's Avatar** | 2.5 | 51.31% | +0.4 | Eight mana in a format that is faster than it looks. The mythic tax on a deck that has to survive to cast it. |
| **Cavern of Souls** | 3.1 | 51.36% | −0.4 | A Constructed rare doing nothing in Limited. |
| **Matzalantli, the Great Door** | 3.7 | 50.04% | +0.2 | The literal coin-flip card of the set. |

**Underdrafted (late ALSA, real win rate):**

| Card | ALSA | GIH | IWD | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Staggering Size** | **7.3** | 57.49% | +1.7 | A top-12 common wheeling past pick 7. The card that closes RG games, and the field never respected it. |
| **Fanatical Offering** | 6.7 | 55.57% | **+3.8** | Highest IWD of any late-wheeling common — a real engine in BR/BG attrition that nobody took. |
| **Another Chance** | 6.5 | 55.60% | +3.2 | The recursion piece the descend decks are built on, free at pick 6-7. |
| **Unlucky Drop** | 7.8 | 56.66% | −0.5 | Wheels almost every time; a fine playable, if a replacement-level one. |
| **Brackish Blunder** | 6.0 | 56.80% | +0.5 | Cheap blue tempo in the best colour, at pick 6. |

**The top of the set, for anchoring:** Bonehoard Dracosaur (**70.15%** GIH, ALSA 1.1) is the best card
in LCI by a clear margin, then Aclazotz, Deepest Betrayal (68.36%) and Palani's Hatcher (67.48%).

### Cross-source disagreements, left unresolved on purpose

- **Pit of Offerings.** LoL's Ben thinks it's strong in control shells; Ethan called it "quite playable"
  then hedged on actual picks. No settled verdict across the sampled episodes.
- **How good caves really is as an archetype.** Ethan built an entire Arena Open deck around it; the
  2024-01-24 video shows him going 0-3 with it. Ben was the skeptic. Both agree Calamitous Cave-In is
  the linchpin — the disagreement is floor versus ceiling, and the sample is one Open run.
- **Descend threshold design.** Ben thinks 4 and 8 are close to right; Ethan would have shipped 3 and 6.
  Philosophical, but it maps onto a real question: whether descend was under-supported or just slow.
  The data's answer — UB 7th, BG 6th — leans Ethan.

### Calibration note

**Lords of Limited** — 6 episodes, 2023-11-10 → 2024-01-24, so the full arc of the format. The
2023-11-10 early-access draft is blind first reps and should be read as hypotheses only; the
**2024-01-08 "Redesigning Cards for LCI"** post-mortem is the authoritative source and the
**2023-12-04 "Is Black the Worst Color"** pod is the best archetype reference. Where the guide's notes
conflict internally, the later episode wins, and the supersession table in the source guide records six
cases where it does.

**Numot (Kenji)** — a tier-2 sample for him: 4 VODs, all from **2024-01-15 → 2024-01-18**, i.e. the
last week of the format. That means his reads are settled but narrow, weighted toward the two decks he
actually played (green-black turbo self-mill, blue-white flyers). His notes cover 11 cards on this page
versus LoL's 57 — thin coverage, high confidence on what it covers.

Neither source graded the set card-by-card, and **no reviewer grade file exists for LCI at all** — no
CardGameBase, Draftsim or Limited Resources list. The GIH WR column here is doing more work than on any
other set in this reference, and at 27.8M games it can carry it.
