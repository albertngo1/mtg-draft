## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

Every number lives on the card tiles below and in the archetype table above. This brief is the other
half: what the expert guides worked out that a win-rate column structurally cannot say — how to turn
on Delirium, what Manifest Dread is actually doing, which removal answers what, and when to commit to
a lane.

**Sources:** Lords of Limited (six episodes, from a pre-release crash course through to a settled
Gruul mythic-ladder grind months later) and NumotTheNummy (a thin tier-2 end-of-format sample). No
Limited Resources or Limited Level-Ups guide exists for this set — DSK is one of the narrowest-sourced
sets in this reference, so lean on the tiles.

**One calibration to carry into the tiles:** the colours here are close enough that colour-level
differences are near noise. What separates cards is whether drawing one changed the game.

### What the data says

*(The full archetype table renders directly above this brief.)*

**The two white pairs are the best archetypes, and the graveyard decks are not.** That is the single
most useful thing on this page, because DSK reads like a graveyard format — Delirium, Manifest Dread
and the Eerie/reanimator shells are what the set is *about*. But **Golgari, Orzhov and Dimir all
finish in the bottom half**, while Boros and Azorius — the two pairs least entangled with the
graveyard — take the top two spots and a third of the metagame between them.

**The bottom four pairs are genuinely bad and genuinely rare.** The format is top-heavy: six real
decks and four that aren't.

**Colour order: white, blue and red bunched at the top, green just behind, black last.** Black drags,
and its pairs are scattered through the bottom of the table.

**The best uncommons are white and cheap.** **Sheltered by Ghosts** is the best card in the set
outside the rares, then Midnight Mayhem, Unnerving Grasp, Optimistic Scavenger, Oblivious Bookworm,
Under the Skin, **Disturbing Mirth** (which wheels) and Gremlin Tamer.

**Best commons:** Glimmerburst, **Trapped in the Screen**, Scorching Dragonfire, Spineseeker
Centipede, Glassworks // Shattered Yard, Unable to Scream, and **Final Vengeance** — which comes back
later than it should.

### The draft plan

1. **Default to a white pair — Boros or Azorius.** They are the two best archetypes and between them
   a third of the metagame, which means both are contested and both win anyway.
2. **Take cheap exile removal above its rate.** Putting things in graveyards *helps* your opponent in
   this format, so exile and tuck effects are worth a premium. Trapped in the Screen, Sheltered by
   Ghosts, and Scorching Dragonfire's exile clause are the benchmarks.
3. **Four toughness is the magic number.** The two premier cheap removal spells are a deal-3 and a
   −3/−3, so a body above that line dodges a huge share of the format's interaction. Prioritise
   creatures that live through it, and expect your own deal-3 effects to underperform their
   reputation.
4. **Take powerful mono-coloured cards early and commit late.** Both hosts land here: starting a
   colour pair too early traps you in a weak seat. Exception — open a signpost bomb with signals that
   it's wide open, and commit immediately.
5. **You need a turn-two play.** The format's must-answer two-drops — Gremlin Tamer, Irreverent
   Gremlin, Fear of Infinity — punish a slow start hard enough that hands without one are a mulligan
   question on the draw.

### What the numbers can't tell you

- **Artifacts are the hardest Delirium type to get naturally.** That is the whole practical difficulty
  of the mechanic, and it's why the "glue" cards matter more than their rate suggests — the artifact
  equipment, the fetch-land, the artifact-creature bodies, and the common that puts a sorcery plus a
  creature-or-land in the yard in one shot.
- **Manifest Dread does two things at once and you choose which.** A face-down 2/2 you can flip later
  *and* a card into the graveyard for Delirium. Which half you need changes turn to turn — that is
  the read a win rate can't show you.
- **Rooms are a deckbuilding commitment, not a free inclusion.** They count as enchantments for Eerie
  and as permanents that left the battlefield for the sacrifice payoffs, but a deck that wants them
  has to actually be built for them.
- **Exiling versus killing matters more here than almost anywhere.** Against a Delirium or reanimator
  opponent, a kill spell is doing part of their work. Read the removal on your tiles with that in mind.
- **Survival triggers in the second main phase**, which rewards attacking and snowballs — but see
  below on how that actually worked out.
- **Plan your splash early.** Green gets extra fixing on top of the colourless fixing everyone has,
  which is what makes three-colour graveyard piles reachable. Boros is the one archetype that does
  **not** want to splash and benefits least from the options.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **16–17 lands.** With the fetch-land, the clunky land-cyclers at common in every colour, and
  Manifest Dread smoothing, 16 is genuinely available in a two-colour deck with fixing.
- **Don't build the graveyard deck by default.** The mechanics point that way and the archetype
  results do not — commit to Delirium or reanimator only when the lane is open and you have multiple
  payoffs, not because the set is themed around it.

### Where the field misprices cards

- **Taken early, and shouldn't be.** **Dazzling Theater // Prop Room is the worst early pick in this
  entire reference** — taken in the first five picks and near the bottom of the set. Behind it:
  Doomsday Excruciator, Maddening Hex, Savior of the Small, Charred Foyer // Warped Space, Orphans of
  the Wheat, Fear of Exposure, Marvin, Patched Plaything, **Kona, Rescue Beastie** (a top-three pick
  that loses games) and Meathook Massacre II.
- **Wheels, and shouldn't** — cheap interaction again: **Commune with Evil**, Vanish from Sight, Turn
  Inside Out, Innocuous Rat, Twist Reality, Winter's Intervention, Don't Make a Sound, Friendly Ghost.
- **Selesnya's Survival package is where the theme failed.** The pair finishes near the bottom and its
  two signposts, Baseball Bat and Shrewd Storyteller, are the evidence. The mechanic snowballs when it
  works, and the decks built for it didn't.
- **Roaring Furnace // Steaming Sauna is the best Room in the set by a distance**, and Izzet finishing
  ninth is not its fault.
- **Nowhere to Run is black's best card** and one of the two removal spells that defines the
  four-toughness line.
- **Threats Around Every Corner is the green uncommon that actually delivers;** Wickerfolk Thresher is
  the one taken far above its worth.
- **Terramorphic Expanse is fixing plus a Delirium type, not a high pick.** It enables; it doesn't win.
- **Rite of the Moth and Miasma Demon are the Orzhov reanimator signposts**, and Orzhov is near the
  bottom. The reanimator plan is available and it is not good.
- **Late for a reason:** Cracked Skull, Cautious Survivor, Intruding Soulrager, Ghostly Keybearer.
- **Pyroclasm is a sideboard card, not a maindeck sweeper** — it kills most of your own board outside
  a dedicated control build.

### Open questions the data can't settle

- **Is the graveyard theme worth building for at all?** The mechanics are deep, the payoffs are real
  card-for-card (Drag to the Roots, Broodspinner, Under the Skin), and the archetypes still finish in
  the bottom half. The most likely reconciliation is the one the guides gesture at: the graveyard
  cards are good, the *dedicated* decks are too slow, and the right amount of Delirium is
  "incidental" rather than "built-around." The aggregate cannot separate those two builds, so this
  stays open.
- **How aggressive is the format really?** The guides call it midrange and note that five- and
  six-drop uncommons are legitimate early picks, yet the two winning archetypes are the two white
  tempo pairs. Treat the format as midrange in card quality and tempo in tactics.
