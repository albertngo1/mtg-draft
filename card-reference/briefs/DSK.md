## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

The 17Lands numbers on these tiles are the spine of this document. What the two expert guides in
`draft-guides/` add is everything a win-rate column structurally cannot measure: how to turn on
Delirium, what Manifest Dread is actually doing, which removal answers what, and when to commit to a
lane. That is what this brief collects.

**Sources:** Lords of Limited (six episodes, from a pre-release crash course through to a settled
Gruul mythic-ladder grind months later) and NumotTheNummy (a thin tier-2 end-of-format sample). No
Limited Resources or Limited Level-Ups guide exists for this set — DSK is one of the narrowest-sourced
sets in this reference, so lean on the numbers.

**Calibrate to the baseline:** game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR runs **54.9% to 56.3%** — the four
non-black colours sit inside 0.6pp of each other, so colour-level differences are near noise here.
**IWD is what separates.** The sample is **34.6M games across 272 measured cards**.

### What the data says

*(The full archetype table with metagame share renders directly above this brief.)*

**The two white pairs are the best archetypes, and the graveyard decks are not.** That is the single
most useful thing on this page, because DSK reads like a graveyard format — Delirium, Manifest Dread
and the Eerie/reanimator shells are what the set is *about*. But **Golgari finishes 6th, Orzhov 8th
and Dimir 10th**, while Boros and Azorius — the two pairs least entangled with the graveyard — take
the top two spots on 33% of the metagame between them.

**The bottom four are all under 53%** and together make up only **21%** of the field, so the format is
genuinely top-heavy: there are six real decks and four that aren't.

**Colour order:** **W 56.26 > U 56.08 > R 56.03 > G 55.74 > B 54.87.** Black is last and it drags —
its three pairs finish 5th, 6th, 8th and 10th.

**The best uncommons are white and cheap:** **Sheltered by Ghosts (61.6%, IWD +7.9, ALSA 2.1)** is the
best card in the set outside the rares, then **Midnight Mayhem (61.2%, +5.3)**, **Unnerving Grasp
(60.8%, +6.9)**, **Optimistic Scavenger (60.7%, +6.0)**, **Oblivious Bookworm (60.6%, +7.0)**, **Under
the Skin (60.3%, +6.7)**, **Disturbing Mirth (60.0%, +6.9 — and it wheels to ALSA 5.1)**, **Gremlin
Tamer (60.0%, +6.0)**.

**Best commons:** Glimmerburst (57.6%, IWD +3.5), **Trapped in the Screen (57.6%, +3.1 at ALSA 2.8)**,
Scorching Dragonfire (57.4%, +2.8), Spineseeker Centipede (57.4%, +3.4), Glassworks // Shattered Yard
(57.2%, +3.3), Unable to Scream (57.1%, +3.2), **Final Vengeance (56.8%, +4.3 at ALSA 5.4)**.

### The draft plan

1. **Default to a white pair — Boros or Azorius.** They are the two best archetypes and between them
   a third of the metagame, which means both are contested and both win anyway.
2. **Take cheap exile removal above its rate.** Putting things in graveyards *helps* your opponent in
   this format, so exile and tuck effects are worth a premium. **Trapped in the Screen (57.6%)**,
   **Sheltered by Ghosts (61.6%)**, and Scorching Dragonfire's exile clause are the benchmarks.
3. **Four toughness is the magic number.** The two premier cheap removal spells are a deal-3 and a
   −3/−3, so a body above that line dodges a huge share of the format's interaction. Prioritise
   creatures that live through it, and count on your deal-3 effects underperforming their reputation.
4. **Take powerful mono-coloured cards early and commit late.** Both hosts land here: starting a
   colour pair too early traps you in a weak seat. Exception — open a signpost bomb with signals that
   it's wide open, and commit immediately.
5. **You need a turn-two play.** The format's must-answer two-drops (Gremlin Tamer 60.0%, Irreverent
   Gremlin 57.6%, Fear of Infinity 54.0%) punish a slow start hard enough that hands without one are
   a mulligan question on the draw.

### What the numbers can't tell you

- **Artifacts are the hardest Delirium type to get naturally.** That is the whole practical difficulty
  of the mechanic, and it's why the "glue" cards matter more than their rate suggests — the artifact
  equipment, the fetch-land, the artifact-creature bodies, and the common that puts a sorcery plus a
  creature-or-land in the yard in one shot.
- **Manifest Dread does two things at once and you choose which.** A face-down 2/2 you can flip later
  *and* a card into the graveyard for Delirium. Which half you need changes turn to turn — that is
  the read the win rate can't show you.
- **Rooms are a deckbuilding commitment, not a free inclusion.** They count as enchantments for Eerie
  and as permanents that left the battlefield for the sacrifice payoffs, but a deck that wants them
  has to actually be built for them.
- **Exiling versus killing matters more here than almost anywhere.** Against a Delirium or reanimator
  opponent, a kill spell is doing part of their work. Read the removal on your tiles with that in mind.
- **Survival triggers in the second main phase**, which rewards attacking and snowballs — but see
  below on how that actually worked out.
- **Plan your splash early.** Green gets extra fixing on top of the colourless fixing everyone has,
  which is what makes three-colour graveyard piles reachable. Boros is the one archetype that does
  **not** want to splash and benefits least from the options.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **16–17 lands.** With the fetch-land, the clunky land-cyclers at common in every colour, and
  Manifest Dread smoothing, 16 is genuinely available in a two-colour deck with fixing.
- **Don't build the graveyard deck by default.** The mechanics point that way and the archetype
  numbers do not — commit to Delirium or reanimator only when the lane is open and you have multiple
  payoffs, not because the set is themed around it.

### Where the field misprices cards

- **Cards taken early that shouldn't be** (negative IWD by pick 5): **Dazzling Theater // Prop Room
  (39.8%, IWD −8.7, still taken by pick 4.7 — the worst early pick in this entire reference)**,
  Doomsday Excruciator (44.2%, −4.8), Maddening Hex (47.0%, −2.4), **Savior of the Small (50.9%,
  −1.9)**, Charred Foyer // Warped Space (49.7%, −1.2), **Orphans of the Wheat (53.1%, −1.4)**, Fear of
  Exposure (52.8%, −1.1), Marvin, Murderous Mimic (50.5%, −0.9), Patched Plaything (54.1%, −0.9),
  **Kona, Rescue Beastie (50.3% while being taken at pick 2.6)**, Meathook Massacre II (49.2%).
- **Cards that wheel and shouldn't** (ALSA 5.5+): **Commune with Evil (56.8% at 5.5, IWD +5.4)**,
  Vanish from Sight (56.8% at 6.5), Turn Inside Out (57.0% at 5.8), Innocuous Rat (55.7% at 5.6),
  Twist Reality (55.5% at 6.6), Winter's Intervention (55.1% at 5.7, +3.1), Don't Make a Sound (55.2%
  at 6.9), Friendly Ghost (55.6% at 6.1). Cheap interaction again.
- **Selesnya's Survival package is where the theme failed.** WG is 7th at 52.74% and its two signposts
  are the evidence: **Baseball Bat 51.0% (IWD +0.1)** and **Shrewd Storyteller 50.7% (−0.9)**. The
  mechanic snowballs when it works and the decks built for it didn't.
- **Roaring Furnace // Steaming Sauna (60.3%, IWD +10.2)** is the best Room in the set by a distance,
  and Izzet finishing 9th is not its fault.
- **Nowhere to Run (57.9%, IWD +6.2 at ALSA 2.7)** is black's best card and one of the two removal
  spells that defines the four-toughness line.
- **Threats Around Every Corner (58.6%, IWD +5.5)** is the green uncommon that actually delivers;
  **Wickerfolk Thresher (55.0%, IWD +0.8 at ALSA 2.9)** is the one taken far above its worth.
- **Terramorphic Expanse is fixing plus a Delirium type, not a high pick** — 55.3% with **IWD +0.7**.
  It enables; it doesn't win.
- **Rite of the Moth (54.7%) and Miasma Demon (53.4%)** are the Orzhov reanimator signposts, and
  Orzhov is 8th. The reanimator plan is available and it is not good.
- **Cracked Skull (52.3% at ALSA 7.7)**, **Cautious Survivor (52.5% at 7.0)**, **Intruding Soulrager
  (53.1% at 6.8)** and **Ghostly Keybearer (52.9%)** are all late for a reason.
- **Pyroclasm (52.4%, IWD +2.7)** is a sideboard card, not a maindeck sweeper — it kills most of your
  own board outside a dedicated control build.

### Open questions the data can't settle

- **Is the graveyard theme worth building for at all?** The mechanics are deep, the payoffs are real
  card-for-card (Drag to the Roots 57.2%, Broodspinner 59.7%, Under the Skin 60.3%), and the
  archetypes still finish 6th, 8th and 10th. The most likely reconciliation is the one the guides
  themselves gesture at: the graveyard cards are good, the *dedicated* decks are too slow, and the
  right amount of Delirium is "incidental" rather than "built-around." The aggregate cannot separate
  those two builds, so this stays open.
- **How aggressive is the format really?** The guides call it midrange and note that five- and
  six-drop uncommons are legitimate early picks, yet the two winning archetypes are the two white
  tempo pairs. Treat the format as midrange in card quality and tempo in tactics.
