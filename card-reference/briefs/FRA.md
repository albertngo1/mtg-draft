## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

Reality Fracture is a slow, ten-archetype format built on two shared engines and an unusually
cheap answer pool. Jace Beleren's Echoverse gives every colour a way to build the same
planeswalker token, every allied pair a recurring free spell at common, and every colour pair a
dual land at common. Fixing is nearly free and splashing is cheap. Games are decided by card
advantage, removal, and whether anyone attacked the Jace.

> **How to weight this page.** 17Lands numbers (2026-10-08, day ten, ~251k PremierDraft decks)
> beat everything else; the sample has settled (no card with 1,000+ games moved more than 1.2pp
> from day six to day ten). Post-play expert takes come next: Limited Resources 874 (10-09),
> Rough Drafts 81–82, Lords of Limited 499, Limited Level-Ups' State of the Format, Numot's Arena
> Direct runs. Pre-release predictions are weakest. They're kept only where they still hold, and
> the ones that failed are listed in the last section.

### The format in six lines

1. **Slow.** Games average 9.68 turns, on-the-play wins only 52.1%, and boards stall at 15–20
   life by turn 5–6. The player who runs out of things to do with their mana loses.
2. **Blue is the best colour; W/U (56.9%) and U/B (56.6%) are the best pairs.** W/B and B/R are
   in the tied bottom four (53.3–53.7%). The spread is only about 4pp, so every pair is playable.
3. **Card draw and removal win.** Play 6–7 removal spells (counterspells count) and 3–4 card-draw
   spells. Cheap bodies with no lasting impact fall off.
4. **Two colours, plus a splash.** Splash freely, even double pips, with 7–8 sources. True
   three-colour decks win 53.6% vs 55.4% for two-colour decks.
5. **Jace is a resource and a target.** Run two or three Ways at most, fire the −3 whenever you
   can, and attack the opponent's token by default.
6. **Aggro is niche.** The only real aggressive decks are W/U fliers and go-wide, plus
   near-mono-red when red is wide open. Prowess and +1/+1 counters underperform.

### The draft plan

1. **Take blue and the best removal early.** `Mindseeker Oculus` is the best common (60.2%) and
   is taken early (average last seen at pick 3.7). `Countersculpt`, `Kindred Judgment`,
   `Twinned Vision`, `Unsummon` and `Icy Reception` all beat their grades.
2. **Aim for W/U or U/B; take black for U/B, not B/R.** Black is 1.7pp better than its set-wide
   level in U/B and 2.5pp worse in B/R.
3. **Value card draw like removal.** `Protege's Awakening`, `Sphinx's Approach`, `Twinned
   Vision` and `Countersculpt` all moved up after play (LR 874, LLU).
4. **Take duals only for blue decks**, and landcyclers for any deck. They're good early plays
   and good late draws.
5. **Use the prepared creatures that wheel as a signal** for which pair is open. Every deck in
   those colours wants them, so they read more cleanly than the gold cards.

The allied pairs are the colleges of Hexhaven; the enemy pairs are built around the Echoverse
versions of major characters. Numbers after a card below are its IWD inside that pair (how much
drawing it raised win rate in that pair's decks); set-wide figures are in brackets.

**W/U Fatehold — surveil fliers (1st).** *White carries it*: W/U's white cards run slightly above
their set-wide level (+0.6), while its blue cards run 1.3pp below (`Icy Reception`, `Mindseeker
Oculus` are both weaker here than anywhere). LR 874: the synergy "you basically get for free",
because play good blue cards and supportive white cards and the triggers just happen.
- Engine: `Fblthp, Impossibly Lost` +7.8 (evasion turns it into a draw-two every turn), `Way of
  the Healer` +6.2 (its −2 makes a Cadet *and* surveils), `Desperate Futurescribe` +5.2,
  `Thalia, the Survivor` +5.4.
- Every empower card is a surveil source, because the token's −1 is Surveil 1. That makes 35
  enablers in the set, and `Eye of Jace` surveils every upkeep.
- `Desperate Futurescribe` checks at beginning of combat, so surveil in your first main phase.
  `Surveillance Phantasm` still blocks as a 2/3 flier without the trigger.
- Trap: only 13 removal spells. Take `Prophesied End` and `Your Fate Ends Here` early.

**U/B Theorix — removal and two-for-ones (2nd).** The only pair where *both* halves over-perform
(U +1.2, B +1.8). What wins is not turbo-mill — threshold is printed on only six cards, and
`Theorix Metamage` is just +1.4 here across 13,009 games — but the value shell.
- Engine: `Break Under Pressure` +9.5, `Extended Absence` +8.4, `Tam's Resistance` +8.2
  (splashed off the hybrid), `Fblthp, Impossibly Lost` +8.1, `Multiply by Zero` +7.8,
  `Countersculpt` +7.1, `Mindseeker Oculus` +7.1, `Last Gasp` +5.7, `Icy Reception` +5.5,
  `Unsummon` +5.2, `Prudent Fateseer` +4.4, `Silence the Echo` +3.6, `Rank Rat` +3.5.
- It wins without bombs. A rareless U/B deck of discard, removal and three or four `Theorix
  Metamage` went 7-1 (Lords of Limited). LSV won two Sealed boxes with few-rare Grixis decks.
- Self-mill is a side plan: `Fblthp` wins the game if you draw from an empty library, and
  `Paradox Shaper`'s bottom-of-library ability is your brake. Reach seven cards in the
  graveyard only with about four mill-3 effects (`Omit Variables` carriers, `Theorix Charm`).
- Self-mill decks play exactly 40 cards unless they lack Fblthp, a second Paradox Shaper and
  real finishers (Rough Drafts). Be scared when a U/B opponent is on a few cards and still
  casting `Omit Variables`.

**B/G Garruk's Bestiary — graveyard grind (3rd).** Deathtouch is incidental; the engine is
recursion and big bodies, often splashing blue (Sultai) for `Rewrite Regrets`.
- Engine: `Break Under Pressure` +5.3, `Hapatra, the Desert Fang` +5.0, `Extended Absence` +5.0,
  `Ghalta the Unstoppable` +4.4, `Wrecking Gecko` +4.2, `Winter, Tormented Loner` +3.4.
- `Hapatra` reads the greatest mana value in your graveyard, so a landcycled `Apex Witchstalker`
  makes it six −1/−1 counters.
- Black loses some edge here (−1.4): `Multiply by Zero` drops to +1.1. Skip `Formidable
  Commons` (−3.5).

**R/G Konstrari — ramp into two uncommons (4th).** Below rare there are only three Heartwood
makers and one artifact payoff, so this is not an artifact deck. It is "make mana, cast `Kiora of
Fire and Ashes` (+9.5) or `Craftwork Crusher` (+8.6)". It is also the base for 3–5-colour decks.
- `Murmuring Volume` is a top-tier mana rock (LR 874), and `Konstrari Improviser` is "curve in a
  can".
- Green can splash red's double pips through Heartwood for Crusher; red can't do the reverse.
- Skip: the dual (−3.7), `Eardrum Rattler` (−6.7), `Inspired Tethermage` (−3.4), `Awaken the
  Inferno` (−0.8 vs +1.4), `Something Worth Saving` (−1.4 vs +1.2).

**U/R Chandra's Prowess — red removal plus blue cards (5th).** *Red is the better half here*
(+1.1; blue −1.2). Prowess creatures "aren't good at being aggressive enough" (LR 874).
- Red's removal plays best here: `Violent Echoes` +7.4, `No Admittance` +5.3, `Way of the
  Warlord` +4.2 (a noncreature spell that adds a removal ability to Jace), `Wrath of the
  Bloodmane` +3.3. `Kiora of Fire and Ashes` peaks here at +10.2.
- Off-pair duals play well here: `Fatehold Annex` +6.7, `Stingerquill Annex` +4.4 (about 1,000
  games each).
- 27 noncreature spells at common and uncommon. Pick one lane — red spells-aggro or blue
  control — and don't mix the payoffs. `Twinned Vision` is glue.

**G/W Vigorbloom — lifegain into counters (6th).** Eighteen lifegain sources at common and
uncommon, only four payoffs (`Unflinching Hortimancer`, `Titanbones`, `Bloombrute`, `Way of the
Mentor`).
- Repeatable gain is what matters — `Greenhouse Propagator`, `Medic's Kitesail`, `Koth of the
  Homestead` landfall — because `Bloombrute` triggers once per turn.
- Engine: `Generous Revival` +4.3, `Way of the Paradox` +4.2, `Bloombrute` +4.2, `Koth` +4.0.
- Skip the dual (−2.6), and don't take `Greenhouse Propagator` early. The lifegain payoffs are
  poor in Sealed (LR 874: "just don't have [Hortimancer] in your deck").

**The tied bottom four (53.3–53.7%).**
- **R/W:** both halves under-perform (W −1.8, R −1.2). It is an aggro deck in a slow format and
  needs `Mabel, Valley Hero` and `Warrior's Blades`. Mabel "really underperformed" and `Graft
  Surgeon` is one to avoid (LR 874). R/W's white is weak: `Fateshaper Aspirant` −5.3, `Saheeli,
  Consul of Oversight` −3.8. Best cards here are generic: `Prophesied End` +3.8, `Jiang Yanggu,
  Alone` +3.3. Rough Drafts' ep 82 ranks it the pair to avoid.
- **B/R:** the most removal of any pair, and still bottom. Black's control cards don't fit a
  face-damage deck. The payoffs check for noncombat damage *this turn*, and `Stingerquill
  Voxmancer` is the only repeatable source below rare. If you are B/R, draft red removal and the
  gold cards (`Violent Echoes` +5.1, `Kiora of Fire and Ashes` +4.8, `Fulminous Forte` +3.3)
  and treat pings as reach.
- **G/U:** the least-drafted pair. Its green cards fit better than any other half in the set
  (+2.6): `Arcane Amphisbaena` +7.5 (vs +1.6), `Way of the Wildspeaker` +6.5, `Keeper of the
  Quiet Hour` +6.1, `Compel Brutality` +4.3. It loses on removal count. Stay if you land here,
  but don't aim for it. Max out cheap empower and play `Kiora of Salt and Sand`, whose −8 needs
  real loyalty density; `Mind Meanderer` +6.9 is its removal.
- **W/B:** draft it as black removal plus white bodies; the sacrifice theme is thin and few
  pairs are lighter at two mana. `Tinybones, Pocket Nuisance` +6.4 and `Extended Absence` +5.3
  lead; `Void Extrapolator` is −5.1 here (no self-mill to turn on threshold).

### Engines and the mana

**The Jace token.** Thirty-five cards empower Jace, eleven at common, across all five colours.
You only ever have one token, and every empower card feeds it. It surveils for one loyalty and
draws for three.
- **Activate once a turn, so play two or three Ways, not more.** The best are Healer, Mind
  Sculptor and Paradox (LLU); Pyromancer, Warlord, Deathbringer, Mentor and Necromancer are weak
  or niche. The cheap Ways are mediocre; the 4–5-mana ones have all been fine (LR 874).
- **Draw first, then empower again.** With Jace on 4, −3 to draw, then empower and −3 again. A
  tall Jace gives no extra activations, so fire the −3 whenever you can.
- **Kill your own Jace to activate twice.** −3 it to 0; it dies, and the next empower card makes
  a fresh token that can activate again that turn. (Not with `Sanctum Lurker` out, which keeps
  0-loyalty planeswalkers alive.)
- **It turns on the mana.** All ten common duals enter untapped if you control a planeswalker.
- **`Compel Brutality` turns it into removal**, and **`Silence the Echo` eats a spent token** for
  free in blue decks.
- **`Ajani Unrelenting` (74.0%, the best card in the set) feeds on it.** It makes a Cadet on
  *any* loyalty activation, so keep a Jace on 1 to fire every turn. Its −3 spares your tokens and
  kills everything with toughness 4 or less.
- **Attack the opponent's Jace by default** (Lords of Limited). A Jace also soaks attacks like
  life gain, which is part of why the format is slow. Numot disagrees: he ignores a 1-loyalty
  Jace when his own deck has plenty of empower.
- Cheapest loyalty at common: `Arcane Amphisbaena` and `Academic Ascent` for two, `Campus Crier`
  from the graveyard for one, `Protege's Awakening` for six loyalty at four mana.

**Prepared.** Twenty-four cards, ten at common. A prepared creature carries a spell on its other
face; while prepared you may cast a free copy, which unprepares it. Each allied pair owns one
spell: `Peer Review` (W/U, a 2/2 Cadet and surveil), `Omit Variables` (U/B, mill three),
`Vicious Verse` (B/R, 1 damage), `Soul Tether` (R/G, a Heartwood token), `Seed Suture` (G/W, a
+1/+1 counter and 1 life). Three uncommons re-prepare every upkeep — `Stingerquill Voxmancer`
54.0%, `Paradox Shaper` 55.7%, `Woodwork Prodigy` 52.8% — and all sit at or below the set median.
They read as engines but play as fine cards, and `Command the Stage` is 49.6%.

**The mana.** Ten common duals (untapped with a planeswalker), basic landcycling on big commons
in four colours, plus `Room of Refuge` and `Murmuring Volume`.
- **The duals are only good in blue decks**, because blue makes the most Jace tokens. In its home
  pair every blue dual is zero or better and every non-blue dual is negative:

  | Blue pairs (dual IWD) | Non-blue pairs (dual IWD) |
  |---|---|
  | G/U `Transformative Commons` +3.0 · U/B `Theorix Annex` +1.6 · U/R `Innovative Commons` +0.8 · W/U `Fatehold Annex` +0.5 | B/R `Stingerquill Annex` −4.0 · R/G `Konstrari Annex` −3.7 · B/G `Formidable Commons` −3.5 · R/W `Dedicated Commons` −3.1 · G/W `Vigorbloom Annex` −2.6 · W/B `Meticulous Commons` −1.5 |

  Outside blue, a dual is a tapland: take it late and play it only for a real splash.
- **Landcyclers are all good.** Cycling on turn 2 is fine in a format this slow.
- **Sequencing:** a cheap empower spell before the dual is often worth a full turn.

### Where the removal is

Sixty removal spells: black 14, red 11, white 7, blue 5, green 5, and 18 gold or colourless. At
common: red 4, black 3, green 3, white 2, blue 2. The black-and-red edge is concentrated at
uncommon.
- **Blue does not kill things.** Its answers bounce, tap, counter or strip abilities. That plays
  better than it reads here — `Unsummon` (57.8%) is real removal against tokens and big bodies —
  but a blue deck still needs a second colour that kills.
- **Green's answers are fight effects** and need a body on board. Count them at a discount.
- **No cheap instant removal.** Removal is excellent from 2–4 mana, but nothing at 1–2 mana is an
  instant, so sticky one-drops survive and a fast curve-out is what the format is worst at
  beating (LR 874).
- **Sweepers exist:** `Kindred Judgment` (61.5%), `Overwrite the Multiverse`, `Austere Command`
  (bonus sheet), `Rise of the Deathbringer` and `Ajani Unrelenting`'s −3. Don't dump your hand.
- **Three damage kills 68% of creatures**, and four damage kills 19 points more. A third of
  creatures have toughness 4+, so `Surgical Precision` (57.3%, 12th-best common) is live.
- **Leave the colour hosers in the sideboard in Bo1:** `Refute Destiny` 51.3%, `Terminal
  Criticism` 51.1% (also `Essence Burn`, `Flourishing Grapple`, `Precise Redaction`). Board them
  in when the matchup is known.

### Deckbuilding and Sealed

- **Draft:** 17 lands. Prefer two colours plus a splash for removal or a bomb, and don't run bad
  two-drops to fill the curve. A low two-drop count is fine in a format this slow, and Grixis
  control can spend the two-mana slot on removal or card draw. Never run blue as your only
  interactive colour.
- **Which two-drops (Rough Drafts 81):** 3/1s trade up because there are no 1/1 tokens below rare
  (`Campus Crier`, `Sureshot Sower`); 1/3s like `Paradox Shaper` and `Tetsuko Umezawa, Fugitive`
  hold the ground in long games. `Arcane Amphisbaena` blocks well but belongs in slower green
  decks, not attacking ones.
- **Read low win rates carefully.** High win rates are reliable. C−/D+ win rates often belong to
  build-arounds played without support (`Danitha, Sword of Hope` wants 9–10 targeting effects).
  Discount `Fblthp`'s 60.9%: it shuffles back in and gets redrawn in winning games.
  `Theoretical Necromancer`'s 55.9% understates it because you want to mill it, not draw it; play
  two in every black deck.
- **Sealed** is usually three colours, sometimes four (LR 874). Cut every D-level card: each card
  should remove something, demand an answer, or advance mana or cards. Synergy-only cards drop
  out (`Unflinching Hortimancer`, `Titanbones`, `Emergency Phytomedic`, `Vigorbloom Vanguard`).
  Aggro Sealed works only about 5–10% of the time. The self-preparing uncommons are better in
  Sealed's long games than in draft.
- **Play around instant-speed removal in every colour.** `Prophesied End` kills anything for two
  and draws only if the creature wasn't attacking, so attacking into it is the cheaper line.

### What the experts and Reddit got wrong

**Pre-release predictions the data overturned:**
- **"The pick is B/R"** (most removal of any pair) — it is tied bottom. **"G/U is the trap"** —
  still bottom four, but its cards synergise better than any other pair's.
- **"Games are fast-ish; cap draw spells at one or two"** (LR 872) — the slowest format in a long
  time, and card draw is what wins.
- **"White is weak"** (Rough Drafts, Reddit, LLU's Mark) — white is the half carrying W/U. It is
  bad only outside W/U.
- **G/W and the +1/+1-counter decks near the top** (LR 872) — G/W is 6th and R/W bottom four.
- **R/G ramp skepticism** ("happy to pay three for a mana rock?") — R/G is 4th and Murmuring
  Volume is good. **Prowess skepticism** was right.
- **"The re-preparing uncommons are major engines"** — all three are at or below the set median.

**Reddit's trophy feed (survivorship-biased):**
- "B/R is great": the feed is full of B/R 7-0s, but the pair is tied bottom.
- "The set wants three colours": two-colour decks win more, and every three-colour deck with
  white is 52.5% or worse. Splash a card; don't play a third colour.
- "`Surgical Precision` is bad": it is the 12th-best common.
- "`Mindseeker Oculus`, `Kindred Judgment` and `Countersculpt` go late": average last seen at
  picks 3.7, 3.2 and 4.2, so they're taken early.

**Still disputed:**
- **Whether to attack a small Jace.** Lords of Limited says yes by default; Numot ignores a
  1-loyalty Jace when his own deck has plenty of empower.
- **`Rescue Girl, First Responder`** — 17Lands D-level vs LSV's build-around B.
- **Ramp ceilings.** Lords of Limited's ramp combo is `Way of the Pyromancer` + `Way of the
  Paradox` + `Inspired Tethermage`, but Pyromancer is 52.1% and Tethermage under-performs
  everywhere except G/U. Treat it as a ceiling play, not a default.

### Open questions the data can't settle

- **Whether the echoed-pair legends matter mechanically.** Several cards check for a legendary
  creature, and with 32 characters printed twice the density may make those checks effectively
  free.
- **How much the colour-hosed removal costs you in Bo3.** Bo1 data says sideboard them; in Bo3
  against a known pair they may be the best removal in your pool.
- **Whether the off-pair-dual result in U/R is real** (about 1,000 games each). Watch it as the
  sample grows.
