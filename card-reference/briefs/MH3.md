## Format brief — everything that isn't a single card

> **No reviewer grade exists for MH3**, so the GIH WR column and the two expert guides carry the whole
> read. At 26.2M PremierDraft games with 307 of 321 cards measured, the win rates can carry it — but
> note that **bonus-sheet reprints sit in the low thousands of games** (Fury 4,493 · Solitude 4,155 ·
> Persist 1,887) against 300k+ for a common, so those tiles deserve far wider error bars than the cards
> beside them. Eighteen cards also have no oracle text in the source data; their 🤖 takes say so.

### The draft plan in five lines

1. **The pair matters; the colour barely does.** Game-weighted mono-colour GIH WR spans just **1.07pp**
   — G 56.75% · R 56.37% · W 56.33% · U 55.98% · B 55.68% — while the ten colour pairs span **5.7pp**.
   Asking "am I in green?" is the wrong question. Ask which pair you're in.
2. **Be aggressive or go over the top; the middle loses.** **RW (59.32%)** and **RG (59.20%)** are 2.4pp
   clear of third place, and the bottom six pairs are packed inside 2.1pp. Kenji's framing: "you really
   need to be aggressive or have reach; the Eldrazi can go very, very big over the top of you."
3. **Craft a wedge, not a colour pair.** Jeskai = Energy, Abzan = Modified, Temur = Eldrazi, plus
   standalone UB draw-three and BR artifacts. Draft the mechanic first and let the colours follow.
4. **Commit by pack two — pivoting across wedges is close to impossible.** Kenji: "a lot of the cards
   don't have great overlap in other archetypes." Pivot *within* a wedge (RW → UR → WU all stay Jeskai).
5. **Double-colorless costs cannot be paid with basics.** You need landscape (DFC) lands or Eldrazi
   Spawn as real colorless sources. This is the constraint that silently breaks otherwise-fine decks.

### Gameplay rules that actually change results

- **Energy is a bank, not an income.** Only **Bespoke Battle Wagon** and **Royal Cartographer** make
  repeated energy; everything else is one-shot. Evaluate every energy card as what-you-see-is-what-you-get,
  and treat a card that brings 3 and spends 2 as a legitimate feeder. Spending energy as fast as you
  make it is the most common way the Jeskai decks lose.
- **Take the Eldrazi ramp, not the Eldrazi.** Nobody else at the table can cast the huge ones, so they
  wheel; the two-drop acceleration does not. A pile of build-around enchantments with no ramp loses.
- **Landscape (fetch) lands are triple-duty** — fixing, landfall/energy, spawn generation, a shuffle for
  Brain Surge, and a colorless source for double-colorless costs. Take them well above "it's a land."
- **The UB control deck wants to be strictly two colours.** Landscapes dilute a mana base that needs
  double-blue *and* double-black. This is the one deck where the fixing is a liability.
- **Sweepers barely exist** — Wrath of the Skies at rare, Ral and the Implicit Maze as a partial at
  uncommon. A resolved wide board usually stays. And Wrath kills everything at or below the energy you
  pay, **yours included**; with only four energy available it is often a loss condition.
- **BR artifacts: commit by pack two or bail.** Only 31 artifacts exist in the set, 18 of them in red or
  black. If you are four picks in with no Cranial Ram, the deck does not exist.

### Deckbuilding doctrine

- **17-18 lands.** 18 with real top end or a splash; 17 for a tight low-curve energy deck. MDFCs count
  as partial lands — Ethan ran 16 plus 2 MDFCs in blue-red energy.
- **MDFCs are a C+ baseline.** Always play them in your colours as free fixing; never prioritize them
  highly in the pick order.
- **Removal priority:** Galvanic Discharge (R) → Wither and Bloom (B) → Breathe Your Last (BB) →
  Horrific Assault (G, best in the Temur deck) → Lethal Throwdown (B, free with spawns or living
  weapon). Red has **no** clean "deal 2" or "deal 5+", so Galvanic Discharge fills the entire scalable
  role in the colour.
- **Caves of density over singletons.** Every wedge wants a critical mass — 9+ artifacts for BR, real
  energy feeders for Jeskai, 2-3 two-drop ramp for Temur. One copy of a payoff with no engine is a
  sideboard card.

### Where the experts were wrong — judged by the data

Scored against the 17Lands numbers on this same page.

**Archetype calls:**

| Call | Verdict |
|---|---|
| LoL rated **UR energy tempo "A"** and the hosts drafted it repeatedly | **Wrong — 9th at 53.72%.** The Bespoke Battle Wagon engine deck is the single biggest overrate in the guide. |
| LoL rated **BG modified "A"**, on Cursed Wombat being "nuts" | **Wrong — 8th at 53.72%.** The Wombat is a real card (54.91%); the archetype around it is not. |
| LoL rated **UB draw-three "C+"** — "hard to assemble, no strong payoffs at common" | **Wrong the other way — 5th at 55.71%**, best of the non-aggro pairs. See the sleeper table below; the whole UB package was wheeling. |
| LoL rated **GW "B"** while hedging "coverage thin — may be stronger" | **The hedge was right — 4th at 56.48%.** |
| LoL rated **RW "S"** | **Right — 1st at 59.32%**, and the most-drafted pair. |
| LoL rated **RG "A"** | **Slightly low — 2nd at 59.20%**, a statistical tie with RW. |

**Card calls — the glue pieces were systematically overrated:**

| Card | The expert call | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| **Golden-Tail Trainer** | Ben: "a house in green-white" | **47.70% GIH, IWD −6.6, ALSA 6.5.** The worst-performing card either guide named, in a pair that was otherwise good. |
| **Inspired Inventor** | LoL: "the archetypal pivot card, great early pick" | 54.55%, **IWD −2.2.** Playing in all three wedges made it mediocre in all three. |
| **Tune the Narrative** | Ethan's #1 blue common; "the critical cantrip, high pick" | 55.60% and it **wheels to pick 6.0**. Fine card, not a high pick. |
| **Evolution Witness** | LoL: "key glue piece, higher on it post-crash-course" | 54.27%, **IWD −0.7.** |
| **Muster the Departed** | Ethan: "Lingering Souls in disguise" | 51.94%, IWD −1.4, in the format's worst pair. |
| **Writhing Chrysalis** | Kenji: "the single best common in the format" | **Right, emphatically — 64.08%, +9.1 IWD**, a number most uncommons never reach. |
| **Galvanic Discharge** | Ben: "probably the best common in the set" | **Close — 60.10%, 3rd best common.** Kenji's call beat Ben's. |
| **Malevolent Rumble** | Ethan graded it C-, then corrected upward | **The correction was right and still too low — 60.36%, 2nd-best common.** |
| **Ral and the Implicit Maze** | Ben: B-minus | **Underrated — 58.64%**, a top-tier uncommon. |
| **Solar Transformer** | Ben B vs Ethan C+ — unresolved in the guide | **Ben wins: 56.64%, +2.4 IWD.** |

The pattern: **the guides read the aggressive decks correctly and misread both blue-based value decks in
opposite directions** — overrating UR energy tempo, underrating UB control — and consistently overvalued
the "glue" cards that play everywhere over the cards that are simply strong.

### Traps and sleepers the data settled

**Overdrafted (early ALSA, mediocre win rate):**

| Card | ALSA | GIH | IWD | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ulamog, the Defiler** | **1.6** | 51.77% | +0.3 | Taken second overall on average and performs like a filler common — the archetypal "cast it and still lose" Eldrazi. |
| **Ulalek, Fused Atrocity** | 3.5 | **44.27%** | −6.7 | Five colours. The worst card in the set by a wide margin. |
| **Thief of Existence** | 2.6 | 51.61% | −1.0 | Goes pick 2-3 on the name and does nothing. |
| **Ral, Monsoon Mage** | 3.1 | 50.58% | −1.2 | A mythic planeswalker that loses games. |
| **Echoes of Eternity** | 4.0 | 51.80% | −0.2 | Kenji: "if the opponent resolves it, we are beyond belief dead." Terrifying to face, unwinnable to build around. |
| **Eldrazi Ravager** | 3.8 | 51.70% | −1.7 | The colorless trap — reads like Eldrazi payoff, plays like a bad body. |

**Underdrafted — and it is almost all one archetype:**

| Card | ALSA | GIH | IWD | Pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sneaky Snacker** | **6.8** | 59.31% | **+6.6** | UB |
| **Mindless Conscription** | 6.0 | 58.91% | +6.1 | UB |
| **Deem Inferior** | 6.2 | 58.47% | +4.0 | U |
| **Riddle Gate Gargoyle** | 6.3 | 58.42% | +3.1 | WU |
| **Horrid Shadowspinner** | 6.3 | 57.90% | +5.7 | UB |

**Five of the seven late-wheeling winners are the UB draw-three deck** — the archetype the guide rated
C+. That is the whole story in one table: the field read the guide, nobody drafted UB, its cards came
around at pick 6-7, and the people who took them won 55.71% of their games.

One card deserves its own note: **Solstice Zealot** posts a 56.62% GIH on an **IWD of −1.5**. High win
rate, negative improvement-when-drawn — it is a card that good decks happen to contain, not one that
makes a deck better. Treat it as a body, not a reason.

### Cross-source disagreements, left unresolved on purpose

- **Ether Spike vs Amped Raptor for the best blue-adjacent uncommon.** Ethan is all-in on Ether Spike
  (he ran 4 copies on MTGO); Ben prefers Amped Raptor for raw card-advantage ceiling. Amped Raptor's
  64.18% is the highest uncommon in the set, but the two cards want different decks, so the split is
  real rather than settled.
- **How hard to commit to Eldrazi at the start.** Ben warned against taking build-around enchantments
  first without the payoffs; Ethan argued to take ramp and assume the payoffs arrive. The data leans
  Ethan — the huge Eldrazi genuinely do wheel — but Ben's failure case (a deck of enchantments and no
  acceleration) is also real and the guides never resolved it.
- **Descend-style thresholds on the draw-three payoffs.** Neither host demonstrated the UB deck working
  in the sampled episodes, so their pessimism was never tested against a real build. The numbers now say
  they were wrong; the *reasoning* was never refuted, because nobody in the sample drafted it well.

### Calibration note

**Lords of Limited** — a crash course plus early-access and RCQ episodes, so the guide is weighted toward
**pre-release and first-week reps**. It carries four documented self-corrections (Bespoke Battle Wagon,
Malevolent Rumble, Reckless Pyro-Surfer, Writhing Chrysalis) in which the early grade was too low every
time — a systematic direction of error worth knowing when reading any ungraded call in it. Its notes
cover the large majority of the guide coverage on this page.

**Numot (Kenji)** — a tier-2 sample: four VODs, one from **2024-07-11** and three from the
**2025-10-15 → 2025-10-17 flashback event**. That is an unusual shape — a first-month read plus a
settled read eighteen months later, with nothing in between. His calls are the sharper of the two on
this page (Writhing Chrysalis, the aggression requirement, the double-colorless constraint), which is
what a year of hindsight buys, but his coverage is thin and weighted to the two decks he actually played.

Between them the two guides attach notes to **75 of 321 cards**. The other 246 tiles run on the win-rate
column and the 🤖 take alone.
