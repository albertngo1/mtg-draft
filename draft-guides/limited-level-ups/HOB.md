# HOB — The Hobbit (Limited Level-Ups)

## ⚠ Recency rule (read first)

**Every take in this file is a pre-gameplay prediction.** All five episodes aired between 2026-07-30 and 2026-08-06 — before the paper prerelease (Aug 7) and before HOB hit Arena (Aug 11). Not one game had been played. On any conflict, the **newest source wins**, and **17Lands GIH WR supersedes this entire file** the moment it exists.

**No per-card letter grades this set.** This is a contract deviation worth knowing about: LLU normally splits its set review into per-color episodes and grades every card. For HOB they ran a **three-part "primer" instead of a full set review** — Alex, on air: *"we haven't been talking about grades today... we're not really talking about grades."* They published a separate tier list on the LLU website (not captured here). What this file preserves instead is **list position and whose list it was**, which is the only ranking signal the episodes contain. The two grades that did get said out loud are marked inline.

**Coverage is partial by design.** Alex estimates the primer covers *"a little more than a third"* of the card file: top 3 commons per color, top 10 power uncommons, top 10 synergy uncommons, top 10 rares, top 3 mythics. Cards absent from this file were not discussed, which is **not** evidence they are bad.

**Two reviewers, and they disagree a lot.** Alex (host) and Mark (guest) built their lists blind and compared on air. They agreed on **9 of 10** power uncommons but only **4 of 10** synergy uncommons. Where they split, both positions are recorded — the split itself is the signal.

## Supersessions

Only one week separates the first episode from the last, so there are no true reversals — but several positions moved, and several on-air disagreements resolved. These are the least stable calls in the file.

**Between episodes (First Impressions → primer):**

- **The dual-land cycle (Elvenking's Halls and friends).** Alex on First Impressions: *"these are worse than let's say surveil lands or scry lands."* By the Archetypes episode he had flipped to Mark's view — *"the more I thought about it, the more I'm like, no, actually I agree with your take."* Now rated genuinely strong, because sacrificing for two +1/+1 counters on a creature type your deck is already full of needs no build-around. Mark's kicker: in B/G it can make your signpost five power, turning on ferocious.
- **Storied timing.** A First Impressions commenter asked whether storied would be too slow. Alex's answer hardened across all three later episodes: **storied is not a build-around**, it turns on incidentally, and you should build R/W as a straight aggro deck. Mark: *"more like a tiebreaker instead of a true build-around"* — your 23rd card might become an extra artifact, nothing more.

**Resolved on air (Alex ↔ Mark):**

- **Rhovanion Rampager** — the biggest split of the primer, **six grades apart**: Mark **A−**, Alex **C−**. Alex conceded during the discussion (*"I agree too low, I don't think C− is the right grade"*) and they settled somewhere in the B range, **closer to Mark's side**. Mark's floor argument: a 3-mana 3/2 that dies into a 3/3 is a B in most sets before you count the sacrifice synergy.
- **Head of the Hunt** — Alex's list only. Mark, immediately: *"this card is very good, this should have been on my list."* Treat as a consensus top-10 rare.
- **Tom, Bert, and William** — Mark's list only. Alex: *"I should have had this on my list."* Same, consensus.
- **Great Ugly-Looking Goblin** — Alex's list only. Mark: *"this should have been on my list probably, over the Prowler."* Same.
- **Dwarven Mauler** — Alex opened skeptical (*"a little too vanilla a little too often"*), Mark argued the equipment-discount fixes exactly the one-drop failure mode, Alex folded: *"you're talking me up, I'm not going to push back."*
- **Bifur, Melodic Rider** — Alex's list only. Mark assumed R/W wanted to end games before turn six; on reflection: *"if it grinds at all, this card becomes exactly what it wants. This card could be very good."*
- **Silvan Reveler** — unresolved. Mark's **#1** synergy uncommon; absent from Alex's list entirely. Alex admits the omission is downstream of thinking blue-green is bad, not of the card being bad. Mark: *"if it's purely because you think blue green is going to be bad, I can understand it."*
- **Down, Down to Goblin-town** — both flagged mid-discussion that they'd ranked it too low. Alex: *"we might even be a little bit too low, this maybe should have been a little bit higher."*

## Format speed / meta read

- **Small set, 193 cards, five archetypes, built for Pick-Two Draft** (4-player pods). Two allied pairs (R/W, W/U) and three enemy pairs (B/R, B/G, G/U). Alex is glad it is *"the last small set for the foreseeable future."*
- **Each archetype cares about two things — a mechanic and a creature type.** That doubling is deliberate and is what gives a five-pair set replayability: R/W can lean dwarf-tribal *or* equipment *or* storied; B/R can lean go-tall amass *or* sacrifice-loop.
- **Color depth at common is lopsided.** Mark's ordering: *"white, then gap, then green, then gap, then the other three."* Red and black each have **five or six commons they're happy to play**; roughly half of red's common file is a two-for-one or better. All three of white's top commons are, in Mark's tier list, **a straight C** — while black's *fifth*-best common is a C+ or better. Alex: *"I would be very sad if I had to first pick any of these"* (the white commons).
- **White is carried entirely by rares.** Both reviewers' top three rares are the same three cards in different orders, and **all three are white**. Mark's math: at an 8-person table each drafter sees about half of each rare, so four busted white rares means ~2 opened per table. The play pattern this creates: **people avoid white early because the commons are bad, so the busted white rares wheel.**
- **Alex's early pick for best deck: black-red.** *"If I were to seed my sealed pool... I think it's red black. The red black cards are quite strong across the board at all rarities... it's going to be hard to build red black wrong."*
- **Blue-green is the weakest supported pair** on both lists. It is short on two-drops you want to play, its payoffs are unexciting, and its failure mode is *"drawing cards and putting small bodies into play"* without ever turning the corner — no removal, no attrition, no closers.
- **Watch the unsupported Grixis pairs.** U/R and U/B have no signposts, but blue, black and red are the three deepest colors. Alex: *"don't count out the Grixis shard."* Prefer a supported pair when it is close, but a pile of good blue rares plus red removal is a real deck. Mark's structural note: this is *"very reminiscent of Lord of the Rings where it was very much a Grixis format."*
- **Very few sweepers, so going wide is rewarded.** Mark: *"I think your boards are pretty safe. So going wide is going to be heavily rewarded."* The full list to play around: **Settle the Wreckage** (the one that matters), **Desolation of Smaug** (3 to all), **Easy Pickings** (Glóin the Mighty's adventure, 1 damage to each opposing creature), and the −1/−1 mode on **Gnashing of Teeth**. There is no black wrath.
- **Anthems everywhere.** An unusual density of team-pump at uncommon and rare, which compounds with the lack of sweepers.
- **Less exile than usual.** White's removal doesn't exile, black's doesn't either. This raises the value of dies-triggers and recursion (**Tom, Bert, and William**) and of stacking counters on a single creature.
- **The set is pushed.** Alex, comparing to the previous set: *"where Marvel kind of felt like they were tempering things slightly, I think with this one they're pushing things slightly."*

## Archetypes

The contract's usual 10-pair ranking doesn't apply — HOB has five. Ranked by the primer's combined read:

**1. B/R — Goblins / amass / sacrifice.** Signposts **Bolg of the North** (gold) and **Fearsome Goblin Pair** (hybrid). Deepest color pair at every rarity, plus the best removal access. Two builds: **go tall** (stack counters on one army, kill them with it) or **sacrifice-loop** (amass one, sac it for value, repeat). Alex: don't be too precious about which — *"the more red you have, the more aggressive you are; the more black you have, the more attrition-y mid-range-y you are."* A near-mono-red deck is plausible given red's common depth.

**Amass mechanics that matter:** it's a **go-tall** mechanic, not go-wide — the first amass makes a 0/0 army and puts the counters on it; later amasses stack onto the same army. If the army dies you start over. Corollary: **one removal spell erases the whole investment**, and a stacked army is effectively hasty (adding counters to an existing army means those stats attack immediately).

**2. R/W — Dwarves / equipment / storied.** Signposts **Thorin Oakenshield** (gold) and **Dáin's Company**, **Dwalin, Weaponmaster**, **Nori, Teller of Tales**, **Bifur, Melodic Rider** (hybrid). Build it aggressive — Alex: *"nine out of ten times your pool will want you to build that as aggressive as you can."* Mark is *"looking forward to playing"* this one most, for the decision density: where to move equipment, when to attack, tricks vs. value.

**Storied** = if you control three or more artifacts, legendaries, and/or sagas, you have an enduring story for the rest of the game. It's a **player designation** like the city's blessing — once earned it can never be lost, even if your permanent count drops. Support exists in all three categories (many legends, mostly-equipment artifacts, a saga cycle), so it turns on incidentally. Note the tension: trading off your legendary creatures can cost you the third permanent before you get there.

**3. B/G — Ferocious / wolves, bears, spiders.** Signposts **The Chief Warg** (gold) and **Large Bear**, **Duskwatch Hunter** (hybrid). This is **B/G aggro**, not the usual graveyard grind. Its commons are unimpressive; **the power is concentrated at uncommon**, so the aggressive build only exists if you get the good uncommons (**Nasty Little Rabbit**, **Desolation Prowler**, **Nighthowl Pursuer**). Without them, build a normal mid-range deck — that's fine too.

**Enabler count for ferocious: eight to ten** four-power creatures and power-boosters, counting equipment and counters, not just naturally-large creatures. Mark's caveat: eight enablers is worse than it sounds if three or four of them are six-drops, because every turn you wait to trigger is a turn you don't attack. Target shape: *"one third payoffs, one third four-power creatures, one third removal plus other stuff."*

**4. W/U — Humans / draw-two / recruit.** Signposts **Bard the Bowman** (gold, the only signpost on neither reviewer's list) and **Patient Instructor**, **Eagle's Rescue** (hybrid). Mark: *"mostly a blue deck"* — the enablers are strong, the payoffs are thin, and white contributes little at common.

**Recruit** = draw a card, then discard a card; if you discarded a nonland card, create a 1/1 white Human Soldier token. Slightly better than connive on average, and different in kind: connive makes your deck more aggressive, recruit *prolongs* games by leaving ground blockers. In a set full of equipment, any 1/1 body is a real threat waiting for a weapon. Every card with recruit on it is worth playing.

**5. G/U — Elves / landfall.** Signposts **Silvan Reveler** (gold) and **Thranduil, Sindarin Liege** (hybrid). Weakest of the five on both lists. Alex: *"the worst or the least supported or the one with the most junky cards."* Short on playable two-drops, and the elf payoffs are underwhelming. Alex's Sealed advice: *"if it's at all close, this is the deck that I would maybe lean away from."*

**Unsupported pairs:** W/B, W/G, U/B, U/R, R/G have no signposts and no payoffs. Of these, U/R and U/B are live off raw card quality (see the Grixis note above).

## Fixing

There is **a lot** of fixing and, in both reviewers' read, **very little worth splashing for**. The archetypes are on rails, the gold rares are not splashable, and the good uncommons are mostly locked into their pair. Mark: *"most of the top uncommons we're going to look at are not that splashable. So I expect people have really good mana bases."*

**What to actually splash for: removal.** Alex — *"splashing for removal might be the most common thing you splash for in this set,"* because some colors get it much better than others. **Celebrate the Mountain-king** is the archetypal splash target.

- **The dual-land cycle** (Elvenking's Halls, Goblin-town, Iron Hills, Lake-town, Mirkwood) — enters tapped, taps for two colors, and sacrifices for four mana to put two +1/+1 counters on your pair's creature type. Roughly **50% of packs** (TMNT's cycle was 60%). Genuinely strong; no build-around needed since any deck in the pair has 10+ targets. The R/W one is the most feared in play; B/G's hits bears, wolves *or* spiders and can push your signpost to five power.
- **Hobbit Hole** — Evolving Wilds with **halfling cycling {4}**. Alex: you don't need much upside on an Evolving Wilds to play it, and any upside that keeps flood useful is fantastic. Double landfall triggers in G/U.
- **Thrór's Map** — {2} legendary artifact, fetch a basic to hand, then {2},{T}: loot. The best of the colorless fixing; a repeatable loot engine that also feeds draw-two. Mark's worry (three of five archetypes are aggressive) resolved to: just don't cast it on turn two, play it as a four- or five-drop.
- **Giant's Boulder** — {1}, scry 2 on entry, taps for any color, seven-mana sac to destroy a permanent. The scry is the real text; the fixing is close to irrelevant. Best in G/U where you run more lands. Alex explicitly rejects the Arcum's Astrolabe comparison circulating on Twitter.
- **Troop of Ponies** — {2} 2/1, sac for two basics (one to battlefield tapped, one to hand). Held back by needing to be cast on turn two and then *not* traded away. Both reviewers uncertain.
- **Old Thrush** — {2} 1/2 flier, gain 2, put a basic on top. Flying plus lifegain are meaningful upgrades on this template, but Mark: *"I've never played this type of card when I wasn't three colors... I could see myself going the entire format without ever putting this card in the deck."*
- **Long-Bodied Grey Dog** — {3} 2/2 flash reach, makes a tapped Treasure. Both reviewers baffled by it. Little flying in the set makes reach near-dead. No home.

## Card tips

Format: `- **Card** — list position (source). Note.` Positions are the primer's rankings, not grades. "both" = it appeared on both lists.

### White

- **Dwarven Shortsword** — top common #1 (both, avg. order). Four mana for effectively a 3/4, and it's a dwarf *and* an equipment for storied. Mark's tier list: **straight C**. Fine, not exciting.
- **Magnificent End** — top common #2 (both). White's **only** removal spell. Four mana instant, costs {3} less targeting a tapped creature, deals 5 damage. Both reviewers baffled it's damage rather than destroy/exile — Alex calls it a possible color-pie break. Five damage misses more than you'd like: +2 toughness equipment and stacked amass armies both climb out of range. Mark: **straight C**.
- **Dwarven Provisioner** — top common #3 (both). {1}{W} 2/2 dwarf with {3}{W}: team gets +1/+1. This template has always been decent. Plays in both W/U (go wide via recruit) and R/W (creature type, aggressive ability). Mark: **straight C**.
- **Celebrate the Mountain-king** — power uncommon #1 (both). Mini Ravenous Chupacabra: exiles a nonland permanent until it leaves, *and* you recruit. Removal plus a body plus a loot. **The premier splash card in the set.**
- **Stone by Sunlight** — power uncommon #3 (both). Two-mana modal instant: destroy a creature with power 4+, or give a creature indestructible + artifact type. Both modes live at maybe 70/30, unlike Murder's Crusade where you never used mode two. Instant speed makes it a blowout against equip-heavy turns and against stacked amass armies. Alex: *"there's an entire deck whose point is to give you a target for this."*
- **Bofur, Reliable Guardian // Concerted Care** — synergy uncommon (Alex). A one-mana lifelink body *and* a protection instant (hexproof + indestructible) in one card. Mostly you hold it as the protection spell and get a creature on the end of it — the Hippogriff pattern. Curving it into **Dwarven Mattock** is the dream.
- **Dáin, Lord of the Iron Hills** — synergy uncommon (Mark). Two-mana 2/2 vigilance; with an enduring story, creatures can't attack you unless their controller pays {1} each. Mark: *"that ability is so annoying."* Alex's reservation: it cuts both ways in a set with both go-wide and go-tall pairs. Fragile.
- **Iron Hills Blacksmith** — not ranked; discussed as an enabler. {1}{W} 1/1 double strike dwarf that makes an Axe equipment token. About as small as incidental equipment gets, but it feeds storied and equipment-matters.
- **The Mountain-king's Return** — not ranked; discussed. {2}{W} saga: recruit, then reanimate a ≤3 MV creature (often the one you just discarded), then a +1/+1 counter.
- **Thorin's Last Stand** — not ranked. {2}{W}{W} instant, modal: +2/+1 to your team **or** destroy an artifact/enchantment. Mark nearly put it in his top three white commons — *not* because the set goes wide, but because **instant-speed artifact destruction is genuinely good in a set this full of equipment**, and it answers The Arkenstone.
- **Well-Worn Spatula (?)** — not ranked. Referred to only as *"a one mana short sword that I think gains two life."* {1} is the only common equipment matching. The 23rd card you play to reach equipment critical mass for **Thorin, Mountain-king**.

### Blue

- **Bilbo Baggins, Burglar // Take a Glance** — top common #1 (both). Possibly **the best common in the set**. Three-mana 2/1 that draws a card, with a {U} scry-2 adventure. Absurd in an opening hand (keeps one-landers), still live in top-deck mode. Legendary, but Alex would happily play three.
- **Plunder the Trollshaws** — top common #2 (both). {1}{U} instant, draw one; draw two instead if cast from the graveyard; flashback {3}{U}. Better than the previous set's Futurist Forge because it's an **instant** — you can flash it back on the opponent's turn and still get a draw-two trigger, which is normally impossible since your draw step spots you the first card. Mark: *"the number one card I want to discard to recruit."*
- **Long Lake Nuisance** — top common #3 (Alex). Four-mana 3/1 flier that recruits on entry. Alex is more confident in this than Mark's pick. Mark's one hesitation: the set has one-damage sweepers that eat it for free.
- **Uneasy Partings** — top common #3 (Mark). Top-or-bottom removal, costs {1} less targeting an attacking **nontoken** creature. The nontoken clause is a deliberate anti-amass rider — you still *can* hit an army, just not at the discount. Mark: *"I'm not super confident this is going to be a top common, but I'm confident it'll at least be fine."*
- **Bilbo, Luckwearer // Burglar's Plot** — power uncommon #2 (Alex) / #7 (Mark). Two-mana unblockable 1/1 that loots on combat damage, with an adventure that swaps control of two permanents sharing a card type. The adventure is narrow but the creature guarantees the card never rots in hand. Alex: *"really, really powerful."* Mark's reservation: many draw-two payoffs are combat-centric, and this triggers *after* combat damage.
- **Great Gilded Boat** — top-10 rare (Alex). {2}{U} 4/4 vehicle, crew 2, recruits **whenever you attack**. Recruiting nearly every turn is the pitch. Mark had it just off his list.
- **Thranduil's Decree** — not ranked; called out as easy to underrate. Six-mana counter that lets you **cast the countered card for free**. Good blue top-end; you make the mana back countering anything at 3+.
- **Lakeshore Apothecary** — not ranked; discussed. {1}{U} 2/1 vigilance human that grows on your second draw each turn. Alex normally dislikes creatures that start understated, but expects draw-two to trigger consistently enough — *"might just be a vigilant Tarmogoyf,"* said semi-cheekily. The key is that it's a **two**-drop, not a three-drop.
- **Enchanted River's Grasp** — not ranked, named only as *"the lockdown enchantment in blue."* Alex: *"always close to a top common."*
- **Confusticate and Bebother (?)** — not ranked, named only as *"a counterspell that I think's pretty good."* The only {2}{U} common instant.

### Black

- **Bilbo's Deadly Slice** — top common #1 (both, by consensus placement — **neither reviewer actually ranked it first**). Reprinted Murder at {1}{B}{B}. The usual C+ to B− efficient common you're always happy to play.
- **Crude Bent Blade** — top common #2 (Mark). Three-mana equipment; on entry the opponent sacrifices a creature of their choice; +2/+1, equip {2}. Alex: *"I wish I had this on my list."* The Cornered by Black Mages lesson — **an edict that leaves you a permanent is much better than an edict**. Even hitting a 1/1 recruit token still removes material, a trump blocker, and a double-block. And because it's sorcery-speed *you* choose the timing: attack their 1/1 away first, then cast it into their real creature. Also pushes B/G creatures to four power for ferocious. Stats and equip cost are reasonable, unlike Ragged Short Spear's.
- **Stony-Voiced Goblins** — top common #3 (Mark). {1}{B} 1/1, each opponent discards. Strong when black's attrition plan is live; there are sac outlets for it, and the creature types matter. Mitigating factor: **flashback exists in this set**, so a discarded flashback card costs them little.
- **Rage into the Valley** — top common (Alex). {2}{B} sorcery: draw a card, lose a life, amass Goblins 2. A Phyrexian Rager that's worse in multiples (you'd rather have separate bodies than a taller army) — but not much worse. Great art.
- **Stir Up Trouble** — top common (Alex). {B} sorcery, destroy target creature; additional cost of sacrificing a creature **or artifact**, or paying {4}. **This is the card that decides whether black sacrifice works.** Being able to feed it an artifact — a spent Goblin Plate Mail, a used equipment — rather than a body is the whole ballgame. Mark has talked bone-splinters effects down in past sets and explicitly won't here; **C+** on his tier list.
- **Desolation Prowler** — power uncommon (both). {1}{B} 2/2 wolf, pay 2 life for +2/+2, **once each turn — meaning once on your turn and once on theirs.** Putrid Leech is back. Near-impossible to block early; the threat of activation alone brick-walls 3/3s. Instantly turns on any ferocious one-drop. Mark: *"the play pattern is usually just kill them."*
- **Down, Down to Goblin-town** — power uncommon (both; both said afterward they'd ranked it too low). {2}{B} saga: ch.I Thoughtseize their hand, ch.II amass Goblins 1, ch.III–IV drain 1. The Torment of Hailfire family — take a card, make a body, see their hand — and the double drain at the end is the part people underrate.
- **Nighthowl Pursuer** — synergy uncommon (Mark, his #10). {B} 1/1 menace; attacks as a 3/3 menace with ferocious online. Analogous to Nasty Little Rabbit. Mark, candidly: *"this card's just fine... I probably should have replaced this with one or two of the cards on your list."*
- **Great Ugly-Looking Goblin // Clap! Snap!** — synergy uncommon (Alex; Mark conceded it belonged on his). Effectively a **two-drop that draws you a six-mana 4/4** whose ability (each creature with a +1/+1 counter has menace) is exactly what your amass army wants. The adventure amasses Goblins 2 for {1}{B}.
- **Head of the Hunt** — top-10 rare (Alex; Mark: *"should have been on my list"*). {2}{B}{B} **flash** 4/3 wolf; opposing creatures that would die are exiled instead, and you get a 2/2 wolf each time. Ambushes attackers, replaces itself on every trade, and is four power for ferocious. Answers the format's shortage of exile.
- **Rhovanion Rampager** — top-10 rare (Mark **A−** / Alex **C−**, settled ~B). {2}{B} 3/2 wolf; sac a creature on attack to stack that creature's power as counters; on death, amass Goblins equal to its power. Floor is a 3/2 that dies into a 3/3. Enabler *and* payoff for sacrifice. The low-exile format makes the counter-stacking pay off more often. Alex's objection (they kill it in response to the sac trigger) was answered: that's still a two-for-two, and you needn't do it into open mana.
- **The Great Goblin** — top-10 rare (Alex). {1}{B/R}{B/R} 3/2; deals 2 to an opponent whenever you put counters on a goblin/orc/army, and exiles-to-play off the top whenever one dies. An engine that also kills them. Mark's reason for omitting it: no ETB, no protection, dies to any removal spell.
- **Gathering of Darkness** — not ranked; discussed. {3}{B} sorcery: raise a creature from the graveyard to hand, amass Goblins 3. Gravedigger has been power-crept out, but the 3/3 of stats — often effectively hasty if you already have an army — makes it playable and probably good.
- **Fearsome Goblin Pair** — not ranked, referenced as *"the red black hybrid uncommon, a three mana 1/1 that dies to amass 4."* A card you **want** to sacrifice.

### Red

- **Goblin-town Flunkies** — top common #1 (both). {1}{R} 1/1 haste goblin, amass Goblins 1 on entry. Mark's case: it blocks well, attacks well, grows your army, gives you one or two bodies you're happy to sacrifice, and carries relevant creature types. The haste matters more than it looks, and amass onto an existing army is effectively hasty too. Compare Rally at the Hornburg from LTR.
- **Pinecone Strike** — top common #2 (both). {1}{R} instant, choose one **or both**: 3 damage to a creature (exiled if it would die), and/or destroy an artifact token. Few artifact tokens exist — Treasures and the Axe tokens on the dwarf cards — but even blowing up a Treasure so they can't spend it next turn is upside on an already-fine two-mana deal-3.
- **Ragged Short Spear** — top common #3 (Mark). {1}{R} equipment; on entry, discard a card to draw two; +2/+0, equip {3}. A clunky equipment stapled to a Tormenting Voice — but in a format that cares about equipment, leaves an artifact for storage and sacrifice, and gives you a late-game mana sink. Alex compares it to Edge of Eternities' Melded Moxite, which overperformed.
- **Gundabad Opportunist** — top common #3 (Alex). {3}{R} 4/2 goblin, exile-and-play the top card until end of next turn. Four mana, trades easily, two-for-one. Compare Marvel's operative, which was good despite red being bad there.
- **Tidings of War** — not ranked; Mark raised it after sending his list. {R} sorcery, amass 1, **flashback {4}** amassing 3. Turn one into Goblin-town Flunkies has you **attacking for three on turn two**.
- **Burn, Burn, Tree and Fern** — power uncommon #4 (both). {3}{R} saga: ch.I 6 damage to a creature, ch.II destroy an artifact, ch.III–IV add {R}. Getting both halves is *"absurdly good"*; even just deal-6-for-four-mana plus two turns of ramp is fine. Alex's aside: *"this one can deal six — why can't the white one?"* Splashable in G/U off the colorless fixing.
- **Misty Mountains Raider** — power uncommon #2 (Alex) / #9 (Mark). {4}{R} 4/? goblin, amass Goblins 2 **whenever you attack**. With a 2/2 army out you attack as a 4/4 immediately, so it's partly hasty. Mark's discount: no ETB, no protection, five-mana body that does nothing if you can't attack. Compare LTR's version and DSK's Hoardeller — both of which had haste, *"a big difference."*
- **Gandalf, Spark Starter** — power uncommon #3 (Mark) / #10 (Alex). {4}{R}{R} 4/3 reach, 3 damage divided among up to three targets, and it can go to face. Alex's reservation: a 4/3 for six is underwhelming, and 3 damage may not kill anything relevant against the four-power-matters deck or a big army. Both agree the **post-combat** application — attack, let them block, then split the damage — is the exciting mode.
- **Bothersome Noisemaker** — synergy uncommon #1 (Alex's list order). {1}{R} 2/2 goblin, amass Goblins 1 on every **noncreature** spell. A Young Pyromancer for amass. Terrifying in combat since every trick or removal spell also grows your army. Red-black decks routinely run ~11 real creatures with many "creature" slots filled by amass cards, so the noncreature count is higher than you'd guess. Mark nearly filed it under **power** uncommons instead. R/W has equipment with creatures stapled on, which also triggers it.
- **Dwarven Mauler** — synergy uncommon (Mark). {R} 2/1 dwarf; equip abilities targeting it cost {2} less. Alex's initial worry — a one-mana 2/1 goes blank by turn five — is precisely what the discount fixes: you stack everything onto it and it stays a scary attacker into the late game. **Wants 4–5 equipment.** Don't play it in most B/R decks unless you're hyper-aggressive.
- **Snowslope Hunter** — synergy uncommon (Alex). {2}{R} 2/3 goblin; sac a creature **or artifact** to exile-and-play the top card, once each turn on your turn. Understated body, but Alex believes it's the **only repeatable sacrifice outlet in the set** — a real engine for the attrition build. Being able to eat artifacts (a spent equipment, a used Ragged Short Spear) rather than bodies is what makes it work.
- **Dáin Ironfoot** — top-10 rare (Mark). {2}{R} 1/4 legendary dwarf; makes an Axe token and auto-attaches it; on attack, **every equipped attacking creature gains double strike**. Mark: *"good enough to splash in most decks"* — a claim that surprised Alex. On turn four it attacks as a double-striking 2-power creature with no mana spent, and it stacks with every other equipment you have.
- **Thorin, Mountain-king** — mythic #3 (Mark). {3}{R} 3/4 trample; **on entry**, attach any number of your equipment to a creature, and that creature deals damage equal to its power to a creature. Four mana, 3/4 trample, plus killing their best creature. A genuine build-around: **five equipment is the minimum, six is good, seven-plus is extremely good.** Alex had it #4 — the failure case is having no equipment on board when you cast it.
- **Smaug, the Great Calamity // Spew Flame** — not ranked; called out as easy to underrate. Both halves are clunky (seven-mana 5/5 flier; five-mana sorcery-speed deal 5) but adventures let two clunky halves add up, and Sealed games get to seven mana.
- **Desolation of Smaug** — not ranked, listed as a sweeper. 3 damage to all creatures.
- **Dori, Bearer of Friends** — not ranked; discussed. {2}{R} 3/2 trample legendary dwarf making a Treasure. Cheap storied fuel: curve Thorin Oakenshield into this and you're two-thirds of the way there on turn three.

### Green

- **Quarrel** — top common #1 (**the only common both reviewers agreed on**). {1}{G} instant fight-style removal. Mark: *"it's not a very exciting card... this being potentially green's best common is not a great sign for the color overall."* Plays especially well with Attercop.
- **Wood Elves** — top common #2 (Mark). {2}{G} 1/1 that fetches a Forest **untapped** (unlike most versions), triggering landfall. Mark is honest about the ceiling: *"I basically am never planning on putting this in 99% of my green black decks."* Alex: *"you do not want to be playing a three mana 1/1 on turn three."*
- **Ordinary Bear** — top common #3 (Mark). {3}{G} vanilla 4/5. Five toughness blocks almost everything and four power starts your ferocious engine early. Mark: *"very low confidence in this one — more a factor of green's card file being slim pickings than me thinking this card is fantastic."*
- **Attercop** — top common #2 (Alex). {1}{G} 2/1 spider with reach, deathtouch and landfall (+1/+1 until EOT). A good defensive two-drop for G/U and a decent beater for B/G, and a deathtouch creature makes Quarrel punch far above its weight. Mark: *"totally reasonable pick."*
- **Boughside Wanderers** — top common #3 (Alex, low confidence). {4}{G}{G} 4/4 elf; dig four for a permanent; landfall +2/+2. An expensive value creature. Reaching six mana isn't hard for the elf deck; the question is whether you want a six-drop that isn't a game-ending rare.
- **Nasty Little Rabbit** — synergy uncommon #1 (Alex) / #8 (Mark). {G} 1/2 with ferocious: a +1/+1 counter at the beginning of combat if you control a four-power creature. **It keys off itself once it reaches four power** — Alex wonders aloud whether that's an oversight. Curve it into Desolation Prowler for a terrifying start. Mark's discount: only **one common** (Duskwatch Hunter) reliably turns it on by turn three, so realistically it's turn four, and every turn of delay costs you an attack. Alex's counter: *"a one-drop that eventually becomes a threat — almost like a suspend card."* Compare Nessian Hornbeetle, a two-mana 2/2 with the same text that was a house.
- **Mirkwood Pathmaker** — synergy uncommon #10 (Mark). {2}{G} elf whose P/T equal your land count. A three-mana 3/3 that scales, triggers ferocious on turn four, and is an elf. Compare the Edge of Eternities five-drop with the same text plus ward 2, which was a top uncommon.
- **Troll Negotiations** — synergy uncommon #10 (Alex; Mark's #11). {2}{G}{G} sorcery: two +1/+1 counters on your creature, then fight. This is Pitiless Fist from Bloomburrow, *"one of the most underrated cards"* of that set — put it on a 3/3, kill their best thing, swing for a huge tempo hit. Great in the elf deck, **great** in B/G aggro.
- **Woodland Weavemaster** — synergy uncommon (Mark). {1}{G} 1/2 vigilant elf that grows when another elf enters and taps for X mana (X = its power) **restricted to elf spells and elf abilities**. Explosive turns are possible — pump, attack with vigilance, deploy post-combat. But it **wants ~12 other elves**, which the archetype's thin two-drop count makes hard. Mark: *"probably one I'm a little bit too high on."*
- **Beorn the Fierce** — **mythic #1 (both).** {3}{G}{G} 6/6 trample; other bears get +2/+2; each combat, turn one of your creatures into a trampling bear, and draw two if you control three bears. **Easy A+, and you don't need a bear deck** — with no other bears it's still five mana for 10/10 worth of stats plus two cards, so long as it survives a turn cycle. First-picking it should nudge you toward bears, not commit you.
- **Dancing from Dark to Dawn** — mythic #3 (Alex). {3}{G}{G} enchantment: creature spells put X +1/+1 counters (X = its MV) on a creature; landfall makes a 2/2 bear. Shields-down on turn five, but pays off with either lands or spells from then on.
- **Little Bear** — not ranked; discussed. {2}{G} 3/2 flash that untaps another creature (+1/+1 counter if it's a bear).
- **Gigantic Big Bear** — not ranked, referenced only as *"like a 10/7 bear at rare."*

### Multicolor and hybrid

- **Goblin Plate Mail** — top "other" common #1 (both). {1}{B/R} equipment: amass Goblins 1 and auto-attach; +1/+0 and menace; equip {4}. Effectively a two-mana 2/1 menace goblin that leaves an equipment behind. Alex: *"this reads like an uncommon... compare this to any of the white commons."* The equip {4} is often bypassable via the set's snap-on effects, and both halves are sacrifice fodder.
- **Mirkwood Nurturer** — top "other" common #2 (Mark). {2}{G/U} 3/2 elf; bounce one of your permanents to add a +1/+1 counter to itself. The set is dense with ETB creatures, adventure creatures, and a strong uncommon saga cycle — all good to re-buy. It can bounce a **land** to re-trigger landfall, and it can target **itself** to become a four-power creature for ferocious. Alex, more measured: *"really going to be a fairly filler-feeling card, but in some decks... a really nice engine piece."*
- **Patient Instructor** — top "other" common #3 (both). {2}{W/U} 2/2 vigilance human that recruits. Straightforward: 2/2 plus a 1/1 plus a loot, goes wide, turns on draw-two on turn three. Curves off the blue two-drop that wants it.
- **Duskwatch Hunter** — not ranked, but flagged as important. {2}{B/G} 3/1 wolf, can't be blocked by tokens, +1/+1 counter on entry. **The only cheap four-power common in the set** and the single most important ferocious enabler at common. Neither reviewer could name a four-power black or green three-drop at uncommon. Only B/G wants it, so you should get all of them.
- **Large Bear** — power uncommon (both). {3}{B/G}{B/G} 5/5 with reach, trample **and** haste. Just a pile of stats and keywords.
- **Thorin Oakenshield** — synergy uncommon #3 (both). {R}{W} 3/2 trample legendary dwarf with storied; with an enduring story, your artifacts and creatures have **ward 1**. Good stats, right creature type, and trample matters in an equipment deck (no chump-blocking). Ward 1 is *"more annoying than you might think."* Important caveat: **you keep the story, but you lose the ward when this dies** — and it's a combat creature you'll be trading off early.
- **Thranduil, Sindarin Liege // Silvan Rally** — synergy uncommon #4 (both). {2}{G/U}{G/U} 2/3 legendary elf: elf lord plus a 1/1 elf on every landfall. Compare Magda from Kaldheim (a five-drop with similar text that was strong) — this is a four-drop. The adventure (mill four, take up to two lands) fuels landfall. Mostly a G/U card since it only pumps elves. Alex: elves lack good payoffs, so having at least one real one is what makes elves worth playing at all.
- **Silvan Reveler** — synergy uncommon **#1 (Mark) / unlisted (Alex)** — the primer's biggest unresolved split. {2}{G}{U} 3/2 elf: loot, and if you discarded a land, return it from the graveyard to the battlefield tapped; then pay {1}{G/U} on landfall to return it from your graveyard to hand. **A one-card value engine at uncommon.** Mark: four mana 3/2 draw-a-card is already a fine rate, and the rebuy solves G/U's real late-game problem (drawing blank lands and running out of steam). Also a nice splash for W/U, where it doubles as a recruit. The awkward case is having no land to discard — a four-mana 3/2 loot is bad. Alex's objection is about the archetype, not the card: *"slow... not what the format really rewards."*
- **Bifur, Melodic Rider** — synergy uncommon (Alex; Mark conceded). {4}{R/W}{R/W} 4/5 legendary dwarf with storied; a +1/+1 counter on entry or attack, and with an enduring story **every dwarf ability you control triggers twice**. Six or seven stats' worth on a body that keeps generating. Assumes you'll have storied by turn six, which you will.
- **Bolg of the North** — synergy uncommon (Alex, B/R signpost). {3}{B}{R} 5/5 legendary goblin; sac a creature to deal its power to another creature, then amass Goblins equal to the excess. Doesn't do the trade-my-small-thing-for-your-best-thing that Stir Up Trouble does — it cashes in a body to kill a comparable body and grow your army. Wants **high-power creatures and power-boosting equipment**, so it's a synergy card. Both reviewers low-confidence. Trick: equip something one turn, plan to fling it the next.
- **The Chief Warg** — synergy uncommon (Alex, B/G signpost). {2}{B}{G} 3/3 menace with ferocious: draw a card and lose a life **whenever you attack** (it needn't attack itself). Its real contribution is **card flow**, which B/G aggro otherwise entirely lacks. It also survives more often than you'd expect, because opposing removal is busy answering Nasty Little Rabbit and Desolation Prowler. Pure payoff, no enabler — Mark wants another four-power three-drop to pair with it.
- **Bard the Bowman** — the W/U signpost, **on neither list**. {1}{W}{U} 1/3 reach; a +1/+1 counter and lifelink when you draw your second card. Small on arrival, but easy to trigger and effectively 2/4 worth of stats for three mana. Fine, unexciting.
- **Dwalin, Weaponmaster** — not ranked; discussed for the new **home counter** mechanic. {1}{R/W} 2/1 first strike; on entry or attack, put a home counter on each equipment you control, each granting an extra +1/+0.
- **Bard's Company** — top-10 rare (both). {2}{W}{U} 2/3 human; **flash if you control a human**; other creatures get +1/+1; recruits on entry **and on attack**.
- **Chief Warg's Company** — top-10 rare (both). {1}{B}{G} 5/3 trample; can't attack unless you control two other wolves; makes a 2/2 wolf every upkeep. It **can** block regardless, so it's a 5/3 wall that builds a board. Skill-testing: knowing when to stop making 2/2s and actually attack.
- **Dáin's Company** — top-10 rare (Alex). {R}{W} 2/2 dwarf with lifelink while you control another dwarf; digs four for a dwarf or equipment. Hit rate depends on your count — Mark estimates ~9 hits (≈50%), Alex 10–13, and both note you can fudge your deck toward it.
- **Thranduil's Company** — top-10 rare (Mark; Alex conceded he downgraded it purely for being blue-green). {2}{G}{U} 3/4 elf: play an additional land each turn while you control another elf, and every landfall puts two +1/+1 counters on a creature plus vigilance. **The landfall payoff G/U was missing.** Splashable as a plain five-drop even with no elves. Play it on turn five with a land in hand — opponents **cannot respond to a land drop**, so the first trigger is guaranteed if it resolves.
- **Tom, Bert, and William** — top-10 rare (Mark; Alex conceded). {3}{B}{G} 5/? legendary troll: {1}, sac another creature, draw cards equal to its power, then discard one; when it dies **it returns as an artifact**. Instant-speed and **not once per turn** — leaving one mana up with four-power ferocious creatures around is a nightmare for the opponent. The low-exile format makes the dies-trigger reliable.
- **The Arkenstone // Seek the Heart** — mythic #2 (both). {5} legendary artifact: +1/+1 to your team and draw a card each end step; adventure is {2}{W} tutor a legendary creature to hand. Both reviewers think **the adventure is a trap** — given the choice on turn three between casting Seek the Heart and playing a two-drop, play the two-drop. Widen your board so the anthem matters; once the artifact lands and draws every turn, you win anyway.
- **Smaug the Magnificent** — not covered by LLU. (Named in outside coverage as a four-mana 4/3 haste flier that ramps.)

### Colorless

- **Dwarven Mattock** — synergy uncommon (Mark). {2} equipment: auto-attaches to a dwarf on entry; +2/+2 and ward 1; equip {3}. The one-into-two curve attacks for four on turn two. **Pretty bad without the free attach — wants ~10 dwarves.** Note it does *not* get Dwarven Mauler's equip discount on the free attach, so that combo loses a little value.
- **Thrór's Map**, **Giant's Boulder**, **Troop of Ponies**, **Old Thrush**, **Long-Bodied Grey Dog**, **Hobbit Hole** — see [Fixing](#fixing).

### Rares neither reviewer ranked but both flagged

- **The Eagles Are Coming!** — top-3 rare on **both** lists (order differs). {1}{W} instant, kicker {2}{W}: return your own creature(s) to hand; at the next upkeep, make a 4/4 white flier for each. Alex: *"this is either busted or very busted."* Four distinct modes: (1) turn-two return a one-drop, attack with a 4/4 flier on turn three; (2) a two-mana protection spell that leaves a 4/4; (3) kicked offensively at six mana on your turn — birds arrive on their upkeep, can block, attack next turn; (4) kicked defensively as a combat blowout, birds arrive summoning-sick. **Timing warning: the birds appear at the next upkeep, so you must cast it on your own turn if you want to attack with them.** It picks up nontokens without letting you recast them — irrelevant, they're 4/4 fliers now.
- **An Unexpected Party // At the Door** — top-3 rare on **both** lists. {2}{W}{W} enchantment: choose a creature type (dwarves or humans), those creatures get **+2/+2**. The adventure At the Door is {X}{2}{W} making X 2/2 red Dwarf tokens. Usually you cast At the Door for three tokens on turn six, then the enchantment on turn seven for **12 power and toughness from one card**. But there are real turn-four anthem plays — most anthems are +1/+1, and **+2/+2 is huge**. Mirkwood Nurturer can re-buy the enchantment in a grindy game.
- **Fíli the Pathfinder** — top-3 rare on **both** lists. {3}{W} 2/2 legendary dwarf with storied: with an enduring story your creatures get +1/+1, and every nontoken dwarf entering (including itself) makes a 2/2 dwarf token. Four mana for two 2/2s minimum, and you'll usually have storied by then or the turn after. Mark: **"a straight A in any red-white deck, roughly C+ in a non-dwarf deck"** — it needs both abilities, so it is *not* an exciting splash.
- **Kíli the Resourceful** — top-10 rare (both). {1}{W} 1/2 legendary dwarf with storied: with an enduring story, the first equip each turn costs {0}; and whenever another dwarf or equipment enters, **draw a card — but only once each turn**. Mark's correction to Alex: the once-per-turn gate matters more than it sounds, because several equipment come stapled to a dwarf and would otherwise double-trigger. Still a two-drop that snowballs and fuels itself. **Must-kill.**
- **Settle the Wreckage** — **deliberately excluded from both top 10s.** Alex thinks it's overrated by trauma: *"there are fewer situations where this card is going to be the blowout you think it is, and more situations than you might think where you have to use it on one creature"* — four mana to remove one creature and ramp them is a bad card. It also misses noncombat threats entirely (any landfall lord triggering every turn), and good players play around it. Alex's prediction: **this will be one of the biggest deltas between top-player and bottom-player stats on 17Lands.** He'll still play it; he just won't take it early. **Know it exists** — an opponent with {2}{W}{W} open should change your attacks; swing with five creatures instead of seven.
