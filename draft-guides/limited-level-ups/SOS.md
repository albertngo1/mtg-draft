> Source: **Limited Level-Ups** (host **Alex / Chord_O_Calls**; co-host **Mark** on the six commons/uncommons review episodes, guest **Sam** on the Rares+Mythics and Bonus Sheet reviews) — **22 Secrets of Strixhaven videos** spanning the prerelease window through the format send-off (2026-04-11 → 2026-05-29). These are spoken-word videos, so card/mechanic/college names are heavily mangled — corrected against the SOS card list where confident, uncertain readings marked `(?)`.
>
> This is the **largest single-set source in the repo** (~200k words). The channel splits its set review across **seven dedicated per-color episodes** (White / Blue / Black / Red / Green + Multicolor/Colorless + Rares & Mythics) plus a **Bonus Sheet** review, so nearly every card in the set gets an **A+→F letter grade** from Alex (with Mark's grade shown where they split). Those grades are this channel's distinctive product — preserved inline on every `## Card tips` bullet.
>
> ⚠ **PROMINENT CAVEATS:**
> - **The per-color set-review grades (04-14 → 04-17) are PREDICTIONS made before a single game was played.** They are the core graded product but they are still theory. Alex himself catalogues where they went wrong in the 05-29 send-off — read Supersessions before trusting any April grade.
> - **The First-Impressions (04-11) and Prerelease Guide (04-16) takes are the weakest evidence** — blind reads recorded before/around the card-by-card review.
> - **The 05-29 send-off ("What We Got Wrong About Strixhaven Draft") is the most authoritative source in the run** — a full month of play, 17Lands data in hand, and Alex explicitly re-grading his own review.

## ⚠ Recency rule (read first)

- **The newest source wins on conflict.** Order of authority for SOS, weakest → strongest: First Impressions 04-11 → per-color reviews 04-14 → Prerelease Guide 04-16 → Rares/Mythics + Bonus Sheet reviews 04-17 → Early Access draft 04-20 → draft VODs 04-22 → 05-12 → **State of the Format 04-25** → **Format Update 05-02** → Underrated Rares 05-08 → Mystical Archive ranking 05-16 → **Format Send-Off 05-29 (most authoritative).**
- **Every April letter grade is a PREDICTION.** Treat them as hypotheses. Alex's own 05-29 verdict is that the review had a *systematic* bias: it under-rated (a) the converge/5-color payoffs and the fixing that enables them, and (b) expensive over-the-top spells. If a card is in either bucket, shift its April grade **UP**.
- **The 04-25 State of the Format address is the best single "how to draft this" document** in the run; the 05-02 update revises it (soup got contested, Lorehold stayed underrated), and 05-29 settles it.
- **This guide decodes 17Lands rather than ranking beneath it.** SOS win rates are archetype-conditional to an extreme degree — the same card reads completely differently in a Lorehold aggro deck and in a 5-color converge control deck. Use the guide to tell which deck a number came from. See `AGENTS.md`.

## Supersessions (grade/opinion flips between first-impressions and later reviews)

The **05-29 send-off (LLU #247) is a gift** — Alex spends half the episode explicitly re-grading his own set review. He found something he says is unusual: *a systematic pattern* rather than scattered misses.

### The two systematic misses (Alex's own framing, 05-29)

- **MISS #1 — everything that supported the Converge / 5-color deck.** Both the converge payoffs *and* all the fixing that enables them. Alex: "I would call us **directionally correct**… I remember thinking, wow, Snarl Song looks awesome, Arcane Omens looks awesome. All of those were **with the caveat of *when you get there*.** We just assumed you wouldn't get there all the time. More often than not, that wasn't the case — **you did get there.**" He notes he even predicted the cause in his own Discord ("I worry it might get a little too soupy because of how much good fixing there is") and failed to connect soupy → converge. **Verdict: shift EVERY converge payoff and EVERY piece of fixing UP from its April grade.**
- **MISS #2 — the over-the-top expensive spells.** "We kind of assumed, okay, maybe you draft these really slow over-the-top blue control decks **one in 10 times**… that just wasn't the case. **Those slow decks were like half the format.**" He also under-counted how much top end a deck wants: "I was fine with **two seven-mana spells, three sixes, and a lot of fives** — a little more top end than you might expect." **Verdict: shift every 6+ mana haymaker UP.**

### Individual card reversals (05-29 unless noted)

- **Mathemagics** — review **C+/C++** → **"an A-level card."** The XX blue mythic draw spell; it decks people. Alex's biggest single miss.
- **Wisdom of Ages** — review **D+** → **"closer to a B-"**; "I think this card is legitimately good, I've had **multiple trophy decks** with it" (04-25). Every slow deck wanted a copy or two, and slow decks were half the format.
- **Divergent Equation** — review **D+** → **"closer to a B-."** "This card is really strong and doesn't look like it" (04-25). Cast for ~7, get back three spells, win.
- **Pursue the Past** — **"the number one underrated card in this set"** — underrated both in that nobody had it on their radar *and* in how good it turned out. Top common, happy to first-pick it, happy to splash it, will play **three copies** (05-02). Why the review missed: it's real card advantage (not a Tormenting Voice filter) because of flashback, the 2 life matters a lot when you're spending mana without affecting the board, *and* it triggers/feeds the whole leaves-the-graveyard theme. **Verdict: premium Lorehold common; big UP.**
- **Tome Blast** — review dismissed it ("two mana deal two, who cares if it has flashback") → **big overperformer**, "probably the card that has improved the most in my estimations" (04-25). Alex's self-diagnosis: he failed to compare to a past card — **it is Firebolt at two mana instead of one**, and "if somebody shoved a picture of Firebolt in my face I'd have been like, oh no no no, this is going to be pretty good." Generalizable rule he draws: **"removal spells with flashback tend to be very, very good — even when the flashback cost is clunky, assume they're better than they read."**
- **Molten Note** — underrated at review, and **still going 5th–7th pick mid-format** ("I'm really happy to first-pick this card"). The 8-mana flashback is "very much not a fake line of text" — you get there most games. The untap-your-creatures line: called "such a nothing line" on 04-25, then explicitly walked back on 05-02 — **"it does actually matter quite a bit"** (untap Rubble Rouser for extra mana/ping, untap Charging Strikeknight, or flash back Group Project for free).
- **Great Hall of the Biblioplex** — **"I gave it a really low grade in the set review"** → mid-format it performs at **B-** on 17Lands and **"almost getting to the A range" in top players' hands** (05-08). The #1 land for converge decks; also fine in Jeskai and straight UR as a creature-land mana sink. **Verdict: big UP — a fine P1P1/P2.**
- **Diary of Dreams** — review **C** → **"kind of ended up being more of a D."** The worst of the format's many card-draw options; you can't afford a turn-2 do-nothing vs Silverquill/Lorehold, and in the blue mirrors it gets punished by Banishing Betrayal and Proctor's Gaze. Rule Alex draws: **"when a format has an abundance of an effect, the conditional versions fall by the wayside."** Exception: it was genuinely good in one **Mardu (WB+red) control** deck where blue's card draw wasn't available.
- **Send in the Pest** — review **B-** → **"more of a C or C-, and in Silverquill more of a D-level card."** The Virus Beetle effect needs a format where you can keep the game small; SOS had far too much card draw. Still above average *in Witherbloom* where the life gain matters.
- **Repel Calamity** — mid-format **UP** (05-02): "I think this card is great, I take it really highly." Alex first-picked it over **Consigliere's Duelist** (the WWBB rare) — "I definitely wouldn't have done this on week one." 17Lands has the two neck and neck. Its virtue is **flexibility** in a 5-archetype set: the mono-colored card fits soup and Jeskai too. Note the cheap white/black creatures scale (counters), so it has targets even against aggro.
- **Strixhaven Skycoach** — under-taken; performs at the level of Consigliere's Duelist / Silverquill Charm / Elite Interceptor / Repel Calamity **in Silverquill** (05-02). "If you're not taking the Skycoach highly, I would change that." Play it in every deck with a real creature count, splash or no splash.
- **Sundering Archaic** — "very underrated"; **the one converge card that's passable on just two colors** (05-02), and a repeatable graveyard-exile engine in Lorehold (untap with it and Garrison Excavator makes three 2/2s).
- **Lorehold Charm** — "didn't think much of it at the beginning of the format… I've been playing it more and more" (05-02). Main-deck answer to **Potioner's Trove**, which is often load-bearing, plus a real go-wide finisher mode.
- **Tablet of Discovery** — **"I've gone way up on this card. I love this card now"** (05-02). Effectively a three-for-one (two spell-mana + a card); "the Potioner's Trove of my less colorful decks." Compares it to the Ravnica bounce lands — mana sources are cards.
- **Flashback (the 1-mana red rare)** — underrated until ~05-08; recursion is what wins the over-the-top mirrors. Don't first-pick it, but take it early once you're on a spells path.
- **Mana Sculpt** — goes too late; "I get it passed to me fifth pick sometimes and I'm like, slam." A modern Mana Drain in a format where everyone taps out for 5–7-drops.
- **Library of Alexandria** (Arena-only special guest) — **"I would first-pick Library out of the vast majority of packs."** Good in aggro decks too, not just control.
- **Harmonized Trio** — poor 17Lands stats → Alex disagrees with the data: the card is fine, people just play it in 7-creature blue decks. **Wants a normal ~15-creature deck; better in Quandrix than Prismari.**
- **Resonating Lute** — average-player **C**, top-player **B** (the #2 biggest top-player gap in the set). Alex: "one of my favorite cards in the set"; he has first-picked it. Requirements: essentially all-spells, **6+ removal**, 5–6 card draw, ideally some X spells.
- **Zaffai and the Tempest** — the **#1 biggest top-player gap** in the set: **D** in average hands, **C** in top hands. Not a high pick; only good in a genuinely creature-light, expensive deck.
- **Jhoira's Arthropod** — **D+ → C+** in top hands. Only play it with **three or more X spells**; don't add bad X spells (Procrastinate, Wild Hypothesis) to enable it.
- **Ral Zarek, Guest Lecturer** — **the most overrated rare in the set.** Not terrible, but the black decks (Silverquill especially) want to end games, not grind. "I'd rather they'd cast an Imperious Ink-Mage."
- **Practiced Offense** — Alex's format villain: **"if I could delete a card from the format, I would absolutely delete this card. It's egregious at rare."** Three mana, team-wide counter + lifelink or double strike, flashback for two.
- **WITHERBLOOM (BG) — the archetype-level reversal.** 04-25 and 05-02 are near-continuous Witherbloom bashing ("this is turning into Witherbloom bashing hour"; "I was so hoping I'd say things self-corrected — it's still really overdrafted"). 05-29 walks it back: **"I ragged on this deck a lot… I think it was more a product of the Arena metagame than the actual cards. The power level is there."** He cites Pro Tour tables where the 3-0 bracket was Witherbloom mirrors, and notes the casual players who first-pick life-gain frogs drifted away. **Verdict: card quality is fine; on Arena it was over-drafted, so it was a "be the only one at the table" deck. Late in the format it was genuinely fine.**
- **CONVERGE / SOUP — up, then down, then "too easy."** 04-25: loved it, all converge cards over-delivered. **05-02: "I like these decks a lot less than I did last week"** — the cards no longer wheel and, more importantly, **fixing goes early now** ("Potioner's Trove, people are second-picking this card — and I say people, I am people"). He lost repeatedly to too many tap lands and too few cheap plays, and drifted back toward 2-color-plus-a-splash. **05-29 settles it: the deck was too easy to draft and outshone the five colleges** — "when people think back to Strixhaven in a year or two, this deck is going to be a huge part of what they think about." **Verdict: real and powerful, but contested — draft it when the fixing is flowing, not by intent.**
- **QUANDRIX (GU) — never became its own deck.** 04-25: "blue-green straight up is not a great place to be." 05-29: **"It's kind of funny — I drafted Red-Green more than I drafted straight Blue-Green. You almost always wanted to splash in blue-green."** He wanted a couple more pushed tempo cards (Cuboid Colony-style) to justify a streamlined UG. **Verdict: don't draft GU as a two-color deck; it's the best *base* for a soup deck.**
- **LOREHOLD (RW) — confirmed and re-confirmed, never reversed.** Called the best deck on 04-25, "criminally underrated on Arena" on 05-02 (last-pick Wilt in the Heat and Pursue the Past happen), and "safe to say most people would say the best of the bunch" on 05-29. **The single most-confirmed take of the run.**
- **Prepared (the mechanic)** — hyped as a headline mechanic at review → 05-29: **"ended up being just kind of a background mechanic… honestly kind of just felt like a different exhaust."** Fine, not memorable. **Verdict: don't pay a premium for "it's prepared"; grade the body and the spell separately.**

### Archetype-level reversals from the prerelease predictions (04-16 → the data)

These are the two biggest misses of the whole run and Alex owns both on camera.

- **LOREHOLD (RW): predicted the WORST college → the BEST.** Prerelease Guide, 04-16: *"I do think it's potentially **the worst out of the five decks**… you're very rarely going to be able to out-grind one of the blue decks, and I think probably black-green out-grinds you pretty well too."* By 04-23 the early 17Lands story is *"basically **Lorehold is the best, Witherbloom is the worst**"*; by 04-25 it's *"fair to say Lorehold is the best deck in the format"*; by 05-29 it's *"safe to say most people would say the best of the bunch."* His diagnosis of his own error: he mis-modelled RW as a would-be grinder rather than as the **proactive deck that punishes the grinders** — *"before playing this set I wouldn't have pegged red-white as one of the better decks, but after playing even one red-white deck I was very impressed."* **Verdict: shift the entire Lorehold common/uncommon suite UP; and expect it to still be underrated on Arena — 2–2.5 drafters per table is supportable.**
- **WITHERBLOOM (BG): predicted GOOD → the worst college.** 04-16: *"just like Prismari, I think all three of Witherbloom's commons are pretty good."* The 04-11 first-impressions video *had* the right worry ("the enablers are abundant… the payoffs leave a little to be desired") and the Prerelease Guide talked itself out of it. His settled explanation (04-23) is **consistency, not power**: *"if you kill their frog, the one that gains life, all their house of cards kind of falls apart… all the other decks are more robust, have interchangeable pieces, aren't as easy to pick apart."* With the 05-29 walk-back on top, the final read is: **fine cards, fragile plan, over-drafted on Arena.**
- **PRISMARI (UR): predicted his prerelease pick → held up, but the *average* UR deck got worse.** 04-16: "if I had to pick, Prismari or Quandrix — I'm personally going with Prismari." 04-29 amendment: *"the **ceiling** of the blue-red decks has not changed. The **average** blue-red deck is a little worse than week one, when people weren't onto those decks"* — the cheap spells (Tome Blast, the UR golds) stopped tabling. **Verdict: still a top-two college, but you now have to actually be open in it.**
- **Prismari-as-prowess:** 04-11 predicted UR would be *controlling*, not a prowess deck, and Mark put it at ~90/10 controlling-to-prowess in the review. **Confirmed** — but Alex later stresses the 10% deck is real and **forceable** because nobody else takes the pieces.

### More individual reversals (review grade → later verdict)

- **Potioner's Trove** — review **D+ (Alex and Mark both!)** → **the keystone common of the format.** The review's objection was that a rock wants to be tapped for mana every turn and this one makes you choose. In practice the modes are both live and the 2 life is enormous: "the trove just gained us so much life in important spots" (04-20); "the best manalith of recent sets — better than the Tarkir: Dragonstorm one" (04-30); "Trove just makes your mana base so much easier, you play one of each and maybe not even one basic" (05-12). **By 05-02 it is being SECOND-PICKED** — "and I say people, I am people." **Verdict: from D+ to a genuine early pick — the single largest grade error of the review.**
- **Resonating Loot / Resonating Lute** — review **Alex F**, explicitly "the set reviewer's F" (a card most people are better off never playing) → **"one of my favorite cards in the set,"** first-picked several times, and the engine of a 6-2 run (05-12: "the games that Loot came down… were not close, we just took over"). 17Lands: **C in average hands, B in top hands — the #2 largest top-player gap in the set.** Requirements Alex spells out: essentially all-spells, **6+ removal**, 5–6 card draw, ideally X spells (Divergent Equation, Mathemagics, Chromatic Critique(?), Jhoira's(?)). **Verdict: F → a real first pick *if* you can build the deck. The F was directionally honest about the average player and badly wrong about the card.**
- **Great Hall of the Biblioplex** — review **Alex D / Sam D+** → 04-20 "an all right land" → 04-29 "I've come up on this card quite a bit" → 05-12 **"Hall's great. I love Hall. Hall's shot up way, way high in my pick order."** 17Lands ~**B-**, and near **A** in top players' hands. Same cause as everything else: nobody clocked how central converge would be. **Verdict: big UP — a fine P1/P2.**
- **Mathemagics** — review **Alex C+ / Sam D→C-** → **"an A-level card"** (05-29). Also an alternate win condition: it decks people.
- **Wisdom of Ages** — review **Alex D+ / Sam D-** → **"closer to a B-"**, "legitimately good — I've had multiple trophy decks with this."
- **Pursue the Past** — review **C- from BOTH hosts** → **"the number one underrated card in this set"**, a top common, first-pickable, splashable, played in threes. The clean measure of the review's blind spot.
- **Ral Zarek, Guest Lecturer** — review **Alex C+ / Sam B** → **"probably the most overrated rare in the set"** (05-08). Not weak, just misaligned: the black decks want to end games, not grind.
- **Arcane Omens (and converge cards generally) as *early picks*** — 04-25 loved them → **04-29: "I've come down a little bit on the converge stuff, like taking it early. I lost a lot this weekend just from being too clunky — too many colors, too many tap lands, people just killing me."** The cards stayed great; taking them early became the trap.
- **Elite Interceptor** — predicted at first impressions to be "one of the best commons in the set, hard for it not to be" → **confirmed and called "the best common in the set"** (04-25). It kept wheeling early on: "people just haven't caught on that this is just as good as Thraben Inspector."
- **Social Snub** — flagged a trap at the Prerelease Guide (04-16), graded **Alex D+ / Mark D**, and **confirmed a trap in play** (04-29: "not a good card, not a card I want to play"). One of the review's clean hits.
- **Sundering Archaic** — review **Alex C / Mark C+** → an overperformer and "very underrated" (05-02). It is the one converge card that is fine on two colors, it kills the format's many expensive tokens, its graveyard-bottoming mode eats opposing flashback cards, and in soup it is **anti-decking insurance.**
- **Diary of Dreams** — review **C from both** → **"more of a D"** (05-29) — with one carve-out: it is genuinely good in a **Mardu / non-blue control deck** that lacks blue's card draw.
- **Vibrant Outburst, Flow State, Lecturing Scornmage, Render Speechless, Strixhaven Skycoach, Snarl Song, Exhibition Tidecaller** — all called out in the VODs as playing at or above their review grade. Exhibition Tidecaller in particular: **~65% GIH WR on 17Lands** by 04-23 and the MVP of that draft.
- **"Tam"(?) — the slow leveling card-draw permanent** — 04-29 "I'll call it a trap; I see it on the battlefield more than I should" → 05-12 partial walk-back: "I've come up on it a little. I like it in the **non-blue** decks — a Mardu control deck is the perfect home. I don't love it in the blue decks." *(Caption unresolved; may be conflated with Diary of Dreams.)*

### Bonus-sheet reversals (04-17 grade → 05-16 tier list, one month of reps)

The 05-16 episode re-ranks the whole Archive with played experience. The biggest moves:

- **Brain Freeze** — 04-17 **sideboard card** → **A tier.** The single biggest bonus-sheet reversal. The storm deck is real: cantrips (Flow State, Deduce, Sleight of Hand) + mana (Rubble Rouser, Potioner's Trove) + **Flashback as the #1 combo card** + Sonar to tutor + Rapturous Moments as the go-off turn. First-pickable build-around.
- **Awaken the Woods** — 04-17 **Alex A-** → **D tier.** The biggest *downgrade*; Sam's April objection (average in Brothers' War data) was right. There are better mana sinks here.
- **Bring to Light** — 04-17 **Alex F** → **C tier.** Self-corrected on air: "when we first looked at this I was like, maybe you'll do converge sometimes" — and converge turned out to be the format.
- **Prismatic Ending** — **D+** → **B, but only in the 5-color converge deck** (in two colors it's a 23rd card that kills a one-drop).
- **Daze** — **C** → **B.** "One of the most underrated cards in the bonus sheet — nobody in this set ever stops tapping out." (Playing around Daze is named as a real format skill.)
- **Repel Calamity** — **C** → **B.** The April fear (Prismari 3/3 tokens dodge it) was wrong; everything in this set is big or scales.
- **Abrade** — **C+** → **B.** Incidental artifact removal is worth a lot when Potioner's Trove is everywhere.
- **Brotherhood's End** — **C+** → **B.** Not just control — Lorehold can build around surviving it.
- **Hop to It** — **C+** → **D.** The white go-wide plan never materialized and the card touches no set mechanic.
- **Zombify** — **D+** → **F.** "Not a big-creature set" — Alex flagged this as a surprise to himself.
- **Shamanic Revelation** — **C-** → **F**; **Duty Beyond Death** C- → D; **Sheoldred's Edict** C- → D; **Royal Treatment** B- → C.
- **Empty the Warrens** — **D+** → **C** (doesn't need the full storm build); **Shared Roots** D+ → C; **Feed the Swarm** D → C; **Ad Nauseam** F → D (Witherbloom really does cast it at 40 life); **Vampiric Tutor** F → D (only for Arc of Hunger (?) / Together as One); **Triumph of the Hordes** F → D; **Armageddon** stays F but is named a **real white-aggro sideboard card vs the blue control decks.**
- **Subterranean Tremors** — the April episode's biggest host disagreement (**Alex A- / Sam C-**) resolved **in Alex's favor**: A tier. Nine mana is reachable with Trove + Rubble Rouser.
- **Stargaze** — **C-** → C on the tier list, but flagged as **"one of the few remaining hidden gems"** he plays more and more. Directionally a bigger up than the tier shows.


## Format speed / meta read

**Alex's overall grade for SOS: 7.5/10** — "a good, fun, interesting format, quite a bit of depth, but it probably **indulged a little too much into the over-the-top flashiness of it all.** A case of a little too much of a good thing… a little too often the games devolved into who can make the most mana, who can draw the most cards, who can cast the best top-end cards" (05-29).

- **This is a POLARIZED format with two poles, and the middle gets crushed.** Pole A: the **blue over-the-top decks** (UR control, UG-base soup, 4–5 colors) that survive, kill everything, draw a million cards, and cast spells the opponent cannot beat. Pole B: the **white aggro decks** (Lorehold RW and Silverquill WB) that exist to punish them. Alex explicitly compares the shape to Tarkir: Dragonstorm. **Everything in between suffers** — which is exactly Witherbloom's problem.
- **Aggro → control spectrum:** Silverquill (fastest) → Lorehold → **Witherbloom (the squeezed middle)** → the blue decks. Witherbloom isn't fast enough to get under the blue decks, and the white decks grind well enough (flashback, Pursue the Past) that Witherbloom's usual midrange edge against aggro is negated.
- **Only ~50% of success is archetype knowledge; the other 50% is draft navigation.** Alex's headline framing (04-25): unlike Lorwyn-style guild sets where studying the five decks gets you 80% there, in SOS each college can be drafted **multiple different ways**, so "study the archetypes" only gets you halfway. The rest is in-the-moment reads — when to splash, when to narrow, whether your deck needs removal or draw or finishers.
- **Removal is excellent in every color, and Alex — usually a removal-skeptic — loads up.** "I'm usually somebody who's a little lower on removal than most folks. That is **not** the case for this format." Benchmarks: red is best and cheapest (Abrade, Burst Lightning, **Impractical Joke** — 1 mana deal 3, **Tome Blast**); white (Erode, Ajani's Response, **Wilt in the Heat**); black (Last Gasp, Bitter Triumph, Wander Off); blue (Essence Scatter, Brush Off, Mana Sculpt); **Repel Calamity** as the flexible colorless-ish answer. Counts: **5–6 removal in a white deck, 10–11 in a control deck.**
- **Removal + card draw is the format's core engine.** So much card draw exists that removal never goes dead and card draw always has something to buy. Also, **almost nothing on board is ignorable** — "most of the cards the opponent plays are like, oh yeah, that's a problem, I might just die if I don't deal with that."
- **Counterspells are genuinely good and go too late.** "This is just a very strong format for counter spells… whenever people are casting five, six, seven mana spells with consistency, counters are really great." Alex's broader advice: **most Limited players should bump their counterspell evaluations up a notch or two.**
- **Fixing is exceptional — "some of the best fixing we've had in a while, maybe even better than Tarkir: Dragonstorm, the actual three-color set."** Dual lands, Terramorphic Expanse, treasures, **Potioner's Trove**, the green fixers (Environmental Scientist, Studious First-Year), Strixhaven Skycoach, Great Hall of the Biblioplex. This is *why* converge worked.
- **Play two tap lands even in a two-color deck.** "Two is the Goldilocks zone." Reason: the set is dense with **gold two-drops** — Alex had a Lorehold deck where every two-drop was a gold card.
- **Play more top end than you think.** Two seven-drops, three sixes, and a lot of fives was comfortable. The review's "you only want two expensive spells" instinct was wrong.
- **Games are swingy — a handful of cards flip a game on the spot.** Named: **Tide Mystic** (1-mana 0/2 that mills a bunch), **Practiced Offense**, **Mathemagics**, **Crackle with Power**. "You're both halfway through your deck thinking: what's the card they have that completely changes this game?"
- **Expect your rares to die.** "There's so much removal running around — you should expect your good cards not to stick around very long." Practical corollary: **hold value permanents until you can bank at least one trigger** (Pensive Professor, Stirring Hope Singer) rather than running them out on curve.
- **The bonus sheet is the format's best feature.** Alex's hot take: "Limited would be made better if **every** format had its own Mystical Archive" — a slot in every pack that is almost always removal, card draw, or utility, is synergy-agnostic, and lets you delay committing to a deck. "A bit of a free bingo space."
- **Three-color decks that actually happened:** **Jeskai** (UR base splashing the good Lorehold cards — Pursue the Past, Molten Note, Ajani's Response, Practiced Scrollsmith, Colossus of the Blood-Age) is a genuine pillar; **Sultai** (UG base adding red or black) came together a decent amount. **Mardu and Abzan basically never happened** — the WB decks don't want to splash, and the cards seeded to encourage it (Social Snub, Eternal Student) weren't juiced enough to be worth it.
- **Mechanics, settled:** **Prepared** = fine but forgettable, "a different exhaust." **Repartee** = real; you want **8–9 ways to target**, and the one-mana tricks (Interjection, Masterful Flourish, Dig Site Inventory) table if you need more. **Opus / big opus (5+ mana)** = the aggressive UR/RW prowess deck's engine. **Increment** = under-supported, suffered with Quandrix. **Converge** = the format's defining mechanic; the sweet spot is **X = 4 or more colors**. **Lorehold's leaves-the-graveyard** = the design win, "its theme worked, which is a lot more than you can say the first time around."
- **The set-review-era power ranking of the colleges never really moved: Lorehold ≥ Prismari > Silverquill > Witherbloom > Quandrix,** with Converge/soup sitting above the colleges in raw power and below them in reliability once the table catches on.

## Archetypes (the five colleges + Converge, ranked)

*(SOS is a five-college set, not a ten-pair set, so the contract's 10-pair layout is replaced by the five colleges, the sixth unwritten Converge archetype, and the three-color shells that actually materialized. Ranked by Alex's settled read across 04-25 → 05-29.)*

### 1. Lorehold (RW) — cards leaving the graveyard. **Alex's best deck in the format, and criminally underrated on Arena.**
- **Plan:** A textbook good midrange deck — plan A is beat down, but if you get brick-walled you grind with flashback and recursion. "It has a good proactive plan… and if the opponent has a lot of removal, it's not dead in the water." Your job in the metagame is to **pressure the blue decks before they go over the top.**
- **Do NOT splash.** "Resistant to splashing" — you want two-on-two, three-on-three, four-on-four, not a pile of tap lands. Alex basically never adds a color to Lorehold.
- **Key commons:** **Rubble Rouser** (2-mana 1/4; ETB rummage, then tap + exile a card from your yard for red mana *and* ping each opponent — "the most important common for the deck" and the only repeatable leaves-the-graveyard engine), **Stone Docent** (the W 3/1 for two that exiles from the yard to gain 2 + surveil 1 — "my most prioritized two-drop in Lorehold"), **Spirit Mascot** (RW 2/2 that grows on leaves-the-graveyard; only good *with* Rubble Rouser), **Tome Blast**, **Wilt in the Heat** (2RW instant, 5 damage + exile, costs 2 if a card left your yard), **Pursue the Past**, **Ascendant Spirit Speaker** (5-mana 3/4 flyer, ETB counter + exile a card from a graveyard at each combat — a second engine).
- **Key uncommons:** **Practiced Scrollsmith** (hybrid RW 3/2 first strike; ETB exile a noncreature from your yard and cast it — "the best red-white uncommon"), **Colossus of the Blood-Age** (4RW 6/6 artifact; drain 3 on ETB, loot-plus-one on death — "the most juiced Ravenous Lindworm you've ever seen"; **second-best RW uncommon**), **Living History** (1R enchantment; 2/2 spirit + a free +2/+0 whenever a card left your yard), **Carrel, History Buff (?)** (RW 2/3 that becomes prepared — liked, not loved: "there's so much to do with my mana in Lorehold"), **Startled Sloth Relic**, **Molten Note**, **Garrison Excavator** (4-mana 3/4 menace making a 2/2 per leaves-the-graveyard trigger), **Lorehold Charm**.
- **Sub-variants:** (a) standard leaves-the-graveyard; (b) **go-wide tokens** (Hop to It, Eager Glyph Mage, Group Project) — the Lorehold Charm pump mode gets real here; (c) **RW prowess** built on 3–4 **Expressive Fire Dancer** plus Monstrous Rage / Ancestral Anger / Dig Site Inventory / Kiroa's trample. Alex: "really important to hone in on which flavor of red-white you're drafting" — you'll end the draft with ~30 playables.
- **Watch for:** **Summoned Dromedary** (3W 4/3 vigilance, 2 to return from yard to hand) is great *specifically* when you have discard outlets (Pursue the Past, Charging Strikeknight) and absurd with **Hardened Academic**.

### 2. Prismari (UR) — the control base, and the most versatile college.
- **Plan A (the main one):** blue-red over-the-top control. Alex's recipe: **4–5 card-draw effects, 8–9 pieces of interaction (counterspells count), 3–5 ways to go over the top and win, ~6–7 actual creatures**, rest flex.
- **Plan B (the other deck):** the **most explosive aggressive deck in the format** — Expressive Fire Dancer / Deluge Virtuoso / Tackle Artist / Spectacular Skywhale plus cheap spells (Ancestral Anger, Monstrous Rage, Bulk Up, Procrastinate, Tome Blast). Alex's tip: **you can force this** because the pieces (Skywhale, the angers, Bulk Up) go late — take them all early and the rest arrives.
- **The critical discipline:** *know which deck you're in.* "Somebody sends me a blue-red deck tech and I take out nine cards" — aggro creatures stuffed into a control deck. A four-mana 1/4 flyer is not a control card.
- **Key cards:** **Sonar, Unfinished Genius** (UR 0/4 that enters prepared, taps for a treasure after you cast a spell, and whose prepared spell tutors an instant/sorcery — "one of the coolest cards in the set… such a nightmare to see on turn two"), **Potioner's Trove**, **Tablet of Discovery**, **Resonating Lute**, **Visionary's Dance** (the common 7-mana two-3/3-flyers finisher — "the poor man's finisher, totally solid, but you can do better"), **Divergent Equation**, **Wisdom of Ages**, **Exhibition Tidecaller**, **Mathemagics**, **Crackle with Power**, **Brain Freeze** + **Empty the Warrens** (real storm build-arounds, made much smoother by the red **Flashback** rare), **Rapturous Moments** (double-opus and storm enabler), **Zaffai and the Tempest**.
- **Splash direction:** into white (see Jeskai below) or into green for fixing.

### 3. Converge / 5-Color Soup — the unwritten sixth archetype. Enormously powerful, and by week two, contested.
- **Plan:** get to **four or five colors** and cast payoffs nobody can beat. Usually a UR or UG base.
- **The payoffs:** **Snarl Song** (5G sorcery; two fractals with X counters each + gain X — "at five colors, two 5/5s and gain 5. Incredible"), **Arcane Omens** (4B; opponent discards X — "such a backbreaker"), **Archaic's Agony** (4R; X damage to a creature, excess damage exiles that many cards from your library to play — **"I think this might be the most underrated card in the format"**; it's a split card of clunky removal / draw three), **Sundering Archaic** (6-mana 3/3, exiles a creature with MV ≤ colors — passable on two colors), **Transcendent Archaic** (7-mana 7/7 drawing per color), **Together as One** (6-mana **A+ rare**: draw X, deal X, gain X — and it decks people).
- **The enablers:** **Potioner's Trove** (Alex plays the 1st copy in every 3-color deck, the 2nd once he's more colorful; more copies means you want more card draw), **Great Hall of the Biblioplex** (the best land for the deck), Terramorphic Expanse, **Environmental Scientist**, **Studious First-Year**, **Strixhaven Skycoach**, the dual lands.
- **The self-check (05-02):** *"Am I seeing enough fixing to consistently get converge to X=4?"* If you're mid-pack-two with one triome, one dual, and three converge cards — **pivot out.**
- **The trap:** too many tap lands and too few cheap plays loses to Silverquill/Lorehold, and there is always a bigger fish (Wisdom of Ages, Mathemagics, Exhibition Tidecaller). "The deck has cannibalized itself a little bit."

### 4. Silverquill (WB) — repartee aggro. The format's necessary check on the blue decks.
- **Plan:** the **fastest deck in the format**; gives up late game relative to Lorehold but is more explosive. **No splashing, essentially ever.** Alex's one structural criticism of the whole set: Silverquill is the college with the *least* variation — every other college can be drafted slow or fast, Silverquill only wants to be aggressive.
- **Repartee math:** want **8–9 ways to target**. Removal and tricks count. If you get to pack three and find you have four removal spells and one other targeting card, buy up the one-mana tricks — **Interjection** (W, +2/+2 + first strike), **Masterful Flourish** (B, +1/+0 + indestructible), **Dig Site Inventory** (W sorcery, counter + vigilance, flashback for W) all wheel.
- **Key commons that curve into each other:** **Elite Interceptor** (**"the best common in the set"** — 1-mana 1/2 wizard that enters prepared; the Rejoinder spell taps/untaps a creature and draws; "slightly worse than Thraben Inspector, but not by a lot"), **Inkling Mascot** (WB 2/2, gains flying + surveil on each target), **Imperious Ink-Mage** (3-mana 3/3 vigilance, ETB surveil 2 — "great filler, maybe great filler plus"), **Render Speechless** (4-mana: look at their hand, take a card, two +1/+1 counters — **"awesome, very strong, happy to play three copies," and it still goes last-pick**).
- **Rares/uncommons:** **Consigliere's Duelist** (WWBB 4/3 rare — draw + drain on ETB, blink on repartee), **Silverquill Charm**, **Graduation Day** (1-mana W enchantment; "**B+ if you're always facing white aggro, D if you never are**" — a genuinely bimodal card), **Moment of Reckoning** (the 7-mana BBWW rare that kills and/or reanimates four things — so busted you just play it in your aggro deck).
- **The trap:** **Social Snub.** "It's a trap. This just doesn't do at all what Silverquill decks want to do." It's a seed for Witherbloom that nobody could use.
- **Play the Skycoach** — it performs in WB at the level of the Duelist and Elite Interceptor.

### 5. Witherbloom (BG) — life-gain creatures. Fine cards, terrible Arena positioning (until late).
- **Plan:** build it **aggressive**, not midrange. Curve **Bogwater Lumina** (BG 2/2 that gains a life on each creature ETB — "the most important common for this deck; you just want to start every game with one on turn two") into **Pest Mascot** (3-mana 2/3 trample that grows on each life gain) and keep growing. Do **not** try to out-grind the blue decks — "that's barking up the wrong tree," and the do-nothing cards make you worse against the white decks.
- **The Arena problem (04-25, 05-02):** it is by far the most over-drafted college. The **top-picked commons on 17Lands were mostly black and green** — Last Gasp, Studious First-Year, Wander Off, Grapple with Death, Bogwater Lumina, Pest Mascot. "There are at least two people trying to draft black-green at most tables — **which is why Lorehold tends to be wide open.**" Alex's advice at the time: bias away from it on Arena; he'd happily draft it at a PT/LGS where the field evaluates correctly. **05-29 walks this back — the deck was fine, the metagame wasn't.**
- **Secondary structural problem:** it's the one deck with **a distinct lack of things to spend mana on** — the only college where Alex found flooding to be a real issue.
- **Key cards:** **Professor Dellian Fell** (the BG planeswalker — "excellent, phenomenal," and **splashable into anything**), **Blex, Loathing Pest** (1BG 3/4 rare that counters up every Pest/Bat/Insect/Snake/Spider on each life gain — "gets out of hand real, real quick"), **Cauldron of Essence** (1BG artifact; drain on each creature death, and a 3-mana sac-to-reanimate — the *one* grindy card Alex endorses here, because the blue decks struggle to answer an artifact), **Pest-Proof Sloth (?)** (4-mana 4/4 reach dying into two Pests — great in any green deck; **infinite loop with Cauldron**), **Luin, Exchange Student** (4-mana 3/4 prepared, re-preparable by exiling a creature from your yard), **Essence Guildmage**, **Grapple with Death**, **Last Gasp**.
- **Witherbloom the Balancer** (the 8-mana affinity-for-creatures 5/5 flying deathtouch mythic) — **not a reason to move in.** "Generally a five-mana 5/5 deathtouch flyer that makes your spells cheaper. That'd be a great card in 2015."
- **The viable pivot:** Witherbloom's green fixers + black removal make a **good base for a Converge control deck.** That's the better use of an early BG start.

### 6. Quandrix (GU) — increment. **Basically didn't exist as a two-color deck.**
- **Plan:** don't. "It's very rare that you should play straight-up two-color blue-green" (05-02); "I drafted Red-Green more than I drafted straight Blue-Green" (05-29). Its gold cards mostly don't line up with the over-the-top plan it wants.
- **What's actually good:** **Embrace the Paradox** (5-mana UG instant, draw 3 + land onto the battlefield — a real card), **Proctor's Gaze** (4-mana Simic instant: bounce a nonland + fetch a basic — also the format's best punish for Diary of Dreams), **Environmental Scientist** and **Studious First-Year** (the two best converge-base creatures), **Cuboid Colony** (2-mana flash flyer with trample + increment — the one card that hints at a real UG tempo deck), **Quandrix the dragon** (6-mana 6/6 flying trample cascade mythic — but **cut counterspells, combat tricks, and X spells** from a Quandrix deck; you want good flips), **Jhoira's Arthropod** (only with 3+ X spells), **Pensive Professor**, **Harmonized Trio** (better here than in UR — wants a real creature count).
- **What's filler:** **Terraclactyl** (X UG flyer) and **Fractal Mascot** (6-mana 6/6 trample) — "extremely filler; they don't reward you for being straight blue-green."
- **Use it as:** the most **consistent base for a soup deck.**

### Honorable mention — Jeskai (UR + white) control
Alex's most common three-color landing spot and "kind of a pillar of the format for me." UR control base splashing **Pursue the Past** (when short on card draw), **Molten Note**, **Ajani's Response** (5-mana W destroy, 2 if the creature is tapped), **Practiced Scrollsmith**, **Colossus of the Blood-Age** (when short on a finisher), **Repel Calamity**, **Arc of Hunger (?)**. **Sultai** (UG base + red or black) also came together regularly. **Mardu and Abzan did not.**

## Draft navigation (Alex's two strategies, 05-02)

Alex splits his whole approach on one question: **did you start with an aggressive white card?**

- **Strategy A — you opened a strong white card.** Put the blinders on. "There are enough good white cards to go around that you can kind of just draft that deck — roughly **70–80% of the time, if you start white, you end up in a good white deck.**" The only question you're answering as the pack moves is *Lorehold or Silverquill?* If the white lane looks contested around pick 4–5, **lean into the red cards specifically** — red is the flexible color, and its best cards (Molten Note, Pursue the Past, the burn) are equally good in RW aggro and in UR control, so red keeps both doors open.
- **Strategy B — you started anywhere else.** Stay maximally flexible. Early picks should be **A/B-level cards, removal, and things that open pathways** — Potioner's Trove, Terramorphic Expanse, a speculative dual. Don't lock colors in pack one. **Goal: identify a two-color base by the end of pack one; lock it by the middle of pack two; then decide how many extra colors (possibly zero) to add.**
- **Read signals at picks 4–5, and read the wheel at pick 9.** "You can't deduce much from pick two. By pick four or five the shape of what's open starts to surface." Rubric: **zero playables per pack = closed; one = medium/contested; two or three = wide open.**
- **Narrow or widen — pick one, deliberately.** Widen when (a) your two-color lane isn't open, (b) you see converge cards or off-color rares worth the fixing tax, or (c) **you're short on a role** (removal, card draw, a finisher) and a splash fills it. Otherwise narrow: "it is a big benefit to be just two colors if you can get a strong enough deck."

## Two-card interactions worth clocking (05-02)

- **Informed Innkeeper + Spirit-Cast Enthusiast** — the Innkeeper makes a 1/1 flyer on each reanimate; the Enthusiast becomes prepared on each token ETB and its prepared spell is 1W for two creatures +2/+2. With both out you loop 1W → +2/+2 twice and a flyer, as often as you can pay.
- **Pest-Proof Sloth (?) + Cauldron of Essence** — sac the Sloth, make two Pests, reanimate, repeat. Builds an unattackable board and drains them out.
- **Potioner's Trove + any infuse spell** — cast the spell, hold priority, tap the Trove for 2 life. **Every infuse card is enabled for free** (e.g. Withering Curse becomes a one-sided wrath).
- **Elite Interceptor's Rejoinder + Aziza** — Rejoinder untaps your own creature; Aziza copies it by tapping three. Net: tap one creature to copy Rejoinder and **draw two**.
- **Group Project + Molten Note** — Group Project's flashback is "tap three untapped creatures," and Molten Note untaps your team. Free flashback.
- **Brain Freeze / Empty the Warrens + Flashback (the red rare)** — Flashback adds a storm count *and* rebuys the storm card, so you kill from storm 3–4 instead of 6.
- **PSA: Improvisation Capstone exiles, it doesn't bottom.** Alex watched two winning opponents mill themselves out with it. "They did it again, took their draw step, and died."

## Card tips

Grades below are Alex's per-episode letter grades on an A+→F scale. **Attribution:** the commons/uncommons reviews (W/U/B/R/G + Multicolor/Colorless, 2026-04-14) are Alex **with co-host Mark**; the **Rares + Mythics** and **Bonus Sheet** reviews (2026-04-17) are Alex **with guest Sam (Rough Drafts)** — Mark was travelling. Where the two hosts split, shown as `Alex X / Mark Y` or `Alex X / Sam Y`. Two channel-specific qualifiers recur: the **"coward C"** (a card with a genuinely wide outcome range, not a confident middling read) and the **"set reviewer's F"** (an F meaning *most people are better off never playing this*, not *this is unplayable* — Alex expects to play it himself).

**Layout:** commons and uncommons are grouped by color (W/U/B/R/G), then the gold cards by college, then colorless/artifacts, then lands. Because this channel reviews **rares and mythics in one dedicated episode**, those are kept together in their own block after Lands, sub-grouped by the same color order — followed by the bonus sheet.

**⚠ Every grade here is a PREDICTION recorded before the format existed.** Read Supersessions first. The two systematic corrections: **converge payoffs + fixing are all under-graded**, and **expensive over-the-top spells are all under-graded.** Where a later episode moved a card, the played read is flagged inline.

### White (W)

*Commons and uncommons from the white review (2026-04-14). Color-level read: **"White's got a lot of rock-solid C two-drops."** The set is slower than it looks — more toughness than power, Lorwyn-style — and you don't want to attack with prepared creatures. It's also a **pushed** set, which pushed several white commons down a grade. Note white has **no Pacifism / Oblivion Ring**, so white removal mostly has to actually kill. Running trend in this episode: Mark D+ where Alex is C-.*

- **Elite Interceptor** — **B (white review, both) — "maybe the best common in the set."** 1-mana 1/2 wizard that enters prepared; Rejoinder (2 mana) taps *or untaps* a creature and draws a card. The Thraben Inspector comparison. The risk is they kill it before you cast Rejoinder — but almost no removal in the set cleanly trades with it on mana, and "a turn-one 1/2 you never have to attack with is genuinely hard to attack into." **04-25 promotes it flatly to "the best common in the set." Predicted at first impressions, confirmed in play, and it still wheeled early on: "people haven't caught on that this is just as good as Thraben Inspector."**
- **Matterbending Mage** — B+ *(listed under Blue in the review; see Blue)*.
- **Quillblade Laureate** — B- (white review) / Mark B. 2-mana 1/1 double strike with a burst-damage prepared spell. "A two-mana 1/1 double strike is almost playable on its own," and **every Silverquill counter naturally lands on this thing — scary, scary card.**
- **Primary Research (?)** — B- (white review) / Mark B. 5 mana: reanimate a ≤3-MV permanent, draw immediately, then it's an engine. Alex: **"this one has a legitimate chance"** of actually out-carding the blue decks, since drawing extra cards also refills the graveyard.
- **Group Project** — B- (white review) / Mark C+. 2-mana sorcery for two 2/2s, flashback by tapping three untapped creatures. "Giving up temporary tempo for permanent tempo is usually a pretty good deal," plus a leaves-the-graveyard trigger. Alex flagged low confidence at B-. **Played read: he later named this as the format's **second** card he'd delete ("a little less egregious" than Practiced Offense) — it's a banger. Combos with Molten Note (free flashback) and Lorehold Charm's go-wide mode.**
- **Eager Glyph Mage** — C+ (white review, both). 3W for a 3/3 plus a 1/1 Inkling flyer. Tokens shrank since original Strixhaven, "but it's still a banger." **04-25 caveat: it's a good Limited card that does nothing for Lorehold's theme, so you'd happily upgrade it to something synergistic.**
- **Stone Docent** — C (white review, both). 1W 3/1; pay W and exile it from your graveyard to gain 2 + surveil 1 (sorcery speed). The only 2-mana 3/1 in the set; part of Alex's evidence that the format is slow (more toughness than power). **04-25 upgrades it: "my most prioritized two-drop when I'm playing Lorehold."**
- **Shattered Acolyte** — C (white review, both). 2-mana 2/2 lifelink. No synergies, but solid, and "really nice with the white-black combat tutorials."
- **Alora, Historian (?)** — C (white review, both). 2W 2/3 flyer, ETB surveil 1, grows on each card leaving your graveyard. Glider Kids with the type swapped for a Lorehold pump; "evens out, maybe slightly better." **Mid-format 17Lands has it as a benchmark C card.**
- **Graduation Day** — C (white review) / Mark D+ then bumped. 1-mana W enchantment: a +1/+1 counter on a creature whenever you cast a targeting spell. Alex started low, saw LSV rating it highly, and **"refused to be a coward"**: a turn-one play gets four or five triggers in an average game, which is a lot of stats for one mana — but it's terrible off the top and your ducks all have to be in a row. **04-25 sharpens it into the format's most bimodal card: "B+ if you're always facing a white aggro deck, D if you never are."** 04-29 cut it from a Lorehold deck with only six targeting cards — **wants ~7+.**
- **Ajani's Response** — C (white review) / Mark C-. 5-mana W destroy a creature; **2 mana if it's tapped.** "They print this card every set under a different name." First copy in most decks. **Played read (05-02): a favorite Jeskai splash — "really nice efficient removal for your control decks."**
- **Stand Up for Yourself** — C (white review) / Mark C+. Alex went the other direction from Mark: yes there are lots of big things and big tokens, but also lots of two-power creatures with good abilities and prepared spells, so it balances out.
- **Rehearsed Debater (?)** — C- (white review) / Mark C. Mark preferred Alex's lower grade — "there are a lot of three-drops in this set and you'll be flooded for options, so don't stack these."
- **Summoned Dromedary (?)** — C- (white review) / Mark C. 3W 4/3 vigilance, pay 2 to return it from your graveyard to hand. Alex finds it vanilla and it trades down — **but 05-02 rehabilitates it**: it's a piece of cardboard that cycles yard→hand→yard, so you want it **when you have discard outlets** (Pursue the Past, Charging Strife Knight) and it is absurd with **Hardened Academic**. Alex also supplies the format point that makes recursion good here: white has no Pacifism and blue has no lockdown enchantment, so **things actually have to die.**
- **Dig Site Inventory (?)** — C- (white review) / Mark D+. 1-mana W sorcery: +1/+1 counter + vigilance, flashback for W. Alex expects it to be **overrated, "maybe even including by me,"** and to come down to D+ once he plays it. Mark: "I don't want to play cards to trigger my effects, I just want to play normally good Magic cards." **Played read: it's one of the cheap repartee-fillers that wheels, and it's a real piece of the RW prowess build (Dig Site Inventory + Fire Dancer = attack for six).**
- **Ascendant Dust Speaker (?)** — C- (white review) / Mark D+. Common 5-drop 3/4 flyer; ETB a counter on another creature, and exile a card from a graveyard at each combat. Alex started it at C and slid down because "this is a pretty powerful set overall, the cards are fairly pushed"; Mark's objection is that common five-drops lose the slot to uncommon five-drops. **04-25 walks it back up — "a card that has surprised me," because the repeatable exile does the same job as Rubble Rouser and also hates on opposing flashback.** Caution: it triggers *their* leaves-the-graveyard payoffs too.

### Blue (U)

*Commons and uncommons from the blue review (2026-04-14). Color-level read: the set has **slightly fewer creatures than average**, which is the one ding on Essence Scatter. The **cheap blue trifecta** (Essence Scatter / Quick Study / the top-or-bottom spell) supports a blue-red draw-go deck — and that is the real Prismari deck. The prowess/opus aggro payoffs are **graded a notch low on purpose** because "that deck isn't going to be the default," so they should table. Two-toughness blue creatures are awkward in combat, and increment creatures line up badly on curve, especially on the draw.*

- **Matterbending Mage** — B+ (blue review, both). Man-o'-War. "They've printed this every set lately and every time — yep, this one's great." Bouncing punishes big fractals and elementals; the X-spell unblockable rider is a bonus. Also a **Wizard** for Mana Sculpt.

Important rules note: **prepared creatures' spells do NOT count**, so the sorcery half is harder than people realize. "B cards are cards that you first-pick and you're not that unhappy about." **Played read: Alex ran THREE in one deck and won — "triple Flow State was great, obviously" (04-20); 05-08 calls Expressive Iteration "Flow State-adjacent, often a top-10 card in my deck." ### Black (B)

*Commons and uncommons from the black review (2026-04-14). Color-level read: **black is "a little more aggressive this time around,"** which is why the slow grindy cards get docked. The set has **very few tap-down or exile-style answers**, so protection tricks beat most black removal. The two black two-drops define the split — the repartee drain 2/2 for the aggressive builds, the 1/1-pest discard for the controlling ones. **Pest/token density is lower than people assume**, which drags the pest payoffs down. Discard is better than usual because pitching a land is a real cost. Lifegain is common enough that infusion triggers reliably.*

- **Foolish Fate (?)** — **B (black review, both).** 3-mana destroy a creature + they lose 3 if you gained life. **"I love this design."** Single-pip, so you can basically always cast it on three — "way better than Murder." Alex is explicit on the range: **B- in Silverquill with zero lifegain, and "honestly like an A-" in Witherbloom with guaranteed lifegain.**
- **Send in the Pest** — **B- (black review, both) — "one of the top commons."** 1B: a 1/1 Pest and each opponent discards. Burglar Rat plus a body, and discard hits harder in a set where hitting land drops matters, so pitching a land is a real cost. Downside: not a creature, so you can't blink it. **THE REVIEW'S BIGGEST OVERSHOOT: 05-29 re-grades it "more of a C or C-, and in Silverquill more of a D-level card" — the format had far too much card draw for a Virus Beetle to keep the game small. Still above average in Witherbloom, where the life gain matters. DOWN.**
- **Cost of Brilliance** — B- (black review) / Mark C+ (who wished he'd joined Alex). The black combat tutorial: put a counter on something, draw two, lose two. Alex kept moving up on it — when this effect last appeared (Final Fantasy) it was far better than anyone expected, "stats stapled to your card draw is a big deal," and the two life isn't a true downside because you can point it at the opponent for the last two points. *(Cross-source: LR's end-of-format verdict is "saw tons of play, totally solid" — one of black's best commons.)*
- **Last Gasp** — C+ (black review, both). **"Efficient removal, nothing else to say."** *(Note: this was the single **highest-picked common in the set** on 17Lands as of 05-02 — a big part of why Witherbloom was over-drafted and Lorehold was open.)*
- **Burgog Bainmaker (?)** — C+ (black review, both). Unassuming but **"surprisingly annoying on both defense and offense"** — the threat of activation means you can basically always attack with it, and a 1/1 deathtouch blocker is good with or without mana up.
- **Lecturing Scornmage** — C+ (black review) / Mark C. A 1-drop that stacks repartee counters. "A one-drop is so easy to fit," and once the counters stack it holds back creatures and becomes a threat they have to spend a real card on. **There aren't many one-drop creatures in the set.** **Played read (04-22): "I think Scornmage is really, really good. I'm a fan… this thing is awesome." UP.**
- **End the Hunts (?)** — C+ (black review) / Mark C-. "Get rid of their biggest mana-value thing is surprisingly good." Slightly worse when the biggest thing is a fractal token — which is Mark's whole objection, along with all the small prepared creatures you'd rather kill.
- **Eternal Student** — C+ (black review) / Mark C- (bumping up). 4-mana 4/2; exile it from your yard for 2 to make two 1/1 spirits. Mark worried the 4/2 trades down so often the token rate is fake; Alex won the argument — "six mana to trade with a 2/2 and make two Inklings is still fine" — and flagged it as **a secret Mardu card you can discard to the Lorehold looters and never have to cast.** **Played read (04-29): a viewer called it underrated and Alex agreed — "I like this card a lot. I'm a fan." 05-29 names it as one of the cards seeded to push three-color decks that wasn't quite juiced enough.**
- **Wander Off** — C (black review) / Mark C-. Plain 4-mana exile. Annoying that it can't hit the ward four-drops, and they usually want a 4-mana black catch-all to also make a treasure or a food — but **exile does matter here.** *(Third-highest-picked common on 17Lands as of 05-02; Alex takes it later than the field does.)*
- **Rabid Attack** — C (black review, both). Really nice with pests that are already priced into attacking, since it makes their good blocks stop mattering; it's also a targeting spell, so Silverquill is happy. Both hosts like it better than Dissection Practice. *(Cross-source: LR's "looks good when you read it, doesn't see play" pick at end of format. Treat with caution.)*
- **Melancholic Poet (?)** — C (black review) / Mark C-. 2-mana 2/2 that drains on each repartee trigger. Alex was down on it until he saw how it curves into the black combat tutorial; in most Silverquill decks it drains for at least two before it trades, and it's easier to trigger than the Fell Omen raven from OTJ. *(Mid-format 17Lands has it as a benchmark C card.)*
- **Sneering Shadow Raider (?)** — C (black review) / Mark C-. "A solid C" — you won't always play it, because it won't be your five-drop of choice if you have better stuff.
- **Arnyn, Deathbloom Botanist** — C (black review) / Mark C- ("closer to a D+"). Mostly a pest payoff — and both hosts talked themselves into the **token count being lower than people realize** (at common in black it's basically just Send in the Pest), so it won't trigger that much.
- **Poisoner's Apprentice (?)** — C (black review) / Mark C- ("could even see C+"). Morbid Banshee is the warning case, but **"there's life gain all over the place"** so you get the -4/-4 pretty often; the Bogwater Luminary → this curve on turn three is clean, and worst case you throw away a Pest and it's Bone Splinters. Don't do it into open mana.

- **Dissection Practice (?)** — C- (black review) / Mark D+. Skulduggery was a huge overperformer in Ixalan, a set built on cheap creature combat, and has never come close since. - **Leech Collector (?)** — C- (black review) / Mark D. The **once-per-turn clause** is the problem: you can Bloodletting repeatedly but only become prepared once a turn, and a 2-mana 2/2 that dings for two is roughly a three-mana card. Alex conceded "I might be too hot on it."
- **Masterful Flourish** — D+ (black review, both). 1-mana +1/+0 and indestructible. Filler — **but because the set has so few tap-down / Banishing-Light effects, indestructible answers most of the removal, so it could overperform.** **04-25 flags it as one of the two 1-mana tricks that always wheel when you need repartee count.**
- **Cheerful Osteomancer** — D+ (black review, both). The Wilds of Eldraine Fell Horseman, a mana cheaper. **"A boomer check card — a free Raise Dead, whoa."** A 4/2 for four isn't great; very replaceable.
- **Forum Necroscribe** — D+ (black review) / Mark C-. Ward-discard-a-card is a painful tax in a set where all your cards and mana matter. Mark's counter-case: it holds off small stuff, and in a slower BG or midrange WB build **the repartee reanimation "just goes nuts,"** especially cast on seven with a one-mana targeting spell. Alex unconvinced; Mark's overperformer candidate.
- **Olnite Alley Shopkeeper (?)** — D (black review) / Mark D-. 2/3 menace that infuses into a 4/3. "Not terrible but not very exciting" — basically a worse Pest Mascot. Mark could see playing more of them than he graded.
- **Scathing Shadowlock (?)** — D (black review, both). A 5-mana 4/6 that only becomes prepared at your first main phase. **"You've got to give me something for playing a five-mana 4/6 with no protection onto the battlefield"** — and Venomous Words isn't good enough either.

### Multicolor — gold commons & uncommons (multicolor review, 2026-04-14)

*Structural note from this episode: SOS has **3 gold commons + 7 gold uncommons per college**, "even more than normal for a gold set." Consequence — **anchor in a two-color base.** The cheap gold cards fill your early curve and the mono-colored two-drops are weaker, so "you cannot play a red-white two-drop and a black-white two-drop and have a mana base that supports that." If you're the only drafter in a college you get ~2.4 of each gold common + 1 of each gold uncommon ≈ **13 cards, over half your deck**, before rares or splashes.*

**Silverquill (WB)**
- **Stirring Honormancer** — B (multicolor review) / Mark B-. 2W(W/B)B 4/5; ETB dig X where X = creatures you control. Caps the gold curve and is **splashable into Lorehold** because it fills the graveyard. Odd that the cycle's most expensive member sits in the most aggressive pair. Called "just a good card, full stop" at first impressions.
- **Scolding Administrator** — B- (multicolor review) / Mark B. WB 2/2 menace that grows on repartee and passes its counters on death. **"It gets big when you play a game of Magic"** — a real groan-test card.
- **Render Speechless** — B- (multicolor review) / Mark C+. 2WB sorcery: look at their hand, take a card, two +1/+1 counters. "Anytime we've had something like this it's been really strong" — Alex flagged his own grade as possibly optimistic. **It wasn't: 04-22 "Render Speechless is great… very easy to underrate this kind of card"; 04-25 "awesome, very strong, happy to play three copies"; 05-02 "still going very, very late — you get this last pick sometimes. Don't be the one to underrate it." Big UP.**
- **Killian's Confidence** — C+ (multicolor review, both). WB sorcery: +1/+1 and draw, recurs from the yard on combat damage. "Possibly just a worse three-mana draw-two-put-a-counter common — no idea how it plays." **This was Alex's single most-hedged first-impressions card: "I can see a world where I look back and say I way overrated that… I can also see a world where it gets to that obnoxiously good level."**
- **Silverquill Charm** — C+ (multicolor review, both). All three modes near on-rate. **The drain-3 mode taxes every block** — "if they don't block they go to three; if they block, it becomes a bad block."
- **Abigail, Poet Laureate** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark C-. 1WB 2/3 flyer preparing Heroic Stanza (2 mana, one counter). The episode's first big split — Alex: a must-kill engine that triggers all your synergies. Mark: a hard-to-cast 3-mana 2/3 flyer with a bad prepared rate.
- **Inkling Mascot** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C-. WB 2/2 with repartee (flying + surveil 1). The bread-and-butter two-drop of the most aggressive college; triggers maybe half to two-thirds of turns.
- **Imperious Ink-Mage** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C+. 1WB 3/3 vigilance, ETB surveil 2. Good body, good ability, and the surveil has bonus value because Lorehold wants cards in the yard. **05-02: "a little bit underrated… great filler, maybe even great filler plus."**
- **Snooping Page** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C-. 1WB 2/3 that goes unblockable on repartee and loots on damage; the trigger is so easy you'll almost always get it. **Played read (04-29) is colder: "this card's not very good" — only when short on twos with a high curve.**
- **Social Snub** — D+ (multicolor review) / Mark D. 1WB copiable edict + drain 1. **"Absolutely not doing what the other Silverquill cards are doing"** — it looks like a black-green card, and ~20% of opponents have Pests to blank it. Alex's D+ is purely as an occasional splash. **Confirmed a trap at the Prerelease Guide, in play (04-29 "not a good card"), and at the send-off ("so clearly an Abzan card… not a card for black-white at all"). The set's clearest designed-but-failed three-color seed.**

- **Vibrant Outburst** — B (multicolor review, both). UR instant: 3 damage to any target + tap a creature. **"A hell of a Lightning Strike"** — works on offense and defense. **Played read: an explicit end-of-VOD standout twice ("this card's great… vibrant outburst very good"), and still a first-priority pick three weeks in.**
- **Stress Dream** — B (multicolor review) / Mark B+. 3UR instant: 5 damage + dig two. **"It's Prophetic Bolt basically. Instant two-for-one — what's not to love?"**
- **Prismari Charm** — B- (multicolor review, both). **Mark's pick for the best charm in the cycle**, and Alex's too at the Prerelease Guide. Bouncing a 3/3 flying token for two is excellent; finishing creatures off is great; scry-2-draw is fine.
- **Elemental Mascot** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark B- ("I love this thing"). 1UR 1/4 flyer with vigilance and opus. The 5-mana threshold is easy via flashback, X spells, and prepared spells. - **Sonar, Unfinished Genius** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark C. - **Abstract Paint Mage (?)** — C- (multicolor review) / Mark D+. 1(U/R)(U/R) 2/2 adding UR for instants/sorceries at your main phase. Mark's comparison: Palladium Myr, a D-level card. Alex: "if you untap with this thing, good things are bound to happen." **Played read (04-23) sides with Mark: "the worst way to get to a lot of mana — compared to the Tablets or the Seething Song guy… a 2/3 being better than a 2/2 is a big deal."**
- **Rapturous Moments (?)** — D+ (multicolor review) / Mark D-. 4UR: draw 3, discard 2, add UURRR. Nets zero cards; exists only to double up big opus triggers — "win more." Alex thought even D+ might be too high. **Played read (05-02) rescues it in exactly two decks: double-opus turns, and as the go-off card in a Brain Freeze / Empty the Warrens storm deck.**

**Witherbloom (BG)**
- **Essence Nits Scholar (?)** — B- (multicolor review, both). B(B/G)G 3/1 that makes a Pest and draws at end step if a creature died. Two well-statted bodies; you can flash it in post-combat for the draw. (Mark: Prismari Charm answers it cleanly.)
- **Bogwater Luminary** — C+ (multicolor review, both). BG 2/2 that gains a life on its own ETB and every other creature's. - **Witherbloom Charm** — C+ (multicolor review, both). Sac-for-two-cards (**note: you can sac a land**), gain 5, or kill a MV-2 permanent. Weaker than Silverquill's, but Alex expects the gain-5 mode to get used more than the sacrifice mode.
- **Grapple with Death** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark C. 1BG destroy artifact or creature + gain 1. Sorcery speed is a big knock and it competes for the mana that turns on your other infusion cards — "but you're not cutting it." *(Fifth-highest-picked common on 17Lands as of 05-02.)*
- **Pest Mascot** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C-. 1BG 2/3 trample, counter on each lifegain. **"Did they finally do it?"** — a three-mana grower Alex actually likes, because lifegain triggers are everywhere and the extra toughness plus trample carry it. "I expect it to be a 3/4 by the next combat."
- **Teacher's Pest** — C (multicolor review, both). BG 1/1 menace, lifegain on attack, recurs for BG. Only want it with a pile of lifegain payoffs; Mark thinks they may both be too low.
- **Mind Roots** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C+. 1BG discard 2 + steal a discarded land. "Essentially a three-for-one," and the set's card draw keeps hands stocked — but it doesn't affect the board, so it's for the **slow grindy BG deck**, not the attacking one.
- **Luin, Exchange Student** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark D+ — **the episode's biggest split.** 2BG 3/4 that enters prepared and re-prepares by exiling a card from your yard; its prepared spell makes a Pest. Alex: a Pest factory and an excellent late-game draw. Mark: an understated four-mana no-ETB, no-protection creature — "but I'm probably too low, I'm not going to defend my grade." **Played read settles it decisively in Alex's favor — Prerelease Guide, 04-16: "I played a lot of this card today. I played AGAINST this card a lot today. It was such a monster."**
- **Root Manipulation** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C-. 3BG: +2/+2 and menace. Five-mana Overruns are historically bad, but menace makes blocks impossible, it stacks a pile of lifegain triggers, and **it punishes opponents who spent turns not blocking to protect their prepared creatures.**
- **Old-Growth Educator (?)** — D+ (multicolor review, both). 2BG 4/4 vigilance reach with infusion for two counters. "This does not excite me — this is just a bunch of stats." Mark's defence: vigilance is worth extra when you make weird attacks with Pests. **Alex used this exact card at first impressions as the example of Witherbloom's payoff problem.**

**Lorehold (RW)**
- **Practiced Scrollsmith** — B (multicolor review, both). R(R/W)W 3/2 first strike; ETB flash back a noncreature card from your yard. **"Card's insane"** — with the caveat that it's worse *here* than in any other set, because so much of what's in your yard already has flashback. Alex: don't be afraid to just play it on three as a 3/2 first striker. **04-25 calls it "probably the best red-white uncommon."**
- **Colossus of the Blood-Age** — B (multicolor review, both). 4RW 6/6 artifact creature; ETB drain 3, and on death discard any number and draw that many **plus one**. ETB + card advantage + huge body. "The most juiced Ravenous Lindworm you've ever seen." Its existence is the signal that **Lorehold wants the game to reach turn six.** Called "just an awesome card" at first impressions; **04-25 names it the second-best RW uncommon; 05-02 a premium Jeskai splash when you need a finisher.**
- **Wilt in the Heat** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark B-. 2RW instant: 5 damage + exile, costs 2 less if a card left your yard. **"Two mana, deal five, exile is excellent" — at common.** Caveat: almost never discounted on the opponent's turn. **05-02: still going last-pick sometimes, which is absurd.**
- **Molten Note** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark C. XRW: damage equal to total mana spent + **untap your creatures**, flashback 6RW. Neither host could explain the untap clause ("we have no idea"); Mark thought the eight-mana flashback was fake and Alex disagreed. **All three later reads go UP: 04-25 "eight mana in your red-white deck seems like so much, but games go relatively long"; 05-02 "I see this fifth, sixth, seventh pick sometimes — I'm really happy to first-pick it… the untap does actually matter quite a bit"; 05-29 lists it with Tome Blast as a flashback-removal miss. Big UP — first-pickable, and a great splash.**
- **Carrel, History Buff (?)** — C+ (multicolor review, both). RW 2/3 legend preparing Pack a Punch (1RW: mill 1, two +1/+1 counters, trample). Mark likes it more than Abigail — better body, better ability, harder to prepare. **04-25 is lukewarm: "I like this card, I don't love it — there's so much to do with my mana in red-white that Pack a Punch isn't your go-to." But 05-02 flags it as a real piece of the RW-prowess build, because Fire Dancer badly wants trample.**
- **Pursue the Past** — **C- (multicolor review, BOTH)** — the single cleanest measure of the review's blind spot. RW sorcery: gain 2, may discard 1 to draw 2, flashback 2RW. Graded down as "leans to the controlling end — not every RW deck plays it." **05-29: "the number one underrated card in this set."** Three reasons the review missed it: flashback makes it **real card advantage**, not filtering; **2 life matters a lot** when you're spending mana without affecting the board; and it both **triggers and feeds** the leaves-the-graveyard theme. **05-02: happy to first-pick it, will play three copies, and it's the top Jeskai card-draw splash. MASSIVE UP.**
- **Spirit Mascot** — C (multicolor review, both). RW 2/2 that grows on each leaves-the-graveyard trigger. "Another fixed grower — usually bad, and I think this one's pretty good." **Play caveat (04-25): it's only good WITH Rubble Rouser. "If you don't consistently have cards leaving the graveyard, this isn't all that impressive."**
- **Startled Relic Sloth** — C- (multicolor review, both). 2RW 4/4 trample lifelink that exiles a graveyard card at each combat. Good stats, no ETB, no protection. Mark's upside: **it eats their flashback cards**, and removal being taxed elsewhere means it may live longer. **04-25 promotes it to "a better version of Ascendant Dust Speaker."**
- **Lorehold Charm** — C- (multicolor review, both) — **the weakest charm.** The sac-a-nontoken-artifact mode almost never matters, so it's really reanimate-a-two-drop or a go-wide pump. **05-02 rehabilitates the artifact mode specifically: it's a main-deck answer to Potioner's Trove, which is often load-bearing — "sometimes the game ends right there when you kill their Trove." Plus the pump mode is real if you incidentally end up with Hop to It / Eager Glyph Mage / Group Project tokens. UP.**
- **Borrowed Knowledge (?)** — **F (multicolor review, both) — "the set reviewer's F."** 2RW: discard your hand, draw equal to their hand or to what you discarded. Great in a narrow spot against a stocked blue deck, wildly inconsistent, and the set's curve is too high to empty your hand.

**Quandrix (GU)**

- **Paradox Surveyor (?)** — B- (multicolor review) / Mark B. G(G/U)U 3/3 reach; ETB dig 5 for a land or an X spell. You rarely hit the X spell, so it's mostly "a three-mana 3/3 reach that finds a land — and that's still really good." **It can grab the duals, not just basics.**

- **Embrace the Paradox** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark C. 3GU instant: draw 3 + put a land from hand into play. - **Terrafractal** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C-. XGU 0/1 flyer with X counters, gain 2. - **Fractal Mascot** — C (multicolor review, both). 4GU 6/6 trample; ETB tap + stun counter. Fine filler; "would be a great top end for an aggressive deck, but Quandrix isn't that." **05-02: "kind of just a big dummy… extremely filler."**
- **Quandrix Charm** — C (multicolor review, both). Quench + destroy enchantment + base 5/5 (great on 0/0 fractals and Terrafractal). Mark on the Quench mode: "an evergreen *it depends* — but if there's ever a Quench set, this is it," because everyone is spending all their mana on expensive things. - **Fractal Tender (?)** — C- (multicolor review) / Mark C. 3GU 3/3 ward 2 with increment; makes a 3/3 Fractal token when it gets a counter. **"Almost immediately fixed Tishana"** — it has protection, needs no extra mana, and catches you up on tempo instantly. Downside: undersized, and it doesn't block an equivalent five-drop.
- **Tam, Observant Sequencer (?)** — D+ (multicolor review, both). 2GU 4/3 legend; landfall makes it prepared, and the prepared spell is Deep Sight (GU: draw + gain 1). ### Colorless / artifacts

- **Potioner's Trove** — **D+ (multicolor review, BOTH) — the review's largest single error.** 3-mana rock: tap for any color, **or** tap to gain 2 after you've cast a spell (not both). Graded down as "awkward, because a rock wants to be tapped for mana every turn"; called "a very playable three-mana rock for four- and five-color decks, not for two-color Witherbloom." **Alex had a better instinct at first impressions ("I've got my eye on this card — one of the more desirable commons for the slower decks") and at the Prerelease Guide ("a bit of a sleeper — I gained a lot of life on my Trove today"). Every later read escalates: 04-20 "the Trove just gained us so much life in important spots"; 04-25 "card is so good, does everything you want — I always play the first copy in a three-color deck"; 04-30 "the best manalith of recent sets, better than the Tarkir: Dragonstorm one"; 05-02 IT IS BEING SECOND-PICKED; 05-12 "Trove just makes your mana base so much easier." Rule of thumb Alex gives: the more copies you run, the more card draw you want. MASSIVE UP.**
- **Strixhaven Skycoach** — C+ (multicolor review) / Mark C. 3-mana 3/2 flying vehicle, crew 2, ETB fetch a basic to hand. Solid even in two-color decks. Caution both hosts raise: lots of cards fetch basics, and jamming off-color basics wrecks your turn-2/3 pips — the classic reason to run 18 lands. **Played read: "I would just put the stamp of *just good* on it — play it in any deck and you'll be fairly happy" (04-23). 05-02 is emphatic: it performs in Silverquill at the level of Consigliere's Duelist, Silverquill Charm, Elite Interceptor and Repel Calamity. "If you're not taking the Skycoach highly, I would change that." UP.**
- **Sundering Archaic** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C+. 6-mana 3/3 converge; exiles a nonland permanent with MV ≤ colors spent, plus a 2-mana instant-speed bottom-a-graveyard-card. Their method: evaluate it as "I will have four colors," which needs **guaranteed three sources by turn six and a good shot at four (≈4–5 sources of the third color).** Even mono it can snipe a big token, and the bottoming mode eats opposing flashback. **Played read: "the Archaic felt pretty good — it always stabilized us nicely" (04-20); "very underrated, and the ONE converge card that's passable on just two colors" (05-02); in Lorehold it's a repeatable graveyard-exile engine (untap with it + Garrison Excavator = three 2/2s). Also anti-decking insurance in soup. UP.**
- **Transcendent Archaic** — C (multicolor review) / Mark C-. 7-mana 6/6 vigilance converge; draw X then discard 2. At four colors that's draw 4 / discard 2 on a huge body. **05-29 names it (with Sundering) among the converge cards they were "directionally correct but too cautious" on.**
- **Diary of Dreams** — C (multicolor review, both). 2-mana Book: a page counter per instant/sorcery, activated draw costs 1 less per counter. "Like Waterbending Scroll, except every deck has 13 Islands" — the requirement is trivial here. Alex's own counterpoint at the time: **you never run out of mana sinks in this set, which makes it worse.** That counterpoint won. **05-29: "kind of ended up being more of a D" — the worst of the format's many card-draw options, punished by Banishing Betrayal and Proctor's Gaze in the mirrors, and unaffordable on turn two against Silverquill/Lorehold. One carve-out: it's genuinely good in a MARDU control deck that has no blue draw. DOWN.**
- **Rancorous Archaic** — D+ (multicolor review, both). 5-mana 2/2 trample reach with converge counters. You need four colors to make it worth it, and at four colors you can find something better for five mana. *(Their general converge rule, stated here: **three colors is passable, four is the sweet spot, five is gravy — don't build toward the fifth.** If GU is your base, red and black are the natural third and fourth.)*
- **Mage Tower Referee** — D+ (multicolor review, both). 2-mana 2/1 artifact creature; a counter whenever you cast a multicolor spell. Worse body than the RW two-drop but triggers about as often, and **it's a two-drop you can always cast on two even when missing a color.** Both said it could be higher.
- **Page, Loose Leaf** — D+ (multicolor review) / Mark D. 2-mana 0/2 legend that taps for colorless, with a grandeur dig for an instant/sorcery. Only for decks short on twos with lots of expensive spells and no other ramp.
- **Biblioplex Tomekeeper** — D (multicolor review) / Mark **F**. 4-mana 3/4; ETB make a creature prepared or unprepared. Mark: **"this screams major trap"** — the payoff needs you to cast a creature, have it live, use its spell, spend four mana to re-prepare, and have it live again. Alex conceded: "many a hoop to jump through… I respect the F."

### Lands

- **The campus dual cycle** — C+ (multicolor review, both). Enters tapped; taps for either color, or pay 2 + tap for its ability. **Appears in ~50% of packs** (the land slot). **"You want the first one, you're pretty happy with the second, I wouldn't play the third."** Harder to use the ability than the original Strixhaven versions, so splashers just take them as color sources for converge. **04-25 raises the floor: "even in my two-color decks I've really prioritized my two dual lands, because there are so many gold cards in this set — a lot of gold TWO-mana cards, and you need both colors on turn two. Two is the Goldilocks zone for tap lands." UP.**
- **The untapped rare dual cycle** — Alex C for all of them, saying on air he should bump to at least C+ / Sam B- (rare/mythic review). They enter untapped from your third land onward, are better than the surveil taplands, and **basically never cost you anything in an opener.** Take these to lock splashes.
- **Terramorphic Expanse** — C+ (multicolor review, both). Note it's a **common**, not a land-slot card, so it appears *alongside* the campus lands — roughly the same number opened per pod. Extra upside: **a zero-mana way to put a card in your graveyard** for the Lorehold cards, and a shuffle for Harmonized Trio's Brainstorm.
- **Great Hall of the Biblioplex** — **D (rare/mythic review) / Sam D+.** Taps for colorless, or pay 1 life for any color **for instants and sorceries only**, plus a 5-mana mode that turns it **permanently** into a 2/4 Wizard that pumps on each instant/sorcery. Graded as niche; Alex hadn't noticed the animation was permanent until Sam pointed it out. **05-08 is a full reversal: "I gave it a really low grade in the set review." It's the #1 land for converge decks, fine in Jeskai and straight UR, performs at ~B- on 17Lands and near A in top players' hands. "I wouldn't be super sad to start my draft with this card." BIG UP.**
- **Skycoach Waypoint** — Alex "a bit higher than you" (no letter) / Mark **D** (multicolor review). Uncommon land: taps for colorless, 3 + tap to make a creature prepared. Mark: you cannot run a colorless land when your two-mana charms and mascots tax your pips this hard. Alex: an 18th land in a deck with lots of *good* prepared abilities.
- **Petrified Hamlet** — **F** (rare/mythic review, both). "This is just a Constructed card. Never play this in your Limited deck."
- **Library of Alexandria** *(Arena-only special guest, on the bonus sheet)* — **not graded in either review episode**, but 05-08 is unambiguous: **"I would first-pick Library out of the vast majority of packs."** Play it as your first land; the mini-game of staying at seven cards can bank you three or four cards for free. Weakest against the white aggro decks, where you must affect the board — but Alex thinks it's good in aggro decks too.
- **Paradox Gardens / the triome-style lands** — not graded individually; taken as speculative fixing in the soup lane (04-25's Strategy B explicitly names "speculating on a dual land here or there" as a correct early pick).

### Rares & mythics (Rares + Mythics review, 2026-04-17 — Alex with guest **Sam**)

*Format-level notes from this episode, which turned out to be the most predictive thing in the whole review run:*
- ***There is a lot of ramp in every color** — mana dorks in blue, red and green plus "a Manalith that I think is pretty good" (Potioner's Trove) — so **a seven-drop plays more like a six-drop.***
- ***"A lot of the games are going to be about just going over the top somehow."** They said it, then still under-graded the over-the-top cards.*
- ***You never run out of things to do with your mana** — "there's always a flashback card, an expensive card."*
- ***Not a fast format.** "There aren't many starts in this format — maybe from Silverquill — that are just curve out, curve out, kill your four-drop, you take seven." This makes **no-ETB four-drops much safer than usual.***
- ***Archetypes leak.** "You will draft decks fairly often, like 20–30% of the time, that don't neatly fit what the archetypes ask you to do."*
- ***Lifegain is everywhere and life totals get silly** — Alex was "at like 30 life in the midgame a lot of the time" in Witherbloom, which discounts every pay-X-life cost in the set.*
- ***Double pips are a recurring ding**, and double-pip rares raise the value of picking up one or two dual lands.*

**White rares/mythics**
- **Antiquities on the Loose** — A (rare review, both). 3 mana for two 2/2s, with a WW flashback that upgrades all your spirits. **"One of the better rares in the set"** — the only explicit best-rare-style claim in the episode. RW has extra ways to cast it out of the graveyard.
- **Practiced Offense** — A- (rare review) / Sam B+. 3 mana: team-wide +1/+1 counters, plus double strike or lifelink on one creature, **flashback for two.** "Better than Overrun for three mana less, you can do it twice a turn, and it feeds both white synergies." **The format's villain in Alex's own words (04-29): "This card is so absurd. I have lost so many games where I'm in such a good position and they just Practiced Offense and kill me… if I had to delete a card from the format it might be this one." Reconfirmed 05-02: "egregious at rare." Play around it; it is the reason you don't over-extend against a white deck with three mana up.**
- **Informed Ink-Treader** — B+ (rare review, both). 1W 2/2 vigilance making a 1/1 flyer on every repartee trigger. A Young Pyromancer / Hero of Precinct One effect on a body that's already on rate, in a set full of targeting spells. **Half of the format's best two-card engine with Spirit-Cast Enthusiast.**
- **Stirring Hopesinger** — B+ (rare review) / Sam B. 3-mana 1/3 lifelink flyer that pumps the whole team on each repartee trigger. Alex jammed sealed with it and found "you almost never run out of targeting things" in Silverquill. **Play note (05-08): don't dangle it on turn three — play it on four holding a trick, or on five with Rejoinder up, so you bank a guaranteed trigger before it eats removal.**
- **Emeria's Call of Truth (?)** — B+ (rare review) / Sam C+ (bumped to ~B-). Alex drew it repeatedly in play and **never once prepared it**, since the mandatory 1/1 makes it very unlikely you're three creatures behind — so bank it as a 3-mana 3/3 + 1/1 with occasional Swords upside.
- **Joined Researchers** — C+ (rare review) / Sam C-. Grade it as a French-vanilla 2-mana 2/2 first strike; the Secret Rendezvous prepared spell is a real but rare "I'm at zero cards, they're at three" button.
- **Erode** — C+ (rare review) / Sam C. 1-mana W: destroy a creature or planeswalker, they fetch a basic tapped. **05-08 makes it a play-pattern card, not a grade card: "you really, really want to NOT cast Erode before turn five or six" — under normal circumstances you two-for-one yourself. Acceptable early only to blow out a double block or a trick, or to kill a must-answer bomb. Hidden mode: target YOUR OWN creature to ramp in response to their removal (or to fix a mana screw).**
- **Restoration Seminar** — C ("the coward C") (rare review) / Sam B. 5WW mythic paradigm: reanimate a nonland permanent every turn. Alex hedged because you need something worth getting back and few turns remain — but the set's ramp makes seven mana play like six. **05-08 walks it up: "just pretty good, you can throw it into most white decks; I'd prefer it in Lorehold." Build-around enablers: Seize the Spoils, Big Score, Rubble Rouser — pitch the permanent you want back, and the treasures ramp you to seven.**

- **Emeritus of Ideation** — **A+ (rare review) / Sam A — the highest grade in the set.** 5-mana 5/5 flyer with ward 2 and Ancestral Recall attached, re-preparable by exiling eight from your graveyard. **"Card's absurd."**
- **Jadzi(?), the 3-mana 2/4 loot-twice rare** — B+ (rare review), Alex "could see it being a little better." Hard to kill, loots two on ETB, and has an XX fractal-maker you save up for. - **Harmonized Trio** — "B range" (rare review, both; no letter stated). **Alex's near-favourite card in the set**: a must-kill one-drop that taps three creatures for a real Brainstorm, and the format has enough shuffle effects (Terramorphic, Environmental Scientist, Studious First-Year, Strixhaven Skycoach) to make it a *true* Brainstorm. **05-08 defends it against bad 17Lands numbers: "a lot of that is people putting it in decks with low creature counts. - **Skywhaler Constructor (?)** — B (rare review) / Sam B-. 3-mana 2/3 flash/flying/vigilance — a blue Restoration Angel you bank in installments. It becomes a lightning rod that protects whatever else you have.
- **Pensive Professor** — B (rare review) / Sam C-. 3-mana 0/2 wizard that draws off any spell via increment. Alex admits it's exactly the double-pip small-body three-drop he usually warns people about, and expected to come down. **05-08 turns it into a play-pattern rule: "don't just run it out on turn three hoping it sticks — hold it to turn four or five so you bank at least one card. - **Exhibition Tidecaller** — C+ (rare review, both). 1-mana 0/2; mill 3 on each instant/sorcery, mill **10** off a big opus. "It does literally nothing if you're not opusing" — a build-around that changes how you play, but one mill-10 can flip a game. **The 04-23 draft's MVP: won ~4–5 games by decking opponents, "very very scary card," and Alex quoted it at ~65% GIH WR on 17Lands. Also a top-of-the-list "bigger fish" that beats the soup decks (05-02). CRITICAL SEQUENCING (04-23): cast the SORCERY first, put the mill trigger on the stack, THEN hold the instant — "if you do it the other way, they kill it in response to the instant and you can't activate the sorcery." BIG UP.**
- **Mana Sculpt** — C+ (rare review, both). 1UU counter; if you control a Wizard, add colorless equal to the mana spent on the countered spell. A fixed Mana Drain. **C- with no Wizards, B range with them** — and there are ~20–40 Wizards, mostly blue (Hydro Channeler, Matterbending Mage, plus surprises: **Studious First-Year and Elite Interceptor are Wizards**). **05-08: "I get it passed to me fifth pick sometimes and I'm like, slam." It goes too late. UP.**
- **Mathemagics** — **C+ (rare review) / Sam D→C-.** XX blue mythic: target player draws 2^X. Alex framed it as "six mana, draw four, with a cheap cycle mode," and liked it because the set has huge mana production and games decided by going over the top. **05-29: "an A-level card" — the review's biggest single miss. With Resonating Loot and six lands you can make 12 mana, so X=5 and the OPPONENT draws 32. It doubles as an alt win condition. MASSIVE UP.**

The rate alone is great, and even ignoring miracles, free looting every turn is good. *(Paper warning: look before the card touches your hand.)*
- **Hardened Academic** — A- (rare review) / Sam B. 2-mana 2/1 flying haste with a +1/+1 counter whenever cards leave your graveyard. Sam: **the best leaves-the-graveyard trigger in Lorehold**, since most others are incidental — and the Flashback rare being an instant means you can even do it at instant speed. **05-02 names the combo: Hardened Academic + Summoned Dromedary = pay two, put a counter on anything, repeat forever.**
- **Arc of Hunger (?)** — B+ (rare review) / Sam C+. 4-mana artifact that drains the opponent and gains you life on each leaves-the-graveyard trigger, plus a tap-to-impulse-draw. Alex's case: gaining life while drawing beats Phyrexian Arena's life loss, it triggers multiple times a turn, and it turns on the rest of your Lorehold triggers — but he conceded to Sam that you already never run out of mana sinks. **05-02: "one of the best cards in the set, obviously you're happy to splash this."** *(Cross-source: Limited Resources called it "never beaten it — unbeatable artifact.")*
- **Suspend Aggression** — B (rare review) / Sam A-. **"One of the coolest designs they've had in a while"**: exile a nonland permanent plus your top card, use-it-or-lose-it. A bounce-and-draw that puts opponents in a weird pinch, clears an attack at end of turn, and **straight-up kills the format's many big tokens.**
- **Aziza, Mage Tower Captain** — C (rare review) / Sam C+ (Alex said he'd come up to Sam's grade "maybe even a bit higher"). A Kykar-style tap-three-to-copy — and copying a *spell* is much better than copying an ability, especially with this set's spells. Tapping three is a real cost and you can't be precious with a 2-mana 2/2. **Niche combo (05-08): with Elite Interceptor's Rejoinder, untap your own creature, Aziza-copy it targeting a creature you just tapped — so you effectively tap ONE creature to copy Rejoinder and draw two.**

**Multicolor rares/mythics — Quandrix (GU)**
- **Quandrix the Proof (?)** — B+ **→ Alex joins A on air** (rare review) / Sam A. 6-mana 6/6 flying trample with cascade that also gives your instants/sorceries cascade. Better body than Enlisted Wurm (good on the MKM bonus sheet), and Quandrix's X-spell density makes untapping with it absurd. **⚠ DECKBUILDING RULE (05-08): with Quandrix, CUT your counterspells, combat tricks, and X spells — you want to minimize bad cascade flips, and you literally cannot cast an X spell off a cascade.**
- **Applied Geometry** — A (rare review) / Sam C+ → B. A 4-mana clone that's always a 6/6 — this fixes the classic clone problem (you can actually curve out with it, and you don't care what you copy). **From chat: you can copy an untapped LAND for ramp.** Downside: you can't cast it into open mana.
- **Mind into Matter** — B+ (rare review) / Sam C+. XGU: draw X and put a permanent of MV ≤ X onto the battlefield tapped. Big X spells are exactly what this format rewards; Sam dinged it because the common five-mana draw-three-plus-land does much of the same job.
- **Geometer's Arthropod (?)** *(also captioned "Jhoira's Arthropod")* — C (rare review) / Sam **"a build-around B"**, and **Alex defers to Sam's grade.** A 2-mana 1/4: whenever you cast a spell with X in its cost, look at the top X and take one. Playable at 2–3 X spells; north of five it gets out of hand because casting one X spell finds the next. **05-08 tightens it: "I would only play the Arthropod with THREE or more X spells" — and do NOT add bad X spells (Procrastinate, Wild Hypothesis) to enable it. 17Lands: D+ in average hands, C+ in top hands — the #3 largest top-player gap in the set.** Alex's own methodological note here: his C was averaging in *how often you should take it*, "which is where the grading system meets its limits."
- **Burda, Wise Extrapolator (?)** — C- (rare review), says on air he'd moved it to C+ / Sam B. 4-mana 1/4 that effectively makes every spell cost one less and dumps leftover mana into Fractals. **Four toughness is a big breakpoint** (blocks every two-drop, dodges 3-damage removal). Alex lost to it twice in play, and notes a no-ETB four-drop is far less risky in a format this slow.

**Other multicolor / colorless rares**
- **Together as One** — A- (rare review, both). 6-mana colorless converge sorcery: target player draws X, deal X damage to any target, gain X life. **The threshold is low — even three colors is great** — and Alex hit four or five colors regularly with *two-color* stream decks thanks to treasures and Potioner's Trove. Sam: "pretty much anything that's not Silverquill, I'd be looking to play this." **04-25 calls it "an A+ rare converge card": at five, deal five / draw five / gain five, "absurd, and you can buy it back." ⚠ Reading-the-card tip (05-08): you can DECK your opponent with it — Alex has won several grindy games that way. Also one of only two good Vampiric Tutor targets.**
- **The Dawning Archaic** — B (rare review) / Sam C+. 10-mana 7/7 reach discounted by instants/sorceries in your graveyard, recasting a spell free on attack. In a Prismari deck it's realistically a five-mana-or-cheaper 7/7 by turn six or seven — but no ETB, and it must survive to attack.

### Bonus sheet (the Mystical Archive slot — one card in every pack)

*Two episodes cover this pool: the **04-17 Bonus Sheet review** (Alex with guest **Sam**) and the **05-16 solo tier list** (LLU #246, ~one month of play). **⚠ The captions on the 04-17 episode make host attribution genuinely unreliable** — two independent passes over the transcript disagreed on which host gave which grade for several cards. *Framing (04-17): one bonus card per pack, and the vast majority of the time it's an **uncommon** — treat those as extra set uncommons, because "they're very common." The rare/mythic rate was cut relative to the last Strixhaven bonus sheet ("they're going to feel like the Final Fantasy bonus sheet"), which Alex regrets, because the fun build-arounds live at rare. Practical effect on the format: **the sheet materially raises removal and card-draw density in every color**, and it supports three decks the main set doesn't — white go-wide, UR prowess, and storm. If their life total is ≤ half your board power, flying + double strike ends it, and it's splashable on a single pip. *(Honest caveat Alex raises: 17Lands has it performing at a B level in SOS vs A+ in Final Fantasy, and he can't explain the gap — he still plays it as an A.)*

## How to draft SOS (Limited Level-Ups read)

A synthesis of the review grades corrected by the 04-25 / 05-02 / 05-08 / 05-29 episodes. **Where these conflict with a set-review grade, these win.**

- **Pick priorities, roughly:** (1) **removal** — Alex, normally a removal skeptic, says flatly "that is not the case for this format," and by 05-12 it's "the number one thing I want; I'm just going to take removal over basically everything"; (2) **bombs and splashable rares** (Practiced Offense, Emeritus of Ideation, Moment of Reckoning, Professor Dellian Fell, Germination Practicum, Antiquities on the Loose, Together as One, Arc of Hunger (?), Lorehold the Historian); (3) the **rival-rare commons/uncommons** — Elite Interceptor, Pursue the Past, Molten Note, Wilt in the Heat, Render Speechless, Environmental Scientist, Snarl Song, Vibrant Outburst; (4) **fixing** (Potioner's Trove, Great Hall of the Biblioplex, Terramorphic Expanse, Strixhaven Skycoach, two campus duals); (5) synergy payoffs, once your lane is confirmed.
- **Ask "which pole am I on?" every draft.** SOS is polarized: **blue over-the-top control** vs **white aggro (Lorehold / Silverquill)**. The middle gets crushed — that's Witherbloom's whole problem. Do not build a red-white deck that is "somewhere in the middle, kind of midrange."
- **Lorehold is the best deck and it stays open on Arena.** People first-pick the black and green commons (Last Gasp, Studious First-Year, Wander Off, Grapple with Death, Bogwater Luminary all sit at the top of the 17Lands picked-first list), which is precisely why Lorehold keeps flowing. Two to 2.5 RW drafters per table is supportable. Expect last-pick Wilt in the Heat and Pursue the Past.
- **Bias AWAY from Witherbloom on Arena** — not because the cards are bad (they aren't; 05-29 walks that back) but because it is the most over-drafted college. At a Pro Tour / LGS / MTGO table, draft it happily.
- **Two colors is a real goal; splashing is a real tool. Pick one deliberately.** Aggro decks (RW, WB) should be **two colors, no splash**, with **two tap lands** as the Goldilocks number — the set has a lot of gold *two-drops*, so you need both colors on turn two. Control decks splash freely; Jeskai is the most common landing spot.
- **Converge/soup is powerful but self-cannibalizing.** Draft it when the fixing is flowing, not by intent. The self-check: *"Am I seeing enough fixing to consistently get converge to X = 4?"* Three colors is passable, **four is the sweet spot**, five is gravy. **Taking converge payoffs early became the trap** (04-29) — the fixing goes early now, the payoffs still come.
- **Play more top end than feels right:** ~two seven-drops, three sixes, and a lot of fives is comfortable, because ramp makes a seven-drop play like a six.
- **Control-deck recipe (04-25):** 4–5 card-draw effects, 8–9 pieces of interaction (**counterspells count, and they're good**), 3–5 ways to go over the top, ~6–7 actual creatures, rest flex.
- **Repartee count:** ~8–9 targeting cards. Removal and tricks count. If you're short, the one-mana tricks (Interjection, Masterful Flourish, Dig Site Inventory) wheel every time.
- **Overrated / be careful:** Ral Zarek (the most overrated rare); **Send in the Pest** (the review's biggest overshoot); Diary of Dreams; Social Snub (a trap); Tam, Observant Sequencer (?) (Alex's called trap); Terrafractal; Hop to It; Awaken the Woods; a straight two-color Quandrix deck; forcing soup; and **greed** — "I lost a lot this weekend just from being too clunky: too many colors, too many tap lands, people just killing me."
- **Underrated / sleepers:** **Potioner's Trove** and **Great Hall of the Biblioplex** (both graded near the bottom and both format-defining); **Pursue the Past**, **Tome Blast**, **Molten Note**, **Archaic's Agony**, **Divergent Equation**, **Wisdom of Ages**, **Mathemagics**, **Render Speechless**, **Strixhaven Skycoach**, **Sundering Archaic**, **Brush Off / Essence Scatter / Mana Sculpt** (counterspells generally go too late), **Hydro Channeler**, **Resonating Loot** (if you can build all-spells), **Daze**, **Library of Alexandria**, **Flashback**.
- **Play patterns that decide games:** hold value permanents (Pensive Professor, Stirring Hopesinger) until you can bank one trigger — **your rares are going to die**; don't cast **Erode** before turn five or six; **Exhibition Tidecaller** wants the sorcery cast first with the trigger on the stack; **kill a prepared creature with the "becomes prepared" trigger on the stack** so they never get the spell; play around **Daze**; and in soup, **look for the two-turn kill instead of extending the game** — "there's always a bigger fish."
- **Alex's #1 coaching note of the format (04-30):** *"You need to identify if this game is about **value** or **tempo**. A lot of the time that's hard."* The corollary: **mistakes are extra punishing here** because the power level is high and things snowball — "you skip a beat pressuring the blue decks and they just draw a million cards and you lose."
- **Sealed:** "pretty complex this format — there's very rarely an obvious build." Be greedy but respect the mana base; it's a math problem, not a vibe.
- **Format expectation:** a **7.5/10** format — deep, interactive, lots of decisions, and genuinely good in all five colleges plus soup. Its flaw is that it "indulged a little too much into the over-the-top flashiness," so games can devolve into who makes the most mana and casts the biggest spell. Individual haymakers flip games out of nowhere (Tide Mystic, Practiced Offense, Mathemagics, Crackle with Power). The bonus sheet is the best thing about it.
