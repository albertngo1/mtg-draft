# MSH — Limited Level-Ups draft notes

> Source: **Limited Level-Ups** (host **Alex / Chord_O_Calls**, with co-host Mark on the review episodes) — **20 MSH (Marvel Super Heroes) videos** spanning the prerelease window through the format's send-off (2026-06-13 → 2026-07-25). Built 2026-06-23, updated 2026-06-24 with the first ranked-draft VOD, and updated again 2026-08-23 with the full run of week-2-through-format-end content (draft VODs, a sealed direct, the "3 Levels of Gameplay" strategy episode, and the format send-off/retrospective). These are spoken-word videos, so card/mechanic names are heavily mangled — corrected against the MSH card list where confident, uncertain readings marked `(?)`.
>
> This is the channel's **deepest set coverage in the repo**: a full **per-color graded set review** (White / Blue / Black / Red / Green + Multicolor/Colorless + a dedicated Rare/Mythic review), two prerelease framing videos, a state-of-the-format address, six draft/sealed gameplay VODs, a dedicated gameplay-skills episode, and an **end-of-format retrospective (7/25)** that explicitly revisits the set review's grades. Alex grades nearly every card on an **A+→F letter scale**, with **build-around** / **sideboard** / **bomb** qualifiers. The grades are this channel's distinctive product — preserved inline on every `## Card tips` bullet, with dated supersession notes appended where the retrospective or a gameplay VOD corrected the original read.
>
> ⚠ **PROMINENT CAVEATS:**
> - **The 7/25 retrospective is the single most authoritative source in this file.** It's an explicit "what we got wrong" episode — grade corrections from it are cited inline as `**SUPERSEDED (7/25):**` and collected in `## Supersessions`.
> - **The per-color set-review grades (6/16–6/17) are PREDICTIONS made before the hosts had played meaningful games** — the core graded product, but still theory, and several were later corrected.
> - **The First-Impressions video (6/13) is the weakest evidence** — blind prerelease guesses recorded before the card-by-card review.
> - **Overall format verdict (7/25, settled): below-average for a large Universes Beyond set.** Good "nuts and bolts" gameplay and workhorse mechanics (power-up, teamwork), undercut by **pronounced color imbalance** (white/blue clearly best, red worst — see `## Supersessions`) and a **light-theming, low-synergy design** ("your deck is the sum of its parts" — almost no deck felt like "I did the thing"). Alex speculates the Universes-Beyond-from-the-ground-up design (so much card real estate spent on "here's a character" legendary uncommons) crowded out the archetype-support infrastructure a typical set gets.
> - **Never invent.** Where a caption couldn't be mapped, the bullet is dropped or hedged with `(?)`.

## ⚠ Recency rule (read first)

- **The newest source wins on conflict.** Order of authority for MSH, weakest → strongest: First-Impressions 6/13 → per-color reviews 6/16 → Rare/Mythic review 6/17 → Prerelease Guide 6/18 → early-access Draft VOD 6/19 → ranked-draft VOD 6/24 → weeks 2–5 draft/sealed/gameplay VODs (6/25–7/11) → State of the Format Address 7/4 → **format send-off / retrospective 7/25 (most authoritative — the settled, played-out verdict, and the only episode that explicitly revisits and corrects the set-review grades).**
- **Per-color review grades are PREDICTIONS.** Treat the letter grades as hypotheses, not data. They are the channel's product, but a take resting only on a release-week grade is suspect until superseded by a played read.
- **The 7/25 retrospective is the endgame tiebreaker.** It explicitly names underrated cards (Hero in Training, Red Guardian, Murdoch's Crusade, Wakandan Drone Flock, Giant-Sized Flying Ant, the land-cycler cycle, blue's draw-three commons, S.H.I.E.L.D. Helicarrier) and overrated cards (every plan except Political Triumph, HYDRA Troopers, Agents of HYDRA, red's aggressive creatures wholesale) — see `## Supersessions`. Where it doesn't speak to a card, the 6/24–7/11 played reads are next in line.
- **This guide decodes 17Lands rather than ranking beneath it.** MSH is a 10-pair synergy set (heroes/villains tribal, teamwork, draw-your-second-card/connive, power-up, plan, artifacts, +1/+1 counters, attack-alone, graveyard) — but the retrospective's verdict is that the synergy is *light* and most decks played as goodstuff midrange. Trust the WR where a card is generically castable (removal, efficient bodies, fixing); discount it on build-arounds and synergy payoffs that only shine in their shell. See AGENTS.md.

## Supersessions (grade/opinion flips between first-impressions and later reviews)

This channel's spread is narrow (the reviews came only ~3 days after first impressions), but the run does reveal several reversals — and the **6/19 early-access draft** then the **6/24 ranked draft** are the reality checks on the grades:

- **FORMAT SPEED: "racy/midrange-tempo" (6/19) → "SLOW" (6/24) — the headline reversal.** The 6/19 early-access read leaned racy/tempo. After actually drafting the launch format (6/24), Alex flatly reverses: "this set feels like *I got a two-for-one, holy crap*" rather than "everything draws a card, you never run out." He pins it as **old-school resource-management Limited** — explicitly "Dominaria United / original Dominaria," a "similar cadence to Final Fantasy, **maybe even slightly slower**." Games went long (cited deck counts: opponents at 12 cards while he sat at 16, lots of board stalls, "a lot of 4/4 flyers, board stalls, flying's good"). **Verdict: SLOW, grindy, removal-and-timing-driven. The newest read wins — this is the over-arching format correction.**
- **Removal: "weak" → "actually pretty good" (within 6/13), then "SCARCE in practice" (6/19), reinforced (6/24).** The 6/24 deck-review verdict is emphatic: "removal is at a premium in this set **more so than usual**." A slow format makes the thin removal hurt more, not less.
- **"Random four-mana 4/4s" / no-ETB midrange bodies: better than their grade in the slow format (6/24).** Because you want to **bait the opponent's removal onto your medium threats** and save yours for their good cards, vanilla-ish 4-drops gain value ("makes random 4-mana 4/4s better — if they have to use removal on that rather than my actual great cards, that's a win"). A partial walk-back of the "expensive vanilla = bottom-tier trap" rule *for the mid-curve*; the top no-ETB fatties still get raced.
- **Removal: "weak" → "actually pretty good" (within 6/13), then "SCARCE in practice" (6/19).** Alex's blind fear was that removal would be weak to prop up the expensive no-ETB legends; he reversed *within the first-impressions video* ("turns out the removal in this set is pretty good"). But after actually drafting (6/19) he flipped back partway: **removal felt scarce and was a recurring draft complaint** — prioritize it. **Verdict: removal is fine in quality (Lightning Strike, Dark Deed, Web Up, Cruel Alliance) but THIN in quantity.**
- **Plans: blanket optimism (6/13) → "good in sealed, jury's out for draft" (6/18) → solid value engines that rarely reach the final chapter (6/19).** Alex spent the most tier-list time on plan/saga cards and softened from "more good than not" to format-split. In the VOD he repeatedly cast sagas but "never got to the third chapter" — **late chapters are aspirational; value the early chapters.**
- **Atlantis Attacks: C+ (blue review) — the one copy is good, the second is a trap (6/19).** Played read validates the cautious C+: "the number you want is ONE… there were spots I wished it wasn't in my opener. It's still a seven-drop." Verdict: fine one-of, never two.
- **HULK SMASH!: C (red review) → "more of a constructed card than a limited card" (6/19).** The cheap-with-a-villain upside matters far less when you rarely have spare turn-one mana. Verdict: below its grade in practice.
- **Madame Hydra: D+ (multicolor review) → "okay, four mana is really small, not huge on it" (6/19).** Reliably triggers (everything's a villain) but underwhelmed even when working. Verdict: the D+ holds.
- **Artifacts (UR): "pretty well supported" (6/18) → "really just the one payoff, I cut the whole subtheme" (6/19).** The artifact deck dried up in his draft; he pivoted to connive/villains. Verdict: artifact-matters is the **shallowest** of the supported themes — don't force it.
- **Blue: "I think blue's the best color" (6/13 hedged prediction) → CONFIRMED & strengthened every step.** The blue review calls blue "the best color by a clear margin," 6/18 names it a sealed pillar, and the 6/19 VOD title/content is built around **blue being the standout color.** This is the single most-confirmed take of the run. (6/24 nuance: with a deck that splashed into white/black off green, Alex still rated the blue-flyer decks his opponents played as the scary ones, and reiterated "I like the blue cards" as his only firm color read.)
- **S.H.I.E.L.D. Helicarrier: Alex C / Mark D+ (multicolor review) → "playable, fine, not awesome" — Alex defends it (6/24).** He ran it over community pushback ("what's gotten into you, Alex? a crew-6 that doesn't self-crew?") and it carried multiple games as a 4/5 flyer that brings two bodies. Verdict: a real playable in the slow format ("vehicles that come with value/tokens are always underrated"), roughly holding its C — *not* Mark's D+.
- **Madame Masque & Ms. Marvel, Kamala Khan: over-/around-grade in practice (6/24).** Madame Masque was again "a good one — one of the best uncommons in the set, if not the best" (echoes 6/19's "a little too good"). Ms. Marvel, Kamala Khan (the rare, C+ in review) "has been a little better than expected" as a 5-mana flash flying lifelinker with an indestructible power-up — a genuine bomb in the games shown.
- **Atlantis Attacks: re-affirmed "appropriately costed common seven-drop," not broken (6/24).** Pushed on chat calling it outrageous, Alex held the cautious line: "a pretty balanced card… a good card but appropriate for a common seven-drop." Consistent with the 6/19 "one good, second a trap" read.

### 7/25 retrospective corrections (the endgame verdict — mine this section hardest)

- **FORMAT VERDICT (7/25, final): below-average large UB set.** "Good nuts-and-bolts Limited magic overshadowed by color imbalance and a lack of depth." Gameplay itself stayed good (what kept Alex playing week 3–5), but he didn't want a "third or fourth slice." Two named causes: (1) **color imbalance** — white/blue get 5–6 "power-level-outlier" commons (Hero in Training, Web Up, Trickster's Stratagem, S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drone) that have no red/black equivalent ("where is red's version of this card? Is that supposed to be Lightning Strike?"); (2) **light theming** — this was "an extremely my-deck-is-the-sum-of-its-parts format," with only the UR artifact deck ever feeling like "I'm doing the thing." Confirmed on 17Lands: real red-black 3-0s existed at the Pro Tour (skilled fields self-correct), but Arena's wide skill range doesn't self-correct nearly as well.
- **Hero in Training: B-/B+ (white review) → SUPERSEDED, settled ~B (7/25).** "It ended up being a B+. Any seasoned Limited player knows incidental lifegain is a lot better than it looks — I should have given this at least a B." Confirmed independently by the 6/27 State of the Format episode as one of the format's 5–6 power-outlier commons.
- **Red Guardian, Super-Soldier: C (white review) → SUPERSEDED, ~B (7/25).** "Ended up being closer to a B-level card." The new template (instant-speed, kills a creature that dealt damage this turn) let Alex attack a small creature into a mirror, then kill the blocker post-combat-damage — a play pattern he underrated because the exact template was unprecedented.
- **Murdoch's Crusade: C-/C (white review) → SUPERSEDED, ~B/B- (7/25, reinforced 6/27 & 7/2 gameplay).** "This kind of effect — 2 mana, exile a 4-power/toughness creature — you should start high on; we've seen it overperform time and again." Called a genuine sleeper independently in three later episodes (Alex: "people don't know this card is good," "such a blowout on mana — they play a five-drop, you exile it for two").
- **Wakandan Drone Flock: C-/D+ (white review) → SUPERSEDED, ~B- (7/25, matches 6/27 17Lands read).** "This kind of card is never good. This time it was." Two drivers: white's rising tide, and **flying was the best ability in the format** — even vanilla-ish flyers with a small ETB overperformed. Giant-Sized Flying Ant (blue) got the identical correction in the same breath — sat at B- on 3,000+ plays as of 6/27, settled ~C+ by 7/4 ("still a really good card... it rewards evasion, rewards the ability that's good in a race").
- **Land cyclers (the whole cycle — Borough Backup, A.I.M. Scientists, Savage Land Dinosaur, Roxxon Brutes, etc.): D+/C- (per-color reviews) → SUPERSEDED, meaningfully better (7/25).** Two things Alex missed: (1) actually **casting** them was fine, especially Borough Backup ("wouldn't hate playing that card even without the land cycling"); (2) critically, **these fix your mana** (get the land of your actual colors), not just fetch a basic — a much bigger deal than a normal cycler, confirmed by Pro Tour teams valuing them highly for splashing bomb rares in scrappier pods. Independently reinforced across 6/25, 6/30, 7/2, 7/4 draft VODs as a default splash-enabler.
- **Blue draw-three commons (Bold Biochemist, Futurist Forge): C-/C- (blue review) → SUPERSEDED, genuinely good (7/25, matches 6/27 read).** "There aren't many cards that can net you a raw three-for-one." In a format without abundant card advantage (unlike Strixhaven), a resolved draw-three forces the opponent to abandon their normal gameplan and race. Bump future commons like this up. 6/27 independently named Bold Biochemist "one of the best two-drops in the format."
- **S.H.I.E.L.D. Helicarrier: C/D+ (multicolor review) → CONFIRMED good, even better than the 6/24 defense suggested (7/25).** "Sitting at a C+ on 17Lands; filter out week one and it's actually in the B- range, close to Herbie and the Vision." The lesson generalizes: a vehicle with a crew cost that comes with bodies to help crew it always overperforms its clunky read.
- **Plans (the build-around enchantments): optimism (6/13/6/18) → SUPERSEDED, confirmed BAD except Political Triumph (7/25, reinforced by every gameplay VOD — "pretty sure all the plans are awful," 6/25).** "Every plan except Political Triumph — I overrated basically all of them. They ask you to cast a 3-4 mana do-nothing enchantment at the exact point in the curve (turn 3-4) you need a creature." Political Triumph is the sole exception specifically **because it costs 1**, so it's never a dead turn. The 6/27 State of the Format episode and the 6/25/7/9 gameplay VODs corroborate: "I don't think they're just great to jam, but this format rewards long-term value, so with token/graveyard support they're playable" — a soft partial defense, but the consensus verdict is clearly negative outside Political Triumph.
- **HYDRA Troopers: C (black review) → SUPERSEDED, ~D (7/25).** "You basically never get the 2/1 token on curve — it's a 3-mana 3/2 that mills you for two, which is just not good." The graveyard-count-2 condition is nearly impossible to hit on turn 3.
- **Agents of HYDRA: C (black review) → SUPERSEDED, overrated (7/25).** "If this set had ANY cheap sacrifice outlet, this card would be so much better — but the set barely has one (the black 2/3 lifelink at 3 mana). It's just a 2-mana 1/1 until you chump with it." Would grade lower on a re-review.
- **Red's aggressive creatures wholesale: optimism (per-color review) → SUPERSEDED, overrated (7/25).** "Red has been the worst color pretty consistently in the play-booster era, and I fell into the trap of assuming a big pile of aggressive creatures meant they'd be good. Red's cheap creatures here are a 1-mana Treasure-maker and a 2-mana 1/3 [HYDRA Assault Robot] — that's not an aggressive stat line." Going forward Alex flags: check a color's 1-/2-drops specifically before trusting its aggressive plan.
- **Shield Spy Kit: C- (white review) → SUPERSEDED, genuinely great (6/27, 7/4, reconfirmed 7/2 gameplay: "did so much work — filtering, trading up, the untap is huge").** 17Lands has it performing at a **B level**; even accounting for white's rising tide it's clearly a real card, not filler. Combat-centric format rewards the +1/+1, the vigilance-via-untap, and the scry all at once.
- **Justice, Vance Astrovik: A- (blue review) → CONFIRMED and strengthened (6/27, 6/25 gameplay: "ridiculously good, basically rare level").** Sitting as an **A** on 17Lands, one of the top-15 cards in the set. The downside (can't bounce a token) doesn't dent how good the tempo/rate is.
- **Stature, Size Shifter: C (blue review) → SUPERSEDED, a surprise premium uncommon (6/27, 6/30 gameplay).** "A card nobody saw coming — packs in for 3-4 points early, then domes them for 6." Independently called out as a card the population undervalued relative to its actual power.

## Format speed / meta read

*(Pre-data. No 17Lands. Games-played input is the 6/19 early-access draft and the 6/24 ranked draft — the latter wins on conflict.)*

- **Overall verdict (6/19): "a pretty good, above-average Limited set… you'll enjoy your first few drafts, but not an all-timer."** Both draft and gameplay rated "totally fine."
- **Speed: SLOW & GRINDY (6/24 — supersedes the 6/19 "racy" read).** The episode title flags it — "Marvel Drafts are Here! (and They're...Slow?)" — and the games confirm it. Alex's read: **old-school resource-management Limited**, "Dominaria United / original Dominaria" vibes, "a similar cadence to Final Fantasy, maybe even slightly slower." What makes it slow: most creatures are defensively-statted, "a lot of 4/4 flyers and board stalls," and **flying is the way through** stalled boards. Card advantage is *less* of a grind-engine than recent sets — "this set doesn't feel like you never run out of cards… it feels like *I got a two-for-one, holy crap*," so individual two-for-ones swing games. Practical consequence: **timing and sequencing matter a lot** ("the little dance between your good cards and removal" — bait the opponent into spending removal on your medium threats, hold yours for their bombs), and **doing nothing is sometimes correct** ("there are going to be turns where you do nothing instead, because it's the better long-term play").
- **Removal: good quality, scarce quantity — and at a premium "more so than usual" in the slow format (6/24).** Lightning Strike (R), Dark Deed (B), Web Up / Super Villain Lockup (W), Cruel Alliance (B), Punishing Punch (G), Frozen in Ice (U) are the benchmarks. Prioritize it hard — you will be short, and a slow format magnifies the gaps. Many legends are synergy linchpins (e.g. Iron Fist, Living Weapon — "snipe it turn three and a chunk of their deck stops working"), so spot removal earns extra value.
- **Mid-curve no-ETB bodies gain value in the slow format (6/24).** Random 4-mana 4/4s and similar "good cards, nebulous plan" midrange creatures play better than their grades because they soak removal you'd rather not eat on your bombs. (The *top* no-ETB fatties still get raced — the bottom bucket below holds.)
- **Board wipes: essentially NONE (6/19, reconfirmed 6/24).** "There aren't very many board wipes in this set." Only "deal 2–3 to everything" effects (Avengers Disassembled, "three mana deal three to everything is one of the few"). **Consequence: go-wide and sticky single threats are unusually safe; white bombs with no sweeper to answer them (Monica Rambeau, Captain America, Super-Soldier, Avengers Assemble!) are punishing. Board stalls form and flyers break them.**
- **"Big dummy" no-ETB fatties get punished.** Alex's explicit three-bucket rule for expensive creatures: (1) expensive **with an ETB/dies effect** = always playable; (2) **Kang-tier** (no ETB but strong if you untap with it) = pretty good; (3) **expensive vanilla-ish with no ETB** (the "plethora of 5-mana 4/4 flyers") = bottom-tier trap.
- **Goodstuff with synergy support, not a hard synergy format.** Themes "come together without much work" (6/13); in practice (6/19) Alex took the better card over the on-theme card repeatedly and let *what was open* (connive/villains) define his deck. The 6/24 ranked deck doubled down: a green base splashing white + black for the best cards (She-Hulk x-was-2, Killmonger, Dark Deed, Ms. Marvel) — "fairly lightly themed, a good mix of creatures and removal… the theme is good cards. It's a midrange deck." His verdict: **"I think this is something you're going to find pretty commonly in this set"** — lightly-themed goodstuff midrange is a default, and **no color pair looked un-viable** ("any color pair not looking viable? Not really — I'm not opposed to anything in particular").
- **Power & complexity live in the legends.** No legendary commons; the uncommon-and-up legends carry the format's depth and several "rival rares" (Justice, Vance Astrovik; Agent Maria Hill; Ka-Zar). The set is **rare-defined** more than uncommon-defined (6/17): your first-pick rare often sets your direction.
- **Mana/fixing: good.** Gain-life tapped dual lands in ~50% of packs; an untapped rare dual cycle ("very close to OG duals in Limited"); colorless utility lands (Surveillance Room, Baxter Building). Default two colors or two-plus-a-splash; **red is the best splash** (its payoffs cast fine a turn late). Lock splashes with the rare duals.
- **Five mechanics:**
  - **Connive** — Alex's favorite ("one of the best Limited mechanics they've ever printed"); smooths draws, central to UB. Caveat: most blue connive commons cost 4, so cheap enablers (Red Room Recruit) are prized.
  - **Teamwork** — pay an alternate cost by tapping creatures with total power ≥ N (crew-for-spells). Played smoothly in practice ("not finicky"); concentrated in WU; rewards a body-heavy build over a spell-heavy one.
  - **Power-up** — exhaust-style mana sink for +1/+1 counters; cost reduced by mana value if cast that turn. Plays well but rewards sequencing patience (don't auto-jam on curve). Self-counters feed the GU counters deck.
  - **Plan** — enchantments that accrue counters off a trigger, then sacrifice for a payoff. Hardest cards to evaluate; better in sealed than draft; value the early chapters.
  - **Transform / "secret identity" hero↔villain MDFCs** — flexibility of counting as both types matters more than the flip itself; type-matters payoffs are everywhere.
- **Color strength (Alex's read): Blue #1, White #2**, with black close behind for the villain shell. Red and green read as supporting/midrange. The sealed pillars (6/18) are **white-based heroes, black-based villains, or any blue deck.** On 6/24, asked for ranked color rankings, Alex stayed loose — "very roughly, I like the blue cards, I like the white cards, the rest I'm unsure of" — and re-stressed no pair looked dead. So **blue/white as the safe defaults holds, but the back half of the color list is genuinely open** this early.

### Settled read (6/27 → 7/25): white/blue soft-force, then midrange fundamentals

- **Color order settled by 6/27, held through the format's end: White #1, Blue #2 (closeish), Green #3, Black #4, Red #5 (worst).** 6/27's framing: "if someone's never drafted this set, the quick-hit advice is draft blue and white cards — soft-force them." Five or six commons in W/U are power-level outliers (Hero in Training, Web Up, Murdoch's Crusade, Trickster's Stratagem, S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drone) — "B+/B level, rivaling some rares" — with no true equivalent in the other three colors. On Arena specifically, both colors stayed **underdrafted relative to their power** for weeks (you'd see them 5th–7th pick), unlike a well-known Pro Tour field where skilled drafters self-correct. **Retrospective (7/25) reconfirms this as the format's #1 structural flaw**, not just an early-week mirage.
- **Green is a genuine, slightly-underrated #3 (6/27).** Pairs with everything except red (where red's weak cards drag it down); Undercover Skrull is green's best common (any-color 2-mana dork that becomes a 3/3 all-types). Green's uncommons (Ka-Zar, Punishing Punch, She-Hulk) are format-defining.
- **Black and red are genuinely the "problem colors" (6/27), but neither is unplayable (6/30, 7/2, 7/9).** "Black and red — their cards just aren't very good... I'm not sure what happened." Both get fine removal (Dark Deed, Cruel Alliance for black; Lightning Strike, Photon Blast Barrage for red) but weak, undersupported creature bases (black has almost no cheap sacrifice outlets despite several cards wanting one; red has no good 1-/2-drop aggressive creature — "the best aggressive red 2-drop is a 2-mana 1/3"). Practical guidance repeated across weeks 2–4: **pair black/red with a strong base color and splash their removal + rares rather than committing to the intended villain-tribal plan** ("I don't think it's a great idea to lean into the black-red villain plan even when it's open — pair it with good cards of another color instead," 7/4). **Exception, proven live (7/9): a dedicated BR villain deck CAN trophy** when you land the right shell — Alex 5-0'd on a Hawkeye/Photon-Blast-Barrage burn sub-package + a light equipment-payoff package (Swordsman, Iron Man Armor, the two Hawkeye's Bows) stacked on top of a pile of good rares (Aries, Whiplash, Cloak and Dagger) and removal (double Cruel Alliance, Claw). His own summary: "we got my first red-black trophy in the format... a lot of the cards were just pretty good, we weren't playing too many dorky little synergy filler cards" — i.e. it worked *as goodstuff*, not because the villain theme came together.
- **Format self-corrects on Arena, slower than at a skilled table (7/2).** By week 3, "things have adapted pretty quickly — people draft the good cards appropriately for the most part," though Alex still rarely found blue/white wide open in his own pods.
- **"Grand unified theory" of a good MSH deck (7/4, hkOlv41KO10 — treat as the format's core deck-building thesis):** every good deck, regardless of color pair, converges on **a mid-range deck favored in creature combat that doesn't run out of ways to spend its mana.** Concretely: (1) 15–16 creatures, don't go below 14; (2) creatures that are "naturally well-statted" for their cost so they can both attack and block — avoid understated bodies that force awkward double-blocks; (3) 5–6 removal spells; (4) 6–7 "mana-sink" cards (power-up abilities, value creatures like Hero in Training, the uncommon mana-sink lands — Avengers Tower, Villainous Hideout, Baxter Building) so you don't stall out in the (~20% of games) board-stall phase; (5) flying and lifelink both overperform because the format is racing-centric. Avengers Tower is Alex's highest-value first-pickable land in the whole set.
- **Splashing is broadly correct in this format (6/25, 7/4).** Because theming is light, a third color rarely dilutes a synergy plan — it just adds power. Fixing is good enough to support it: green's Undercover Skrull/Ant-Man's Army, the land cyclers (now correctly rated — see Supersessions), Surveillance Room, and the hero/villain uncommon lands. 7/4: "I've been three colors more often than I would expect in this format... splashing is not an exclusively green thing." Land cyclers specifically fix your actual colors, not just fetch a basic, which is why Pro Tour teams valued them for grabbing multicolor bomb rares.
- **Gameplay skill layer (7/11, wY19qLrFUtI — dedicated skills episode, no grades but core strategic advice):** this is an unusually **racing- and combat-centric** format (Alex rates gameplay 60/40 more important than drafting here). Core skills: (1) constantly recompute your race-clock math whenever a lifelinker, flyer, or removal spell changes the board — "you cannot race a lifelinker, 90% of the time you lose that race"; (2) default to attacking, treat blocking as something you're forced into, not a first choice; (3) early-game (turns 3–5) avoid multi-blocking without open mana (easy 2-for-1 into a trick); late-game (turn 6+) multi-block much more freely to blunt a removal-spell blowout — except versus black's Widow's Bite (a death-touch trick that punishes exactly this); (4) in board-stall situations, know your remaining deck's win condition (mana sinks, flyers) rather than "hoping the deck provides"; (5) save your last removal spell for a card that either wins the game or that you can't otherwise answer — don't burn it just to push a big attack unless that attack is lethal this turn or next.

## Archetypes (10 two-color pairs, ranked)

*(Ranked by Alex's early read + the 6/19 draft. No win-rate ordering is possible yet — re-rank when Arena data lands. Note: the **hybrid signpost** in each pair is generally at-or-above the gold signpost in power and goes in more decks, so it's the higher pick.)*

### 1. UB — Draw your second card / Connive (Alex's de-facto best in practice)
- **Plan:** The calculating mastermind — connive to smooth draws, trigger "second card each turn" payoffs, grind card advantage. This is what Alex's 6/19 deck became (blue base, R/B splash) and what felt strongest. Connive + villains were *open*.
- **Signposts:** **Kang, Temporal Tyrant** (gold; C/C- — 3/4 connives on attack + drains on your 2nd draw, much better on the play), **Ghost, Spectral Saboteur** (hybrid; D+/C- — 3-mana flash 2/2 unblockable, flexible body, secretly wants connive). Also **Scientist Supreme of A.I.M.** and **Taskmaster, Mercenary Mimic** are UB uncommons.
- **Read:** Connive is Alex's favorite mechanic; the payoffs (Madame Masque, Construct a Cosmic Cube, the token-makers) over-delivered. Watch that the cheap connive enabler (Red Room Recruit) is scarce.

### 2. GW — Heroes matter (strong, low-effort)
- **Plan:** Assemble heroes; payoffs turn on automatically because there are ~91 heroes in the set. One of the two best-supported tribal themes.
- **Signposts:** **Black Panther, Vanguard** (gold; C/C+ — 4/4 that goes wide / pumps when non-token heroes enter), **Spider-Man, To the Rescue** (hybrid; C+ — 3/2 flash/vigilance/reach that grants indestructible). Plus **Black Panther, Wakandan King** and **Storm, Windrider** as GW uncommons.
- **Read:** Rick Jones, Destined Sidekick wants ~10 heroes (easy outside black); Wakandan Royal Guard and The Thing reward going wide. White is the hub — the hero theme flexes into WU and RW too.

### 3. BR — Villains matter (strong, auto-on)
- **Plan:** Flood villain triggers and push damage to the face. "Almost all black creatures are villains," so payoffs are reliably online.
- **Signposts:** **Bullseye, Death Dealer** (hybrid; C — sac-artifact/discard to ping 2 repeatedly, kills the format's many 1–2 toughness creatures and goes face; rated above the gold), **Madame Hydra** (gold; D+ — 2/1 menace token per villain cast, feast-or-famine, dies to Lightning Strike). Plus **Ares, God of War** and **Avengers: Under Siege** (a standout BR saga).
- **Read:** Villain payoffs (Baron Strucker, Madame Masque, The Masters of Evil, Crossbones) auto-turn-on. Avengers: Under Siege overperformed in the 6/19 draft. Madame Hydra underwhelmed even when triggering.

### 4. WU — Teamwork (Alex's preseason "best archetype" pick)
- **Plan:** Tap creatures to upgrade instants/sorceries; untap effects make teamwork "free." Alex's 6/13 pick for best archetype, mostly *because blue and white are the two best colors*.
- **Signposts:** **Captain America, Living Legend** (gold; B- — untaps the first creature tapped each turn, pseudo-vigilance enabler), **Spider-Woman, Secret Agent** (hybrid; C+/B- — flash 1/4 that taps and locks down a creature, strong vs. the format's big bodies). Plus **King T'Challa** and **The Mighty Thor, Jane Foster** as WU uncommons.
- **Read:** Teamwork played smoothly. Wants a body-heavy build (you need creatures to tap). Agent Maria Hill is the one card that specifically rewards paying teamwork.

### 5. RW — Spells / Prowess / Tricks (most aggressive pair)
- **Plan:** Cheap threats + combat tricks + prowess; untap-your-creature and target-your-own-creature synergies recur.
- **Signposts:** **War Machine, Legacy of Iron** (hybrid; C+ — 1/3 flyer that pumps another creature by its power each combat), **Thor Odinson** (gold; C- — 5-mana 4/4 flying vigilance with double prowess, a vanilla-ish Serra Angel that gets punished on Arena). Plus **Daredevil, Man Without Fear** and **The Super Hero Civil War** (an A+ saga) as the premium RW rares.
- **Read:** Take Up the Shield (W) and Blazing Crescendo (R) are the premium tricks. Iron Fist + a white untap trick is a key combo. Favors tricks that keep creatures attacking turn after turn.

### 6. BG — Graveyard (low-commitment, but signpost-dependent)
- **Plan:** Reclaim fallen forces; the key threshold is just **2 creature cards in graveyard**, hit naturally by trading. Not a self-mill deck.
- **Signposts:** **Killmonger, Scourge of Wakanda** (gold; **A-/B+ — the standout of the entire gold cycle**; sac a creature → destroy any nonland permanent, becomes a 5/4 with 2 creatures in yard), **Titania, Rugged Rumbler** (hybrid; D+/C- — a 5/5 with ward you usually cast turn 3 by discarding, but you're unhappy paying full price). Plus **The Serpent Society**.
- **Read:** "If your signpost gets poached, the deck falls apart" — Killmonger is the linchpin. Hellcat is great sac fodder for it. Single-pip green fatties (Savage Land Dinosaur) favor the BG mana base.

### 7. RG — Power-up / Ramp (fine midrange)
- **Plan:** Big bodies + mana sinks; grow creatures via power-up. Alex is mildly skeptical ("growing creatures doesn't deal with a deck drawing cards… but maybe that's enough").
- **Signposts:** **Hulk, Gamma Goliath** (gold; C — 6/5 reach trample that cheapens other power-ups), **Abomination, Terrifying Titan** (hybrid; C — 4/4 trample that power-ups into a 7-mana fight). Plus the **Wolverine** pair, **Alien Invasion**, **Escape to the Wilds**, **Ravenous Tyrannosaurus**.
- **Read:** Power-up is "instant-speed and tried-and-true," one of the better mechanics. Several artifact/villain payoffs (HYDRA Assault Robot, Crimson Operative) are notably weak *in GR specifically*.

### 8. WB — Attack alone (good payoffs, historically suspect theme)
- **Plan:** Send one operative on a solo attack, pile bonuses on it; a small equipment sub-theme rides along (equipment is best when one creature attacks).
- **Signposts:** **U.S.Agent, John Walker** (hybrid; B — 3/2 that enters making and equipping a Sturdy Shield +1/+2, becoming a 4/4; a standalone-good card playable in any W or B deck — rated well above the gold), **Black Widow, Double Agent** (gold; C — 3/2 deathtouch giving the lone attacker first strike + menace). Plus **Winter Soldier, Icy Assassin**, **Cloak and Dagger, Entwined**, **The Kingpin of Crime**.
- **Read:** Alex's most historically-doubted theme ("why attack with one thing?"), but he thinks the payoffs (Agent 13, Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D., Luke Cage, HYDRA Infiltration) are good. Not a sealed pillar. Balance creature count so you always have an attacker.

### 9. UR — Artifacts (shallowest supported theme)
- **Plan:** Artifacts matter — but payoffs are thin. Alex started here in the 6/19 draft and *cut the whole subtheme* when it dried up.
- **Signposts:** **Iron Man, Master of Machines** (gold; B- — flying/vigilance that scales per artifact + draws; clear best signpost), **Speedball, New Warrior** (hybrid; C — effectively spell-hexproof but a weird low-confidence build-around). Plus **Iron Man, Titan of Innovation**, **Vision Quest** (an A-/B tutor), **Armor Wars**.
- **Read:** "You won't get a true all-artifact deck — evaluate each artifact on its own merits." Iron Lad wants ~8+ artifacts. No treasure/clue/food token theme to feed it. Don't force it.

### 10. GU — +1/+1 Counters (weakest pair)
- **Plan:** Pile counters via power-up creatures (which self-counter) more than spells; cash them into payoffs.
- **Signposts:** **Ant-Man, Colony Commander** (gold; D+/C- — pay 1 on attack to counter a creature + spit out insects, wants to be on the play), **Beast, Erudite Aerialist** (hybrid; D — 3/3 that flies + draws on combat damage *only if* you counter it that turn, too few enablers). Plus **Moon Girl and Devil Dinosaur**, **The Astonishing Ant-Man**, **Absorbing Man**.
- **Read:** Alex's most-skeptical archetype both preseason and after review — "the payoff may not keep up with a card-drawing deck." Enablers are abundant, payoffs uninspiring. "A deck you draft once in a blue moon."

## Card tips

Grades below are Alex's **per-color set-review predictions** (recorded 2026-06-16/17, before meaningful games) on an A+→F scale, plus the channel's qualifiers: **build-around** (low floor, high ceiling in the right shell), **sideboard**, and **bomb**. Where Alex and co-host Mark split, shown as `Alex X / Mark Y`. **No 17Lands data exists yet** — these are hypotheses. Where the 6/19 draft moved a card, the played read is flagged inline.

### White (W)
- **Agent Maria Hill** — B (white review). 1W 2/1 hero; +1/+1 counter and draw a card whenever tapped to pay a teamwork cost. The one card that specifically rewards teamwork; a lot of value for one mana, playable in most white decks. "One of the best uncommons" (6/18).
- **Crowd of True Believers** — Alex C / Mark D+ (white review). 1W 1/2; pumps + gains 1 life on the lone attacker. Cheap WB attack-alone support; awkward when you can't attack.
- **Helicarrier Strike** — C- (white review). 1W instant, teamwork 2; deal 2 (4 with teamwork) to an attacking/blocking creature. Efficient defensive removal; exactly what the WB/WU racer wants, weak in WR/WG.
- **Panther Pounce** — C- (white review). 1W instant; investigate + give a creature +1/+0, flying, untap. Divisive value/trick for WR; no toughness so not a true two-for-one, but a copy or two is fine.
- **S.H.I.E.L.D. Spy Kit** — C- (white review). 1W equipment, equip 1; +1/+1 + untap & scry 1 on attacking alone. The best hope for equipment synergy at common. **SUPERSEDED (7/25, matches 6/27/7/4 reads): genuinely great, ~B level on 17Lands.** "It solves everything this format wants: it gives ground creatures relevance late, it's a mana sink for card selection, and it's dirt cheap (1 to play, 1 to equip)." Confirmed 7/2 gameplay: "did so much work — the filtering, trading up in combat, the untap is huge." Take the first copy in nearly any white creature deck.
- **Brave Brawler** — Alex C- / Mark C (white review). 1W 2/1 lifelink with a power-up. Filler two-drop; playable because good twos are scarce.
- **Raft Security Officer** — D+ (white review). 1W 1/3; 2-mana tapper (cheaper vs. power ≤3). Pricey filler tapper; better vs. equipment decks.
- **Take Up the Shield** — Alex B- / Mark C+ (white review). 1W instant; +1/+1 counter + lifelink + indestructible. Top-tier combat trick that leaves a permanent counter; "easy to underrate, play the 1st and 2nd copy" (6/18).
- **Agent of Atlas** — C (white review). 1W 2/2 prowess. Bread-and-butter body for the WR tricks deck.
- **Murdock's Crusade** — Alex C- / Mark C (white review). 1W sorcery, teamwork 4; exile a toughness-4+ creature (and/or a 4+-MV enchantment with teamwork). Scarce answer to big midrange threats; the enchantment mode rarely matters, don't want many. **SUPERSEDED (7/25): settled ~B/B-, a genuine sleeper.** "This kind of 2-mana-exile-a-4-power-or-toughness-creature effect — you should start high on it; we've seen it overperform time and again." Independently flagged as a "people don't know it's good" card in two different gameplay VODs (6/27, 7/2) — "such a blowout on mana; they play a five-drop, you exile it for two."
- **Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.** — C- (white review). 2W 2/4; +1/+1 to the lone attacker (exalted-style). Better than it looks; keeps cheap creatures relevant in WB.
- **Hero in Training** — Alex C+ / Mark B- (white review). 2W 2/2; ETB draw a card, +2 life with another hero. Solid above-rate value common. **SUPERSEDED (7/25): settled ~B/B+ — "the incidental lifegain is a lot better than it looks; I should have given this at least a B."** Independently named one of white's 5–6 power-outlier commons (6/27); requires zero deck support to overperform.
- **Kree Commandos** — D+ (white review). 2W 2/1 flying vigilance prowess. A lot of stats but 1 toughness blocks/survives poorly; for all-in WR.
- **Web Up** — B- (white review). 2W enchantment; O-Ring any nonland permanent (Banishing Light). Reliably good removal, rarely bad.
- **Wakandan Drone Flock** — Alex C- / Mark D+ (white review). 3W 3/3 artifact flyer; ETB scry 2. Fine but unexciting; artifact type barely matters in white. **SUPERSEDED (7/25): settled ~B-, matching 17Lands (6/27).** "This kind of card is never good — this time it was." Two drivers: white's rising tide (great commons/rares inflate the whole color), and **flying was the strongest ability in the format**, so even a small ETB on an evasive body overperformed. Surveil 2 (vs. the usual surveil/scry 1) is also a meaningfully bigger upgrade than it looks.
- **Borough Backup** — Alex C / Mark C- (white review). 4W sorcery; two 2/2 vigilance hero tokens + basic landcycling 2. No real drawback; basic landcycling fixes mana and feeds BG. Mark lower on the cyclers. **SUPERSEDED (7/25): the best of the land-cycler cycle, would happily play it even without the land cycling.** See the land-cycler note under Supersessions — the whole cycle was underrated at review time because these actually fix your colors, not just fetch a basic.
- **Political Triumph** — Alex C+ / Mark C- (white review). 1W plan; scry 1 + plan counter per creature ETB → at 4, draw + team-wide +1/+1. Great cast turn one, weak top-deck. Low confidence (a plan). **CONFIRMED as the one good plan (7/25) — but reinforced (6/25 gameplay) that it needs real support: "I was kind of hot on this card in the set review; I think it's pretty bad, Mark was right about this one — you need token support (Shield Deployment Drone, Falcon, land-cyclers) to hit the fourth counter reliably; four creatures to get the effect is a lot."** The retrospective's final word: still the *only* plan worth playing, specifically because it's 1 mana and never a dead turn — but don't overrate its floor in a light-token deck.
- **Super Villain Lockup** — C+ (white review). 1W flash enchantment; exile a tapped creature (Seal of Removal). Solid removal limited to tapped creatures; combos with Quake.
- **Night Nurse, Healer of Heroes** — Alex B+ / Mark B (white review). 1W 2/1 flash lifelink; ETB return a permanent put in your yard this turn. Easy two-for-one (even a land); flash + lifelink very flexible.
- **Colleen Wing, Street Samurai** — C (white review). 1W 2/2; +1/+1 counter & scry 1 when you target your own creature. Textbook C; C+ in WR, weak with no targeting.
- **Patriot, Shield Wielder** — B- (white review). 1W 2/2; pay 2 + tap to give another creature +2/+0 + hexproof. Annoying repeatable threat-of-activation; can't protect itself.
- **Red Guardian, Super-Soldier** — C (white review). 2W 2/2 flash villain; ETB destroy a creature that dealt damage this turn. Clean two-for-one body; best on defense. **SUPERSEDED (7/25): settled ~B.** "I underrated how well it was going to play — it's instant-speed, so you can attack a small creature into their board, then kill whatever blocks it, post-damage." A genuinely new template Alex had no reference point for at review time.
- **Quake, Agent of S.H.I.E.L.D.** — C+ (white review). 2W 3/3; tap a creature or land on each noncreature cast. Throwback mana-denial; great in WR, combos with Super Villain Lockup.
- **Agent 13, Sharon Carter** — C+ (white review). 2W 3/2; investigate whenever a creature attacks alone. Clue value + pressure; much better on the play.
- **Okoye, Dora Milaje Leader** — B (white review). 3W 3/2 + two 1/1 soldiers; attacking tokens get first strike. Very efficient rate; first strike is minor upside.
- **Mockingbird, Ace Agent** — D (white review). 3W 2/2 double strike; grows when you target it. Nearly an F — a 4-mana 2/2 that trades down to cheap removal before the counter lands.
- **White Widow, Free Agent** — C+ (white review). 2W 2/3; ETB +1/+1 on up to two creatures, or regrow an artifact/enchantment. Flexible; can target itself and recur plans.
- **Luke Cage, Power Man** — Alex C- / Mark D+ (white review). 3W 2/5; +2/+0 + indestructible when attacking alone. A safe WB solo attacker, but Alex thinks a 2/5 that only grows on attack isn't what the deck wants.
- **Captain Mar-Vell, Space-Born** — C- (white review). 5-mana 4/4 flying vigilance; flash-while-opponent-cast. Likely dies on cast like a Serra Angel; if it survives, a real combat mind-game.
- **Invisible Woman, Sue Storm** — D- (white review). 4W 2/5 lifelink; makes 0/4 walls when you counter another hero. Bad in normal decks; the Fantastic Four infinite-combo enabler (confirmed lethal). You'll get it cheap.

### Blue (U)
- **Justice, Vance Astrovik** — A- (blue review). 3-mana 2/2 flyer; bounce a nonland nontoken on ETB, grows when your permanents are bounced. **Best blue common.** Embarrasses the set's no-ETB fatties; vicious with We Say Thee Nay!. Overperformed in the 6/19 draft ("excellent"). **CONFIRMED and strengthened (6/27, 6/25 gameplay): sitting as an outright A on 17Lands, top-15 card in the set** — "ridiculously good, basically rare level" (6/25). ## How to draft MSH (Limited Level-Ups read)

A synthesis of Alex's review takes through the format's end, weighted toward the 7/25 retrospective and the 7/4 "grand unified theory" episode. **This is the settled, played-out read — treat it as final.**

- **Pick priorities, roughly:** (1) bombs/removal that go in any deck (the splashable gold rares — Daredevil, Wolverine, Avengers: Under Siege, Cloak and Dagger, Vision Quest — plus Lightning Strike, Dark Deed, Web Up, Punishing Punch, Cruel Alliance, Frozen in Ice); (2) the standout uncommons that "rival rares" (Justice, Vance Astrovik — settled A; Agent Maria Hill; Ka-Zar; Killmonger; Stature, Size Shifter); (3) efficient, naturally-well-statted 2-drops that can both attack and block (the set is short on good twos — 5-6 is enough, don't force more); (4) synergy payoffs *only once you know your lane is open* — the format's theming stayed light all the way to the end, so raw card power beats archetype fit far more often than usual.
- **Soft-force white, then blue.** By week 2 this eclipsed every other signal: white and blue each carry 5-6 power-outlier commons (Hero in Training, Web Up, Murdoch's Crusade, Trickster's Stratagem, S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drone, Shield Spy Kit) with no equivalent elsewhere, and both stayed underdrafted relative to their power on Arena for weeks. Green is a solid, slightly-underrated #3 that pairs with everything but red. Black and red are genuinely weaker (thin/absent cheap creature bases) — pair them with a strong base color and splash their removal/rares rather than committing to their intended tribal plan, though a dedicated BR villain shell **can** trophy if you land enough good rares + a light equipment sub-package (proven 7/9).
- **Build every deck to the same "grand unified theory" (7/4):** a mid-range deck favored in creature combat that never runs out of mana to spend. Concretely: 15-16 creatures; 5-6 removal spells; 6-7 mana-sink cards (power-up creatures, value ETBs like Hero in Training, the uncommon mana-sink lands); prioritize flying and lifelink, both of which overperform in this racing-centric format.
- **Prioritize removal — you will be short.** Quality is fine; quantity is thin, and it's at a premium "more so than usual" once the format's grindy pace is factored in.
- **Splash liberally.** Because theming is light, a third color rarely costs you synergy — it just adds power. Prioritize the rare dual lands, Surveillance Room, and the (underrated, mana-fixing) land-cycler cycle to enable it; red is still the best color to splash into (its payoffs cast fine a turn late).
- **Overrated / be careful:** every plan except Political Triumph (confirmed bad all format); HYDRA Troopers, Agents of HYDRA (no sac-outlet support exists); red's aggressive creatures wholesale (check a color's actual 1-/2-drops before trusting an "aggressive" archetype pitch); the GU counters archetype (weakest pair, confirmed); forcing UR artifacts (shallowest theme, confirmed); a *second* Atlantis Attacks; the Sentry, Golden Guardian as a build-around (too many clean answers exist).
- **Underrated / sleepers (many corrected only at the 7/25 retrospective — don't undervalue these on sight):** Murdoch's Crusade, Red Guardian, Wakandan Drone Flock, Giant-Sized Flying Ant, Shield Spy Kit, Stature Size Shifter, Bold Biochemist, Futurist Forge, S.H.I.E.L.D. Helicarrier, and — critically — the **whole land-cycler cycle** (Borough Backup, A.I.M. Scientists, Savage Land Dinosaur, Roxxon Brutes), which actually fix your colors rather than just fetching a basic. Also: A.I.M. Synthoids ("don't sideboard it"); H.E.R.B.I.E. Scout Unit; Surveillance Room; Take Up the Shield; Vision Quest (splashable even without a heavy artifact count); Jessica Jones (red's best non-rare card, a real splash target); Swordsman (a genuine equipment payoff if you pick up 2-3 pieces).
- **Signposts to watch while drafting:** flowing connive/draw-2 cards (UB open) → lean into Madame Masque / Kang / Construct a Cosmic Cube; late villain payoffs → BR; an early Killmonger → BG (and don't let yours get poached); white bombs (Monica Rambeau, the Captains) are unanswerable in a sweeperless format, so a hero/white deck is a strong default.
- **Sealed (6/18, reconfirmed 7/7 direct):** lead with your best cards, then aim for one of three buckets — a **white-based hero deck, a black-based villain deck, or any blue deck.** Splash freely with the good fixing (Avengers Tower, Baxter Building, Surveillance Room, land cyclers, the green mana-dork commons). Multiple Web Up copies are consistently the MVP of a strong sealed pool — "we really had no filler" when the removal count is high.
- **Gameplay skill matters roughly as much as deckbuilding here (7/11):** this is an unusually racing- and combat-centric format. Constantly recompute your race-clock math whenever the board changes (you cannot race a lifelinker); default to attacking; avoid unsupported multi-blocks early, embrace them late (except vs. black's Widow's Bite); know your deck's out in a board-stall; save your last removal spell for a card that wins the game or that you truly cannot otherwise answer.
- **Final format verdict (7/25, settled):** below-average for a large Universes Beyond set — good "nuts and bolts" gameplay undercut by pronounced color imbalance (white/blue >> green > black/red) and a light-theming, low-synergy design where most decks converged on goodstuff midrange rather than "doing a thing." No board wipes, sticky threats stick, board stalls form and flyers break them. Still "totally fine, enjoyable" week-to-week Limited — just not an all-timer, and Alex flagged real concern about whether the Universes-Beyond-from-the-ground-up design approach (so much card real estate on "here's a character" legends) crowds out archetype depth going forward.
