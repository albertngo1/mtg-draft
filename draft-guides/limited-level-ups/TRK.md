## Supersessions (grade/opinion flips between first-impressions and later reviews)

### Drafts are Here (11-12) vs the graded reviews

- **Sickbay**: C from both ("very strong" if the format is slow, "very bad" if not) → **"I haven't seen Sickbay be bad ever."** It was one of the deck's two all-stars: "if you can just get over the hump of not doing too much on turn five, it takes over pretty quickly." Scarce removal helps it, because boards stall and the trigger rarely gets blown out by instant-speed removal. It needs a creature-dense deck.
- **Conquest Mission**: D from both ("very very slow") → **the other all-star**. "I think some of the missions are pretty legit." It needs about 12 artifact creatures, and spacecraft count toward it too, since it triggers on any nontoken artifact.
- **The missions as a cycle**: D-range across the reviews, and the Sealed guide (11-05) said "on average, I would recommend not putting them in your deck" → **"probably better than we gave them credit for."** Vengeance Mission (D−) gave Alex "really good experiences", Conquest Mission was the all-star, and blue's Anthropological Mission (D+) "got some legs". Green's Support Mission and red's Exploratory Mission are "mostly bad still". In short, "the Esper ones have some game."
- **Removal priority**: the black and white reviews argued removal matters *less* here, since the top cards are ETB value creatures rather than snowballers (Eject the Warp Core "wasn't quite brave enough to drop to a C"; 70,000 Light-Years is "a third-to-last removal spell") → **"If you see a good removal spell, you kind of have to have a really good reason not to take it."** Removal feels thin, and "I always feel like I end up with less removal than I want." The count is similar to FRA, but the removal is clunkier. The rares review (11-06) had already flagged the shortage.
- **Format speed**: the reviews predicted "slower, grindier games" → the rares review called it "medium speed" with a wide range of game lengths → **"not a blistering fast format, but… slightly too fast for you to consistently do the cool things."** Explosive starts are hard to come back from.
- **Beam me up**: blue review, "the defining mechanic for the set" → **"Beam me up is not a central mechanic… it comes up here and there."** There aren't many beam-up cards, and you can only pick up so many creatures per game.
- **Command Decision**: Alex B-range / Mark C+ → **"very hit or miss"**. Sometimes you face low-to-the-ground aggro with very few targets (it destroys power 4+).
- **Tricorder**: Alex C / Mark C− → **"one of my most appreciated cards for the aggressive decks"**, taken over a second Emergency Medical Hologram.
- **Automated Warfare System**: C− → "looks pretty good… I like this card in general". There's often something you don't mind sacrificing.
- **Romulan Bird-of-Prey**: C+ (11-06, untested) → **"I've had this card quite a bit. Hasn't been good… has not been impressive."**
- **Held up / confirmed:** U.S.S. Enterprise-D (A, "one of the best colorless rares we've had in quite some time"), Gowron (B+/B, "I have died to this card definitely more than any other rare so far"), Malfunctioning Holodeck (B−, "a clear pick"), Salt Vampire (B+, "great"), In the Pale Moonlight (B), Relentless Drednok ("really impressed"), Cloak and Phaser ("really good"), Zero ("good"), Constable Odo ("very good"), U.S.S. ## Format speed / meta read

**Drafts are Here (11-12), Alex after ~20 drafts:**

- **Speed:** medium-fast. "It's not like a blistering fast format, but… slightly too fast for you to consistently do the cool things." **Aggro decks need inevitability**: "you got to have some sort of plan for what if my opponent also starts playing cards and can block." That's why Relentless Drednok, Sickbay and Conquest Mission overperform. The expensive cards "don't catch you up as well" as FRA's top end (Craftwork Crusher, Kiora).
- **Removal:** feels scarce. Take good catch-all removal (Malfunctioning Holodeck, He's Dead, Jim) unless you have a strong reason not to. Fewer removal spells also make the format faster: cheap creatures survive, and tricks and auras get better. "Some of my best decks have been no-removal decks, all gas" (chat), and Alex agrees that's a viable plan here.
- **Artifact removal** is "very, very playable" given how many spacecraft and artifacts there are, but take it around **9th pick**, not highly. You still want most of your removal to hit non-artifacts too.
- **Synergy set with narrow lanes.** "It can feel a little bit on rails… in the right colours, but I'm not really seeing the pieces I need." It's more enjoyable once you find the hidden interactions. Alex rates the draft about **6/10** and the gameplay **8–9/10**.
- **Heuristic: "If the card says U.S.S. on it, you should probably take it."** He named the Enterprise-D (colorless rare), the Enterprise-A (white mythic) and the U.S.S. Discovery (green rare).
- **Artifacts:** "If you just take some artifacts, you end up seeing some cards that care about it."
- **Context:** FRA stays draftable on Arena until about 12-01. The next set's prerelease is the first week of February, so TRK is live for about 3 months. Turnout is low ("Star Trek is just not… the Venn diagram there is not huge").

**Rares + Mythics (11-06), after 1 day of early access:**

- **Most common Arena decks:** **UG-based Strange New Worlds** (often 3–4 colours, "all the fixing in the world") and **WB mostly-aggro artifacts**. Also seen: UB dilemma control, RB, GB leaves-the-graveyard, and Sultai splashing red.
- **No splashable bomb every draft** as in FRA. Packs 2 and 3 often offer "only these two options and not excited by either". Decks win as "the sum of the parts".
- **Snowballing creatures matter.** Watch for creatures that grow on dilemmas or card draw (blue), landfall (green) or deaths: "all they need is one synergy piece."
- **Game length varies a lot:** "multiple games come to decking and… multiple games over on like turn four or five." Seven mana is doable, and eight is where "you got to put the work in". Expensive card draw "gets a little bit of a knock" because of the aggressive decks.
- **Rares point at archetypes** ("here's the dilemma rare, here's the artifact rare, here's the landfall rare…"), and few are true Fs. About 20% of rares are lands, so many packs have no exciting rare. **Green has "the most density of very powerful rares."** "Three quarters of white mythics are just insane."
- **Strange New Worlds** counts hit about 6 on turn 6 if you draft the lands. About 70% of decks will have a second land name.

**Pre-play reads (set review 11-03, Sealed guide 11-05):**

- **Power is lower than FRA:** "average power level of cards is just taken down a notch, maybe even two notches", and "everything's a C". So synergy and your 15th–23rd cards matter more. One host bet TRK would be "less fondly remembered" than FRA. The rares review flipped that: they recommend it for "the first 10–40 drafts" and expect Limited players to remember it fondly.
- **Pack composition:** it's a Universes Beyond set with the same number of commons but 10 more uncommons and 20 more rares, so you see each specific uncommon less than once per table on average. The land slot is a dual half the time.
- **Colours:** blue is "probably the best colour at common" but "one of the worst at uncommon and rare". Red and green have weaker commons but better uncommons. White is "the least exciting at common/uncommon" with strong rares. Black is "middle of the road", with something for every deck.
- **Mechanics:**
  - **Beam me up** is flashback where you also return a creature you control to hand. Pick up *cheap* ETB creatures (1-drops like Emergency Medical Hologram and Federation Probe), never your 5-drop. It works with sorceries only, so you can't use it in response to removal. Milling and discarding beam-up cards counts as card advantage.
  - **Dilemma** pays you for choosing a mode. You want about 7 modal cards for Seven of Nine and about 6 or more for dilemma payoffs.
  - **Strange New Worlds** (UG) counts differently named lands. Sources are the Planet tap-duals (common), shocks (rare), the uncommon Anomaly lands, and New Planet tokens that tap for any colour.
  - **Spacecraft** have lower station numbers than in Edge of Eternities (about 6 instead of 7–9). Run only 2 or 3, because they need creatures to station.
  - **Federation** counts creature *types* (non-Borg) among your creatures, not the number of creatures.
  - **8-power attack triggers** are a RW theme. **Assimilate** returns a creature as a Borg artifact with a +1/+1 counter.
- **Saga tech (Sealed guide):** The Measure of a Man and The Naked Time have a *downside* chapter III. Dodge it by sacrificing the saga with Dahar Master or Targ Wrangler, or by blinking it with Ghost of Illyria.
- **Little flying or reach** outside spacecraft, so cheap evasive flyers connect. 4-mana x/3 bodies with no ETB are marked down. 1-toughness commons are everywhere, which is why Khan, Despot by Design and Encounter the Unknown were graded up.

## Archetypes (10 two-color pairs, ranked)

The hosts give **no formal ranking**. Pre-play, "every archetype and every colour looks like it's got something going on." The order below follows their stated confidence, weighting the 11-06 and 11-12 play evidence most heavily.

1. **WB Borg artifacts / go-wide aggro: the most-backed deck.** Pre-play, it was the pair "going to be one of the tier one archetypes" (Locutus B+/B; "a cheap creature that anthems… almost always really good"). At 11-06 it was one of the two most common decks, and it easily reaches 14–15 artifact creatures. Alex's 11-12 deck was W/b artifacts: Emergency Medical Hologram ×2, Zero ×2, Sulu, Brad, Conquest Mission, Sickbay ×2, Salt Vampire, In the Pale Moonlight, Enterprise-D. The rares that point here are Collation Trimatrix, Overflow Submatrix, Borg Cube, Primary Unimatrix Adjunct and Antagonist Incorporator. Only a light black splash was needed.
2. **UG (often 3–5 colours) Strange New Worlds: the other most common early-access deck.** It's built on the Planet duals, the Anomalies, Xenobotanist and New Planet tokens. Payoffs: Cloak and Phaser ("probably the best payoff"), Ensign Pavel Chekov, Species 8472, The Traveler, Seven of Nine, Unassimilated, Deep Space Scan, Captain Pike and Excalbian Gamemaster. It's base-blue or base-green. The worry that it would turn into five-colour soup was defused by low power ("not many busted cards to splash").
3. **BG leaves-the-graveyard: "might be there" as a top deck.** Vash (B−), Hologram Stalker (Alex B / Mark B+, "the best card we're going to look at today"), Temporal Conqueror ("exactly what the black-green deck wants"), Daniels, Blastoneuron, First Contact and U.S.S. Reliant. Self-mill also bins beam-up cards for value.
4. **RG landfall / ramp aggro.** Multiple-landfall turns are plausible: Erica Ortegas, Will Riker, the instant-speed Planet makers, Genesis Effect, Hit It!. Uncommons like David and Carol Marcus (B/B−) and Syndicate Liquidators carry it. The green review hoped RG and GW "might have enough juice" against the slower green decks.
5. **GW +1/+1 counters.** Phlox, The Doctor, EMH, Dominion Supervisor, Deanna Troi and Sickbay ("home is GW" pre-play). There are more +1/+1 counters than in FRA's GW deck.
6. **UW spacecraft / ETB tempo.** Hemmer (B), Constable Odo and Oberth-Class Starship. There are only 3 common and 5 uncommon spacecraft, so "don't load up".
7. **UR noncreature spells / tempo.** Spock, Always Your Friend (Alex B / Mark B+) wants 10 or more noncreature spells. Academy Instructor, Ravenous Cosmozoan, Encounter the Unknown and a blue instant package (Consider the Prime Directive, Test of Priorities).
8. **UB dilemma.** It splits into a control build and a "nickel and dime" drain build (Elim Garak, Tell Me What You See, the black drain common). Seven of Nine and The Caretaker need 6–7 or more dilemmas. Expect bad all-player stats from unfocused builds. Few payoffs, so "don't go all-in."
9. **BR sacrifice / Klingons: the pair the hosts are most skeptical of.** Sacrifice fodder only showed up late in spoiler season (Tenacious Drone, Starfleet Security, The Duras Sisters). Gowron and General Chang are the payoffs, and Gowron held up on 11-12.
10. **RW 8-power go-wide.** "Eight is not that big of a number", but the rewards need combat, and trades or tricks shrink your power. It may want about 20 creatures. Kirk, Commanding Officer and Worf, Honor-Bound Warrior are "weird". The hosts think a near-mono-red aggro deck may exist.

## Sealed

These notes are from the **Prerelease / Sealed guide (11-05)**: Alex solo, no grades, pre-play.

- **The classic archetypes** are GW +1/+1 counters, UR spells and GB graveyard. **The unusual ones** are UG Strange New Worlds, UB dilemma and WB Borg aggro, and Alex calls WB "a pretty strong deck".
- **Locutus** pumps artifact creatures *including itself*, so alone it's a 2/2. People at prerelease will misread it.
- **Ensign Pavel Chekov** plus beam me up is a real mill win condition in some decks.
- **Species 8472** can't be blocked by artifact creatures and can't be targeted by artifact sources. Both matter, because white's common "O-Ring" this time is an artifact (Malfunctioning Holodeck).
- **Spacecraft are "across the board pretty good"**, so play 2 or at most 3. Suurok-Class Science Vessel is a good common. **U.S.S. Cerritos, Following Up**: "unless your deck is just busted… you should play this in basically every deck." Vehicles and spacecraft that bring their own bodies play well.
- **Hoshi Sato** is "a pretty good card": she counts 2 creature types (Human Scientist) by herself.
- **"If the card looks good, it is good."** The one trap is the **Mission** cycle: too slow, and the payoff is often weak. (Superseded for the Esper missions, see Supersessions.)

### White (W)

- **Malfunctioning Holodeck** — B−. An O-Ring artifact with ward 2. The set has a lot of artifact removal, but "ward two is such a big deal." Called the "top common". **Drafts 11-12:** "Probably just a clear pick"; catch-all removal.
- **Rutherford, Upgraded Ensign** — Alex B− / Mark B+. Digs 6 for an artifact. Mark: "white's top uncommon and actually one of the best uncommons in the set." Really good with beam me up.
- **Assembly Specialist** — Alex C+ / Mark B−. Two artifacts on one card, and "a great thing to beam up."
- **There's Coffee in that Nebula** — Alex C / Mark B−. Looks at the top 5 for a Plains or a permanent with MV ≤3, with beam me up. Alex worried it would whiff, then counted ~7 Plains + ~10 hits and conceded "I was downgrading it too much." **Drafts 11-12:** "I like this card. It's pretty good."
- **Emergency Medical Hologram** — C+. A 1-mana artifact with an ETB +1/+1 counter and 1 life: "it's a beam up, it's an artifact, it's a +1/+1 counter, like it does everything." Every deck wants it. **Drafts 11-12:** confirmed; Alex took two.
- **Command Subadjunct** — C+. A 1-mana 2/1 Borg that gives artifact creatures vigilance, including itself. Pretty much every white deck plays it.
- **Andromedan Automatons** — C+. A 3/1 that tutors every copy. The sweet spot is 3–5 copies; play two reluctantly, never one. One of the rare ETB cards that *isn't* good to beam up. **Drafts 11-12:** "pretty good."
- **Ghost of Illyria** — C+. A 4-mana 3/3 flyer that blinks your nonland permanent. It dodges the downside chapters on The Measure of a Man and The Naked Time, and the set has tons of ETBs.
- **Brad Boimler, Eager Ensign** — C+. A 2/2 lifelink for 2; when tapped, adds an extra counter of any kind. **Drafts 11-12:** in the final deck.
- **Saavik, Stoic Student** — Alex C / Mark C+. A 5-mana 2/2 whose ETB damages an attacker or blocker, counting creature types: "Not Chupacabra, but it's not not Chupacabra."
- **Command Decision** — Alex B-range / Mark C+. Destroy a creature with power 4+, or draw a card. Cycle it early if it won't hit anything soon. **Drafts 11-12:** "very hit or miss"; sometimes the aggro opponent has no targets.
- **Hologram Janeway** — Alex C+ / Mark C. A 3-mana 3/3 artifact with a counter at 8 power. Fits RW, BW and GW. **Drafts 11-12:** "solid."
- **Oberth-Class Starship** — C. Fetches a Plains; station 6 makes it a 2/2 flyer. BW, UW and GW all want it.
- **Shields Up!** — C. "Probably the best combat trick in the set… not saying much." Play around it against white.
- **Free Borg Revolutionaries** — C. A 4/5 artifact that pumps the team on ETB, with basic landcycling. A good RW/BW curve-topper. **Drafts 11-12:** "looks great."
- **Picard, Leading by Example** — C. A 2/2 that taps another creature to pump. It gets better as the game goes on.
- **U.S.S. Excelsior, Cutting Edge** — C. ETB destroys a tapped creature; station 8 makes it a 4/4 flyer.
- **Federation Field Medic** — Alex C− / Mark C. A 3/2 vigilance that draws if you control a permanent with a counter (inactive spacecraft and sagas count).
- **Set Phasers to . . .** — C− / D (speakers unclear). 1 mana: stun, or 3 damage to an attacker. Both think they may be too low, since it's very efficient in a race. **Drafts 11-12:** taken late, "maybe we play it."
