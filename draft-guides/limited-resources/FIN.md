## ⚠ Recency rule (read first)

- **The newest episode wins on any conflict.** A later read supersedes an earlier one.
- **The #807 grades are PREDICTIONS.** They were recorded after the hosts had played only a handful of drafts on release day. Treat the letters as hypotheses, not data.
- **⚠ REAL COVERAGE GAP — the Commons & Uncommons set review is NOT in this batch.** LR reviews a set in two episodes; only the **Rare & Mythic + Bonus Sheet** review (#807) was captured. That means **there are no LR letter grades for any common or uncommon in FIN.** Commons and uncommons appear here only through Cracker Pack commentary (#808/#809/#811) and the Sunset Show (#812) — real opinions, but qualitative, not graded. Where a bullet says "no grade (C/UC review not in batch)," that is a genuine hole in the source, not an omission in this file. Do not infer a grade that LR never gave.
- **The Sunset Show (#812) is the most authoritative** — settled reads after grinding the whole format, cross-checked by the hosts against 17Lands. Supersessions are marked inline.
- **This guide decodes the 17Lands data rather than ranking beneath it.** FIN is the most balanced format LR has ever discussed, so archetype-conditional numbers matter more than usual — a card's win rate is often a statement about which of ten viable decks it landed in.

## Supersessions (release-week grade flips: early take → late take, verdict)

The single highest-value output of this channel. Format: **Card / topic** — early take → late take. Verdict.

**Cards that went UP**

- **Sahagin** — not graded (common), and the whole premise was doubted: "they want to make a blue-red *you cast four-mana noncreature spells* deck. Well, that just sounds like that's not going to work." → #812: **"Sahagin was underrated by just about everyone."** The UR deck became the best-performing archetype in the set and Sahagin is a top-tier blue common. **Verdict: BIG UP — the format's single biggest miss. Take it as a real payoff, not a throwaway.**
- **Fire Magic** — nobody's early priority; "the uncommon red sweeper is usually a D to C" → #812: **"B+ to A− depending on the matchup. Truly excellent."** LSV was on it by #808 ("I like Fire Magic quite a bit more than any common"); Marshall was hit-or-miss and converted on air. One-sided sweeper at 1 / 3 / 6 mana; blanks a Magitek Infantry that has already eaten 10 mana. **Verdict: BIG UP — premium early pick in UR / Grixis / RB. Not for RW.**
- **Call the Mountain Chocobo** — passed over at review → #812: **2nd-best red common** and "one of our favorite cards." A 10-mana four-for-one that also fixes and hits land drops for the flashback-heavy decks. **Verdict: UP — great in UR, Grixis and RB; merely fine in RW.**
- **Sidequest: Card Collection** — read as "four mana and you don't go up in cards, you're just cycling" → #812: **"a pretty big miss. It played out more like four mana, draw two cards, make a 4/4 flyer."** It flips almost for free, and discarding two cards is upside in a set this full of flashback. **Verdict: UP.**
- **Combat Tutorial** — liked, but not this much → #812: **"I would not have predicted this to be the best blue common, and it kind of is."** A +1/+1 counter stapled to Divination "skyrocketed the win rate." **Verdict: UP — best blue common; prioritize two-drops once you have one.**
- **Zodiark, Umbral God** — #807 split: LSV **build-around B**, Marshall **"build-around D almost — you really shouldn't be trying."** → #809/#812: **real, and it won at the Pro Tour.** LSV, Sam Pardee and BK all built near-mono-black around it; Jody Keith drafted mono-black Zodiark in pod one on day two. LSV: "it's good enough that it was worth having your last five cards in your deck be worse than they would be otherwise." **Verdict: UP — P1P1-able if you're willing to go mono-black. Marshall's D was wrong. Still a trap in a normal two-color deck with 10 swamps.**
- **Resentful Revelation** — not graded → #812: LSV thinks it was **underrated even at the end of the format**. "If I didn't have one or two of these in one of my black control decks, I felt like I was at a big disadvantage." Fills the yard, protects against flooding, and the 7-mana flashback ends attrition games. **Verdict: UP — this format resolves to attrition battles more than tempo fights, which is exactly this card's axis.**
- **Sorceress's Schemes** — "looked way too slow" → #812: **great**, provided you have cheap red removal to rebuy (Lightning Bolt, Thunder Magic, Suplex). Loops with **Fight On!** for "infinite grinding" (#809), and shot Choco-Comet up LSV's pick order. **Verdict: UP in RB and UR only.**
- **Travel the Overworld** — pigeonholed as a towns-deck-only card → #812: **"a great card even in a two-color deck."** It's Tidings; two towns makes it five mana and "in almost any Limited format I'd be really happy to have that card." **Verdict: UP — and by extension, take towns higher than feels right even in two colors.**
- **Blazing Bomb** — dismissed → #812: **"in blue-red it was pretty good; people just mostly thought it was bad when the reality is it was actually fine."** LSV stopped cutting it from UR decks. **Verdict: UP in UR specifically; still bad in RB/RW. Marshall stayed lower on it than LSV — an unresolved split.**
- **Exdeath, Void Warlock** — "it looked a little blah to me… in the set review" → #812: **exactly what black-green wants** — speed bump, gains 3, then flips into a 7/3 or 8/3 trampler that steals games. **Verdict: UP in BG.**
- **Shinra Reinforcements** — read as filler-to-begrudgingly-playable → #809: **"the total package of a 2/3 plus the mill three is what I want out of a card."** Especially in a The Final Days deck. **Verdict: UP slightly.**
- **Blitzball** — "how are you ever going to trigger this?" → #808: **"I've already done the second mode two or three times."** A three-mana any-color rock is nearly playable on its own; the cash-in is a free roll. **Verdict: UP — but only in decks that actually want a rock (towns, Grixis, artifacts).**

**Cards that went DOWN**

- **Vincent Valentine** — #807: **B / B+**, "this card's ridiculous" → #812 (filed under *most controversial*): **"the card ended up actually being a lot worse than it looked. It didn't play out that way and most people probably weren't on that same page."** Two toughness for 2BB is a lightning rod. **Verdict: DOWN a full grade.**
- **Golbez, Crystal Collector** — #807: **build-around B** → #812: **"a trap card"** and LSV's *bad card but I love it anyway* pick. "Blue is the artifact color. Blue-black wasn't — why did you put a blue-black artifact build-around in the set?" **Verdict: DOWN hard. Do not go for it.**
- **Gogo, Master of Mimicry** — #807: **B** ("the average deck will have enough stuff that it just works") → #812: filed under *still looks good when you read it, but doesn't see play* — **"Gogo wasn't consistent enough to see a ton of heavy play."** **Verdict: DOWN.**
- **Jecht, Reluctant Guardian** — #807: **B** ("if it hits them, you win the game") → #811, live at the draft table: LSV **"I don't think it's a B-level card. I think it's like a C++. Maybe a B−"** and took Fire Magic over it P1P1. **Verdict: DOWN. Marshall never conceded the point — a live disagreement.**
- **Bartz and Boko** — #807: **build-around B+** ("I'm excited to draft the bird deck") → #812: **"I never really saw that thing just go off."** Same for **Choco, Seeker of Paradise** — "an okay card on its own, but really wanted more birds around it." **Verdict: DOWN — the bird build-around never materialized.**
- **The Wandering Minstrel** — #807: **build-around B+** ("exactly what the town decks want") → #812: **"I haven't lost to this card… it didn't end up being that good, I don't think."** Note the rules trap both hosts missed all format: **the overrun does not pump the Minstrel itself.** **Verdict: DOWN.**
- **The Crystals cycle** (Water / Fire / Earth / Wind / Darkness) — graded D-to-F at review except Darkness Crystal's build-around B → #812: **"the crystals really didn't work out"**, named among the set's build-around misses. **Verdict: the low grades were RIGHT; Darkness Crystal was the generous one and it also flopped.**
- **Tifa Lockhart** — #807: **build-around C** ("build-around C is a special grade" — i.e. bad) → #812: confirmed bad, but flagged as the **most frustrating bad card to lose to** (Marshall ate a 16-power one off a three-land turn). **Verdict: grade holds; respect it from the other side of the table.**
- **Hecteyes** — not graded → #808: "I actually want to say this card is worse than it has been in previous incarnations" → #812: **"underperformed a little… in most sets the Hecteyes of the set is a top black common. Here it's middle of the pack."** Cause: too much card draw and flashback, so the discard rarely hurts. **Verdict: DOWN vs. its usual slot. Play it in BW/BG with sac outlets, deprioritize it in UB.**
- **Summon: G.F. Ifrit** — #811/#812: **"I've started to just try not to play the Ifrit now"** and one of LSV's two *most begrudgingly playable* picks (with **Commune with Beavers**). **Verdict: DOWN to near-cut.**
- **Mysidian Elder** — #812: "on its face a pretty reasonable card ratewise, but it didn't really have much of a home" — wrong for UR, marginal in RW, and LSV barely drafted RB or RG. **Verdict: DOWN by home, not by rate.**
- **Chocobo Racetrack** — dissed at review → #808/#812 confirmed: **"not a good card, and I saw that card played a lot."** You spend five mana and then need land drops you've already used up. **Verdict: the diss was right.**

**Cards LR called right, and it mattered**

- **Ardyn, the Usurper** — #807 build-around A → #812: **the bombiest bomb of the format** and "the card that won me the most boxes" in sealed.
- **Akroma's Will (bonus sheet)** — #807 A, over Marshall's objection ("you talked me up to it") → #812: **an all-time groan-tester. "Akroma's Will should not be in this set. It is such an unfun way to end a game even if you're winning with it."**
- **Aettir and Priwen** — #807: too expensive to bother with → #812: **both hosts' "my opponent played this so now I must win or quit Magic forever" card.** Bad, terrifying, both true.
- **Poison the Waters** — #809 "it averages out to just okay, but it rarely IS just okay" → #812 *looks good when you read it, doesn't see play*: the −1/−1 mode is basically never good. "Imagine if it was target creature gets −2/−2 — that would have been a really good card."
- **Haste Magic** — #809: **"Unplayable."** Lowest-winning red common. But #812: Marshall lost to it constantly ("I'm going to lose to this stupid Haste Magic, and it happened way too often"). **Both are true — it's a bad card that steals games from people who don't respect a turn-three burst.**

## Format speed / meta read

- **FIN is the most balanced set LR has ever discussed.** #812: the archetypes ranked **#9 through #1 are separated by 2.2% total win rate**; Gruul (RG) sits another ~2% below ninth. "That means you can draft anything. You can just draft any of these pairs and have a decent shot of winning depending on how well you draft and play."
- **Both hosts graded the set an A.** Marshall: "the most balanced set that we've ever talked about on the show." LSV: "perfectly balanced, as all things should be." Draft was good, gameplay was good, and it was balanced — "that's very rare that those things come together."
- **Balance makes this the most skill-testing kind of format.** You cannot cling to one archetype; you have to read the table. The explicit stretch goal: know all ten pairs so you can take whichever one is open. **Simic (UG) posted 55.5% but was the least-drafted pair** — learn it for the 1-in-10 draft where it's the open lane.
- **Speed: not slow, but not aggression-driven either.** LSV (#808): "you do really feel it when you're not getting on the board… but it's also a format where it feels like there's so much gas that you don't run out of stuff to do." The consequence is a specific deckbuilding rule: **have good early plays so you still have life points left to use your expensive cards.** Marshall: "the best deck in the format had four seven-drops and an eight-drop." Midrange is fully viable.
- **Games resolve into attrition battles, not tempo fights.** LSV (#812): "these games didn't come down to tempo fights as often as I found — we both kind of answered each other's stuff and whoever can accumulate more won." That is why Resentful Revelation, Sorceress's Schemes, Memories Returning and Travel the Overworld all beat their reviews.
- **Removal is excellent in every color, at common.** Blue — Ice Magic, Syncopate, Stuck in Summoner's Sanctum, Swallowed by Leviathan. Red — Thunder Magic, Suplex, Light of Judgment, Fire Magic. Black — Sephiroth's Intervention, Vayne's Treachery, Cornered by Black Mages. White — White Auracite, Slash of Light. "There is just a lot of ways to mess with your opponent's stuff."
- **This is a PAUPER set.** #812, both hosts: **"you could have a lot of great decks without a single rare."** Because common removal answers the bombs, opening a rare doesn't decide the game. "The rares were good and exciting, but there were enough answers that it didn't feel like you were helpless" — except Ardyn.
- **The bonus sheet was heavier than the main set at the top.** #812: **the top three cards in the format by 17Lands win rate were all bonus-sheet cards — Atraxa, Grand Unifier; Urza, Lord High Artificer; Winota, Joiner of Forces.** LSV: "having the best cards in the set be on the bonus sheet is undesirable, but I'd rather have a bonus sheet than not."
- **Synergy density is about a 4–5 out of 10.** Real theme decks exist (UR four-cost, UW artifacts, BG self-mill, RB wizards) but "the town deck isn't really a synergy deck, it's just a deck full of good cards." Both are viable at once.
- **Take towns higher than feels right.** #812: even a plain two-color deck wants ~2 towns — "you're just going to get mana screwed maybe one less game over the course of the draft. That's huge." The towns/ramp deck wants 5–7. But note **the payoffs are thin**: Travel the Overworld is close to the only town-specific reward; everything else pays you for *three colors* and *big mana*, not for towns as a card type. Colorless towns (Clive's Hideaway, Starting Town in a true five-color deck) are too weak for the deck that most wants towns.
- **Mechanics all landed, which is why the set played so well.**
  - **Job Select — A+.** "They finally fixed equipment. Job Select will be back." You net a 1/1 that comes pre-equipped, so you get the equip cost for free; when the creature trades you still have a piece of equipment that does something.
  - **Tiered** — kicker with a new name, played great. It genuinely matters that you sometimes cast Ice Magic for eight or Thunder Magic for four.
  - **Saga creatures** — LSV was skeptical, they played great. **Rules of thumb: 3-chapter saga creatures you can often just take the hits from; 4-chapter saga creatures you have to kill.**
  - **Four-mana-value-matters** — nailed, because it keys off *mana spent*, not printed cost. "You're supposed to play like five cards that cost four or more and five cheap cards you could then spend four mana on if you needed to."
- **Seat read:** in week one Marshall deliberately avoided white — "I feel like white dries up a little quicker than the other colors" (#808). That is a contested-lane observation, not a power ranking; white ended the format with the set's single best common (White Auracite).
- **Sealed is good too, and it is not the same format.** Both hosts ground the FIN Arena Directs hard. Sealed plays slower and more controlling by default (Sultai, big fixing), white and red are the two weakest colors in sealed because their aggressive themes don't translate, and blue is the most commonly played color.

## Archetypes

Ranked by the settled Sunset Show (#812) read. **Read the caveat first: LR explicitly refuses to separate the middle of this list.** Only two positions are firm — UR is #1 on both play rate and win rate, and RG is last by roughly 2%. Everything between them lives inside a 2.2% band, so the ranking below reflects LR's qualitative comments about which decks *came together* most often, not a win-rate order.

### 1. Izzet (UR) — Four-Mana-Value-Matters — BEST DECK, and it's the only firm #1
- **The verdict:** #812 — "blue-red was the best performing archetype. It was the most drafted and had the highest win rate." Also the miss LR most regrets: the archetype "sounded dicey" at review and worked out great.
- **Plan:** Play payoffs that reward spending four or more mana on a noncreature spell, then play spells that cost four or more. That's it. LSV: "it's a very easy deck to draft, which is kind of nice too."
- **Payoffs:** Sahagin, The Emperor of Palamecia, Shantotto, Tactician Magician, Vivi Ornitier, Tellah, Great Sage, Astrologian's Planisphere.
- **The four-cost spells you actually want:** Thunder Magic and Ice Magic above all (cheap in a pinch, four-mana mode most of the time), Syncopate (turn-two counter early, overpaid trigger late), Call the Mountain Chocobo, Retrieve the Esper, Sidequest: Card Collection, Dreams of Laguna, Warrior's Sword.
- **Key insight — the deck is not a glass cannon.** "The spells you use to support those payoffs are actually just real spells you'd want to cast anyway. You only need to nudge your direction a little further over." Thunder Magic beats Suplex here purely because it can cost four.
- **Key insight #2 — bodies come from noncreature spells.** Warrior's Sword and Retrieve the Esper are below curve on their own but free-roll in this deck: "I get to play creatures, air quotes, removal, and payoffs just for casting these spells."
- **Do not** load it with random creatures, and do not aim for ten four-drops — you'll be too clunky.
- **Cards to avoid here:** Red Mage's Rapier, Mysidian Elder, Instant Ramen, Laughing Mad (playable only if short on spells).

### 2–6. The flat band — WB, BG, UW, RW, UB
LR named these as the decks that "just come together": *"white-black, black-green, blue-white, blue-red, red-white — these are all just fine decks to draft."* Treat the ordering inside this block as noise.

**Orzhov (WB) — go-wide + drain.** Marshall's sealed breakout: The Crystal's Chosen (4 mana three 1/1s / 7 mana four 1/1s + counters), Moogles' Valor, Summon: Knights of Round, plus Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER as the engine. **The best home for Phantom Train** — WB has enough spare permanents to feed it, and Squall, SeeD Mercenary + double strike + sacrificing Squall to the Train is a two-card kill. Marshall: "you know you're doing something right when they Sephiroth's Intervention your 2/2 — they can't deal with a huge board; it's sweeper or bust."

**Golgari (BG) — self-mill into The Final Days.** Wants **every card to be a permanent** — that's why Cornered by Black Mages and Commune with Beavers are actively bad here even though they're fine cards. Town Greeter and Shinra Reinforcements mill creatures; The Final Days converts them into tokens and flashes back for five or six. Exdeath, Void Warlock, Cloud of Darkness, Jenova, Ancient Calamity, Sidequest: Hunt the Mark. **Known weakness: fliers** — leave a slot for Airship Crash.

**Azorius (UW) — artifacts.** The hand-holding archetype: "there's nothing secret. It really does just tell you — this says *when an artifact enters, something good happens.*" White Auracite, White Mage's Staff, Magitek Infantry, Gaelicat (a C++ here and a D− anywhere else — you can get three of them), Tidus, Blitzball Star, Edgar, King of Figaro, Machinist's Arsenal, Urza. Lower curve and slightly more aggressive than the other blue decks. **Play World Map as a fake land to hit critical mass**; the real skill is "trying your hardest not to play non-artifacts in your deck."

**Boros (RW) — Job Select.** An aggro deck that also wins long games, which is the surprising part. LSV: "I've lost some pretty long games to red-white — you kill their stuff and stabilize, but they've left two pieces of equipment behind, so now every card they play is a threat." Freya Crescent (a secret gold card — RW only; wears and moves equipment and taps for equipment mana), Firion, Wild Rose Warrior, Gilgamesh, Master-at-Arms, Cloud, Midgar Mercenary, Joshua, Phoenix's Dominant, Lightning, Army of One, Bruse Tarl, Danitha Capashen. **Do not play Fire Magic here.**

**Dimir (UB) — control.** Emet-Selch, Unsundered, Memories Returning, The Darkness Crystal, Shambling Cie'th (LSV likes it with heavy discard/self-mill; Marshall doesn't — unresolved), Astrologian's Planisphere. **This is NOT an artifact deck** — that's the Golbez trap. Deprioritize Hecteyes here; the discard does the least in the deck with the most card draw.

### 7. Simic (UG) — Ramp / Towns — GOOD BUT RARE
- **55.5% win rate, least-drafted pair in the format.** The lane you must know how to take when it's open.
- Ignis Scientia, The Wandering Minstrel (down from its review, but still the payoff), Thrasios, Triton Hero, Kinnan, Bonder Prodigy, Travel the Overworld, Goobbue Gardener, Town Greeter, Farseek, Reach the Horizon, Ring of the Lucii.
- **The one place Brainstorm is good** — land cyclers and land-searchers give you the shuffle effects it needs.

### 8. Rakdos (RB) — Black Wizards — LOVED, LESS CONSISTENT
- LSV: "I at least didn't experience black-red coming together quite as consistently as the other decks," and it's the color pair he drafted least — which is why Mysidian Elder and Circle of Power look worse in his data than they are.
- Marshall loves it: **"one wizard's annoying, two wizards is a problem, three wizards is game."** You can measure your own success by watching them spend removal on your 0/1s.
- Cornered by Black Mages, Circle of Power, Black Mage's Rod, Mysidian Elder, Red Mage's Rapier, Lindblum's Mage Siege adventure, Kuja, Genome Sorcerer, Garland, Knight of Cornelia, Instant Ramen. Plus the Sorceress's Schemes / Triple Triad control top-end.
- LSV's favorite thing to do in the whole format: "get like three black wizard tokens out and try to kill them."

### 9. Selesnya (GW) — Chocobos — DIDN'T COME TOGETHER
- #812: named alongside RG as a deck that "didn't really come together as often."
- Marshall (#808): "creatures, combat tricks, pretty ABC." **But it is by far the best home for Sidequest: Raise a Chocobo** (flip it and you usually just win) and Summon: Fat Chocobo. Aerith Gainsborough and Yuna, Hope of Spira are the real payoffs.
- Garnet, Princess of Alexandria and Serah Farron underdelivered.

### 10. Gruul (RG) — Landfall — THE ONLY BAD DECK
- The one genuine outlier: ~2% below the ninth-best pair, and already visible in week one (#808: "red-green landfall was lagging behind significantly").
- **Both Pro Tour feature drafters forced it anyway and it went badly** — Matt Nass texted "I don't think I drafted a very good deck," and did not. He went 1-2 with it.
- **Verdict: do not draft it unless it is completely, completely open.** It is the one lane where LR would tell you to bail.

### 3+ color decks (not signposted, but real)

**Grixis (UBR) control — LSV's pet deck.** Not a supported archetype; you fall into it from a UR start that never found its payoffs. Build skeleton: **10–12 removal (counting counters and bounce as removal), 4–5+ card draw including Travel the Overworld, 5–7 towns.** Fire Magic is "one of the main reasons to draft this deck" — it means you never have to spend real removal on small creatures. Also PuPu UFO, Call the Mountain Chocobo, Blazing Bomb, Kefka, Court Mage, Triple Triad.
- **Weakness — you need CHEAP interaction.** "If it's expensive it's going to be just too clunky. That is something you have to really watch out for in the deck." Tap-land-into-tap-land-into-Thunder-Magic is the play pattern that makes the mana work.
- **Cornered by Black Mages is awkward here** — double black on the turn you need it, and edicts go downhill fast if you miss the window.

**Sultai Towns (BUG, often +1) — the format's pure value deck.** LSV took two trophies with it. Entry point is a bomb: Atraxa, Ardyn, Sin, Spira's Punishment, Summon: Bahamut, Muldrotha. Then black removal + green ramp + blue card draw.
- **Ramp preference is specific:** LSV wants **two-mana ramp** (Goobbue Gardener, Farseek, Town Greeter) and **four-mana ramp** (Reach the Horizon, Ring of the Lucii). He does **not** want three-mana ramp — Prishe's Wanderings is "okay but not a card I'm going to prioritize."
- Splashing a fourth color for a great card is allowed; most of the time LSV stayed on three.
- Prime finishers: Coliseum Behemoth, Sin, Summon: Knights of Round, Primeval Titan, Atraxa.

## Card interactions & combos

1. **Astrologian's Planisphere + Combat Tutorial on turn three = a 4/4.** The Planisphere gets a counter for the cast and another for drawing your third card, then Combat Tutorial's own counter lands on top. "That's crazy. It's not that hard to do that."
2. **Planisphere re-equip.** Equip 2 on a rare is the whole card: when they kill the creature, move the equipment cheaply and keep going. "It puts them in a bind."
3. **Sorceress's Schemes + cheap red removal.** Best partner is bonus-sheet **Lightning Bolt** (you can cast it off the floated red mana), then Thunder Magic and Suplex. Rebuy, kill something, and know you get another one later.
4. **Sorceress's Schemes + Fight On!** — "that's infinite grinding right there."
5. **Summon: G.F. Cerberus + Relm's Sketching.** Copy the Cerberus; play a third next turn and you're 5x-ing a spell. One of the set's few build-arounds that actually delivered.
6. **Firion, Wild Rose Warrior + Warrior's Sword (turn 3 into turn 4).** The Sword makes its Job Select token, Firion copies the equipment which makes a *second* token, both have haste — "you attack for eight." Any Job Select card works.
7. **Phantom Train + Squall, SeeD Mercenary.** Attack with Squall alone for double strike, then sacrifice Squall to the Train for the kill. The general Phantom Train pattern: leave it un-activated as a permanent lethal threat, force them to hold up mana, keep developing.
8. **Y'shtola Rhul + any ETB creature or saga creature** — she gives you an extra end step, so you blink twice a turn. Needs ~3 ETB creatures; 5–6 is where she's best. "If you just land this plus one for one turn, that's probably enough."
9. **Jenova, Ancient Calamity + a sacrifice outlet.** Jenova mutates a creature and pumps it; sacrificing the mutant draws cards equal to its power. Live at the table in #811: "you mutant something and then you just sack it and draw five cards."
10. **Stiltzkin, Moogle Merchant + Job Select.** Gift them the 1/1 token — the equipment stays attached to *their* creature until they pay to move it. Also: in response to a removal spell, gift them the target and draw a card; the removal still resolves and kills their own new creature. And in the late game, just gift lands to draw.
11. **Venat, Heart of Hydaelyn + Lunatic Pandora / Ambrosia Whiteheart.** Lunatic Pandora is a *one-mana legend* — an instant Venat trigger. Ambrosia is a two-mana flash legend that also bounces another of your legends for a second trigger (once per turn only).
12. **White Auracite + Eject or any bounce** — re-use the O-ring on a second target. **⚠ ANTI-COMBO: Ultima and White Auracite are not friends.** Ultima ends the turn, but the Auracite's return is a *static* effect, not a trigger, so blowing up your own Auracite gives them their card back. Play the Auracite after the wrath, or point it at a token.
13. **Nibelheim Aflame + a deathtouch creature** — one-sided sweeper. Then the 7-mana flashback re-sweeps *and* refills your hand with four cards. The risk is real: they can kill your chosen creature in response.
14. **Triple Triad + Call the Mountain Chocobo + cheap red removal.** #809 correction from listeners: **you're actually below 50% to get two cards, because ties don't win.** It's a legitimate six-drop in a red control deck and near-unanswerable as an enchantment, but it does nothing the turn it lands.
15. **Instant Ramen + Vayne's Treachery** — a spare artifact you've already cashed in for a card, ready to sacrifice.
16. **Matoya, Archon Elder + surveil.** The whole enabler list is Dreams of Laguna, Lunatic Pandora, Il Mheg Pixie, Swallowed by Leviathan and the four-mana 3/4 — there is **no scry in the set**. This is a pick-6-or-7 spec, never a plan.
17. **Zodiark, Umbral God + near-mono-black.** ~13 swamps plus a couple of duals still functions. You accept that your 19th–23rd playables get worse.
18. **Dark Ritual (bonus sheet) → a real haymaker.** Do NOT ritual out Shinra Reinforcements. Ritual out the eight-drop demon or Sin. "High impact five- and six-drops is the sweet spot."

## Card tips

Grades below are LR's **release-week rare/mythic set-review predictions from #807**, on an A+ → F scale, with `build-around` and `sideboard` as subgrades. Split grades show Marshall/LSV disagreement. **There are no LR letter grades for commons or uncommons in this batch** — see the Recency rule. The Sunset Show (#812) late verdict wins on conflict and is flagged.

### White (W)

- **Dion, Bahamut's Dominant** — A+ (#807 release). Four mana for 5 power / 5 toughness of flying-on-your-turn, and that's before the flip. "It's in the A− range without the other side of the card. This isn't even a mythic — are you crazy?" Splashable; if you open it in a towns deck, just take it.
- **Ultima** — A− (#807 release). 3WW: destroy all artifacts and creatures, end the turn. Hitting artifacts is the key — a wrath that leaves two equipment behind is a disaster. Ending the turn is on balance slightly good for you (stops death triggers and end-of-turn flash creatures). Awkward in aggressive decks.
- **Venat, Heart of Hydaelyn** — A− (#807 release). 1WW 3/3, draw on your first legendary spell each turn; seven mana exiles a nonland permanent and flips it into an indestructible 4/4 god. See combo #11.
- **Summon: Knights of Round** — build-around A (#807 release). Eight mana, 3/3 indestructible, three 2/2 knights per chapter for four chapters. #809 data check: **59.2% GIH WR vs 56.2% for The Crystal's Chosen** — LSV was surprised how high; both hosts take it out of that pack. "If you can cast it, you're going to win the game."
- **Cloud, Midgar Mercenary** — B (#807 release). WW 2/1 that tutors an equipment. "Half of Stone Forge Mystic is great" in a set where the equipment is good. Double white means you'll cast it a bit later than turn two.
- **Aerith Gainsborough** — B (#807 release). 2W 2/2 lifelink with provoke that grows on every lifegain. Needs help to survive combat — pump spells, equipment, or just removal on their first two creatures. Once it's a 3/3 or 4/4 it gets out of control, and its death trigger spreads counters onto every legend you control (and there are many).
- **Stiltzkin, Moogle Merchant** — B (#807 release). One-mana 1/2 lifelink with a huge late-game ability. See combo #10 — "a one-mana card with this big of an effect on the game."
- **Moogles' Valor** — B−/B (#807 release). Five-mana instant: a 1/2 lifelink Moogle per creature you control, then indestructible. "If they attack with four creatures and you have four creatures and you cast this, you're going to win the game." Blank with 0–1 creatures.
- **Machinist's Arsenal** — build-around B (#807 release). Job Select, equip 4, +2/+2 per artifact. UW artifacts only. LSV was unimpressed live: "it's clunky — you make a 7/7 or 9/9 but it's on the ground with no trample and it's expensive to move."
- **Minwu, White Mage** — C+ (#807 release). "A little worse than Aerith. It's a five-mana 3/3, you won't have many clerics, and you need to spend a card to get the ball rolling."
- **The Wind Crystal** — F (#807 release). "I'd be willing to bet a lot of money that any card with this mana cost that just had power, toughness and an ability would win you more games."
- **From Father to Son** — F (#807 release). **#812: Marshall's worst card in the set** — "the biggest blank, the most last-picked one. It felt like it was made for another format."
- **White Auracite** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: the best white common in the set, straight up.** O-ring that also taps for white. Combos with your own bounce; anti-combos with Ultima (see combo #12).
- **Magitek Infantry** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: **"I love Magitek Infantry. Fantastic card."** An army in a can for a one-mana investment; the play pattern that wins is dumping mana into it every turn while never missing a land drop, so they're forced to answer a board while you still hold five cards. Also a self-combo — the only thing you build around it with is more copies.
- **Dragoon's Lance** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #812: LSV's respect pick for uncommon of the set — **"I never wanted to see it. I lost a lot of games to Dragoon's Lance."**
- **The Crystal's Chosen** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808/#809: "continuously great, I'd take it highly." Seven mana of stuff; 56.2% GIH WR. White rarely gets a top-end card that fits, and this one does — especially off White Auracite's mana.
- **Restoration Magic / Slash of Light / Delivery Moogle / Magitek Armor / Weapons Vendor** — no grades. #812 on Slash of Light: "it's fine — a good card if you're in blue-white or red-white, sometimes black-white if you've got a bunch of Black Mage's Rods. Not a card you're going to start with."
- **Cloudbound Moogle** — no grade. #808: **the one bad land cycler.** "The land cyclers are good except for the Cloudbound Moogle. Pretty low power level. Poor Moogle."
- **Wall of Omens** *(bonus sheet, = Imperial City of Arcades)* — B (#807 release). "Aggro decks generally don't like it, everything else does. You should just put this card in your deck."
- **Adeline, Resplendent Cathar** *(bonus sheet, = Hero of Light)* — A− (#807 release). "Frequently a 5/4 that's made multiple creatures. Don't sleep on it."
- **Sram, Senior Edificer** *(bonus sheet, = Firion Swordmaster)* — A− (#807 release). "You can just make this a card-drawing machine. It comes down on two easy-mode and then you follow it up with the Job Select."
- **Ranger-Captain of Eos** *(bonus sheet, = Knights of San d'Oria)* — B+ if you have a one-drop to fetch, D+ if you don't (#807 release). The sacrifice-to-lock-out-noncreature-spells mode is rare but high leverage against a wrath.
- **Akroma's Will** *(bonus sheet, = Blessings of the Oracle)* — A (#807 release; LSV argued A+). **#812: an all-time groan-tester** — "if we ever do a show to pick the top eight groan tests of all time, this will be in contention. It should not be in this set." With three creatures you almost always just win. Doesn't even force you all-in: mode one leaves vigilance, mode two leaves lifelink.
- **Danitha Capashen, Paragon** *(bonus sheet, = Squall Leonhart)* — B+ (#807 release). First strike + vigilance + lifelink is the perfect equipment host, and equipment costs one less.
- **Loran of the Third Path** *(bonus sheet, = Garnet til Alexandros XVII)* — A (#807 release). "A very solid 3/1 that's going to eat a real permanent against basically every single deck in the format. I don't think I've had a deck with no targets yet." Use the shared draw at the end of *their* turn.
- **Mangara, the Diplomat** *(bonus sheet, = Minwu, Strategist)* — B+ (#807 release). 2/4 lifelink that's hard to attack past and constrains double-spelling. People forget one or the other trigger and give you free cards.
- **Stroke of Midnight** *(bonus sheet, = Memories of Nibelheim)* — C (#807 release; Marshall wanted D). Better here than usual. **Play it in decks that don't care about a 1/1** — towns splash, WB, UW. **Don't** play it in RW or GW, where the 1/1 blocks your own two-power creatures.

### Blue (U)

- **Jill, Shiva's Dominant** — A (#807 release). Three-mana 2/2 Man-o'-War (bouncing *any* nonland permanent, theirs or yours), flips into a must-answer saga that bounces again on the way back. "There's no way to lose out with this card." **#812: Marshall's favorite card of the entire format** — "they printed the best Man-o'-War you could imagine and I'm here for it."
- **Astrologian's Planisphere** — A− (#807 release). 1U Job Select equipment, **equip 2**, granting super-prowess (counters, not temporary pumps) plus a counter on your third draw each turn. "Even if this was a one-mana 1/1 with this ability I'd say it was a good card — and it leaves behind an equipment that's only equip 2." #809 confirms: goes especially well in blue-red, the best deck.
- **The Lunar Whale** — A− (#807 release). Four-mana 3/5 flying vehicle, **crew 1**, Future Sight while it has attacked. You see the top card before deciding to attack, and being a vehicle dodges sorcery-speed removal.
- **Summon: Leviathan** — A− (#807 release). Six-mana 6/6 ward 2 that bounces everything that isn't a sea creature, then draws on attack. "Six mana, 6/6, bounce basically everything, next turn attack for six and draw a card. It just does a lot."
- **Y'shtola Rhul** — build-around A (#807 release). See combo #8.
- **Memories Returning** — B (#807 release). 2UU: best three of the top five, opponent chooses which two you don't get. **#809: the fourth most winning blue card**, and the nine-mana flashback is real — "you usually get two lands off it, and you're not missing land drops at that point."
- **Gogo, Master of Mimicry** — B (#807 release) → **#812 DOWN.** Three-mana 2/4 that copies activated/triggered abilities X times. "It does feel scary if your opponent gets to untap with Gogo… but really it wasn't consistent enough to see a ton of heavy play."
- **Edgar, King of Figaro** — build-around B (#807 release). Six mana, 4/5, draw per artifact. Blue-white artifacts only. "Draw three, the card's great. Draw two, okay. Draw one, not enough. Zero, unplayable."
- **The Water Crystal** — sideboard C, or build-around C+/B− (#807 release). Four mana, does nothing, then six mana to mill them. Only main-deck it against a slow deck; it's a straight blank against RW and UR, and both of those exist.
- **Matoya, Archon Elder** — D/D+ (#807 release), build-around B with five or six surveil sources. LSV drafted it twice and was unimpressed: "there's no scry in the set, and 12 cards with surveil including itself. It promises a build-around and doesn't deliver." #809 nuance: worth a speculative sixth- or seventh-pick if you already have Dreams of Laguna / Lunatic Pandora / Il Mheg Pixie.
- **Louisoix's Sacrifice** — C (#807 release). "A textbook definition of a C — a blue-black control deck maybe has one to cover its bases." **#812 confirms:** LSV tested it and "it's not a card you generally want to be doing that with." Note it does counter Job Select equipment spells and creature-making spells.
- **Combat Tutorial** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: the best blue common in the set.** Two-mana draw-two plus a +1/+1 counter. "It just shows you — when a Divination is already 90% of the way there, that counter, boom, great card." **Prioritize two-drops once you have one**, but it's still fine to cast on turn seven and put the counter on a Wind Drake.
- **Dragoon's Wyvern** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #812: **Marshall's pick for best blue common** (17Lands says Combat Tutorial by a hair). "It didn't have that many synergies, but boy, it was good at what it did — stabilize your board, apply pressure."
- **Sahagin** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: the format's biggest miss** — see Supersessions. #808: "it totally looks like exactly the throwaway payoff we always get and never want, and it's just performing quite well. The thing is it picks up +1/+1 counters rather than temporary buffs."
- **Ice Magic** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). Elite blue common all format. "Great at four mana, totally solid at two, and sometimes you cast it for eight." Also one of the reasons blue is secretly a removal color here.
- **Swallowed by Leviathan** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #809: **"just excellent. A great card. Really good."** Surveil 2 plus a counter for single blue — "it actually has just looked so much better than you would expect it to."
- **Syncopate** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: LSV played it aggressively early and backed off slightly but still runs it. "It scales really well with the game — there are board states where I'm ahead with seven mana and I'm just going to force you to cast something. Exiling is good because there's a lot of flashback."
- **Stuck in Summoner's Sanctum** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). Medium removal that's always fine; #812 notes it ended up more useful than Sleep Magic.
- **Sleep Magic** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #811: fine card. **⚠ Anti-synergy with Fire Magic** — "if you Sleep Magic a 4/4 and then eventually cast a Fire Magic, it wakes it up."
- **Scorpion Sentinel** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: "the classic crossover card. It hits basically all the blue archetypes but it's not great in any of them, and that's fine — that's a good spot for a common to be at."
- **Rook Turret** — no grade. #808: "a playable artifact card, gets Suplexed right out. It's okay."
- **Dreams of Laguna** — no grade. **#812: LSV's "LSVest card" of the format** — "I don't think I've cut a Dreams of Laguna in the last couple weeks." Outperforms Brainstorm because you can pay four mana for it and it has flashback.
- **Sidequest: Card Collection** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: a big miss, UP.** See Supersessions. "I feel like half the time I cast it, it just flips over."
- **Retrieve the Esper** — no grade. #808: **blue-red only**, including not blue-white. Four-mana 3/3 flashing back to a six-mana 5/5 is not good enough on rate; it's good because it triggers your four-cost payoffs twice.
- **Magic Damper** — no grade. #808/#809: not a card you want. LSV's bar: "two really good things to protect plus a decent amount of other stuff." Suffers by comparison to the tiered cards, which scale with the game instead of doing the same thing forever.
- **Ether** — no grade. Draws eight with Travel the Overworld; ceiling only.
- **Counterspell** *(bonus sheet, = Wild Rose Rebellion)* — B (#807 release). "You've got to be a heavy blue deck, but it is good in that deck."
- **Urza, Lord High Artificer** *(bonus sheet, = Terra Branford)* — A to A+ in blue artifacts, B+ raw (#807 release). "Take it anyway even if you're blue-black with no artifacts." **#812: one of the top three cards in the format by 17Lands win rate.** Your Job Select equipment taps for mana while the creature is just chilling there.
- **Venser, Shaper Savant** *(bonus sheet, = Master Zandandy(?))* — B+ (#807 release). "The ultimate Man-o'-War. It messes with combat super hard and it kills tokens that have been equipped up."
- **Cryptic Command** *(bonus sheet, = The Crystal Tower)* — A (#807 release). Triple blue is a real cost — "try to have 10 or 11 blue sources" — but tap-their-team plus draw at the stage of the game where you can cast it is a game-ender. "You couldn't pay me to not play Cryptic Command."
- **Brainstorm** *(bonus sheet, = Endwalker)* — C, C+ with work (#807 release). **#812 confirmed: low win rate.** "In part because you just don't have that many ways to shuffle. I like it the most in blue-green when you have land cyclers or land searchers — or World Map." Also good with Town Greeter (mill past the cards you put back, and never miss a land drop).
- **Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir** *(bonus sheet, = Edea Kramer(?))* — C (#807 release). Triple blue, and by the time you cast it the effect isn't big enough.
- **Laboratory Maniac** *(bonus sheet)* — F (#807 release). Both hosts graded F and both admitted they'd try it anyway; LSV actually beat Cheon with it in a random queue.
- **Rhystic Study** *(bonus sheet, = Stay With Me(?))* — F (#807 release). "Compare this to Stone Rain — that card's really bad in Limited. It gives your opponent too much agency and you can't punish them, because this doesn't attack them or do anything on the board."

### Black (B)

- **Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER** — A (#807 release). Three-mana 3/3 that sacrifices on ETB-or-attack to draw, and is a Blood Artist that watches *both* sides. "If four creatures ever die in the same turn you basically win the game." Even stripped of the flip it'd be a B/B+. **#808 deckbuilding note: play it in black-white or black-green, try to avoid blue-black; black-red is fine.**
- **Summon: Primal Odin** — A (#807 release). Six-mana 5/3 saga: ETB kill a creature, chapter two grants "deals combat damage to a player → that player loses the game," chapter three draws two. "I can't find a scenario where you're not doing great off Summon: Primal Odin." Both hosts had already lost to the chapter-two mode within days of release.
- **Ardyn, the Usurper** — build-around A (LSV) / B or B+ (Marshall) (#807 release). Eight mana, 5BB. **#812: the bombiest bomb in the set** — "the card that won me the most boxes for sure. Even if you kill Ardyn the game is just over." Immediately makes a hasty 5/5 lifelink menace demon copy from any graveyard.
- **Cecil, Dark Knight** — B+ (#807 release). One-mana 2/3 deathtouch. "When they play it on turn one, you're going to feel really bad about it." The flip threat warps their attacks and yours — you may not want to chip in and push them toward 10.
- **Vincent Valentine** — B/B+ (#807 release) → **#812 DOWN: "a lot worse than it looked."** 2BB 2/2 that grows on every opposing creature death and flips into a recursive trampling lifelinker. The problem was named at review and turned out to be decisive: "you have dumped two double-black into a two-toughness creature. It is a lightning rod of all lightning rods."
- **Zenos yae Galvus** — B (#807 release). Five-mana 4/4 with a conditional −2/−2 sweeper and an 8/8 dragon on the back. "Really good against white, green, or red-white; might do nothing against blue-black or the towns deck. That all balances out to a B." (The alternate win condition on the flip does literally nothing in one-on-one.)
- **Jecht, Reluctant Guardian** — B (#807 release) → **#811 DOWN: LSV "C++, maybe B−."** Four-mana 4/3 menace; if it connects it flips into a 7/7 menace saga that double-discards, draws, and then edicts two. "A four-mana 4/3 menace is not a card I'm chomping at the bit to play, but if it hits them, you win the game." Marshall never conceded — an unresolved split.
- **The Darkness Crystal** — build-around B (#807 release). "It's one of those finishers that's really hard to beat if you don't have a way to kill artifacts, but it's also sometimes straight up dead in hand." The two extra +1/+1 counters on the reanimated creature are the whole card. Needs removal and defensive pieces around it. **#812: the Crystals cycle as a whole flopped.**
- **Zodiark, Umbral God** — build-around B (LSV) / near build-around D (Marshall) (#807 release) → **#809/#812 UP.** See Supersessions. Take it P1P1 and go mono-black; or move in a few picks later if you're two black cards deep. Do NOT jam it in a two-color deck with ten swamps. Marshall's separate irritation: people who *did* jam it in multicolor decks and got away with it.
- **Ninja's Blades** — C+ (#807 release). Three-mana Job Select equipment, equip 2, +1/+1, connect to loot and drain for the discarded card's mana value. "I could see this being annoying. It's the kind of thing where you're like, I don't really want to deal with this — because of equip."
- **Kain, Traitorous Dragoon** — D, maybe F (Marshall); **LSV explicitly declined to grade it** (#807 release). "I could believe this card could be a B, but I could also believe it's an F." The failure mode is stark: connect, then kill your own Kain, and you've spent three mana plus a removal spell for two treasures and an even card exchange. "A classic prove-it. I am not going to be the first one to bash my head through the wall."
- **Sephiroth's Intervention** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: the best black common.** Four-mana catch-all removal that gains two life. "Sometimes it's the cheaper and more flexible option like Vayne's Treachery, but it wasn't this time."
- **Vayne's Treachery** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #811, looked up live: **57.4% GIH WR** — LSV took it P1P1 over Syr Konrad (55%). Cheap instant-speed exile.
- **Resentful Revelation** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: still underrated at the end of the format** (LSV). See Supersessions. "Revelation into another Revelation — take a removal spell and put the two Revelations in your graveyard. That's the nuts. Two Ancestral Recalls in your graveyard for a mere seven mana."
- **Cornered by Black Mages** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: good, but a real cost — **double black early**, and edicts fall off a cliff if you can't cast them on the turn you need to. **Bad in black-green** (which wants every card to be a permanent) and awkward in Grixis. The 0/1 wizards are worth "half to three-quarters of a card" each, and multiples get gnarly fast.
- **Evil Reawakened** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: **"I just take this card now."** Five mana, reanimate with two +1/+1 counters. "Green-black, black-white, blue-black, all great. Red-black's the one place I don't like it." Great with a self-milled Malboro, Sin or Ardyn.
- **The Final Days** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #809/#811: the BG self-mill payoff — cast it for two tokens, flash it back for five or six. "It does work out pretty well, surprisingly well." Three copies is a real deck; once you have them, don't pass creatures and value permanents higher across the board.
- **Circle of Power** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: very good in black-red, fine as a Grixis card-draw spell with a body attached. Goes down slightly because this set has so much card draw that you don't have to prioritize it. **#812: LSV's actual favorite card of the format** ("I had two Instant Ramens, three Circle of Powers, three Cornered by Black Mages… and I just didn't attack a single time that draft. It was awesome.")
- **Phantom Train** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812, the sharpest split-by-deck card in the set:** "in a lot of decks a pretty bad or unplayable card, and then in white-black or black-red, or sometimes black-green depending on construction, incredibly good." Marshall avoided it all format and still respects it; LSV won a box with it. High risk, high reward — bad timing on their removal or bounce can make you lose to your own Phantom Train.
- **Hecteyes** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812 DOWN** vs. its usual slot. See Supersessions. Play it where you have sacrifice outlets (Phantom Train, Vayne's Treachery); deprioritize in UB.
- **Shinra Reinforcements** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #809: **UP** — "the total package of a 2/3 plus the mill three is what I want out of a card." A pillar of the BG Final Days deck.
- **Poison the Waters** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #809: "I've had this card be awesome for me and awesome against me, and terrible for me and terrible against me." **#812 files it under *looks good when you read it, doesn't see play*** — the −1/−1 mode is basically never good, and even the good mode is a one-for-one where you spent the mana.
- **Shambling Cie'th** — no grade. #809: **live disagreement.** LSV really likes it in blue-black with lots of discard and self-mill (Sidequest: Card Collection, Vayne's Treachery sacrifice, then pay B to get it back). Marshall: "I have not liked it, for whatever it's worth." Unresolved.
- **Undercity Dire Rat** — no grade. #811: a fine two-drop that makes a treasure and goes to the yard for value — a good fit in a triple-Final-Days deck.
- **Sidequest: Hunt the Mark** — no grade. #811: **"this card's also just truly excellent."** Double black is the only cost; the treasure helps cast your splash.
- **Black Waltz No. 3** — no grade. #811: "if this card does stay in play you tend to win the game a lot of the time" — but it dies to Thunder Magic and everything else. Not worth speculating on early.
- **Black Mage's Rod** — no grade. Job Select pinger; the reason black-white sometimes wants Slash of Light.
- **Fatal Push** *(bonus sheet, = Battle at the Big Bridge)* — A− (#807 release). "In the same vein as Murderous Cut — it kills anything early really efficiently but also kills stuff up to the mid game. Tempo is so important these days and this plays right into it."
- **Yawgmoth, Thran Physician** *(bonus sheet, = The Emperor, Hell Tyrant)* — A+ (#807 release), possibly A because it asks you to have creatures. "Protection from humans is not nothing — there are actually a lot of humans in the set. It makes all your creatures not only cash in for a card, they also take out your opponent's creatures. There's really nothing bad to be said about this card."
- **Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor** *(bonus sheet, = The Shadow Lord)* — A− (#807 release). "Good when you get creatures through and good in a board stall — that's a pretty nice combination." The seven-mana ability takes over once the ground gums up.
- **Syr Konrad, the Grim** *(bonus sheet, = Golbez Clad in Darkness)* — B+/A− (#807 release) → **#811 data check: 55.0% GIH WR, below Vayne's Treachery at 57.4%.** LSV took the removal spell over it P1P1. Verdict: still good, but not the A− the review implied.
- **Dark Confidant** *(bonus sheet, = Bob)* — B in an aggro deck / D in a non-aggro deck (#807 release). "The more aggressive you are, the better Bob gets." Keep your average mana value low, have a sac outlet or your own removal ready to get off the Bob train.
- **Dark Ritual** *(bonus sheet, = Darkness of Eternity)* — build-around B (#807 release). See combo #18.
- **Varragoth, Bloodsky Sire** *(bonus sheet, = Ardyn Izunia)* — C+ (#807 release). "Mostly it's a three-mana 2/3 deathtouch."
- **Deadly Dispute** *(bonus sheet, = Baron Rivalry(?))* — C (#807 release). Best in black-white, also fine in black-green. Nice trick: on Arena, hold full control and sacrifice a saga creature in response to its final chapter trigger.
- **Bolas's Citadel** *(bonus sheet, = Kefka's Tower)* — D (#807 release). Consider it only alongside Dark Ritual.
- **K'rrik, Son of Yawgmoth** *(bonus sheet, = Emet-Selch, Ascian)* — F (#807 release). "Every time it's been in a Limited set it's been horrible. You don't want to play two-toughness creatures that cost between four and seven mana and a bunch of life."
- **Diabolic Intent** *(bonus sheet, = Shantotto's Coercion)* — F (#807 release). "Tutors aren't that good, and especially tutors that cost you a creature."

### Red (R)

- **Clive, Ifrit's Dominant** — A− (#807 release). Six-mana 5/5, discard-your-hand-and-draw-devotion-to-red on ETB, then six more to flip into a 9/9 that eats a creature and gives you four red mana back on each subsequent chapter. Five toughness makes it hard to kill. Crucially, "you're not forced to use it — if they pass with six up, do something else instead."
- **Nibelheim Aflame** — B+ (#807 release). See combo #13. "The package of sweeper into seven-mana sweeper plus draw four is an incredible one" — but they can kill your chosen creature in response, and once it's in your graveyard they *know*.
- **Seifer Almasy** — B+ (#807 release). Four-mana 3/4; attack alone for double strike; on damage, free-cast an instant or sorcery of mana value 3 or less from your yard. "That card goes into basically any red deck and will do well."
- **Firion, Wild Rose Warrior** — build-around A in a Job Select deck / B elsewhere (#807 release). See combo #6. "This is the scariest thing you could do on three in that deck." The nice part is the enabler (Warrior's Sword) is a card no one else wants.
- **Gilgamesh, Master-at-Arms** — B, build-around A (#807 release). Six-mana 6/6; on ETB *or attack* look at six and put **every** equipment into play. Pick-order note from LSV: attach a *non*-Job-Select equipment to Gilgamesh himself (he's the samurai), and generally leave the Job Select ones — you already have a 6/6.
- **Raubahn, Bull of Ala Mhigo** — B (#807 release). Two-mana 2/2 with ward-pay-life-equal-to-its-power that attaches an equipment to any attacker on attack — including itself. "If its power gets into the four or five range and you've already taken two hits, they may not be able to kill it without killing themselves."
- **Summon: Brynhildr** — B (#807 release). "A much better Abbot of Keral Keep, and that was a good card. Solid B, good in any red deck, better the lower your curve is." You get three turns to cash the exiled card.
- **Summon: G.F. Cerberus** — build-around B (#807 release). **#812: named one of the set's better build-arounds.** See combo #5. Also a must-kill on sight: "there's no timing window where you say *if they have a spell I'll kill it* — no, if they have a spell, they're copying it."
- **Zell Dincht** — B in a landfall / ramp / adventure-land deck, otherwise avoid (#807 release). LSV played it with the rare red adventure land and it was "really strong" — cast the land out of exile, bounce it end of turn, repeat.
- **Triple Triad** — build-around B (#807 release) → #809 nuance. See combo #14. "I would almost never first-pick it, but imagine mid-pack with a Thunder Magic, a Suplex, a Call the Mountain Chocobo and another blue card — that's the place where it fits." Marshall stayed skeptical all format: "I still haven't seen anybody go off with this."
- **Vaan, Street Thief** — C (#807 release). "You're not often going to get tons of value off it, and there's no way I'm putting bad cards in my deck to try to make Vaan a thing."
- **The Fire Crystal** — F (#807 release). "You don't want to pay four for a cost reduction and haste, and six to copy your creatures. I just don't think you should put this card in your deck."
- **Thunder Magic** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: the best red common, "pretty clear-cut."** Instant-speed tiered removal — and the fact that it can cost four mana is why blue-red wants it over Suplex.
- **Call the Mountain Chocobo** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: the second-best red common; UP from the review.** "This card's a four-for-one. You cast it, you get a 2/2 and a land; flash it back, you get another 2/2 and a land. It's a ten-mana four-for-one — what more do you want?" Fantastic in UR, Grixis and RB; fine but not peak in RW. LSV's only complaint: "I'll play a three-Mountain Grixis deck and run out of Mountains to get."
- **Fire Magic** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812 BIG UP: "B+ to A−."** See Supersessions. Deck-dependent: great in UR and Grixis, fantastic in RB, **do not put it in red-white**. The dream line is letting a Magitek Infantry sink ten mana and then answering it for one.
- **Suplex** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: "two mana removal, extremely cheap, it really does everything you want. Good in all the red color combinations." Held back from the very top only because it can't cost four.
- **Sorceress's Schemes** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#809/#812 UP.** See Supersessions and combos #3/#4. "Not really anywhere except black-red and blue-red, but I have found the card to be good and worth drafting around."
- **Choco-Comet** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: Marshall's uncommon-of-the-set pick.** "Not only just on its face incredible, but if you have Sorceress's Schemes and you can get it back, that can end up being really strong."
- **Blazing Bomb** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812 UP in blue-red only.** "I have not been cutting this from my red-blue decks" (LSV, #809) — but he assumes your deck has four-mana spells; without them it gets much worse. Also fine in Grixis as cheap interaction. Marshall stayed lower — unresolved.
- **Haste Magic** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#809: LSV "Unplayable." The lowest-winning red common.** But #812: Marshall lost to it repeatedly off one-drop-plus-Haste-Magic turn-three bursts. Don't play it; do play around it.
- **Mysidian Elder** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812 DOWN by home, not by rate.** "On its face a pretty reasonable card ratewise, but it didn't have much of a home." Wrong for blue-red (#811: "I feel like you don't want that card there"), okay in red-white as two bodies to hold equipment, best in black-red — which LSV barely drafted.
- **Red Mage's Rapier** — no grade. #808/#809: "not super impressed." Support piece for black-red's spell density and for red-white. **Explicitly not for blue-red** — "blue-red doesn't want random creatures like this."
- **Random Encounter** — no grade. #808: dissed at review, then LSV built a double-Random-Encounter red-green deck with 16 creatures that was "a lot of gas." "It's a powerful card. I'd never take it even on the first go-round — you can just wheel it. But I feel like there is something to this."
- **Aerith Rescue Mission** — no grade. #808: "It's been okay, but not particularly great. It actually does tap down a bunch of attackers."
- **Freya Crescent** — no grade. #808: **"a secret gold card — you're just not playing it outside of red-white."** Wears and moves equipment well and taps for equipment mana; plays like a land elf in a Job Select deck.
- **Light of Judgment / Self-Destruct / Unexpected Request** — no grades. Light of Judgment is named as good red interaction "you don't even really need to get to."
- **Lightning Bolt** *(bonus sheet, = Thrum of the Vestige(?))* — A (#807 release). "It's one mana, it hits anything. Ultra-efficient removal gets into the A category and that counts." Also the single best card to rebuy with Sorceress's Schemes.
- **Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer** *(bonus sheet, = Zidane, Tantalus Thief)* — A (#807 release). "It might be the scariest one-drop you could play in the set. A card that if it's in your opening hand you win 75% of the time." Don't be fooled by a mid-game draw — the dash mode still gives you a card and a treasure.
- **Godo, Bandit Warlord** *(bonus sheet, = Gilgamesh, Weapon Collector)* — A− (#807 release). "In this set, tutoring an equipment onto the battlefield is the equivalent of go-get-a-creature-and-put-it-into-play."
- **Captain Lannery Storm** *(bonus sheet, = Vaan, Aspiring Sky Pirate)* — B (#807 release). "One of the nice snowbally cards where you just get really far ahead."
- **Najeela, the Blade-Blossom** *(bonus sheet, = Cloud Strife)* — B (#807 release). "As a three-mana 3/2 that makes creatures it's pretty reasonable. The activated ability isn't one you really want to work for — it doesn't pump them." Save it for red-white.
- **Mizzix's Mastery** *(bonus sheet, = Dawn Warrior's Legacy(?))* — build-around B (#807 release). Neat in the four-mana-spell deck: cast it *on* a four-drop and you trigger twice.
- **Ancient Copper Dragon** *(bonus sheet, = Dragon of Mount Gulg)* — C+ (#807 release). "It's just a big nice flyer. The treasures are really not that relevant."
- **Light Up the Stage** *(bonus sheet, = A Promise Fulfilled)* — C (#807 release). "The more aggressive you are, the better it is." Nice in the black-red wizard deck where a cheap spell pings them into spectacle range.
- **Purphoros, God of the Forge** *(bonus sheet, = Kefka Palazzo)* — D (#807 release). "You'd combo with this by being heavy red and having a token subtheme, and I don't know if either of those is really going to come in here."

### Green (G)

- **Esper Origins** — A− / B+ (Marshall) / A (LSV) (#807 release). One-mana surveil 2 + gain 2, flashing back for four into Summon: Esper Madeen — reveal-a-permanent, add GG, then a mini-overrun. "It gives you a card right away, then gives you mana to cast it, then gives you a little overrun." Great to mill or surveil with Town Greeter.
- **Traveling Chocobo** — A− (#807 release). Three-mana 3/2 that plays lands and bird spells off the top and doubles land/bird triggers. "Three mana 3/2 that lets you play lands off the top is just a card I'm very happy to play. If your opponent ever casts this, you're really going to want to kill it quickly."
- **Sazh Katzroy** — B+ (Marshall) / A− (LSV) (#807 release). "Four-mana 3/3, goes and gets a land, then attacks as a 5/5 or pumps other things. Just straight up a two-for-one and then a must-deal-with threat — that's a lot for four mana."
- **Summon: Titan** — B+ (#807 release). Five-mana 7/7 reach trample; mill five, return all lands from your yard, then pump another creature by the number of lands you control with trample. "Extremely powerful, but it has to survive — if they kill it after chapter one you get nothing, and that keeps it out of the A range."
- **Bartz and Boko** — build-around B+ (#807 release) → **#812 DOWN: "I never really saw that thing just go off."** At least it's a strong build-around — with birds it costs two or three mana and kills something on the way in; with no birds it's a five-mana 4/3.
- **Ancient Adamantoise** — build-around, no letter given (#807 release). Eight-mana 8/20 with vigilance, ward 3, sticky damage, and it soaks all damage aimed at you and your permanents. Marshall was skeptical; LSV argued him into it. "A good ramp finisher, in the main ramp color."
- **Tifa Lockhart** — build-around C (#807 release) — and both hosts flag that **build-around C is a bad grade**: "you don't even want to play it unless you're building around it, at which point it's not an amazing card." → #812: confirmed weak, but the *most frustrating bad card to lose to.*
- **The Earth Crystal** — D (#807 release). "Four plus six is ten mana. It just looks too slow, and unlike the blue one it also requires creatures on board."
- **Jumbo Cactuar** — D (#807 release). "If it ever hits, it wins. If you can give it trample, it wins. Otherwise it can get chump-blocked all day long, and on defense it's just a 1/7 that doesn't even eat anything. The whole point of seven- and eight-mana cards is that they do the thing on their own."
- **Summoner's Grimoire** — F (#807 release). "Too much has to go right and a lot can go wrong. Just don't play this card."
- **A Realm Reborn** — no grade; the hosts skipped past it ("mercifully few words").
- **Town Greeter** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: Marshall's best green common, and 17Lands agrees.** Two-mana 1/1 that mills four and gains 2 off a town. Fills the yard for The Final Days and Evil Reawakened; also a partial extra color source, since you cast it to find one.
- **Summon: Fat Chocobo** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #812: Marshall's #2 green common. "A pushed card — five mana for a 4/4 plus a 3/2 plus trample." A **four-chapter** saga creature, so you can't just take the hits.
- **Summon: Fenrir** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: LSV's uncommon-of-the-set pick** — "that card really performed well in the decks that I drafted a lot."
- **Goobbue Gardener** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: "a good high pick — it ramps you right past the spot where you need to be. At its best in black-green." A 1/3 body dodges most of the cheap untiered removal. #812 caveat: "it's a good card if you're in green. Not really a reason to go into green."
- **Gigantoad** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). #808: **"it has no home at all. I strive not to play this. Very bad card."** It needed reach.
- **Commune with Beavers** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#812: LSV's most begrudgingly playable card.** Multiple structural problems: the towns decks have too many tap lands to want a one-drop mana sink, and the black-green self-mill decks would rather mill a creature than this. "It almost always makes it to the sideboard if I can help it."
- **Prishe's Wanderings** — no grade. #808: "that card's okay, but it's not a card I'm going to prioritize. I want the two-mana ramp." Marshall on eating one: "it's rare I found a card that made me feel so wronged when somebody got value off of it."
- **Reach the Horizon / Ring of the Lucii** — no grades. #808: the four-mana ramp LSV *does* want (as opposed to three-mana ramp). Ring of the Lucii is a much higher pick if you have four-cost-matters payoffs.
- **Cactuar** — no grade. #808, both hosts, unanimous: **"Never had it in my deck. Every time I played against it, it's been awful."**
- **Sabotender** — no grade. #811: "I really try hard not to play Sabotender, and I feel pretty good when my opponent has that card."
- **Chocobo Racetrack** — no grade. #808/#812: **not good.** "Even in a great scenario where you play it turn six and then play a land, that still isn't very good. You tend to use up your ramp cards before you play the Racetrack."
- **Sidequest: Raise a Chocobo** — no grade. #812: "a midpoint between a quest and just a two-mana 2/2. I flipped this card once and it was incredible — you just win." **Green-white is by far the best deck for it.**
- **Gysahl Greens** — no grade. #811: marginal; a body for self-mill decks, not a card you flash back.
- **Primeval Titan** *(bonus sheet, = Astral Titan)* — A (#807 release). "Prime time in the towns deck. Get out of here."
- **Farseek** *(bonus sheet, = Newfound Adventure)* — "This is great" — no letter given (#807 release). Two-mana ramp for the towns deck; the other ramp cards that fetch towns cost three, and that difference is the whole point.
- **Fynn, the Fangbearer** *(bonus sheet, = Vayne Carudas Solidor)* — B (#807 release). "One-three deathtouch for two is really annoying to attack into and surprisingly good offensively — they take it twice and suddenly they're at six poison and have to start thinking."
- **Nature's Claim** *(bonus sheet, = Search for the Frozen Esper(?))* — B (LSV) / C (Marshall) (#807 release). **Main-deck it** — "there are so many enchantments and artifacts. All the saga creatures are there." One mana, and if it's a saga creature it kills a creature too.
- **Carpet of Flowers** *(bonus sheet, = Fous'y Paradise(?))* — sideboard A vs. heavy blue (#807 release). "If they have two Islands in play, this card is awesome. But keep in mind, we're talking about a very narrow use case." Watch out for opponents whose blue comes from towns.
- **Nyxbloom Ancient** *(bonus sheet, = The Cloud Sea Jin(?))* — F (#807 release). "You can't pay seven mana to then get more mana, and a 5/5 trample isn't worth seven."
- **Azusa, Lost but Seeking** *(bonus sheet, = Princess Sarah)* — F (#807 release). "You just run out of lands so fast and then you have a 1/2 left over."

### Multicolor / gold

- **Kuja, Genome Sorcerer** (RB) — A (#807 release). Four-mana 3/4 that makes a pinging 0/1 wizard every end step, flipping into a 4/6 that doubles all wizard damage. "This card's ridiculous on its own — you don't need the flip. If you ever get two of those 0/1 wizards in play, the game devolves really quickly."
- **Yuna, Hope of Spira** (GW) — A (#807 release). "Five-mana 3/5 trample lifelink ward 2 — you'd probably just put that in your deck, that's a B to start with. But if you ever play this and get one enchantment back, you're already so far ahead. This looks like one of the better cards in the set." Note the buffs only apply on your turn.
- **Joshua, Phoenix's Dominant** (RW) — A (#807 release). "Three-mana 3/4 that's basically Seasoned Pyromancer minus the tokens is already really good," and the Phoenix flip drains, gains, and reanimates. "It's pretty hard for this card not to be awesome."
- **Tellah, Great Sage** (UR) — A− to A (#807 release). Five-mana 3/3; on every noncreature spell make a 1/1, and if four or more mana was spent, **also** draw two. "There's no blue-red deck in the history of this set that isn't going to want this card." The drawback is real — five mana into a removal spell.
- **Lightning, Army of One** (RW) — A− (#807 release). Three-mana 3/2 first strike trample lifelink; connect and everything of yours deals double damage to them until your next turn. First strike is what makes it work. "Some very tense moments with combat when you have Lightning even on your side" — two toughness means declaring attackers can go badly.
- **Emet-Selch, Unsundered** (UB) — B+ (#807 release). "I would play this without the ability to flip — three-mana 2/4 vigilance that loots on ETB and on every attack." 14 cards in the yard is achievable.
- **Terra, Magical Adept** (RG) — B+ (#807 release). Three-mana 4/2, ETB mill five and grab an enchantment (i.e. a saga creature). "I wouldn't play it with no enchantments. If you do transform it, you'll slam for six in the air twice and the game's over." Two toughness gives them a wide window.
- **Squall, SeeD Mercenary** (WB) — B+ (#807 release). Four-mana 3/4; attack-alone double strike; on damage reanimate a permanent of mana value 3 or less. See combo #7.
- **Jenova, Ancient Calamity** (BG) — B+ (#807 release). "It just spits around +1/+1 counters at the minimum. It's not hard to imagine it going really hard if you have a way to sacrifice a creature." See combo #9.
- **Kefka, Court Mage** (Grixis) — B+ (#807 release). "A value machine" — 4/5 that makes both players discard and draws you a card per card type discarded. B+ rather than higher purely because of the mana: "even if there is ample fixing, it doesn't mean there's no cost — you're spending draft picks on it."
- **Sin, Spira's Punishment** (Sultai) — B+ / build-around A (#807 release). Seven-mana 7/7 flier that copies a random permanent from your yard on ETB and on every attack, repeating on lands. **#812: Marshall's favorite-shaped card in the set** — "if I get one remotely early in the draft, I know what I'm drafting towards." Best in black-green splashing blue, and better to splash than a four-mana gold card because you always expect to cast it on seven.
- **Vivi Ornitier** (UR) — build-around B+ (Marshall) / build-around A (LSV) (#807 release). Free X-mana in blue and/or red equal to Vivi's power once a turn, growing and pinging on every noncreature spell. LSV: "play it turn three, they don't kill it, turn four you cast a two-mana cantrip, now it's a 1/4, add a mana, play a three-drop that pumps it — you just go completely nuts." Marshall's hesitation: "I just don't want to get up into the A range for a 3/6." **#812: the card LSV still has to re-read every time it's cast against him.**
- **The Wandering Minstrel** (UG) — build-around B+ (#807 release) → **#812 DOWN.** Lands enter untapped (huge with towns), an all-color 2/2 every combat with five towns, and an eight-mana overrun. **⚠ Rules trap both hosts and Paul Rietzl missed all format: the overrun does not pump the Minstrel itself.**
- **Serah Farron** (GW) — build-around B (#807 release). "This card is just outright bad if there's no legends around. I'd put it into a deck that already had a bunch of legendary creatures kind of coincidentally — you can back your way into this build-around rather than forcing it." #808: surprisingly flippable in practice, still not exciting.
- **Noctis, Prince of Lucis** (Esper) — build-around B (#807 release). "If you're in blue-white already and you see this, it's worth trying to make it work — 4/3 lifelink is really nice, and it accumulates value." **#812 contrast: this is the *right* way to build a blue artifact payoff, and Golbez is the wrong way.**
- **Golbez, Crystal Collector** (UB) — build-around B (#807 release) → **#812 DOWN: "a trap card."** See Supersessions. Both hosts flagged the real problem at review — "it's a build without much of a home" — and it turned out to be fatal.
- **Choco, Seeker of Paradise** (Bant) — build-around B (#807 release) → **#812: a build-around miss.** "It's not really worth stretching your mana for it most of the time. But if you can get away with it, it is pretty good — especially if you can play it and immediately attack with a bird."
- **Balthier and Fran** (RG) — C+ (#807 release). "Mostly as a three-mana 4/3 reach, which is totally fine. Sometimes the vehicle stuff will come up but I don't know how often. You just wouldn't build around it."
- **Hope Estheim** (UW) — C (#807 release). "This mostly plays out as blue-white for a 2/2 lifelink. I've never seen the ability do anything, and it's hard to cast."

- **Dovin's Veto** *(bonus sheet, = Shadowbringers)* — C, but probably always main-deck it (#807 release). "Because of the equipment in the set, people's ratio of creatures to non-creatures is more slanted toward non-creatures."
- **Ishai, Ojutai Dragonspeaker** *(bonus sheet, = Benedikta Harrington(?))* — D (#807 release). "Horrible top-deck, not good against bounce, extremely vulnerable. It's a four-drop — it's already too late."
- **Isshin, Two Heavens as One** *(bonus sheet, = Lightning, Lone Commando(?))* — F / D− (#807 release). "There aren't that many attack triggers, and it's so hard to cast. Too many situational things rolled up into one."

### Colorless / artifacts

- **Buster Sword** — B+ (#807 release). Three-mana equipment, equip 2, +3/+2; connect to draw a card **and** free-cast a spell of mana value ≤ the damage dealt. "It's like you draw a card and a Black Lotus. It's going to feel like a Dauthi Voidwalker in cube — if that thing ever hits me, it's bad times."
- **The Regalia** — B+ (#807 release). Four-mana 4/4 haste vehicle, crew 1, ramps on every attack. "This is unfortunately what they need to do for vehicles before we'll play them. But I'll play this."
- **Summon: Bahamut** — build-around A− (#807 release). Nine mana; chapters one and two each destroy a nonland permanent. "You cannot lose the game if this thing survives. Chapters three and four could just be blank and you'd probably be fine." Only for a real ramp deck.
- **The Masamune** — C+ (#807 release). First strike + must-be-blocked while attacking; equip 2, no stats. "It leverages your opponent's removal spells against you. It was better than I thought on first read — but it doesn't do much on defense."
- **Ultima, Origin of Oblivion** — C+ (#807 release). "It's mostly just a five-mana 4/4 flier, which is fine. You're often going to want to target *yourself* so your lands tap for two — it's too late to attack their mana meaningfully."
- **Aettir and Priwen** — effectively unplayable at six to cast plus five to equip (#807 release: "it's just too expensive, 11 mana. I wouldn't even try to use it with equip-for-cheap deals"). **#812: both hosts' *my opponent played this so now I must win or quit Magic forever* card** — the base power and toughness equal to their life total means the moment it lands you sit up in your seat.
- **Excalibur II** — F (#807 release). "You'd have to gain life three times before this even starts to be good."
- **Genji Glove** — F (#807 release). "Eight mana and no stats. Don't get seduced by the times when you get a random theft of a game you wouldn't normally have won."
- **Blitzball** — no grade (C/UC review not in batch). **#808/#809 UP:** "a three-mana rock that taps for any color is so close to being playable on its own that adding *sometimes you cash it in for two cards* is enough." Play it in towns, Grixis and the artifact deck; skip it where you don't want a rock. "I would have set the over-under at maybe one activation in the whole format, and it actually happens kind of all the time."
- **Magitek Infantry** — see White; it's the UW artifact deck's engine.
- **World Map** — no grade. #808: "Use cases only — a dedicated artifact deck that wants its artifact count up, or a black-mage deck that wants its spell density up. **I would hope not to play it in the Grixis deck** — your fixing's better than that."
- **Instant Ramen** — no grade. #809: **UP** — "I played it a little more than I thought I would." Good in the wizard-token decks (gain life, ping, cantrip) and as a spare artifact for Vayne's Treachery.
- **PuPu UFO** — no grade. #808/#809: **towns and Grixis only.** "First of all it's a two-mana 0/4 flier so it can block pretty well. Casting this into a Combat Tutorial and the game is just over — you have a 1/5, you have a showstopper, you're going to have six lands on turn four." Watch people attack Wyverns into it.
- **Lunatic Pandora** — no grade. #809: a reasonable playable, and specifically a **one-mana legend** — see combo #11.
- **Elixir** — no grade. #809: "It's just not good. I've even put Lab Maniac in multiple decks and mostly had it not be good."
- **Adventurer's Airship** — no grade. #811: Marshall jammed multiples in sealed and "it played extremely well." LSV is cooler on it.
- **Airship Crash** — no grade. #811: the slot that patches BG's flier weakness. "We're not losing on the ground with three Final Days that are fully supported, but we could lose to fliers."
- **Warrior's Sword** — no grade. #808: below curve on its own, but a free-roll in blue-red — a noncreature spell that costs four and makes a body.
- **Crystal Fragments / Qiqirn Merchant / Sage's Nouliths / Stolen Uniform** — no grades. **#812: Stolen Uniform is LSV's worst card in the set** — "it bothered me more because it was an uncommon, so you saw it all the time and no one ever picked or cast it in the history of the format." Qiqirn Merchant: "you'd think I'd like a card like this, but it's just been too slow" (Marshall) — both hosts pass it.
- **Smuggler's Copter** *(bonus sheet, = The Strahl(?))* — A (#807 release). "One of the best things they've ever made. There's a reason this card was banned in Standard."
- **Chromatic Lantern** *(bonus sheet, = Crystal of Ultima Cave(?))* — C (#807 release). "Don't go crazy — it's a three-mana rock that taps for any color and fulfills your color requirements almost entirely by itself. Play it in the towns deck if you're casting Jodah or Atraxa."
- **Thief's Knife** *(bonus sheet)* — no letter given, "a little below the bar, kind of ended up being in that D range" (#812). **Marshall's *bad card but I love it anyway* pick.**
- **Strixhaven Stadium** *(bonus sheet, = Luca Stadium)* — F (#807 release). "That never happens. This is terrible."
- **Command Beacon** *(bonus sheet, = Balamb Garden)* — F (#807 release). It fetches your commander. There is no commander.

### Lands

- **Balamb Garden, SeeD Academy** — A− (#807 release). "It's a blue-green town, that's already really good for the blue-green town deck, and at some point it becomes a 5/4 flier that draws a card. It's the best of all worlds — and unlike the adventure lands, you get all the use of the land first, and it's not sorcery-speed to transform."
- **Zanarkand, Ancient Metropolis** — B, could be B+ (#807 release). The green adventure land: "a six-mana 7/7 or 8/8 card that you play as a land. Land early, spell late, and it affects the board — it's actually a real thing."
- **Midgar, City of Mako** — B (#807 release). "A good gas card in the late game — you can play it late, sacrifice something you don't need anymore, and still cast spells after it, because it's only three."
- **Lindblum, Industrial Regency** — B (#807 release). "Instant speed. Yes, it's still low impact, but that is free-ish and that's nice." The 0/1 wizard token matters in RB.
- **Starting Town** — B (LSV) / B− (Marshall) (#807 release). "It's a dual land for one life, and it's untapped in the early stages. Your black-red deck would definitely rather have this than a tapped black-red land." Marshall's reservation is the life.
- **Ishgard, the Holy See** — C+ (Marshall) / B (LSV) (#807 release). The white adventure land. The gap is about how bad Faith and Grief actually is: LSV — "it's really nice to just have a land that can draw you two cards, even if it's clunky." Marshall — "I'd guess your chances of winning any game go down upon casting it… any game where you cast it, you're not doing great. It's free, so we'll take it."
- **Jidoor, Aristocratic Capital** — D (#807 release). The blue adventure land: "you mill them from 18 to nine — what did that do? Nothing. If towns didn't matter to you, an Island would just be better. If you do have towns as a theme, it's a fine card to play as an additional town."
- **Clive's Hideaway** — D (#807 release). "It's a **colorless** town, and I haven't found I want colorless towns in my town decks — I'm trying to play three, four, five colors. And keeping four legendary creatures on the board, even with how many there are, is going to be very difficult."
- **The towns cycle generally** — **#812: take them higher than you think.** "If you have a two-town, two-color deck, you're just going to get mana screwed maybe one less game over the course of the draft. That's huge." The towns deck wants 5–7. Crossroads Village is the common any-color town.
- **The land cyclers (Malboro, Balamb T-Rexaur, Hill Gigas, Ice Flan, Cloudbound Moogle)** — no grades (C/UC review not in batch). #808: "the land cyclers are fairly pushed these days. They're legit cards, but none of them crack into the top commons where you go *oh, you've got to have it.*" LSV's ordering in #808: **Balamb T-Rexaur and Malboro are pretty good, Ice Flan is a little worse than those two, Cloudbound Moogle is the bad one.** They also give Brainstorm its only real shuffle effects.

## Pitfalls

1. **Don't build the UR deck as "ten four-mana spells."** You want roughly five real four-drops plus five cheap flexible cards you can *choose* to spend four mana on — Thunder Magic, Ice Magic, Syncopate. Keying off mana *spent*, not printed cost, is the whole design.
2. **Don't put random creatures in the UR deck.** Retrieve the Esper, Call the Mountain Chocobo and Warrior's Sword are noncreature spells that leave bodies — those are your creatures. Red Mage's Rapier and Mysidian Elder are not.
3. **Don't force Gruul.** Both Pro Tour feature drafters did and both regretted it. It's the one lane LR would tell you to bail on.
4. **Don't chase Golbez, Crystal Collector.** Blue-black is not an artifact deck in FIN. The artifact deck is blue-white; the correct blue artifact payoff to splash for is Noctis.
5. **Don't chase the Crystals.** All five underperformed. The Water Crystal is a sideboard card, not a main-deck finisher, and it's a straight blank against the two most-played aggressive decks.
6. **Don't put nonpermanents in black-green.** Cornered by Black Mages and Commune with Beavers are perfectly good cards that are actively bad here — the self-mill wants to hit creatures, and The Final Days only counts permanents.
7. **Don't over-splash in Grixis.** The mana works because your interaction is *cheap* — tap land, tap land, Thunder Magic. Expensive interaction plus tap lands is unplayable, and Cornered by Black Mages' double black on turn three is exactly the trap.
8. **Kill the 1/1 hero.** Job Select tokens don't look like much, but a red-white deck that gets three equipment onto the board turns every subsequent creature into a threat you must answer.
9. **Respect 4-chapter saga creatures; you can often ignore 3-chapter ones.** "The four-chapter ones, it's like — wait, you're going to take eight damage off this plus all the other things? That's just not a good strategy."
10. **Don't ritual out a bad card.** Dark Ritual into Shinra Reinforcements puts you down a card and loses the game. Ritual out a high-impact five- or six-drop.
11. **Watch the White Auracite / Ultima anti-synergy.** The Auracite's return is static, not a trigger, so Ultima ending the turn does not stop them getting their permanent back.
12. **Don't play the losing side of Triple Triad math.** You're below 50% to hit two cards because ties don't win, and it does nothing on the turn it lands. It's a red control card, not a value engine.
