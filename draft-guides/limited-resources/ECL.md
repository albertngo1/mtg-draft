## ⚠ Recency rule (read first)

- **The newest episode wins on any conflict.** A later read supersedes an earlier one.
- **Set-review grades (#836 C/UC, #837 R/M) are PREDICTIONS.** Marshall had played zero games; LSV had done roughly ten early-access drafts, which is why his grades are the more reliable half of the pair and why he sometimes overrates what he happened to draft (Goblins — see Supersessions).
- **The Sunset Show (#843, 2026-03-07) is the most authoritative** — settled reads after ~50–60 drafts each, a Pro Tour, a Limited qualifier weekend and multiple sealed win-a-box runs. Supersessions are marked inline.
- **This guide decodes the 17Lands data rather than ranking beneath it.** Nearly every GIH WR in ECL is creature-type-conditional — the same card is a B+ in Elves and a D in Goblins. Use the guide to tell which deck a number came from. Where LR themselves quoted a number on air it is marked as *their* citation, not a measurement.

## Supersessions (newer take wins)

Genuine opinion reversals across the run. Format: **Topic** — early take → late take. Verdict.

- **Goblins (BR) as the strongest archetype** — #836: LSV opened with "black red goblins… this actually has seemed to me like the strongest archetype so far," confidence "high" that Goblins is really good → #838: **"Goblins has not been very good. Like it's been noticeably behind all the rest of it."** #839 (LSV's own on-air 17Lands numbers): Elves ~58% / Kithkin ~57% / Merfolk ~56% / Elementals ~55% / **Goblins ~53%**. #843: "Goblins was not a good deck, but it was a deck you could draft sometimes." LSV diagnosed his own error: he drafted one busted Boggart Mischief deck in early access and played against two more. **Verdict: DOWN a full tier. Draft Goblins only on a loud signal and only if uncontested — Paul Cheon: Elves supports three drafters at a table, Goblins wants to be one.**
- **Elves (BG)** — #836: LSV had it third ("goblins excellent, merfolk excellent, elves good, kithkin good") → #838 & #843: **the standout best deck of the format, not close.** "Its game plan is so robust and its commons are so deep and every rarity level has highlights." Marshall's rare-slot pick-one in the #838 crack-a-pack was an Elves card on lane strength alone. **Verdict: UP to #1. Elves is the deck to fight for.**
- **Rooftop Percher** — #836: graded **D** ("I don't really know why you'd be interested in this card") → #843: **the single biggest miss of the whole set review, and not just for LR.** "Most people were shocked to discover it was actually good. Not 'looked bad but it's playable' — actually quite good." Flying matters more than expected, it's a changeling, and the graveyard-exile clause is live against Elves, the best and second-most-drafted deck. **Verdict: HUGE UP — a real maindeck colorless card in every archetype.**
- **Dawnhand Eulogist** — #836: graded **C in Elves** ("not a C plus cuz it's a four drop"), F/D in Goblins → #843: **the best black common in the set.** "It just really didn't play out that way. It's so much better." Two reasons: the mill-three was integral in a way the review didn't see (Elves simply doesn't function if it isn't milling), and 3-mana 3/3 menace + drain 2 is a lot of card stapled together. **Verdict: HUGE UP — premium Elves common, take it over most uncommons.**
- **Soul Immolation** — #837: graded a hedged **build-around** ("do you always have the thing you need? Is it going to be too much?"), B+ only if you could reliably blight 4 → #843: **Marshall's biggest-groan-test winner of the set.** "Given the heavy creature-based nature of this format and the fact that many of them had a lot of small creatures all the time, it just felt like a one-sided wrath every time. Cards are just absolutely obscene." **Verdict: UP to bomb — first-pickable in any red deck with a three-toughness creature.**
- **Gilt-Leaf's Embrace** — #836: graded **D** ("it just doesn't have a home") → #838: "a little better than I thought, people have been playing it in Elves more than I anticipated" → #843: **LSV's single most underrated card of the set.** "That's the kind of card that frequently is going to get underrated cuz it just looks like a lot of really bad cards." **Verdict: UP — a real combat trick in Elves, and one of the very few tricks worth a slot.**
- **The Eclipsed cycle** (Eclipsed Elf / Merrow / Kithkin / Boggart / Flamekin) — #836: graded **B+ in their own pair, C+/C− outside** → #843: **"they all looked good, but they were A+ great. Like not even in the ballpark of reasonable."** Eclipsed Elf is LR's mythic-uncommon-of-the-set (Elves has the most ways to mill and rebuy it); LSV: "they always bought back the Eclipsed card." Deepchannel Duelist edged it as the highest-win-rate uncommon. **Verdict: UP — first-pickable, and by #838 both hosts were willing to windmill an Eclipsed card pack 1 pick 1 and hang on for the ride.**
- **Kulrath Zealot** — #836: graded **C+** ("it's a good card, I think") → #843: **the best red common in the format**, edging Flamekin Gildweaver. LSV: land cyclers "used to be great, then started to feel more clunky… the payoff is there and this one needed to be pushed a little, and it was." **Verdict: UP.**
- **Safewright Cavalry** — #836: graded **C** ("pretty replaceable"; C+ for what it does but "I'm not going to prioritize it") → #839 & #843: an overperformer. Marshall: "at some point it crossed the threshold from annoying to just good." Threat-of-activation makes blocking it a nightmare, and it rides the best archetype. **Verdict: UP a notch — good, not great, but stop under-drafting it. Caveat (Cheon): Elves is crowded at four mana with Dawnhand Eulogist, so don't take two.**
- **Luminollusk** — #836: graded **B in the five-color vivid deck** ("this is what you want") → #838: "every time I play it, it feels a little out of place" → #843: **Marshall's "still looks good when you read it but doesn't see play" pick — "card kind of sucks."** LSV agreed: "it did not work out well." **Verdict: DOWN hard — playable filler in vivid at best.**
- **Spry and Mighty** — #837: graded **B+ → walked to B/B−** on the targeting-risk discussion → #843: **the most controversial card of the set, and "ultimately the card ended up being not that good."** Setup cost too high; you need a real power gap, not just two creatures. LSV: "the fact that it has a treefolk on it probably should have been a hint." **Verdict: DOWN.**
- **Dream Harvest** — #837: graded **B+** ("if you ever hit multiple cards you're getting your money's worth") → #839: **the worst multicolor rare in the set by win rate — LSV quoted ~50.8% GIH on air, right next to Feisty Spikeling.** Cheon: "it's about as good as a Feisty Spikeling for those of you who think it's a bomb." #843: Marshall's "it's a bad card but I love it anyway" pick. **Verdict: DOWN hard — do not first-pick it.**
- **Hexing Squelcher** — #837: LSV **B−**, Marshall C+ → #839: **a trap.** Cheon: "there are a couple of cards in this format that look really good but are actually mostly traps and I think this is one of them… at the end of the day it's just a two-mana 2/2." Nobody is one-for-one-ing their way through this format. **Verdict: DOWN — don't play it outside Goblins, and barely inside it.**
- **Tam, Mindful First-Year** — #837: graded **B−** ("a two-mana 2/2 that they have to kill or the removal spell turns off") → #839: **same trap category.** Cheon: "everybody just thinks it's a Mother of Runes. It's just not. If your opponent plays a bunch of creatures you have a two-mana 2/2. Do not first-pick it over one of the signpost uncommons." **Verdict: DOWN.**
- **Glen Elendra's Answer** — #837: graded **B−** ("a 1/1 flyer is about the minimum we'd consider a card, but that is a card") → #843: **underrated.** "Countering one thing and making a 1/1 flyer can be really good, and if they ever have a cast-trigger permanent you counter both and make two — you usually just dumpster them." **Verdict: UP.**
- **Shinestriker** — #836: graded a hedged **B in blue-red, B+ in the vivid deck, "maybe B− to B+, the spread is bigger there"** → #843: **"looked a little clunkier than it actually ended up being"**; LSV's favorite card in the set and his self-nominated LSV-est card. **Verdict: UP in any deck that can hit three colors.**
- **Mischievous Sneakling** — #836: graded **C** ("not the most powerful card, but it helps make things work") → #838: **"I find myself taking that card aggressively. I don't think I've cut the card yet."** A hybrid changeling that fits every deck, hedges an uncommitted early draft, and quietly pumps your vivid count. **Verdict: UP as a draft hedge — still a C-level card in the deck, but a much better pick than a C.**
- **Thoughtweft Imbuer / Gravelgill Scoundrel / Omni-Changeling / Wildvine Pummeler** — all graded **C+/B/B−/C** in #836 → #838: all four called out as overperformers. Imbuer: "whenever it's on the other side of the board I feel like I have to kill it." Scoundrel: "just makes all your stuff work" and taps for convoke. Omni-Changeling: "this is one of the really good clones, don't sleep on it." Wildvine Pummeler: reach + trample "just hits right." **Verdict: UP a half-grade each.**
- **Chaos Spewer / Boggart Prankster / Auntie's Sentence / Elder Auntie** — graded **C / C / C / C+ trending B−** in #836 → #838 & #839: all downgraded as collateral damage from the Goblins archetype cratering. Chaos Spewer is "raw stats" in a format that pays for card advantage; Prankster is "just not very good"; Auntie's Sentence is "passable but not exciting." **Verdict: DOWN.**
- **Reaping Willow** — #836 was a rare open split: **Marshall D** ("I can just imagine this thing getting left out in the dark"), **LSV B−** ("I just don't mind waiting on it — that's a big key for this card") → #838: LSV vindicated. "When the card hits the board it's very very good, and the mana is worth spending a little effort to make happen." You need to be heavy in one of the two colors, not literally black-white. **Verdict: LSV was right — treat as B−.**
- **Glister Bairn** — #836: **Marshall D**, LSV B−/C+ → #843: Marshall's "I never win with it but it always beats me" card. "Every time somebody played it I'm like, it's probably not correct to play it… but then people would play it against me and I'm just getting smashed. Maybe I should have played it more." **Verdict: unresolved, but stop auto-cutting it in a real vivid deck.**
- **Removal priority as a whole** — #836/#837 evaluated removal on the usual scale → #838 & #839: **remove-first instincts are wrong here.** Marshall: "I've had the experience of routinely cutting removal spells… I cut like four good removal spells as my last four cuts." Cheon: "people are actually having a lot more success with less removal in this format — 18 or 19 creatures with three or four removal spells." Games go long and grindy, one-for-ones don't decide them, and every non-creature-type slot costs you critical mass. **Verdict: shift all removal down roughly a half-grade relative to on-type creatures.**
- **Combat tricks as a whole** — #836 graded several tricks C/C+ → #839: **"one of the big misses in this set is the combat tricks in general. They have not really been pulling their weight. There's just no good one-mana trick."** Only Blossoming Defense clears the bar; Gilt-Leaf's Embrace is the runner-up. **Verdict: DOWN across the board — Appeal to Eirdu, Riverguard's Reflexes, Aquitect's Defenses, Reckless Ransacking are non-cards.**
- **The five unsupported color pairs (UB / WB / WR / RG / UG)** — #836 sketched them as real if niche ("fairies might be the best of the off-brands") → #843: **"if you combine all the percentage share of blue-black, black-white, red-white, blue-green, red-green, that adds up to like eight percent of a deck."** Marshall: "there's not enough meat on the bone to support even one drafter in any of those sub-archetypes with any consistency." Both hosts now say seeding those cards was a design mistake — a trap that leads new drafters down a road with nothing at the end. **Verdict: DON'T. Take the off-brand cards only as raw-rate playables in a supported deck.**

## Format speed / meta read

- **LSV's one-sentence summary of ECL: "Find the open lane."** Marshall's: **"They aimed kind of low and hit the target."** Five creature-type archetypes, one unwritten sixth (vivid), and effectively nothing else.
- **The format is MID-RANGE and grindy, not fast** — this was the biggest structural surprise. Marshall expected Bloomburrow speed from Kithkin/Goblins/Merfolk and got the opposite: "the games play out more grindy and mid-range rather than just turning things sideways." LSV: "I've had more board stalls and longer games where a random flyer is good, or a way to break through is good, than in most formats." **Consequence: flyers and evasion are premium; card-advantage engines beat raw stats.**
- **But you still cannot durdle.** "You do feel bad when you don't play a card on turn two, and especially if you play against Kithkin or Merfolk they can really punish you for that." Every deck is a creature deck; playing zero board presence loses on the spot.
- **Creature-type DENSITY is the master knob.** You want roughly 16–18 of your type. LSV: "in an elf deck you want 16 or 17 elves — that leaves you six or seven slots, and you just want all of those to be removal." Cards that aren't your type must clear an uncommon power bar to earn a slot.
- **Gathering Stone is the format's litmus test.** LSV: "If your goblin, elf, merfolk or kithkin deck doesn't want Gathering Stone, you probably didn't draft a great version of it." Marshall in #838: "if you can put Gathering Stone in your deck and feel good about it, even if your overall card quality isn't the highest, you're probably okay."
- **Stake your lane in the first few picks, and take the hooks, not the hedges.** LSV: "I'm not looking for flexibility, I'm looking for payoffs. I think you're supposed to try in the first couple of picks to stake your lane." Concretely: take Moon-Vigil Adherents over a Gravelgill Scoundrel even after a Merrow Skyswimmer first pick, because only one of them *makes* you draft a deck. The counter-position (Sir Kibblez, guest on a non-ECL episode) is to burn picks staying open until you know which lane is open; #843 concludes both are reasonable and neither is fun.
- **The format is unforgiving about pivots.** "You really can't wait till pack two to decide what color pair you are." But bailing is sometimes mandatory, and bailing means abandoning what you've drafted. LSV called the day-two qualifier draft where he stayed in Elementals instead of pivoting to Goblins his own biggest miss: "it was skill testing, but in a way I don't find fun."
- **This is a PAUPER set, emphatically.** #843: "very very pauper. Many of the cards I'd want, you start talking about Eclipsed cards right away. The uncommons were super important in this set." The Eclipsed cycle and the signpost uncommons carry more weight than the rare slot.
- **Removal is deprioritized and card advantage is upgraded.** See Supersessions. The winning cards have inherent two-for-ones (Merrow Skyswimmer, Dawnhand Eulogist, Graveshifter, the command cycle), not raw stats (Chaos Spewer, Brambleback Brute).
- **Convoke is a real engine, not a rider.** Marshall: "I had a board state with three lands and six or seven creatures. I was humming right along, and it's because of convoke." Merfolk and Kithkin both convert wide boards into free mana; convoke card draw with a one-drop is "dumb mode."
- **Mechanics scorecard (#843):** Vivid ≈ **a win**, "really cool, I'd like to see that back." Convoke ≈ **a win** ("can we just have convoke forever"). Changeling ≈ essential glue, pulls more weight than it reads. Blight / −1/−1 counters ≈ **the weak one** — "plus-one counters are just a better game piece"; getting Goblins even into the ballpark while shrinking your own board was an achievement. Behold ≈ fine. Evoke ≈ only on the rare Incarnation cycle, barely present. **Marshall's final grade for the set: C.** LSV was somewhat higher on it than he started.
- **Sealed is worse than draft here.** Cheon: pools are spikier because you need multiple good cards of the *same* kindred type; bombs are type-gated (you can't splash Champions of the Perfect without elves), so a pool with one of each Eclipsed card is a blank. Marshall and Cheon both won boxes with clean, streamlined on-type decks and flamed out 0-2 with 5-color-looking pools.

## Archetypes

Ranked by the settled #843 read, cross-checked against the 17Lands color-pair win rates LSV quoted on air in #839 (shown where given).

### Golgari (BG) Elves — Graveyard Grind — BEST DECK / #1
- **Plan:** A grindy, control-leaning creature deck that uses the graveyard as a resource. Self-mill fills the yard, the yard powers the payoffs, the payoffs rebuy the creatures. **~58% (LSV's #839 citation) — best pair, and the second-most-drafted.**
- **Why it wins:** "Its game plan is so robust and its commons are so deep and every rarity level has highlights. The commons are amazing, the uncommons are amazing, the rares are insane, the mythics are great." It has a beatdown gear *and* a control gear and doesn't run out of cards.
- **Enablers (take these highly):** Scarblade Scout (2-mana 2/2 lifelink, mill 2), Dawnhand Eulogist (the best black common — 3/3 menace, mill 3, drain 2), Lys Alana Informant (surveil on entry AND death, and it's an elf in the yard), Midnight Tilling (mill 4, return a permanent — the setup spell), Eclipsed Elf.
- **Payoffs:** Morcant's Loyalist ("every time that card hits the board I feel like I'm going to lose" — lord plus a death trigger that rebuys an elf; two of them is busted), Morcant's Eyes (sac for X 2/2 elves = X elves in the yard — a real win condition, people lose to it for six or seven bodies), Moon-Vigil Adherents (XX where X counts creatures in play *and* in the graveyard — "it's never been smaller than a 5/5"), Lys Alana Dignitary, Gloom Ripper (**the highest-win-rate card in the set**), Bloodline Bidding (mass reanimate with convoke — "some of the most ludicrous turnarounds I've seen"), Champions of the Perfect, Trystan's Command, High Perfect Morcant.
- **Curve note (Cheon):** Elves is crowded at four mana — Dawnhand Eulogist and Safewright Cavalry are both premium four-drop commons, so prioritize the twos (Scarblade Scout, Midnight Tilling, Lys Alana Dignitary) over the second four-drop.
- **Weakness:** It's the most contested lane. It can support ~3 drafters at a table, but you want to be first. And when the graveyard is empty, nothing works: "when you get a couple cards in the graveyard it feels like all your cards work. When you don't, you definitely feel that too."

### Selesnya (GW) Kithkin — Go-Wide Beatdown — STRONG / #2
- **Plan:** The actual beatdown deck of the format. Curve out, go wide, use ETB counts to grow things, use convoke and pump to break through. **~57% (#839).**
- **Key cards:** Eclipsed Kithkin (two mana — the cheapest of the cycle and the most splashable), Reluctant Dounguard, Crossroads Watcher, Bristlebane Outrider (plays like a 5/5 for four that can't be chumped), Bristlebane Battler (2-mana 6/6 that peels a counter off every creature you play), Thoughtweft Imbuer (mass exalted — "whenever it's on the other side I feel like I have to kill it"), Gallant Fowlknight, Kinsbaile Aspirant, Goldmeadow Nomad, Clachan Festival, Surly Farrier, Kinbinding, Brigid Clachan's Heart, Champion of the Clachan, Figure of Fable, Brigid's Command.
- **The card that beats you:** **Hovel Hurler out of Kithkin.** LSV's "I never win with it but it always beats me" pick: "I stabilize against a fast Kithkin draw, think okay now it's my time to shine, and then they play Hovel Hurler, throw a thing at me, I take five, and I'm dead."
- **Strength:** The only deck that reliably punishes a stumbled opening or a durdly vivid draw. Low curve, low removal, high Kithkin count is the winning build — Marshall lost a sealed final to exactly that shape.
- **Weakness:** Runs out of gas against Elves' recursion if the race doesn't finish. Doesn't want Midnight Tilling, Great Forest Druid, or anything that isn't a body.

### Azorius (WU) Merfolk — Tap / Convoke — STRONG / #3, most-drafted
- **Plan:** Cheap white/blue merfolk with "whenever this becomes tapped" triggers, plus tappers and convoke to trigger them at instant speed. **~56% (#839); the most-drafted deck in the format.** LSV's favorite non-vivid deck; Marshall preferred it too "because it's the one that's just down enough where you can still get it."
- **Key commons:** Merrow Skyswimmer (**the format's premium common** — 2/2 flier + a 1/1 token, taps merfolk, convokes, and re-fuels convoke; both hosts took it over everything in the #838 crack-a-packs), Gravelgill Scoundrel (unblockable on demand, taps a merfolk for value, and it's a two-drop that "taps for mana" via convoke), Tributary Vaulter, Wanderbrine Preacher (LSV's best white common), Temporal Cleansing (best blue common), Silvergill Peddler, Unexpected Assistance, Liminal Hold.
- **Key uncommons:** Deepchannel Duelist (**highest-win-rate uncommon in the set** — lord plus an end-step untap), Eclipsed Merrow, Wanderbrine Trapper (one-mana 2/1 that locks down a creature and enables everything), Silvergill Mentor, Pestered Wellguard, Encumbered Reejerey, Meanders Guide, Wanderwine Farewell, Lofty Dreams, Omni-Changeling, Springleaf Drum.
- **Key rares:** Champions of the Shoal, Disruptor of Currents (Marshall's Marshall-est card — a flash Man-o'-War with convoke, "gnarly in the middle of combat"), Adept Watershaper, Sygg Wanderwine Wisdom, Sygg's Command, Deepway Navigator, Curious Colossus (Merfolk draws enough cards to actually get to seven mana).
- **Strength:** Flexible — heavy white or heavy blue both work, it can beat down or grind, and it shares white cards with Kithkin so an early white pick keeps two lanes open.
- **Weakness:** It's the most-drafted deck, so it's contested; and its individual commons are unimpressive on rate (best white common ≈ a 2-mana 2/2 that gains 2 on tap).

### Izzet (UR) Elementals + the Vivid deck — ABOVE AVERAGE / #4
- **Plan:** Two overlapping decks. **(a) Straight UR Elementals:** mana-value-4-or-greater payoffs, ramp into expensive elementals, value ETBs. **(b) Vivid / 5-color:** count colors among permanents, cash them in for cards, life and damage. **~55% (#839).**
- **What actually happens:** LSV: "Elementals is frequently going to be blue-red, but also fairly frequently splashing or playing other colors for vivid stuff." #843: two-color vivid (LSV had a working black-white version) and five-color vivid are both real, leaning on colorless fixers and hybrid cards for color count.
- **Key cards:** Flamebraider ("if you let your opponent untap with that thing it gets gnarly really quick" — LSV's most-missed card), Kulrath Zealot (**best red common**), Flamekin Gildweaver, Shinestriker, Rime Chill, Tanufel Rimespeaker, Stratosoarer, Summit Sentinel, Eclipsed Flamekin, Twinflame Travelers, Explosive Prodigy, Squawkroaster, Prismabasher, Wildvine Pummeler, Shimmercreep, Kithkeeper, Pummeler for Hire, Shimmerwilds Growth, Great Forest Druid, Foraging Wickermaw, Puca's Eye, Changeling Wayfinder, Ashling Rekindled, Sanar Innovative First-Year, Sunderflock, Ashling's Command (**joint highest win rate in the set**).
- **Strength:** The only deck with a different feel; the vivid payoffs are genuinely powerful and mostly uncontested.
- **Weakness (this is why it's #4, per #843):** "The other archetypes got some of their absolute best cards for literally free mana — it didn't matter as long as they had the number of lands — and you were fighting your mana a bit. That just means your win percentage is tough." Marshall goes further: he doesn't think the vivid deck was one you could draft and expect to win consistently. Elemental two-drops are weak (Flame-Chain Mauler, Summit Sentinel), and a rock like Firdoch Core in your deck is a sign the deck failed.

### Rakdos (BR) Goblins — Blight / Chip Damage — WEAKEST OF THE FIVE / #5
- **Plan:** Not an aggro deck by the end — a **mid-range drain-you-out deck.** Go wide on tokens, blight your own creatures for value, chip in with drain triggers. **~53% (#839) — below the average of all ten pairs, which is the real indictment.**
- **Why it's behind:** "The chip damage doesn't add up quickly enough to close out a game," so it gives every other deck time to do its thing. And blight is intrinsically taxing — "any card that asks you to sacrifice stuff you've invested mana and time into has to pay you back in a major way."
- **When to draft it:** Only uncontested, and only with the uncommon payoffs. Cheon: "if you move into this archetype without a lot of those signpost uncommons and try to put together a goblins deck with just a bunch of commons, you're going to be in trouble."
- **Payoffs that make it work:** Boggart Mischief (LSV: "if you have two of these you're going to lose" — the reason to draft the deck), Sting-Slinger, Lasting Tarfire, Boggart Cursecrafter, Sourbread Auntie, Gathering Stone, Grub Storied Matriarch + Grub's Command (the recursion loop — Marshall won a sealed box off exactly this), Champion of the Weird, Taster of Wares, Scuzzback Scrounger, Bloodline Bidding.
- **Good commons:** Cinder Strike, Gristle Glutton, Bile-Vial Boggart, Heirloom Auntie, Bogslither's Embrace, Retched Wretch.
- **Weakness:** Depth. Elves can feed three drafters; Goblins wants exactly one. The commons are the flattest in the set (Chaos Spewer, Boggart Prankster, Boneclub Berserker, Burning Curiosity all fell off).

### Dimir (UB) Fairies — the best of the unsupported pairs — NICHE
- **Reality:** Not a supported pair — no gold uncommon cycle, no Eclipsed card — but the closest thing to a sixth deck. It's a **heavy-blue control deck** that plays instants on the opponent's turn.
- **Plan:** Fairy payoffs that trigger on casting during the opponent's turn, plus black removal and counters, plus whatever merfolk payoffs your blue base supports.
- **Key cards:** Voracious Tome-Skimmer, Nightmare Sower, Unwelcome Sprite, Glamer Gifter, Glamermite, Dream Seizer, Wild Unraveling (LSV's line: "turn five play Tome-Skimmer, pass with two blue up, counter their spell, put two counters on your own thing, draw a card"), Bitterbloom Bearer, Mistbind Clique, Deceit, Dream Harvest, Sygg Wanderwine Wisdom.
- **Why you rarely draft it:** "It's driven by uncommons, which means you won't draft it as often — the decks driven by commons you get frequently. Fairies could be open, but you're not seeing those uncommons, so it's not really open, it's just not there."
- **Verdict:** Both hosts wish it were supported; neither recommends forcing it. Marshall's "bad card I love anyway" was Voracious Tome-Skimmer, "and it's less that it's a bad card and more that you don't get to be blue-black very often."

### Orzhov (WB) Treefolk, Boros (WR) Giants, Gruul (RG), Simic (UG) — DON'T
- **#843 verdict:** all five unsupported pairs combined account for **roughly 8% of decks drafted.** Marshall: "there's not enough meat on the bone to support even one drafter in any of those sub-archetypes with any type of consistency at all."
- **The trap:** the set seeds appealing off-brand cards (Reaping Willow, Hovel Hurler, Glister Bairn, Moonlit Lamenter, Voracious Tome-Skimmer) with no archetype behind them. Both hosts now call that a design mistake. "It felt like a tease — I wanted to go down those roads, but there really wasn't anything at the end of it."
- **When it's OK:** at a Pro Tour table where everyone is scrapping for the same five lanes, and when a specific pair of strong cards actually tables (LSV: "you have some black cards and some red cards, then you table a Reaping Willow and get a really late Moonlit Lamenter — that's the start of something"). A GW go-big non-Kithkin deck did 3-0 at the PT. Simic exists only as the base of the vivid deck.
- **Otherwise:** take the off-brand cards purely on raw rate for a supported deck. Never as a plan.

## Card tips

Grades below are LR's **release-week set-review predictions** (#836 commons/uncommons, #837 rares/mythics + bonus sheet) on an A+ → F scale with `sideboard` and `build-around` subgrades. Split grades are shown as Marshall/LSV. The late verdict (#838 / #839 / #843) wins on conflict and is flagged. Where LR gave different grades per archetype, both are kept — that split *is* the information in this set.

### White (W)

- **Ajani, Outland Chaperone** — A (#837 release). 1WW planeswalker: +1 makes a 1/1 Kithkin, −2 deals 4 to a tapped creature, −8 is a life-total-sized Genesis Wave for 3-drops. "The plus one protects itself and adds to the board, the minus kills something, and it's a three-mana planeswalker." Groan-test winner.
- **Rhys, the Evermore** — B+/A− (#837 release). 2-mana 2/2 flash that gives a creature persist, plus a sorcery-speed counter-remover. "It's mostly just a really good trick — you get to save a creature. That's a pretty good deal." Removes any number of counters, so it's a hard answer to blight.
- **Curious Colossus** — A/A− (#837 release). 7-mana 7/7; every creature the target opponent controls becomes a vanilla 1/1 coward. "Seven is the sweet spot — at eight you have to contort your deck." Merfolk gets there fine because it draws cards.
- **Champion of the Clachan** — A− / build-around A in Kithkin (#837 release). 4-mana 4/5 flash, behold-and-exile a Kithkin, other Kithkin get +1/+1. Also answers lockdown enchantments (behold the enchanted creature). Uncastable outside Kithkin — do not splash it.
- **Slumbering Walker** — A− (#837 release). 3WW 4/7 with two −1/−1 counters; each end step remove one to reanimate a power-2-or-less creature. "You play this, you immediately get a card back, and you have a 3/6." Only knock: giant warrior, off-type.
- **Eirdu, Carrier of Dawn** — A− (#837 release). 5-mana 5/5 flying lifelink; your creature spells have convoke, flips to grant persist. "90% of the card is 5/5 flying lifelink, and that's a good card."
- **Adept Watershaper** — B+ (#837 release). 3-mana 3/4 merfolk; other *tapped* creatures you control have indestructible — perfect with a tapper or an attack step.
- **Brigid, Clachan's Heart** — B+ (#837 release). 3-mana 3/2 that spits a Kithkin every time it flips, and taps for X mana on the back face. Playable outside Kithkin as a 3/2 + 1/1. **Late:** Marshall lost a sealed final to an unanswered Brigid flipping every turn.
- **Kinbinding** — B+ (#837 release). 3WW enchantment: your team gets +X/+X where X = creatures that entered this turn, plus a free 1/1 Kithkin at combat. "A card that presents a puzzle and then also solves the puzzle by itself."
- **Wanderbrine Trapper** — B+ (#836 release). W 2/1; tap it plus another creature to tap an opposing creature. "As good as it gets for Merfolk — bashes early, locks down late, enables your merfolk stuff, and it's good with convoke."
- **Kinscaer Sentry** — B (#837 release; caption "Kinsbaile Century"). 1W 2/2 first strike lifelink that can cheat an attacker in. "I was sold at two-mana 2/2 first strike lifelink."
- **Flock Impostor** — B (#836 release). 3-mana 2/2 changeling with flash AND flying that bounces one of your own creatures. "The flying is really the kicker — it's always a relevant game piece."
- **Meanders Guide** — B (#836 release). 3-mana 3/2 merfolk; attack + tap another merfolk to reanimate a 3-drop. Two toughness is deliberate — usually a one-shot. Much worse in Kithkin (needs a merfolk).
- **Protective Response** — B (#836 release). 2W instant with convoke: destroy an attacking or blocking creature. "Zero-mana removal." Slightly better in Merfolk than Kithkin.
- **Pyrrhic Strike** — B (#836 release). 2W instant, optional blight 2 to get both modes: artifact/enchantment removal and destroy a 3+ MV creature. "Straight-up three-mana kill a 3-drop would be a B−."
- **Spiral into Solitude** — B (#836 release). 1W aura pacifism that later exiles for 1W + blight 1. "A pacifism that lets you get the thing off the board so it doesn't count for their creature type."
- **Clachan Festival** — B in Kithkin / C+ in Merfolk (#836 release). 2W kindred enchantment Kithkin: two 1/1 Kithkin on ETB, then 4W for more. "That's just a lot of material for one card."
- **Encumbered Reejerey** — B in Merfolk / B− in Kithkin (#836 release). 1W 5/4 with three −1/−1 counters that peel off whenever it becomes tapped. "In a merfolk deck there are so many ways to tap it that aren't attacking — it's pretty much a free roll."
- **Personify** — B− (Marshall) / B trending B+ (LSV) (#836 release). 1W instant blink your own creature + make a 1/1 changeling token. **Late (#838): DOWN — "I've had a hard time wanting to fit that in" over removal and creatures.**
- **Evershrike's Gift** — B− in Kithkin (#836 release). W aura, +1/+0 and flying, rebuys from the yard for 1W + blight 2. "Griffin's Boon — very good in aggressive decks." Don't play it in Merfolk. Rebuying twice eats your board.
- **Kinsbaile Aspirant** — B− in Kithkin (#836 release). W 2/1, behold a Kithkin or pay 2; grows on every creature ETB. "Very close to a one-mana 3/2 in your opening hand."
- **Kithkeeper** — B in the 5-color vivid deck (#836 release). 7-mana 3/3 elemental, vivid: makes X 1/1 Kithkin, then tap three creatures for +3/+0 and flying. "I have lost to this card already." Not a Kithkin or Merfolk card despite the name.
- **Liminal Hold** — ungraded, positive (#836 release). 3W enchantment O-Ring plus 2 life. "A nice little O-Ring with upside — a classic beater that pulls you to the colors." **Late: one of the two best white commons.**
- **Wanderbrine Preacher** — C in Merfolk / C− in Kithkin (#836 release). 1W 2/2 merfolk, gain 2 whenever it becomes tapped. **Late (#843): LSV named it the best white common** — "it does kind of show that it's not really the most impressive [color]."
- **Keep Out** — C+ (#836 release). 1W instant: 4 damage to a *tapped* creature, or destroy an enchantment. Kills attackers and kills merfolk that tap for value. "A pretty classic best-of-one card design."
- **Goldmeadow Nomad** — C+ (#836 release), C in Merfolk. W 1/2 Kithkin that exiles from the yard for a 1/1 Kithkin token — effectively two Kithkin ETBs. LSV tabbed it as a possible best-white-common. **Late (#839): DOWN — Cheon: "the body is just not quite good enough, there are too many lifelinkers to brick-wall it." Marshall only plays it in convoke-heavy Merfolk as a pseudo-Llanowar Elf.**
- **Moonlit Lamenter** — C+ (#836 release). 2W 2/5 treefolk with a −1/−1 counter; 1W to cash the counter for a card. "Looks like a B at face value, but it doesn't cleanly fit any deck." Genuinely strong if you can re-blight it.
- **Thoughtweft Imbuer** — C+ (#836 release). 3W 0/5 Kithkin: your lone attacker gets +X/+X where X = Kithkin you control. **Late (#838): UP — "whenever it's on the other side of the board I feel like I have to kill it, and whenever I have it, they have to kill it or they're going to lose pretty quickly." Five toughness is hard to kill.**
- **Crib Swap** — C+ in Merfolk / C− to D+ in Kithkin (#836 release). 2W kindred instant, exile a creature, they get a 1/1 changeling. "We've stress-tested these to the point that they fail." Kithkin especially hates giving a ground blocker.
- **Burdened Stoneback** — C (#836 release). 1W 4/4 giant with two −1/−1 counters; 1W + remove a counter grants indestructible at sorcery speed. "It's a two-mana 2/2 with abilities — you'll play that most of the time." Off-type.
- **Gallant Fowlknight** — C, maybe C+ in Kithkin / D in Merfolk (#836 release). 3W 3/4 Kithkin, mini-overrun with first strike on ETB. **Late (#838): fine but never an early pick — "I don't want to take one of the role-filling cards early."**
- **Reluctant Dounguard** — C (#836 release). 2W 4/4 Kithkin, two −1/−1 counters that peel off on your creature ETBs. "It just doesn't ask that much of you." Don't play in Merfolk.
- **Timid Shieldbearer** — C (#836 release). 1W 2/2 Kithkin with a 4W team pump. "You'll always play it, it'll be fine."
- **Tributary Vaulter** — C in Merfolk (#836 release). 2W 1/3 flier; on tap, pump another merfolk +2/+0. "The three toughness is key." Nearly unplayable in Kithkin — it only targets merfolk.
- **Sun-Dappled Celebrant** — C (#836 release). 4WW 5/6 vigilance treefolk with convoke. **Late (#838): DOWN — "I felt fairly disappointed by it, and most of the times my opponents played it, it wasn't too impressive." There's always a common merfolk you'd rather have.**
- **Appeal to Eirdu** — C, "generous C" (#836 release). 3W instant with convoke, +2/+1 to one or two creatures. **Late (#839): DOWN — Cheon: "Appeal to Eirdu is not a very good card." Hard to cast on offense when your creatures are tapped.**
- **Shore Lurker** — C− (#836 release). 3W 3/3 flier merfolk, surveil 1. "Bad merfolk filler. I wouldn't go out of my way to take it."
- **Riverguard's Reflexes** — C− (Marshall) / D (LSV) (#836 release). 1W instant, +2/+2 and first strike and untap. "You just don't have room, especially with convoke."
- **Kinsbaile Cavalier** — C (#837 bonus sheet). 3W 2/2 that gives *knights* double strike. "There are a decent amount of Kithkin knights — I wouldn't be surprised if it was okay in the right deck. Still feels C-ish."
- **Bark of Doran** — D (Marshall) / F (LSV) (#836 release). 1W equipment, +0/+1 and assigns damage by toughness. LSV: "it's an F in the sense that I don't think I'm ever going to put this in my deck." **Late (#839): Cheon — "if you draft this entire format and never take this card, it'll probably be better for you overall." #843: Marshall's most annoying card to lose to.**
- **Winnowing** — F (Marshall) / low (LSV) (#837 release). 4WW convoke sorcery: each player keeps one creature type, sacrifices the rest. "The better your opponent's deck is, the less good this is against them" — good decks are linear. Possibly fine in sealed.
- **Morningtide's Light** — avoid (#837 release, no letter given). 3W mythic sorcery: exile any number of creatures, return them tapped at end step, prevent all damage to you until your next turn. "This doesn't do anything." Sorcery speed kills the fog application.
- **Idyllic Tutor** — unplayable (#837 bonus sheet). "I could not imagine playing that in this format."

### Blue (U)

- **Loch Mare** — A− → A (#837 release). 1U 4/5 with three −1/−1 counters; 1U + counter = draw a card, 2U + two counters = tap and stun. "It's a two-mana 1/2, then draw, now it's a 2/3, draw, now it's a 3/4… this card's good at any stage of the game." Prefer turn 4 over turn 2 if you have a play.
- **Champions of the Shoal** — A in Merfolk (#837 release). 3U 4/6, behold-and-exile a merfolk; tap/stun a creature on ETB *and* every time it becomes tapped. "You play this, lock down their biggest thing, and next turn attack and do it again." Needs only ~8–9 merfolk counting changelings.
- **Sunderflock** — A in Elementals (#837 release). 7UU 5/5 flier, costs X less where X = biggest elemental MV you control; bounces all non-elemental creatures. "It's trivial to cast this for five mana. Not a difficult card to get to work."
- **Disruptor of Currents** — A− (#837 release). 3UU 3/3 with flash AND convoke, bounce a non-land permanent. "This card will be the last spell cast in a lot of games." Marshall's self-named Marshall-est card.
- **Glen Elendra Guardian** — B+ (#837 release). 2U 3/4 flash flier with a −1/−1 counter; 1U + counter to counter a noncreature spell (they draw). "It's sitting there threatening to stop your opponent from playing a good spell." Also LSV's top ECL cube pick-up.
- **Oko, Lorwyn Liege** — B+ *if you have consistent green mana* (#837 release). 2U planeswalker; +2 grants all creature types, +1 shrinks a creature, flips to a green side whose −3 makes two 3/3 Elk. "If you can't flip this card, the abilities are not that great."
- **Sygg, Wanderwine Wisdom** — B / B+ in blue-white (#837 release). 1U 2/2 unblockable merfolk that grants a draw-on-damage trigger; back face grants protection from each color. "Two-mana 2/2 unblockable is the absolute floor, and that's already a B."
- **Rimekin Recluse** — B+ (#836 release). 2U 3/2 elemental, bounce any creature — including your own. "Excellent card." Doubles with Twinflame Travelers.
- **Silvergill Mentor** — B+ (#836 release). 1U 2/1, behold a merfolk or pay 2; makes a 1/1 merfolk. "The nuts in merfolk — you will take as many as you can get."
- **Omni-Changeling** — B+ (#836 release). 3UU 0/0 changeling clone with convoke, copies either side of the board. **Late (#838): UP — "this card is incredible. Clones vary set to set; this is one of the really good ones."**
- **Wanderwine Farewell** — B+ in Merfolk / C–D in Elementals (#836 release). 5UU kindred sorcery with convoke: bounce one or two permanents, make a 1/1 merfolk for each. "A bomb in Merfolk — this is a card that would pull me into merfolk if I was on the fence."
- **Blossombind** — B (#836 release). 1U aura: enchanted creature enters tapped, can't untap, and **can't have counters put on it** — which hard-locks Gutsplitter Gang (it can't pay its blight-2 upkeep and just loses 3 a turn). "A really efficient good removal spell."
- **Glamer Gifter** — B (#836 release). 1U 1/2 flash flier; makes a creature a base 4/4 with all types until end of turn. "This is what I want out of a combat trick, because it's also a creature."
- **Pestered Wellguard** — B (#836 release). 3U 3/2 merfolk that makes a 1/1 flying fairy every time it becomes tapped. "Cast it, immediately tap it for one of your other effects, and if they don't kill it they're going to lose really fast."
- **Swat Away** — B (#836 release). 2UU instant, costs 2 less if a creature is attacking you: put a spell OR a creature on top or bottom of library. "Sometimes it's a counterspell when you really need it." **Late (#838): "totally fine card," but don't take it early given how deprioritized removal is.**
- **Lofty Dreams** — B in Merfolk / D in Elementals (#836 release). 3UU aura with convoke: draw a card, +2/+2 and flying. "Turn it into a dragon. It can be cheaper than five mana and you get the card back."
- **Shinestriker** — B in blue-red / B+ in the vivid deck (#836 release). 4UU 3/3 flier, vivid: draw cards equal to colors among permanents. "At draw-two it's a B−, at draw-three it's closer to B+." **Late (#843): UP — "looked a little clunkier than it actually ended up being." LSV's favorite card in the set.**
- **Rime Chill** — B− / B+ in the vivid deck (#836 release). 6U instant, vivid: costs 1 less per color among permanents; tap two creatures, stun them, draw a card. "I've cast this for three mana already."
- **Thirst for Identity** — B− (#836 release). 2U instant, draw 3 then discard 2 unless you discard a creature. "These decks are more likely to have 15 or 16 creatures than most." The Elementals deck is the exception.
- **Unwelcome Sprite** — B− (#836 release). 1U 2/1 flier, surveil 2 whenever you cast on the opponent's turn. "For being off-type, this is perfectly fine." A Fairies staple.
- **Tanufel Rimespeaker** — B− (LSV) / C+ (Marshall) (#836 release). 3U 2/4 elemental: draw a card whenever you cast a spell with MV 4+. "If my opponent plays this on turn four and I can't kill it, I'm not going to be happy." Convoke cards count as expensive spells.
- **Glen Elendra's Answer** — B− (#837 release). 2UU uncounterable instant: counter ALL opposing spells and abilities, make a 1/1 flying fairy for each. "At baseline it's a flying Mystic Snake — that's a two-for-one." **Late (#843): UP — underrated. "If they ever have a cast-trigger permanent and they play something, you counter both, make two, and usually just dumpster them."**
- **Flitterwing Nuisance** — B− (#837 release; Marshall C+, LSV B). U 2/2 flier with a −1/−1 counter; 2U + counter grants all your creatures "draw a card on combat damage" this turn. "If you draw two cards off this, you think it's excellent." Note the ability is team-wide — LSV later found it draws 2–3 in a wide board.
- **Mirrorform** — B, speculative (#837 release). 4UU instant: each nonland permanent you control becomes a copy of a target non-aura permanent. "I'm going to draft it at the B level to start with to see." **Late (#843): landed at roughly a coin-flip win rate. It mostly just turns your board into evasive threats and kills them; instant speed makes it a combat trick, and Mirrorforming the Merfolk lord is filthy. Steals games, don't chase it.**
- **Harmonized Crescendo** — build-around B− (#837 release). 4UU instant with convoke: choose a type, draw one per permanent of that type. **Late (#843): "the problem is it's basically just worse than the common version" (Unexpected Assistance). Merfolk-only, and even there it's a hedge.**
- **Temporal Cleansing** — C+ (#836 release). 3U sorcery with convoke: put a nonland permanent second from top or on the bottom. "The double-spell turns are really big." **Late (#843): the best blue common in the set, clearly.**
- **Summit Sentinel** — C+ (#836 release). 1U 1/3 elemental, draw a card when it dies. Cheon told LR he thought the card was bad; LSV kept it at C+. **Late (#839): Cheon confirmed — "sometimes it just stays in play and if you can't cash it in for a card, it's not doing anything." Still the better elemental two-drop over Flame-Chain Mauler.**
- **Unexpected Assistance** — C+ (#836 release). 3UU instant with convoke, draw 3 discard 1. "Merfolk really wants one or maybe two copies — you can really go off." Cheon's warning: he has played "two too many copies" of this more than once.
- **Stratosoarer** — C+ (#836 release), closer to C in two colors. 4U 3/5 flier that grants flying on ETB, with basic landcycling for 1U. "Costed as if it didn't have the landcycling."
- **Noggle the Mind** — C+ (#836 release). 1U flash aura: enchanted creature loses all abilities and becomes a colorless 1/1 Noggle — **it stops being a merfolk/elf/goblin.** "It doesn't kill the creature, which is what I want, but it will deal with the thing." Weakest against convoke decks. **Late (#838): plays better than you'd think, but never take it over real removal early.**
- **Silvergill Peddler** — C, up to C+ (#836 release). 1U 2/3 merfolk, loot whenever it becomes tapped. "Looting's really strong and this doesn't cost mana to do it."
- **Run Away Together** — C (#836 release). 1U instant, bounce one creature from each player. "A card that's base level C but you can work up to a B with enough grit. Bounce your ETB creature they're trying to kill and bounce their token."
- **Glamermite** — C / C− (#836 release). 2U 2/2 flash flier fairy, tap OR untap a creature on ETB. "It'd be a C+ if it were a merfolk." **Late (#838): "a solid card in terms of what it does, but you don't end up putting it in your deck because it's a fairy."**
- **Aquitect's Defenses** — C/C− on power level, "you probably just shouldn't" play it (#836 release). 1U flash aura, +1/+2 and hexproof-on-ETB. **Late (#839): Cheon — "a perfect example of one that's not even close" to the bar for an off-type non-creature slot.**
- **Kulrath Mystic** — C− (#836 release). 2U 2/4 elemental that grows on MV-4+ casts. **Late (#838): "filler, fine, checks the box. I haven't really been looking to play this card very much."**
- **Wanderwine Distracter** — D (#836 release). 3U 4/3 merfolk, −3/−0 to a creature when it becomes tapped. "It's a four-drop. I don't think you prioritize this card."
- **Illusion Spinners** — D, maybe D+ (#836 release). 4U 4/3 flier with hexproof-while-untapped, flash only if you control a fairy. "Hexproof-while-untapped doesn't protect it, because you want to attack with it."
- **Wild Unraveling** — D+/C− (#836 release). UU counterspell, additional cost blight 2 or pay 1. "Probably better in sealed." **Late (#838): UP inside the Fairies control deck specifically — "that card's actually really good with Tome-Skimmer: turn five, play Tome-Skimmer, pass with two blue up, counter their spell, put two counters on your own thing, draw a card and lose a life, and you don't care that your card got shrunk."**
- **Rimefire Torque** — F (#837 release). 1U artifact: charge counters on your type's ETBs, cash three to copy your next instant or sorcery. "It's not an F power-level card, but given the context of the set, playing it at a rate of F would be correct."
- **Spell Snare** — sideboard C, otherwise F (#836 release). U, counter a 2-MV spell. "When it works it's okay, and it often doesn't work. That's not a card I want in my deck."
- **Mistbind Clique** — bomb *with* fairies/changelings (#837 bonus sheet, no letter given). 3U 4/4 flash flier, champion a Faerie, tap all target player's lands. "If you have sufficient fairies or changelings this card is unbelievable — cast it on their upkeep and just make them skip their turn." If you can't champion, you sacrifice it (you keep the effect).
- **Wanderwine Prophets** — F (#837 bonus sheet). 4UU 4/4, champion a merfolk, sac a merfolk on combat damage for an extra turn. "You're down two merfolk in the exchange already."

### Black (B)

- **Gloom Ripper** — A in Elves (#837 release). 3BB 4/4 elf: +X/+0 to yours and −0/−X to theirs, where X = elves in play plus elves in the graveyard. "In Elves that just seems like an A — the two-for-one, and then icing on the cake." **Late (#843): the joint-highest-win-rate card in the entire set.** LSV's dream start was Morcant's Loyalist → Gloom Ripper → Trystan's Command (and he went 1-3 with it — the format punishes speculation even when it hands you the nuts).
- **Bloodline Bidding** — build-around A− (A in Elves, A− in Goblins) (#837 release). 6BB sorcery with convoke: return all creatures of a chosen type from your yard to the battlefield. "The convoke is more to take the edge off of looking down at an eight-mana spell — not to take it from eight to two." **Late (#838): Marshall's "profound impact" rare — "you go Bloodline Bidding, get back eight or ten things, and all these triggers go on the stack."**
- **Dawnhand Dissident** — A− (#837 release). B 1/2 elf: tap+blight 1 to surveil, tap+blight 2 to exile a card, then cast your exiled creatures by removing three counters from among your creatures. "It's a one-mana card that your opponent has to kill. If they don't kill it, you're going to win the game." Needs one three-toughness creature (or two two-toughness) to eat the blight. **Late (#838): Marshall's pack-one-pick-one — "even on a board with five other creatures, you have to kill this one."**
- **Grub, Storied Matriarch** — A− (#837 release). 2B 2/1 menace; raise-dead a goblin every time it enters or flips back; the other face makes attacking token copies of blighted creatures. Marshall had two in his first deck: "completely stupid — an absolutely ridiculous Magic card. The second one always won the game." Loops with Grub's Command.
- **Bitterblossom** — A (#837 bonus sheet). 1B kindred enchantment fairy, the original. "If you just cast Bitterblossom early it's so hard to lose the game — you kill them way before it kills you, and it's an enchantment, one of the hardest things to kill."
- **Boggart Mischief** — B+, "closer to an A" in Goblins (#836 release). 2B kindred enchantment goblin: optional blight 1 for two 1/1 goblins, then drain 1 whenever a goblin token dies. "My first goblins deck had two of these and I just won every game with them. This is a card I would first-pick and stake my claim on." **Late (#843): named the best build-around in the set for Goblins — and, LSV admits, the card that fooled him into thinking Goblins was great.**
- **Bitterbloom Bearer** — B+ (#837 release). BB 1/1 flash flier; each upkeep you *lose 1 life* (not "may") and make a 1/1 flying fairy. "If your opponent plays this on turn two and you can't kill it, you just lose the game. It does become a mediocre draw later." Note the double-black.
- **Champion of the Weird** — B+ in Goblins (#837 release). 3B 5/5, behold-and-exile a goblin; then, for 1 life + blight 2, **your opponent blights 2** at sorcery speed, repeatable and mana-free. "They're just losing their board slowly, and you control when it happens." Goblins champions well because so many goblins have ETBs.
- **Taster of Wares** — B+ (#837 release). 2B 3/2 goblin: opponent reveals X cards (X = goblins you control), you exile one permanently, and if it's an instant/sorcery you can cast it off any mana while this lives. "They never get the card back, so you're always up. Three mana 3/2 exile their worst card would be totally fine."
- **Graveshifter** — B+ (#836 release). 3B 2/2 changeling, raise-dead a creature. "Good in every deck." One of the few genuinely colorless-feeling cards.
- **Unbury** — B (#836 release). 1B instant: return a creature card from your graveyard, or two that share a type. "A two-mana draw-two of good cards. It's called Unbury, but in my experience it really just buries your opponent." Instant speed and cheap enough to main-phase and recast.
- **Nameless Inversion** — B (#836 release). 1B kindred instant changeling: +3/−3 and lose all creature types. "Better than a two-mana deal-3 — stripping creature types can really do something, and it's every creature type itself so the Eclipsed cards can find it."
- **Blight Rot** — B (#836 release; walked from B−). 2B instant, four −1/−1 counters on a creature. "A really good removal spell." Risk: if it doesn't kill, the counters can be removed. **Late (#838): still fine, but "if I see a Merrow Skyswimmer I'm not even looking at the Blight Rot."**
- **Bogslither's Embrace** — B (#836 release). 1B sorcery, blight 1 (or pay 3) to **exile** a creature. "This is much better than the Bone Splinters cards — a normal deck can just put this in and it'd always be great. In Goblins, blighting is an advantage."
- **Heirloom Auntie** — B (#836 release). 2B 4/4 goblin with two −1/−1 counters; surveil 1 and peel a counter whenever another creature you control dies — **and it keeps surveilling even after the counters are gone.** "Close to a three-mana 4/4 that just surveils a bunch. That's a pretty big grade for a common."
- **Iron-Shield Elf** — B in Elves / C+ in Goblins (#836 release). 1B 3/1 elf: discard a card for indestructible + tap. "Extremely strong — every time I drew it, it was great." Attacks poorly into goblin tokens; and you can discard an elf to stock the yard.
- **Moonshadow** — B (#837 release). B 7/7 menace elemental with six −1/−1 counters; peel one whenever a permanent card hits your graveyard from anywhere (dies, milled, or discarded). "You don't need to get it to 7/7 — you just need two things to die and you got your money's worth." Best in Goblins where things die constantly.
- **Twilight Diviner** — B (#837 release). 2B 3/3 elf, surveil 2 on ETB; copies creatures that enter from the graveyard (once per turn). "Mostly this is a three-mana 3/3 surveil-2 elf, which you'll play basically all the time. Don't get too hung up on the ability."
- **Mudbutton Cursetosser** — B− in Goblins (#836 release). B 2/1 that can't block, behold a goblin or pay 2; kills a power-2-or-less creature when it dies. "You're going to cast it for one mana every time. Your opponent leads on this and it's really annoying." **Late (#839): Cheon still takes it — "in a solid goblin deck it's above replacement level" and it combos with Gristle Glutton — but Marshall cooled: "I respect it, but it hasn't really stood out as being very good."**
- **Creakwood Safewright** — B− (#836 release). 1B 5/5 elf with three −1/−1 counters that peel off at end step while an elf is in your graveyard. "Starts as a two-mana 2/2, which isn't even bad. An upside free roll." A great place to dump blight counters — it removes them automatically.

LSV would board it out against a deck with that card.**
- **Retched Wretch** — C+ (#836 release). 2B 4/2 goblin: reverse-persist — it returns if it *had* a counter, minus its abilities. "In a pinch it's a way to blight 2 for free."
- **Requiting Hex** — C+ (#836 release). B instant, optional blight 1: kill a 2-MV-or-less creature, gain 2 if you paid. "You're just always going to play a Shock. The blight part is less about the life and more that the goblins deck actively wants to blight."
- **Scarblade Scout** — C+ in Elves (#836 release). 1B 2/2 lifelink elf, mill 2. "A little life cushion, an elf in the graveyard, and it mills you two." **Late (#839): one of the cheap Elves cards Cheon prioritizes over the crowded four-drop slot.**
- **Bile-Vial Boggart** — C+ in Goblins / C in Elves (#836 release). B 1/1 goblin; a −1/−1 counter on a creature when it dies. "A one-drop that trades up for a two-drop, or you can blight it later and then kill something else." **Late (#839): "if you're not goblins you're just not going to start it; it can be a fine sideboard card against token decks."**

- **Dose of Dawnglow** — build-around B (#836 release). 4B instant: reanimate a creature; blight 2 if it isn't your main phase. "If you have a lot of self-mill in your Elves deck and one or two really good creatures, I'm in. "I think it's a C− just because it doesn't really fit — putting a four-mana creature that's not a goblin or an elf in those decks is a high cost." **Late (#839): DOWN — Cheon: "an underperformer for me personally, this almost never makes my decks."**
- **Moonglove Extractor** — C− (#836 release). 2B 2/1 elf, draw and lose a life whenever it attacks. "You either trade for a token, which was fine but not amazing, or they have bigger creatures and you just cash it in. Not a card I'd go to."
- **Barbed Bloodletter** — C− (#836 release). 1B flash equipment: attaches free, grants wither for the turn, +1/+2, equip 2. "+1/+2 is so much better than +2/+1 — it makes your 2/2 beat a 3/3 and live. Better than a lot of the combat tricks, but on the chopping block if you have ample removal."

- **Blighted Blackthorn** — D (#836 release). 4B 3/7 treefolk: blight 2 on ETB or attack to draw and lose a life. "I played this in Elves and it was fine — I blighted it down to a 0/3 over three turns and it drew me three cards. Not a priority."

- **Mornsong Aria** — F (#837 release). 1BB legendary enchantment: no one draws or gains life; each draw step, lose 3 and **tutor**. "I don't want to be the one to pay three mana for my opponent to just Demonic Tutor whatever they need to win the game."
- **Gnarlbark Elm** — grade garbled in the transcript, but LSV's words were "**this card is incredible**" (#836 release). 2B 3/4 treefolk with two −1/−1 counters; 2B + remove two counters for −2/−2 at sorcery speed. "You just play this in Elves or Goblins and be super happy. It's at its best in Goblins because Goblins keeps re-blighting it." Treat as ~B.

### Red (R)

- **Spinerock Tyrant** — A (#837 release). 3RR 6/6 flying dragon with wither; copies your single-target instants and sorceries (and gives the copies wither). "The vast majority of what you're talking about is that it's a 6/6 flier. Kind of boring in its power level — it's so good you just go, yeah, it's an A."
- **Soul Immolation** — build-around, B+ if you can reliably blight 4 (#837 release). 3RR mythic sorcery: additional cost blight X (X ≤ your highest toughness), deal X to each opponent **and each creature they control** — and you can dump all X counters onto a 1/1. "If you can reliably do it for three you're in great shape; at four it's a game-ender; at two, you don't want to run it." **Late (#843): HUGE UP — Marshall's biggest groan test of the format. "Given the heavy creature-based nature and the fact that many of them had a lot of small creatures, it just felt like a one-sided wrath every time. Absolutely obscene."** Also an argument for firing instant-speed removal on your own turn — you can't Immolate after you've already killed something.
- **Goblin Sharpshooter** — A (#837 bonus sheet). 2R 1/1 that doesn't untap normally but untaps whenever *any* creature dies; tap to ping 1. "Creature combat is impossible with this thing out. It auto-kills all one-toughness, and anything with two toughness just needs one trigger on the stack."
- **Cinder Strike** — B+ (#836 release). R sorcery, optional blight 1: deal 2, or 4 if you blighted. LSV staked a claim: **"I think this is the best common in the set."** "One mana deal four with upside. Every time I've drafted red I've had multiple because I just take it over everything." **Late (#838): still great — "this card goes in every red deck and you're always happy to have it" — though the set's *best* common honors ultimately went elsewhere.**

- **Scuzzback Scrounger** — B+ (#837 release). 1R 3/2 goblin: each first main, optional blight 1 for a Treasure. "A two-mana 3/2 that you can immediately blight to get a Treasure, and that lets you accelerate into something big enough that you can blight *that* and get another one."
- **Goliath Daydreamer** — B+ (#837 release). 2RR 4/4 giant: your instants and sorceries exile with dream counters instead of going to the yard, and on attack you recast one for free. "Cast this on turn four and if they don't kill it, they lose. You just double-cast a removal spell, attack, cast a removal spell again." **Late (#843): "that's a must-kill. They always played it and you had that one-turn window."**
- **End-Blaze Epiphany** — B+ (#837 release). XR instant: X damage to a creature; if it dies, exile cards off *your* library equal to its power and play one until your next turn. "Red X-spells are a little below what we want, but you give me a card and all is forgiven." Careful: the exiled cards don't shuffle back — you can deck yourself late.
- **Sourbread Auntie** — B+ in Goblins / B in Elementals (#836 release). 2RR 4/3 goblin: optional blight 2 for two 1/1 goblins. "This card is just awesome — it puts three goblins into play. The fail case is a 2/1 with two 1/1s, and that can always be it."
- **Goblin Chieftain** — B+ (#837 bonus sheet). 1RR 2/2 haste, other goblins get +1/+1 and haste. "Yes, please. Very solid card."
- **Sting-Slinger** — B in Goblins / C in Elementals (#836 release). 2R 3/3 goblin; 1R + tap + blight 1 to deal 2 to each opponent. "A really dangerous card. Leave it back as a 3/3 blocker so they can't assume it's a 2/2, then finish them at end step." Money in Goblins.
- **Boulder Dash** — B (#836 release). 1R sorcery, Arc Trail. "This card fits really nicely in this format — there are a lot of one-toughness creatures. Merfolk makes tokens, Goblins makes tokens, Kithkin makes tokens, and blight leaves things at 0/1."
- **Explosive Prodigy** — B (#836 release). 1R 1/1 elemental, vivid: deal X to a creature where X = colors among permanents. "Two mana for a 1/1 deal-2 — you'd always play it. In the vivid deck it's a two-mana deal-3 or -4 with a chomper attached." Doubles with Twinflame Travelers.
- **Flamebraider** — B (#836 release). 1R 2/2 elemental bard, taps for **two** mana of any colors, spendable only on elementals. "I lost a game so badly to this — they played it turn two, a five-drop turn three, and six mana of elementals turn four. They just played Sol Ring." **Late (#838/#843): "if you let your opponent untap with that thing it gets gnarly really really quick." LSV's most-missed card.**
- **Sizzling Changeling** — B (#836 release). 2R 3/2 changeling; on death, exile the top card and play it until your next turn. "Excellent card. Good in Goblins, good in Elementals. Just a flat B."
- **Kulrath Zealot** — C+ (#836 release). 5R 6/5 elemental with basic landcycling for 1R; exile-and-play the top card on ETB. "I like cycling and I like landcycling, so having the creatures be actual good creatures is nice. Really good in the five-color deck." **Late (#843): UP — the best red common in the format. "Those have both needed to be pushed a little bit and this one did. It surprised me — I felt like it was a little underrated coming in."**
- **Gristle Glutton** — C+ (#836 release). 1R 1/3 goblin: tap + blight 1 to rummage. "Don't slip on this one. 1/3 is a great stat line — you get the first two blights nearly free, and it lets you block with a 1/1 token and then blight. Actively impressed." **Late (#839): Cheon calls it "probably one of the best commons you want to pick up in these goblin decks," and it combos with Mudbutton Cursetosser.**
- **Tweeze** — C+ (#836 release). 2R instant, 3 damage to any target, optional rummage. "You're not getting a huge mana gain because you're killing things around the three-mana mark, but getting to rummage is nice."
- **Elder Auntie** — C+ in Goblins, trending B− (#836 release). 2R 2/2 goblin that makes a 1/1 goblin. "The kind of card you always want as many copies as you can get." **Late (#839): DOWN — filler, and a casualty of Goblins being weak. Cheon: "this would be awesome as a merfolk because the blue-white deck cares so much about convoking things out. It's at its worst as a goblin."**
- **Squawkroaster** — C+ in the 5-color deck / C in blue-red (#836 release). 3R */4 double strike elemental, vivid: power = colors among permanents. - **Warren Torchmaster** — B− (#836 release). 1R 2/2 goblin: at combat, optional blight 1 to give a creature haste. "If you have the Gutsplitter Gang or Boneclub Berserker, sometimes you just whack them for a ton of damage."

- **Ashling, Rekindled** — B (#837 release). 1R 1/3 legendary elemental: rummage on entry/flip; the blue face adds two mana of one color each main phase, spendable only on MV-4+ spells. - **Feed the Flames** — C (#836 release). 3R instant, 5 damage and exile if it would die. "Roughly an even trade, which means you'll play it. It doesn't jump off the screen. Tweeze trades evenly or up; this trades evenly or down."

- **Flame-Chain Mauler** — C (#836 release). 1R 2/2 elemental, 1R for +1/+0 and menace. "Fine for Elementals, that's about it. - **Giantfall** — C, maybe C− (#836 release). 1R instant: bite, or destroy an artifact. "Instant-speed bite in a color where that's not that good — Goblins can't make use of it and neither can Elementals."
- **Brambleback Brute** — C− (#836 release). 2R 4/5 giant with two −1/−1 counters; 1R + counter to make a creature unblockable. "A three-mana 2/3 that grows as it makes things unable to block is not a weak card. We're in a set where not having the right creature type docks you about half a grade."

### Green (G)

- **Champions of the Perfect** — A (#837 release). 3G 6/6 elf, behold-and-exile an elf; **draw a card whenever you cast any creature spell.** "If there's ever been a must-kill, that's it. It's unbeatable if they don't answer it, and it's a four-mana 6/6." Six toughness needs specific removal.
- **Formidable Speaker** — A− (#837 release). 2G 2/4 elf: discard a card to tutor any creature; also 1 + tap to untap a permanent. "Even without the untap ability I'd be happy — three-mana 2/4, discard your worst card to get your best creature. Discard a land and get your bomb."

- **Sapling Nursery** — build-around B+ (Marshall) / build-around B (LSV) (#837 release). 6GG enchantment with affinity for Forests: landfall makes a 3/4 reach treefolk; 1G exiles it for indestructible. - **Bristlebane Outrider** — B (#836 release). 3G 3/5 Kithkin knight, can't be blocked by power 2 or less, +2/+0 while a creature entered this turn. "It plays a lot like a 5/5 Kithkin for four. Like Gutsplitter Gang — the Kithkin are all 1/1s and 2/2s and then suddenly you have a 5/5."

- **Selfless Safewright** — B (#837 release; Marshall started at C+). 3GG 4/2 elf with flash and convoke: your chosen type gains hexproof and indestructible until end of turn. "Convoke it out and block. It's two-for-one, three-for-one, four-for-one potential."
- **Trystan, Callous Cultivator** — B/B+ (#837 release). 2G 3/4 deathtouch legendary elf: mill 3 and gain 2 on entry, mill 3 and drain 2 on the flip. "A 3/4 deathtouch for three, so we're not complaining, but this one seems really slow — it's not a bomb like the others in this cycle." Note you can deck yourself by flipping it forever.
- **Chomping Changeling** — B− (#836 release). 2G 1/2 changeling, destroy an artifact or enchantment on ETB. "I'm never cutting this out of my deck. It's hard to overstate how good just changeling is in these tribal decks."
- **Assert Perfection** — B− (LSV) / C+ (Marshall) (#836 release). 1G sorcery: +1/+0 then bite. "You trade instant speed for a point of power, and that can be the difference between killing the thing or not." **Late (#843): the best green common in the set. "Whenever a bite spell starts with a little stat boost, they do tend to do pretty well — it increases the range of what they can hit by so much." Not an elf, but a card that goes really well in Elves.**
- **Devoted Druid** — B (#837 bonus sheet). 1G 0/2 elf, tap for G, put a −1/−1 counter on it to untap. "You get to tap it twice for mana in a turn even if you don't mess with it."
- **Regal Force** — build-around B (#837 bonus sheet). 4GGG 5/5 elemental, draw a card per green creature you control. "You've got to do a little work to make sure you can cast it — but an Elves deck with some removal, some elves and a couple ramp cards just casts it and draws four."
- **Morcant's Eyes** — build-around B (#836 release). 1G kindred enchantment elf: surveil each upkeep; 4GG + sac to make X 2/2 elves where X = elf cards in your graveyard. "Midnight Tilling a couple of times and this makes six elves. I had it make seven against me." **Late (#838): UP — "I've lost many, many games where people paid the six, sacrificed the Eyes and got six elves and you're just like, well, I can't beat that now."**
- **Shimmerwilds Growth** — B in the 5-color deck / C+ in Elves (#836 release). 1G land aura: the land becomes a chosen color **and taps for an extra mana of it.** "Why does it make the land the color? For vivid. It's so sick." Ups your vivid count, ramps you, fixes your mana, and it's hard to remove.
- **Luminollusk** — B in the 5-color deck (#836 release). 3G 2/4 deathtouch elemental, vivid: gain life equal to colors. "It's a good blocker, it gains you life — this is what you want." **Late (#843): DOWN HARD — Marshall's "still looks good when you read it but doesn't see play" pick. "Card kind of sucks." LSV: "it did not work out well. It doesn't move you forward as much as you'd want."**
- **Lys Alana Informant** — C+ (#836 release). 1G 3/1 elf, surveil on entry **and** on death. "Probably the best of all the re-treads of this card, because the creature type really matters and the milling is so good in the Elf deck. You'd play three or four."
- **Great Forest Druid** — C+ (#836 release). 1G 0/4 treefolk, taps for any color. "A very clear setup for the five-color deck. Don't play this in Kithkin." Four toughness blocks well and eats blight.
- **Mistmeadow Council** — C+ in Kithkin (#836 release). 4G 4/3 Kithkin, costs 1 less if you control a Kithkin, draw a card on ETB. "Four-mana 4/3 ETB draw a card in a relevant creature type, yes please. You're not cutting it."
- **Vinebred Brawler** — C+ (#836 release). 2G 4/2 elf that **must be blocked**, and pumps another elf +2/+1 on attack. "Must-be-blocked is just powerful text — cast it on turn three and they can't cast a good creature, you're forcing a trade. You're not going to cut this in a dedicated elves deck."
- **Thoughtweft Charge** — C+ (#836 release). 1G instant, +3/+3 and draw a card if a creature entered this turn. "That draw-a-card is just — right. And that's the natural use case anyway."
- **Blossoming Defense** — C+ (#836 release). G instant, +2/+2 and hexproof. "I'll make an exception to our disdain for combat tricks for this one. One mana to win a fight or stop a removal spell or stop a bounce spell — you should generally have this card in your deck." **Late (#839): confirmed as basically the *only* trick worth a slot in this set.**
- **Tend the Sprigs** — build-around C+ (#836 release). 2G Rampant Growth that also makes a 3/4 reach treefolk if you control 7+ lands and/or treefolk. "The best use I found is with changelings — four lands plus two changelings, cast this, get your fifth land, now you're at seven and you get a sprig. It was mostly bad in green-black elves unless I had changelings."
- **Safewright Cavalry** — C, "C+ for what it does but I'd take it at a C level" (#836 release). 3G 4/4 elf that can't be blocked by more than one creature; 5 mana to pump an elf +2/+2. **Late (#839 & #843): UP — an overperformer. Marshall: "it crossed the threshold from annoying to just good." Threat of activation makes blocking it miserable, and if they have multiple elves they can punish any block. Cheon's caveat: Elves is crowded at four, so don't take two.**
- **Crossroads Watcher** — C in Kithkin (#836 release). 2G 3/3 trample Kithkin, +1/+0 per creature ETB. "We've seen this card before. It plays fine but it's not special. Still a 3/3 trample for three in a relevant creature type." **Late (#838): a Kithkin card, not a green card — Elves doesn't want it.**
- **Surly Farrier** — no explicit grade (#836 release), positive. 1G 2/2 Kithkin, tap for +1/+1 and vigilance at sorcery speed. "I've been impressed with this card. The vigilance part is secretly really nice — you make your 3/3 into a 4/4 vigilance, so they can trade up or you get a good blocker."
- **Midnight Tilling** — C in Elves and the 5-color deck / D in Kithkin (#836 release). 1G instant, mill 4 and return a permanent from among them. "Really good in Elves. This sets up all your elves cards. In Kithkin, don't play it — you can't afford to spend mana not on a creature." Note the on-air example: your turn goes from three extra mana to one if you whiff on an elf.
- **Prismatic Undercurrents** — build-around C (#836 release). 3G enchantment, vivid: fetch X basics to hand, play an extra land each turn. "Four-mana ramp doesn't get you ahead of the curve unless you're casting six- or seven-drops. The fail case is too high — if this is the only thing in play, that's atrocious."
- **Celestial Reunion** — build-around C (#837 release). XG sorcery: optionally behold two creatures of a type; tutor an MV-X creature to hand, or straight onto the battlefield if the beholds were paid and the type matches. "If you don't have two in play, you're casting this as a really bad tutor. Primarily an Elves card."
- **Gilt-Leaf's Embrace** — D (#836 release). 2G flash aura: trample + indestructible until end of turn, and a permanent +2/+0. "How are you fitting this in your elf mill deck that really wants all creatures?" **Late (#843): UP — LSV's single most underrated card of the set. #838: "people have been playing this quite a bit in Elves and it's actually been pretty decent — it more or less guarantees you win combat, and combat happens a lot in this format."**
- **Dawn's Light Archer** — implicitly D (#836 release, no letter given). 2G 4/2 flash reach elf. "I don't like this card. I'd play it if you were really hard up for elves. 3/2s and 4/2s just don't really make sense to pay three mana for."
- **Unforgiving Aim** — D, or sideboard C (#836 release). 2G instant: kill a flier, kill an enchantment, or make a 2/2 elf token. "The token isn't an elf when it's in the graveyard — and Elves cares about elves in the graveyard more than in play. This has to replace a legit removal spell, which I don't think it should." **Late (#838): confirmed. "The fact that it makes an elf is kind of fake."**
- **Spry and Mighty** — B+ walked down to B/B− (#837 release). 4G sorcery: choose two of your creatures, draw X and pump both +X/+X with trample, X = the power difference. "At a difference of three you're drawing three and attacking with a 7/7 and a 4/4." Targeting caveat: if they kill one of your only two creatures in response, it does nothing. **Late (#843): DOWN — the most controversial card of the set, and "ultimately the card ended up being not that good." Setup cost too high. "The fact that it has a treefolk on it probably should have been a hint."**
- **Helix Pinnacle** — unplayable (#837 bonus sheet). G enchantment with shroud, win at 100 tower counters. "If you could make 10 mana a turn, it's still 10 turns until you win."
- **Slippery Bogle** — unplayable (#837 bonus sheet). GU 1/1 hexproof. "There's really no use case for it in Limited."

**Green-white (Kithkin) — supported**

- **Brigid's Command** — B+, the weakest of the five commands (#837 release). 1GW kindred sorcery Kithkin, choose two: copy a Kithkin / make a 1/1 Kithkin / +3/+3 at sorcery speed / fight. - **Thoughtweft Lieutenant** — B, closer to B− (#836 release). GW 2/2 Kithkin: +1/+1 and trample to one creature whenever a Kithkin enters. "Not as powerful as you might think for the slot, because it's just one creature — and trample isn't impressive because the Kithkin decks go wide, not tall."

**White-black (Treefolk) — unsupported**
- **Emptiness** — A in black-white / A− if you can cast the black half / B mono-color (#837 release). 4 + WB/WB 3/5 elemental incarnation with evoke: reanimate a 3-MV creature if WW was spent, three −1/−1 counters if BB was spent. Marshall: "I can imagine this thing getting left out in the dark a lot." **Late (#838): LSV was right. **Black-green (Elves) — supported**

- **Stoic Grove-Guide** — C+ (#836 release). 4 hybrid B/G 5/4 elf druid; exile from the graveyard for 1 + hybrid to make a 2/2 elf token. **Three-color**
- **Maralen, Fae Ascendant** — build-around A− (#837 release; caption "Marwyn, the Nurturer"). 2UBG 4/5 flier: exile the top two of an opponent's library whenever an elf or fairy you control enters, and once per turn cast one of those cards free with MV ≤ your elf+fairy count. "If you cast this and they don't kill it, you're going to get ahead pretty quickly. If your opponent casts it and you can't kill it, you feel like you're going to lose." **Late (#843): the card both hosts still had to read every single time it was cast against them — "and there was no good news on that card if your opponent had already played it." Worth a splash out of green-black.**
- **Doran, Besieged by Time** — build-around B (#837 release). 1WBG 0/5 legendary treefolk: creature spells with toughness > power cost 1 less, and your creatures get +X/+X on attack or block where X is the toughness-minus-power gap. "It attacks as a 5/10, which is pretty big. If this cost two green-green we'd give it an A." Only for the vivid deck, and even then most of the giants have square stats so the discount rarely fires.

### Colorless / artifacts

- **Chronicle of Victory** — build-around A (#837 release). 6-mana legendary artifact: your chosen type gets +2/+2, first strike and trample, and you draw a card whenever you cast that type. "A windmill-slam early pick. You cast it and immediately get an overrun — it might just kill them. And if you're unlucky enough not to kill them, you get to start drawing cards." Kithkin or Goblins want it most; just don't be one of the off-brand types.
- **Gathering Stone** — B (#836 release). 4-mana artifact: choose a type; those spells cost 1 less, and each upkeep look at the top card — take it if it's on-type, otherwise you may bin it. "I had this in a Goblins deck and it was excellent. If your goblin, elf, merfolk or kithkin deck doesn't want Gathering Stone, you probably didn't draft a great version of it." **Late (#838): the format's litmus test. "The Gathering Stone is basically a mark of the format — can you put it in your deck? If you can, you're pretty happy."** Wants roughly 13–14 on-type cards (changelings and kindred noncreatures count).
- **Rooftop Percher** — D (#836 release). 5-mana 3/3 flying changeling: exile up to two cards from graveyards and gain 3. "At five mana I'm just not that interested. It doesn't help with vivid. I don't really know why you'd be interested in this card." **Late (#838 & #843): THE BIGGEST MISS OF THE SET REVIEW, for LR and for the whole community. "This card looked bad but it's actually quite good — actively good." Flying is good in a board-stall format, it's a changeling for critical mass, and the yard-exile is live against Elves, the best and second-most-drafted deck. 17Lands is what surfaced it on day three; nobody read it off the card.**
- **Puca's Eye** — C+ in the vivid deck (#836 release). 2-mana artifact: draw a card on ETB, choose a color, it becomes that color; 3 + tap to draw, only with five colors among permanents. "The fact that it cycles and then backstops your vivid at permanent-plus-one is a pretty big deal. I'd play this in a blue-red elementals deck with a couple of hybrids and three vivid cards." **Late (#843): Marshall's favorite card of the set. "I liked everything it did. Not quite good enough in a lot of senses, but it played out really nicely."**
- **Foraging Wickermaw** — C+ in the 5-color deck, don't play outside (#836 release). 2-mana 1/3 artifact scarecrow: surveil 1, and pay 1 to add any color and become that color. "It turns into any color, which boosts your vivid pretty nicely. They packed a lot into this."
- **Springleaf Drum** — C+ in Merfolk / C in the 5-color deck / C− in Kithkin (#836 release). 1-mana artifact: tap it and an untapped creature for any color. "Look at the art — it's a merfolk. You can turbo out your merfolk and get the tapping benefits too. The average merfolk deck would always run this."
- **Changeling Wayfinder** — C+ in the 5-color deck or any three-color deck, C to C+ in normal decks (#836 release). 3-mana 1/2 changeling that fetches a basic to hand. "It's any type, it fixes your mana, little road bump, it's a 1/2 that helps convoke. Going to be an in-demand common." Ironic note: the vivid deck wants color *diversity* among permanents, and this is colorless.
- **Stalactite Dagger** — C (#836 release). 2-mana equipment: makes a 1/1 changeling token on ETB, +1/+1 and all creature types, equip 2 — and note it does **not** auto-equip to the token. "It does potentially put two changelings into play once you equip a second creature. If you were lacking two-drops, this is okay."
- **Firdoch Core** — C− (#836 release). 3-mana kindred artifact changeling: taps for any color, 4 to become a 4/4. "Not having any color is pretty relevant and four to activate is a lot, but if you need the fixing, having your mana rock turn into a 4/4 is nice." **Late (#843): Marshall's "my opponent played this, so now I must win" card — "emblematic of a bad vivid deck. If you had to put that card in your deck, you weren't doing great, because it doesn't increase your vivid count, only your mana."**
- **Dawn-Blessed Pennant** — D (#836 release). 1-mana artifact: choose a type, gain 1 on each of their ETBs; 2 + tap + sac to return a card of that type from your graveyard. "I'm skeptical. It just feels like you'd rather just have a creature of that type." **But: take it very highly if you have Trystan's Command — the two of them loop, netting a permanent and a kill every cycle.**
- **Door of Destinies** — build-around B (#837 bonus sheet). 4-mana artifact, charge counter per on-type spell, +1/+1 per counter to that type. "You only really need to get to +2/+2 before it's already dumb, and +3/+3 is GG kind of easily. It has to hit the battlefield early — with a full board it might do nothing."
- **Manamorphose** — C+ but rarely fits (#837 bonus sheet). "A card you could always put in a red or green deck, and in your vivid deck it'll help fix mana — but it's also not a card that does anything. I'd only play it if I really needed another card, or in a three-to-five-color deck."
- **Dolmen Gate** — F (#837 bonus sheet). "Too much has to go right. You play Kithkin and this and just attack with everything — but their 2/3s still block your 2/2s."
- **Mirrormind Crown** — F (#837 release). 4-mana equipment, equip 2: your first token-creation each turn instead makes copies of the equipped creature. "Easiest F of my life. You'd be doing your opponent a great service by putting Mirrormind Crown in your deck." **Late (#843): Marshall's "my opponent played this so I must win" card — nobody ever did.**
- **Painter's Servant** — F (#837 release). "Worse by a significant margin than the common that fixes your mana and turns into a different color" (Foraging Wickermaw), because it colors *everything*, including theirs.
- **Thousand-Year Elixir** — F (#837 bonus sheet). "It's going to need another thousand years to cook."

### Lands

- **The five shocklands** (Hallowed Fountain, Blood Crypt, Temple Garden, Steam Vents, Overgrown Tomb) — C+ each (#837 release). "These are all basically C+ level cards. They're just fine."
- **Evolving Wilds** — play one by default (#836 release). "It's never that bad — you should default to playing Evolving Wilds. But keep an eye on your early curve: with three one-drops I might not, and with five one-drops I definitely wouldn't."
- **Eclipsed Realms** — avoid (#836 release). Uncommon land: choose a type, taps for colorless or for any color spendable only on that type. "Would you play this in your elf deck? I would hope not to. It doesn't cast your non-elf spells, and it doesn't cast your mana-fixing. Wouldn't you rather have a Forest a decent amount of the time?" The Elementals deck is the only plausible home. **Both lands are a deliberate signal from design: the baseline decks are meant to be straight two-color.**

## General format tips

- **Find the open lane, and stake it in the first three picks.** LSV's one-sentence summary of ECL. Take the payoff, not the flexible card — a Gravelgill Scoundrel doesn't make you draft Merfolk, but a Moon-Vigil Adherents makes you draft Elves.
- **Count your on-type cards, not your good cards.** Target 16–18. When you've got that, your six or seven remaining slots want to be removal — and only removal.
- **Use Gathering Stone as the self-check.** If you can happily play it, your deck is fine even if the card quality isn't. If you can't, you're in trouble regardless.
- **Prioritize removal LESS than your instincts say.** Both hosts routinely cut good removal as their last cuts. Cheon's winning shape is 18–19 creatures and 3–4 removal spells. Games are grindy; one-for-ones don't decide them.
- **Don't play combat tricks.** Blossoming Defense yes, Gilt-Leaf's Embrace in Elves yes, everything else no. There is no good one-mana trick in the set and the removal is good enough to blow you out.
- **Flyers and evasion are premium** because the format board-stalls. This is why Rooftop Percher was the biggest miss, and why Marshall lost a 12-turn sealed stall for want of a trampler.
- **Off-type cards must clear an uncommon power bar.** "Anytime you're putting a card into your deck that isn't the creature type you care about, it really has to pull its weight." That bar is Rooftop Percher / Pummeler for Hire / Goliath Daydreamer level, not "a perfectly fine common."
- **Never force the five unsupported pairs.** UB / WB / WR / RG / UG together were ~8% of decks drafted. Go there only when it falls into your lap (a tabled Reaping Willow plus a late Moonlit Lamenter), or at a Pro Tour table where everything else is being fought over.
- **Take the Eclipsed card.** Both hosts will windmill one pack 1 pick 1 and hang on for the ride. They were the most underrated cycle in the set.
- **In sealed, count your type first, not your rares.** Scroll right, count Merfolk, count changelings. Type-gated bombs (Champion of the Clachan, Champions of the Perfect) can't be splashed. Pools are spikier than usual — a 1-of-each-Eclipsed pool is a blank; two or three of the same type is a bonkers deck.
- **Play around the format's two hardest locks.** Blossombind on Gutsplitter Gang means it can't pay its blight upkeep and just bleeds 3 a turn — board it out against blue. And Champion-style behold-and-exile cards *answer* lockdown enchantments: behold the enchanted creature and get it back clean.
- **Against High Perfect Morcant, concentrate blight counters on one creature.** Spreading them means proliferate shrinks your whole board; stacking them means it kills one thing and leaves the rest intact.
