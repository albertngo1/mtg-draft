# FRA — MTG Arena Zone Limited Set Review (Reality Fracture)

Per-card review by **J2SJosh** (The Draft Lab), published 2026-09-21 across five colour articles,
captured the same day. Ratings are the reviewer's own **0–5**; they are held in
[](../../grades/mtgazone_FRA.json) and render as the **AZ** column.

⚠ **Pre-release opinion.** Published four days before FRA's prerelease and eleven before release, so
every rating is a prediction made with no games played. 17Lands GIH WR supersedes all of it once the
set is on Arena.

Notes below are the reviewer's own words, trimmed to the first sentences of each review.

## Card notes

- **Academic Ascent** — Sometimes the smallest details are what separate a solid trick from filler. In this case, Academic Ascent really needed to Empower Jace for three instead of two. That extra point would have allowed it to function as a cantrip instead of essentially giving you a double surveil.
- **Ajani Resolute** — Two-mana planeswalkers are always a dangerous design space, even in Constructed, so seeing one show up in Limited immediately gets my attention. Thankfully, Ajani isn’t going to protect himself very well right away unless you already have another source of life gain.
- **Ajani Unrelenting** — Yes, I am well aware that this costs six mana. I am also aware that it is completely busted in half. No matter which option you choose, you get a 2/2 thrown in for free.
- **Ajani's Anguish** — This probably looks a little high because an X-spell that deals damage to any target isn’t remotely as terrifying as it was back in the day. I promise I haven’t completely lost the plot, though. The flexibility to either clear out a blocker or just dome your opponent is still incredibly valuable.
- **Apex Witchstalker** — This is another mediocre piece of top-end curve filler that becomes playable mostly because basic landcycling bails it out. It obviously gets a little better in a deck packed with life gain triggers, where the extra life can actually matter instead of just being decorative text.
- **Arcane Amphisbaena** — A cheap deathtoucher can usually put in some work, especially when you pair it with cards like Compel Brutality . It can also just sit there and make your opponent think twice about sending their biggest ground pounder into combat.
- **Arni, Humble Scribe** — Let’s start with the good. This is a looter that doesn’t cost any mana to activate, and people tend to forget just how powerful that effect can be. Arni can even loot multiple times in the same turn just for doing something you were probably planning to do anyway: playing creatures.
- **Arni, Renowned Champion** — I feel like this could be really good at three mana, but four pushes it firmly into “depends on the deck” territory. There are going to be builds where Arni does some serious work because you have enough large creatures to make the ability matter.
- **Artifist Acumen** — This can fill a pretty useful role whether you’re in a spells deck looking for cheap triggers or an aggressive deck that doesn’t mind cashing it in for a little extra oomph in combat.
- **Awaken the Inferno** — The base spell would be actively good at four mana, but it’s still perfectly reasonable at five. The real thing helping it out is basic landcycling, which is usually worth about half a point by itself. Early on, it can bail you out of a sketchy hand or fix your colors, and later you can just cast the actual spell.
- **Bestial Incursion** — Not to harp on power creep again, but this is a straight-up better Shadowbeast Sighting which was a top common in Midnight Hunt. Granted, that format had a lot more cards that actively cared about the graveyard, and it was almost certainly slower than this one is going to be.
- **Blazing Crescendo** — We’ve seen this card before, and it has always ended up as pretty middling filler. The idea of a two-mana combat trick that replaces itself sounds great, but only getting to play the exiled card until your next turn makes things awkward.
- **Bloodline Recollector** — The only thing I’ll be recollecting is all the times I got excited about opening a mythic, only to realize it was this one. Don’t get me wrong, if you manage to get even one cast off this, it goes from being a generic Grizzly Bears to generating some absolutely ridiculous value.
- **Blossom-Blessed Angel** — This does a wonderful job of clogging up the board while still being able to peck in for some damage through the air. Nothing spectacular, but sometimes being mildly annoying in multiple directions is a perfectly acceptable career path. I actually really like how the card is structured.
- **Break Under Pressure** — While this won’t always hit the exact target you want, it is at least guaranteed to take out the most expensive thing they have. Most of the time, that’s going to line up pretty well with what you wanted to kill anyway.
- **Budding Insurgent** — With all of “The Way” enchantments floating around, there’s a nonzero chance you actually get to draw a card when you cash this in on one of them. The baseline rate is perfectly acceptable, and there’s enough incidental upside here to make it a little more interesting than generic curve filler.
- **Campus Crier** — I’m not going to cry about this one not giving you the full card back because you’re already getting a perfectly serviceable two-drop out of the deal. How good a 3/1 for two is will depend a lot on how many 1/1 dorks your opponent is throwing around, but it definitely has the potential to trade up.
- **Carnivorous Cultivator** — The rate on a 2/3 deathtoucher for two mana is already fantastic. Whether you get any extra value out of the ability remains to be seen, but you honestly don’t need much. If this manages to draw you even a single card over the course of the game, it goes from solid to great in a hurry.
- **Cast Away Doubt** — Here’s the problem: aggressive decks usually don’t often want to take a turn off just to gain a little card advantage, while control decks usually don’t want to keep taking damage while they’re desperately trying to stabilize. It does at least provide a little extra reach for the aggressive decks.
- **Chandra's Emberling** — This can grow pretty quickly in the right deck, and there are definitely going to be builds where you draft it more like a premium common. You can almost look at the ability like a mini combat trick that’s already sitting on the battlefield.
- **Chandra, Chill of Compliance** — I’m pretty chill on Chandra unless you’ve managed to assemble the spells deck. If you have, she might end up performing more like a 4.5. In a normal Limited deck, getting to surveil every turn is certainly useful, but you’re probably not going to be hitting a ton of free cards off it.
- **Chandra, Torch of Defiance** — I know what you’re thinking. Josh has finally lost his marbles, or someone at the old folks’ home forgot to bring him his meds. We already know how good this card is. Chandra has been around for years, and she’s been completely ridiculous for most of that time.
- **Command the Stage** — A 3/3 for three that pumps all of your other Wizard tokens is already a reasonable card on its own. Unfortunately this only pumps your OTHER wizard tokens so you’re only getting a 2/2 out of the deal. The moment you bring this back once, though, we’re getting dangerously close to good.
- **Compel Brutality** — A bite spell is one of those bread-and-butter cards that most green decks are happy to have in Limited. It’s cheap removal, even if you usually need to take board position and potential tricks into consideration before firing it off.
- **Countersculpt** — The times this is straight-up better than Counterspell are going to feel insane. The times it’s basically a better Cancel will be merely fine. That said, I love when small bonuses get stapled onto a card that was already hovering around playability.
- **Craterclaw Colossus** — This is certainly no Craterhoof Behemoth , even if it does have some real potential in a deck that can flood the board with cheap artifacts and Heartwood tokens. That pretty firmly puts its best home in Gruul. If you can consistently set that up, there’s something here.
- **Cruel Calculations** — Most of the time, this is a stone-cold nothing for three mana, which makes me incredibly wary of putting it anywhere near my deck. There are a few self-mill effects that can help enable it and squeeze a little more value out of the card, but the juice just isn’t worth the squeeze.
- **Cryotheory Adept** — This would be significantly better with just one extra point of toughness. Starting at one makes it really difficult to protect this while also using your spells somewhere else, which is exactly where you’d like to be getting value from it. I do like the potential graveyard value, though.
- **Curse-Marred Demon** — A 4/4 flying trample creature for four mana is still an absurdly efficient rate and something I’m basically never cutting. The Gamble effect is just bonus upside that will occasionally pay off in a huge way.
- **Danitha, Spear of Agony** — You definitely don’t want to be running this if you don’t have enough ways to trigger it. Of course, if your black deck somehow doesn’t have any removal spells, you probably have bigger problems anyway. The nice part is that instant-speed interaction can do double duty by pumping Danitha at the same time.
- **Danitha, Sword of Hope** — There’s a pretty large delta on this card because it can be almost unplayable if your deck doesn’t properly support it. You really want a healthy number of cheap Equipment, Auras, combat tricks, and other spells that target your own creatures.
- **Dark Matter Manipulator** — There’s some real upside here in the right deck, but you almost definitely don’t want to just toss this into a random pile and hope for the best. Even having a few other mill cards isn’t enough to get me especially interested.
- **Darklight Phoenix** — You definitely want to be playing Silence the Echo if this is in your deck. That’s by far the cleanest way to make sure two creatures die before combat and turn the Phoenix back on. Otherwise, you’re going to have to get a little creative. If your deck can reliably bring it back, there’s some real value here.
- **Diviner of Victory** — This card feels so close to being great, but it comes up just a little short on almost every path. It’s sort of a Man-o'-War , but it’s limited to creatures with mana value three or less while also costing double blue if you don’t pay in installments.
- **Divining Duelist** — I’m usually pretty low on three-mana creatures with only two toughness because that’s a great way to spend three mana just to have your opponent trade up on mana with a cheaper creature. None of the three abilities here are especially exciting on their own, but the modality does add some real value.
- **Draconic Visitor** — Another beefy flying rare with a pile of upside stapled onto it. Apparently Dragons showing up and just being huge isn’t enough anymore. There aren’t a ton of Treasure or Food generators floating around to really abuse the ability, but turning your random Heartwood tokens into fresh Dragons is one hell of an upgrade.
- **Eardrum Rattler** — This type of card has been a low-end playable in formats a lot less powerful than this one. I’m not going to blow out your eardrums babbling about it, though, because this is never going to be more than curve filler.
- **Edgar, Moonlit Sovereign** — Apparently, Edgar switched from Team Edward to Team Jacob. I could keep going with the Twilight jokes, but my wife reads these and it’s not worth the debate when I know which team she is on.
- **Enlightened Confidant** — The floor on this can’t be too low because, at the end of the day, it’s still a two-power lifelinker for two mana. Granted, that’s fallen a long way from the days when that alone would make people start getting excited.
- **Essence Burn** — There’s one of these in every color, so I feel like I’m starting to repeat myself. You’re usually going to get burned if you put this in your main deck, but it can be a fantastic sideboard card when the matchup lines up.
- **Extended Absence** — This is exactly the kind of card that would have easily been the best common in a set five years ago. It’s still a top common now, but four-mana removal is a little harder to justify loading up on these days. A clean exile effect that also hits planeswalkers is already great, and the bonus drain is just gravy.
- **Extrapolate the Impossible** — This isn’t entirely impossible to play, but it’s getting dangerously close. I know it looks like some weird Demonic Tutor where your opponent has to choose the lesser of two evils, but that’s just not going to play nearly as well in Limited as it sounds.
- **Face Yourself** — This is going to have a massive delta in how well it performs from game to game. There are going to be times where it ends the game on the spot. There are going to be times where it drags you back into a game you had absolutely no business winning.
- **Fateshaper Aspirant** — This feels like a card that’s fated to be a one-of in a whole lot of decks. I actually like most of the things it aspires to do, but five-drops can clog up your hand faster than Taco Bell clogs up…well, you know where this is going.
- **Fblthp, Impossibly Lost** — I would windmill slam a two-mana card that draws two cards with absolutely no questions asked. Unfortunately, this one asks a few too many questions for my liking. If you have a free attack, then it can be really good. If you don’t, then congratulations—you just played a two-mana 1/1 in 2026.
- **Fblthp, Knows the Way** — I’ll start by saying that I’m genuinely happy for Fblthp. After all these years, the little guy finally knows where he’s going. Easily the best change Jace made to reality.
- **Flickering Hound** — Your mileage is going to vary quite a bit on this one. The more ETB effects you have floating around, the more disgusting this gets. There are going to be games where Flickering Hound plays like a straight-up 5.0 and buries your opponent in value.
- **Flourishing Grapple** — You shouldn’t have to grapple with yourself over whether this belongs in your starting 40. It doesn’t. This is the kind of card that can absolutely flourish out of the sideboard when the matchup lines up, but main decking it is just asking to stare at a dead card at the worst possible time.
- **Fulminous Forte** — This would already be a 3.0 if all it did was deal five damage for three mana. That’s just a damn good rate. What pushes it higher is having the option to ping their entire side for one at instant speed. That’s the kind of mode that can sit quietly in your pocket until your opponent walks straight into a blowout.
- **Gallia, Tragic Host** — We all know Gallia loves to party, and apparently becoming a Zombie isn’t going to change that. She’s already a two-power menace creature for two mana, which is an easy include in basically any aggressive deck. The party doesn’t end there, though.
- **Gallia, the Merrymaker** — Ain’t no party like a Gallia party, cuz a Gallia party don’t stop. Gallia makes sure every new attendee downs a couple of Red Bulls on the way in, pumps up the jams, and heads straight into the red zone.
- **Gardenize** — They really don’t print many cards anymore that I can confidently say should never make your deck, but congratulations Gardenize —you found a way. First, you have to spend three mana doing absolutely nothing. That’s already a giant red flag in Limited. Then you need to have creatures. Then those creatures need to die.
- **Garruk, Curse Breaker** — Hey look, another planeswalker that generates card advantage while protecting itself. At this point, that seems to be the easiest way to earn a 5.0 in this set. A 4/4 trampler for five mana that draws you a card when it enters would already be a perfectly playable Limited card.
- **Garruk, Veiled Butcher** — This doesn’t read as overwhelmingly powerful, but Garruk is good in so many different situations that I’m still very high on him. Giving something -4/-1 might not outright kill many creatures, but it will usually put them on the sidelines for a turn.
- **Geist of Saint Thalia** — This is another card that’s going to have a massive range of effectiveness depending on the deck around it. In a spell-heavy deck, this could be one of the key cards that helps pull you ahead by generating mana tempo over the course of the game.
- **Generous Revival** — How generous this card actually feels is going to depend heavily on how many cheap creatures you have with ETB effects worth bringing back. Even without any fancy nonsense, it can still be a two-for-one, which is never a bad place to start.
- **Germinate Recruits** — It’s going to take a very specific deck before I’m interested in playing this. A lot of the life gain cards in the set are handing out life one point at a time specifically to trigger your payoffs, which doesn’t exactly help this get where it wants to go.
- **Ghalta the Immovable** — While you’ll usually be able to cast this for less than nine mana, you can’t completely ignore that massive starting cost. There are definitely going to be games where it gets stranded in your hand while you stare at it wishing it was basically anything else.
- **Ghalta the Unstoppable** — I don’t know if we’re talking Shaq-in-his-prime levels of unstoppable here. Sharptooth might be overselling himself a little considering there are plenty of cards in this set that can stop him just fine.
- **Gideon the Oathless** — Why does Jace have so much beef with all these former planeswalkers? He must really have it out for Gideon considering the guy literally sacrificed himself to save Liliana. Now we’ve got a former hero wandering around bitter and hating on everybody. Sounds like Jace is projecting a bit.
- **Gideon's Memorial** — Jace must have had beef with Gideon if he did him dirty like this. This card does a lot of different things without doing any of them particularly well.
- **Graft Surgeon** — Sometimes an entire review can be summed up in one word. Meh. Mid. Whatever the current term is for, “Yep, that sure is a Magic card.” There’s nothing offensively bad here, but there’s also absolutely nothing making me care.
- **Greenhouse Propagator** — This is another card that basically asks one question: how many cards in your deck actually care about gaining life? If you have multiple payoffs, this can become a key enabler and I’d value it quite a bit higher. If you don’t, then you’re mostly looking at a slightly overpriced ramp creature.
- **Guiding Hydra** — This can take over a game in a hurry if your deck goes even slightly wide. We’re not talking mega thick here, just having a normal amount of creatures on the battlefield is enough to make this thing get out of hand. So why am I stopping at a 4.0?
- **Hallway Heckler** — You might get heckled for playing this guy too often. A three-mana rummager is perfectly fine, but without something like Madness around, it’s not doing anything especially exciting.
- **Hapatra, the Desert Frost** — I was torn between a 2.5 and a 3.0 on this one, but I ended up leaning a little higher. A four-power Frost Lynx for four mana is already a solid card, but adding the ability to untap any creature pushes it over the top for me.
- **Heartstring Puller** — Creatures that give you two bodies for a reasonable rate tend to pull on my heartstrings, and this one is no different. It feels like a minor upgrade over the usual version of this effect thanks to the three-power trampler and the potential Wizard token synergies floating around the set.
- **Heartwood Crafter** — Not being able to cast spells from your hand with the mana from this is a pretty enormous drawback unless your deck is absolutely loaded with Adventure or Prepared cards. Turns out Magic gets a little harder when half your hand is just decorative cardboard.
- **Hexhaven Battalion** — This might seem a little high at first, but it reminds me quite a bit of a previously underrated card in Imperial Oath . The slow double surveil isn’t quite on the same level as getting an immediate triple scry, which is part of why I have this rated a little lower.
- **Hexhaven Invigorator** — Obviously, the biggest issue here is the quadruple-green casting cost. Of course, when has a casting cost ever stopped a Limited player from jamming a bomb into a deck with six Forests and saying, “I’ll probably get there”? Because if you can actually cast this thing, a 6/6 vigilance for four mana is disgusting.
- **Hungering Puppetbeast** — You can tap and sacrifice the token immediately to come crashing in for six damage, which is a pretty rude way to introduce yourself. If that’s not the right line, you can just hang onto the token for protection instead.
- **Hunter's Axe** — Strangely enough, this might be one of the hardest cards in the set for me to rate properly. If the equip cost were only one, I think this might actually be kind of disgusting.
- **Icy Reception** — I really want to love this card because a cheap creature counterspell that can also double as a combat trick for only two mana sounds great. The problem is that there are going to be plenty of spots where you desperately wish this were just an Essence Scatter or, better yet, a Mana Leak . A guy can dream, right?
- **Identity Echo** — Maybe you can board this in against someone who showed you a ton of enchantment removal, but even then, I’m pretty sure you can find something better to do with the slot. This has Constructed plant written all over it. Leave it there.
- **Infinite Coursework** — Infinite Coursework sounds like one of the creative punishment divisions in hell. Granted, it goes on your opponent’s creature, so that’s their problem to deal with. This style of removal has gotten noticeably better now that these effects tend to shut off abilities instead of just keeping the creature tapped.
- **Inspired Tethermage** — Mana sinks just don’t inspire me the way they used to. Cards are so efficient and packed with value now that I’m treating that part more like bonus upside than a reason to draft this.
- **Jace's Machinations** — This should usually play out like an instant-speed Divination with some upside. You can cast it on your opponent’s end step, draw a card, untap, draw again, and still have two counters left over.
- **Jace, Reality Sculptor** — This version of Jace is definitely lagging behind the other one in the set. You need to be pretty heavily blue to get much out of the first ability, and even then you’re still dealing with the built-in time gate on Empower Jace abilities. There’s value there, but it’s not exactly coming at lightning speed.
- **Jiang Yanggu, Alone** — I assume we’re all just calling this guy John Wick. Something clearly happened to his dog, and now half the multiverse is about to find out why that was a terrible idea. As a card, there’s some potential here, but it’s a little too pricey for my liking.
- **Jiang Yanggu, Never Alone** — I’ve already said how much I love cards that give you more than one reasonably sized body, and this one goes a step further by giving you a total of 5/5 worth of stats for four mana. That’s just a really good rate.
- **Kindred Judgment** — I’d be all in on this at six mana, but seven is an entirely different ballgame when it comes to sweepers. It’s also not guaranteed to leave your best creature behind. There are going to be plenty of boards where you have to name a different creature type just to deal with whatever nonsense your opponent has assembled.
- **Kiora of Fire and Ashes** — This is a wonderful example of just how much power creep has changed Limited over the years. Back in my day, a 5/5 flyer for six mana was a certified bomb. You opened that thing, slammed it on the table, and started mentally filling out your Top 8 paperwork.
- **Koth of the Homestead** — Continuous source of life gain triggers? Check. Pumps up your dudes whenever you play a Plains? Also check. Does all of that for three mana? Yeah, I’ll take that deal before somebody realizes they priced it wrong. This does exactly what the life gain deck wants while still providing plenty of value on its own.
- **Koth, the Geomancer** — This can be really nice when you play it on curve, untap, drop a Mountain, and jump straight into a five-drop. That’s the kind of start that can put you comfortably ahead without doing anything especially fancy. It can also give you some much-needed reach if you’re struggling to drag yourself across the finish line.
- **Last Gasp** — Last Gasp has made plenty of appearances over the years, and it’s never really been disappointing. It’s cheap, efficient removal that can cleanly answer smaller creatures while still doubling as a combat trick against bigger monsters. There’s not much more you can ask for out of a two-mana common.
- **Lich's Relic** — Remember Crude Bent Blade ? This is basically that card after somebody decided it needed a massive upgrade. Getting to actually target the creature instead of letting your opponent choose what to sacrifice is a gigantic swing in the right direction.
- **Liliana the Faultless** — We’re not even going to get into how messed up it is that Jace’s idealized version of his ex is literally called the Faultless . Then you read the flavor text — “All mistakes can be fixed. Even you.” — and somehow it gets even worse. As for the actual card, this is an absurdly good one-drop.
- **Liliana the Repentant** — This grade can definitely go up if your deck is built around keeping a well-stocked graveyard. The flip side is that drawing Liliana too late can make her considerably less impressive if you’re about to deck yourself.
- **Loot, the Anomaly** — Let me get this straight. Cards named Loot have spent a lot of time being completely busted, and this one shows up as basically a 2/4 for three mana. What happened here? Once you hit Threshold, it turns into a sacrifice outlet, which at least gives it something to do.
- **Loot, the Nexus** — Are you playing multicolor shenanigans? Then go ahead and bump this little dude up because he can fix for basically anything. He can even double-ramp you early as long as you have another creature with a different power, which can lead to some pretty gross starts. Would I like him more at two mana? Of course.
- **Loyal Tutor** — I’m usually terrified for my sanity whenever someone starts a sentence with “hear me out.” That said, hear me out on this one…there are actually times I would play it.
- **Lyra, Archangel of Dawn** — There aren’t a ton of Angels flying around Reality Fracture, so Lyra is mostly going to be pumping herself. Fortunately, there are plenty of ways to gain life, so she shouldn’t have too much trouble getting large and in charge. Even at the base level, though, a 3/3 flyer for three is already a perfectly solid card.
- **Lyra, Tolarian Archangel** — I’m really close to giving this a 4.0, but I’m landing here because there are going to be games where she just eats a removal spell the second she hits the battlefield. The big question is how you’re going to trigger the extra card draw, and Lyra conveniently answers that herself.
- **Mabel, Bitter Recluse** — If your deck wants a 1/1 deathtouch for one, then Mabel certainly provides that. The other ability is much harder to evaluate because it’s going to range from doing absolutely nothing to completely wrecking someone depending on the matchup and board state.
- **Marwyn, the Clearcutter** — Savannah Lions certainly aren’t what they used to be, but they’re still a perfectly nice way to kick off a game—especially when they don’t become completely useless later. Marwyn lets you cash in extra artifacts or lands for fresh cards once they’ve outlived their usefulness.
- **Marwyn, the Preserver** — If only they had printed an Evolving Wilds in this set. Alas, you’re probably going to have to mill a land or two if you want to squeeze any extra value out of Marwyn. Making your lands hexproof is about as exciting as it sounds in Limited, which is to say it’s probably not coming up very often.
- **Massacre Girl, Most Wanted** — Another legend with multiple busted previous versions showing up as a bit of a disappointment. This is definitely better off than Loot, but five mana is still five mana, and that slot gets crowded fast. The upside is real enough that I can see this outperforming a lot of the other generic five-drops floating around.
- **Master of Barbs** — A two-power menace creature with a little bit of upside is certainly worth playing in an aggressive deck. You’re probably not going to be Lightning Bolt ing your opponent’s face like this is Constructed, but there are still plenty of ways to trigger the ability.
- **Memory Trap** — In my memory, this type of card has never really been a trap. Oblivion Ring s are going to Oblivion Ring . This is just a solid removal spell that can hit basically anything on your opponent’s side of the battlefield.
- **Mindseeker Oculus** — I do love me some creatures with card advantage built in so I’ll definitely be seeking these out in draft. You can immediately cash this in for a card and still have a surveil waiting for you on the next turn.
- **Multiply by Zero** — First off, I’m mildly disappointed this card isn’t atrocious so I could give Multiply by Zero the only truly appropriate grade: 0.0 . Unfortunately, they had to go and make it good. This is awfully close to the 4.0 range because a two-mana instant that can kill basically anything is just that damn efficient.
- **No Admittance** — This might seem a little high to some people, but stapling surveil onto an already efficient two-mana removal spell is pretty big game.
- **Omnipresence** — I would say they really don’t print 0.0s anymore, but I’m pretty sure I already used that line recently. Apparently, Reality Fracture took that as a challenge.
- **Overwrite the Multiverse** — I might be a little lower on this than some other people. Don’t get me wrong, I’m still slamming it P1P1. I think it’s great—I just don’t think it belongs in the absolute top tier of bombs you can open. This was obviously designed to be a twisted version of Sunfall , but let me tell you: this is no Sunfall .
- **Perfected Theory** — In theory, this feels like removal. Unfortunately, the plan is far from perfect. Outside of combat, it’s not doing much of anything, and using it can leave you wide open to getting blown out if your opponent has the right response. That’s a pretty big downside for something you’d like to rely on as interaction.
- **Pia, Aether Ascetic** — This is dangerously close to unplayable if your deck doesn’t have any enchantments to support it. If you do, though, Pia can be a perfectly solid contributor.
- **Pia, Determined Rebuilder** — While I usually prefer my Preening Champion s to have the 2/2 portion flying, I can still work with what Pia is building here. The six-mana ability is dangerously close to being a nothing burger, but I’m sure there will be games where simply threatening the activation makes combat awkward enough to matter.
- **Plan for All Outcomes** — The going rate for an Uneasy Partings effect is right around four mana these days, with the real value coming from whatever extra goodies get stapled onto it. You give up instant speed here, but in exchange you get to Empower Jace every turn as long as you keep casting noncreature spells.
- **Pompous Battlemage** — This feels like another card designed primarily for Constructed that just happens to have a minor role in Limited. A 1/1 prowess creature for one can range from pretty meh to legitimately solid depending on the deck around it.
- **Precise Redaction** — This should be in precisely zero of your starting decks. I do appreciate seeing some old-school color hosers show up as sideboard options, but that’s exactly where this belongs.
- **Predictive Preparations** — I predict this is going to see more play than it probably should because it reminds people of Travel Preparations . Sure, this only requires one color, but you’re also paying twice as much to flash it back, which takes a lot of the shine off that comparison.
- **Proft, Consulting Detective** — You don’t need to consult a detective to figure out that you want plenty of scry and surveil cards in your deck to maximize this. The grade might seem a little high at first, but you’re already getting an on-rate two-drop before factoring in the upside.
- **Proft, Sinister Mastermind** — Obviously, a 5/5 menace for three mana is an absurd rate. How you’re supposed to reliably hit threshold fast enough in this format is another question entirely. Dimir and Golgari can probably get it done easily while the other combinations might run into more trouble.
- **Prophesied End** — It doesn’t take a prophecy to see that this is going to end up squarely in the filler removal slot. You aren’t going to prioritize it if you already have a few solid pieces of removal, but it’s not completely embarrassing if you need some interaction.
- **Protege's Awakening** — This is three cards for four mana, which is still a pretty solid deal even by today’s standards. Inflation, am I right? Or power creep. Whatever term you want to use.
- **Puppet Crafting** — Ensoul Artifact used to be a busted Constructed card. This is basically a juiced-up version of that effect, just in a color that’s a lot less naturally interested in having piles of artifacts lying around. Green does at least have plenty of enchantments to work with.
- **Pyre Rhymer** — I’m basically going to ignore the applications of the Molten Tide portion of this card because High Tide has never exactly been something you’re dying to play in a normal Limited format. The one thing I will give that half is that it basically functions as a free spell to trigger prowess.
- **Rampart Hunter** — The ability seems like it would be at its best in either an extremely aggressive deck or one loaded with massive tramplers. The problem is that neither of those decks is going to be especially excited about playing a Hill Giant just to get that little bit of upside.
- **Rank Rat** — Another spin on Ravenous Rats . This time it’s a Zombie and gets a little better in multiplayer, which are both pretty close to irrelevant changes for Limited. Aggressive decks probably don’t want to start piling these up because a tiny body isn’t exactly helping you beat someone down.
- **Refute Destiny** — It’s this card’s destiny to end up in the sideboard. If you’re playing Bo1, this type of narrow answer should almost never make your starting deck.
- **Repurposed Enforcer** — I actually think this is closer to a 3.25 than a 3.5, but I rounded up because I felt bad for him having to go through life like that. There are definitely games where this comes down on turn two and lets you squeeze a pile of value out of it.
- **Rescue Girl, First Responder** — This grade is definitely assuming you have a healthy number of ETBs that you’re interested in abusing. If you do, Rescue Girl can generate some pretty nice value by letting you reuse them. The big knock is the “only during your turn” restriction.
- **Restore with Empathy** — A Regrowth that can only grab permanents is obviously a little limited, but in green that’s usually what you were planning to get back anyway. What pushes this up for me is the instant speed and the four-life tacked on.
- **Return to the Light Realms** — This costs nine freaking mana and doesn’t outright win the game when it resolves. That makes it very tempting to give it the old 0.0 and move on with my life. My argument for the 1.0 is that there are going to be some decks that actually want this, especially out of the sideboard in slower matchups.
- **Rewrite Regrets** — I won’t have any regrets to rewrite when I take this. Maybe I’ll have a few about some of my grades later, but that’s future Josh’s problem. Four mana is usually about the point where I’m happy playing a reanimation spell straight up.
- **Rise of the Deathbringer** — Five mana is a lot, but you don’t exactly see instant-speed sweepers every day. That timing can let you squeeze out one last juicy chunk of value by letting your opponent walk straight into it. The catch is that -3/-3 isn’t going to clean up every big threat on the battlefield.
- **Ruric Thar, Biomagus** — Now this is a card that really makes you feel the power creep. Back in my day, Mahamoti Djinn was a legitimate bomb. This feels like a significantly better version of that card, and somehow, we’re still only calling it solid. That says a lot more about modern Limited than it does about Runic Thar.
- **Ruric Thar, Magecrusher** — I don’t really know why, but this just feels like it should have haste. At least it does a decent job protecting itself from pesky removal until it gets a chance to actually connect. Vigilance and reach on something this large are a pretty big deal because your opponent can’t just shrug and try to race you in the air.
- **Saheeli, Consul of Oversight** — I could go on another rant about power creep here, but I’m pretty sure you’ve heard that speech enough times already. How good Saheeli ends up being is really going to depend on how many random scrys and surveils your deck is already generating.
- **Samut, Hazoret's Champion** — This is another one where people may question how high I am on it, but giving everything haste is a legitimately game-changing effect. The best part is that you’re not even going down a card to get it because the ability is stapled onto a perfectly serviceable Grizzly Bears .
- **Samut, Tyrant of Naktamun** — There are definitely going to be games where Split Second absolutely wrexxors your opponent out of nowhere. There are also going to be plenty of games where it barely changes how the card would have played out anyway.
- **Sanctum Lurker** — This can immediately bump your Empowered Jace up to three while draining your opponent along the way. That leaves Jace perfectly set up to draw you a card on the next turn without even having to worry about losing him when the loyalty hits zero.
- **Screeching Soulbreaker** — Do you have a bunch of cards that trigger off gaining life? If so, this can become a key enabler and I’d draft it more like a 2.5. The same applies if you have a bunch of cards that care about noncombat damage.
- **Seasoned Cryomancer** — “Seasoned” sure is a polite way of saying old, but in this case I think we can also let it mean spicy. You can’t pull the same empty-handed nonsense that made Seasoned Pyromancer so absurd, but there’s still a ton going on here. You get to loot away whatever garbage you don’t need and toss around some stun counters.
- **Semester Foreseer** — I don’t foresee wanting to play very many copies of this, but I’m perfectly fine with the first one even if the price is a little higher than I’d like. It feels like it costs about one mana too much, but that becomes a lot more acceptable when you’re getting both effects bundled together.
- **Shatterwing Pegasus** — Five mana to pump your team is a pretty significant step down from the four-mana version we just had on Dwarven Provisioner , and that card was already considered extremely meh. That was also in a format where going wide was much easier, and the effect came stapled to a two-drop.
- **Silence the Echo** — Five mana at sorcery speed is an awful lot to be paying for a removal spell in this economy. Even the discounted rate is two mana, and we just had Stir Up Trouble doing something similar for one mana with a bunch of easily sacrificed artifacts floating around to fuel it.
- **Simulacrum Shaper** — Solemn Simulacrum has been a great card in both Limited and Constructed for about a billion years. Now you’re telling me I get a cheaper version? Sounds like a bargain to me.
- **Skilled Battlecarver** — This certainly isn’t going to do you many favors on defense, but it’s a great two-drop for an aggressive deck. The threat of pumping a first striker is often enough to make your opponent decide blocking just isn’t worth the headache.
- **Solve for Disappointment** — I won’t be disappointed to have one of these in my deck, but it’s definitely not a card I want to start stacking up on. Taking any nonland card while also getting a surveil is right around the rate I want for two mana.
- **Something Worth Saving** — This does a bunch of little things that can add up to something pretty useful in the right deck. You get some card selection, a little graveyard filling in a format with plenty of ways to profit from that, and a life gain trigger thrown in for good measure.
- **Sphinx of False Conclusions** — I hope my conclusions about this one are true because I think the card is even better than it reads. An instant-speed four-power flyer for four mana that loots when it attacks would already be a really good card.
- **Sphinx's Approach** — The whole “cast five of these” thing is absurd and should basically be ignored. First, you’d have to put at least five copies of this card in your deck. Then you’d need a Sphinx worth all that nonsense. It’s not happening. This becomes a lot more palatable if you just approach it as an instant-speed Divination .
- **Stingcaster Mage** — It’s really going to sting when this guy flashes back a removal spell and then immediately starts swinging. You’d never be excited to play a plain 2/1 haste for two in a modern Limited deck, but there are absolutely situations where drawing exactly that body is still useful.
- **Sureshot Sower** — Normally, “destroy target creature with flying” is code for “please leave me in the sideboard until further notice.” Sureshot Sower gets to dodge that fate because it’s still a perfectly acceptable two-drop when your opponent forgot to bring any birds, dragons, angels, or other airborne nonsense.
- **Surgical Precision** — We all remember Murdock's Crusade being a massive overperformer in Marvel Super Heroes. I don’t think this is going to be quite as good because this format doesn’t have Power Up making it much easier to guarantee you have a target.
- **Surveillance Phantasm** — It used to be a tradition for blue to get a slightly overstatted two-drop with defender in almost every set. Usually, it could attack once you jumped through whatever hoop the card asked for. Most of those ended up being curve filler that was pretty easy to cut. So why do I have this one a little higher?
- **Tarmogoyf** — Oh, how the mighty have fallen. There was a time when Tarmogoyf showing up across the table made people start reconsidering their life choices. Now we’re looking at it in Limited and saying, “Yeah, that’s a pretty good two-drop.” It’s still going to be a whole lot of stats for the low, low price of two mana.
- **Terminal Criticism** — You’re going to get criticized pretty hard if you’re running something this narrow in your main deck. As a sideboard card, though, it can be absolutely brutal when the matchup lines up.
- **Tether Technician** — Another five-drop filler creature that you can probably do better than. That said, there are absolutely going to be games where your opponent stabilizes at two life, you rip this off the top, and suddenly this mediocre piece of curve filler looks like an absolute hero.
- **Tethermage's Advantage** — Do you need a cheap combat trick and mostly just care about getting some extra stats? Cool, this does the job. The reach and untap clauses will absolutely matter from time to time, but they also tempt you into casting this during combat while your opponent is sitting there with open mana and a suspicious amount of conf…
- **Tetsuko Umezawa, Fugitive** — I wasn’t really expecting too many straight-up reprints in this set, but here we are. Tetsuko saw a decent amount of play back in its original release, but there just aren’t enough creatures running around in this format that actually benefit from the ability.
- **Tetsuko Umezawa, Pursuer** — The random pings are actually pretty relevant because they punish your opponent when they inevitably start chump blocking this thing. A double striker with prowess is already a very real threat, and once you start stacking a couple of triggers on it, Tetsuko can hit them like an RKO out of nowhere.
- **Teyo, Diamondblade Mage** — This is going to perform a lot better if you have a few first strikers floating around. The +1/+1 counter can turn what your opponent thought was a fair trade into a complete disaster, and Teyo can also just give himself deathtouch when needed.
- **Teyo, Lightshield Expert** — This is one of those incredibly unassuming cards that is going to cause a ridiculous number of random blowouts. Sometimes it’s going to completely wreck a removal spell. Other times, it’ll pump a creature just enough to flip combat in your favor and make your opponent regret all of their decisions.
- **Thalia, the Survivor** — A 3/4 lifelinker for four is about the rate you’d expect to pay for one these days, so the baseline is perfectly acceptable. The ability is really going to depend on your opponent’s deck. Against a spell-heavy deck, it can be downright crippling.
- **The Theorist, Jace Beleren** — It’s his set, so of course Jace was going to make himself completely ridiculous. He can protect himself in two different ways without forcing you to choose between staying alive and generating card advantage, which is usually the little detail that keeps planeswalkers from becoming completely obnoxious.
- **Theoretical Necromancer** — They had a theory of a plan, and eventually decided becoming a necromancer was the right career choice. Clearly, things have not gone especially well. This would be an amazing card if it actually Reanimate d something instead of just giving you a glorified Raise Dead .
- **Theorist's Proxy** — I’m sure there will be a few times throughout the set’s existence where the ability actually matters. It’s just not going to happen often enough for me to factor it heavily into the grade.
- **Tinybones, Pocket Nuisance** — How much discard are you realistically going to be playing? It’s not like your opponent is showing up with some dedicated cycling deck either, so that second ability is almost always just going to be one damage when Tinybones enters plus an occasional ping off a landcycler.
- **Titanbones, Towering Heart** — There’s going to be a massive — get it, because he’s a titan? I r clever — difference in how good this card is from deck to deck. On its own, it’s a slightly under-statted creature that you’re probably hoping never actually makes your final 40.
- **Tomik, Izzet Sparkmage** — This feels much more like a low-end Constructed engine than something I’m especially excited about in Limited. You can definitely bump the grade if your deck has a bunch of ways to randomly ping your opponent and keep the triggers flowing which is conveniently the plan for Rakdos.
- **Tomik, Orzhov Lawmage** — You really can’t go too wrong with a two-power flyer for two mana. That’s already a perfectly acceptable rate before you even start looking at the extra text. The first ability obviously needs a planeswalker around before it matters, so I’m treating that as more of a bonus than part of the baseline.
- **Traxos, Academy Guardian** — Casting a noncreature spell isn’t exactly asking you to solve the mysteries of the universe, and getting this thing down for two mana is an absolute bargain. This is definitely the kind of card Sir Mix-a-Lot would approve of.
- **Undulating Witness** — The card itself feels like pretty underwhelming curve filler. You usually have your pick of several mediocre options at five mana, and this one doesn’t exactly jump off the page. What makes it perfectly whelming—Young Justice reference for those of you with ball knowledge—is the basic landcycling.
- **Unflinching Hortimancer** — Don’t flinch when it comes to taking this if you’re in the life gain deck. It can be a pretty important piece there, even if it takes a couple of triggers before it really starts doing anything impressive. The nice part is that you probably won’t have to fight too hard for it.
- **Unsummon** — Ermahgerd, a nonbasic land card from Alpha showing up in a 2026 set. We’ve gotten about a billion variations on Unsummon over the years, but sometimes it’s best to just go old school.
- **Variable Chaser** — A 2/3 flyer for three mana isn’t much more than low-end curve filler these days. The wheel effect needs a whole lot to go right before it becomes especially relevant.
- **Verdant Kraken** — I’ll be real here: I am very close to giving this a 5.0. The only things holding me back are the seven-mana price tag and the fact that, if you’re not careful, your opponent can still kill it before you get any value. Make no mistake, though—this card is absolutely cracked.
- **Vinelasher Adept** — I’m putting this right alongside the other low-end basic landcyclers. On raw rate, it’s the kind of top-end card you only really consider when your deck is running out of better options.
- **Violent Echoes** — Four-mana removal isn’t what it used to be, but that equation changes in a hurry when you get to draw a card off of it. Even when you don’t get the full value, I don’t think you’re going to complain too much about blowing up a big creature and getting a surveil out of the deal.
- **Void Extrapolator** — I feel like this really needed something like menace once you hit Threshold. It can be a key card in Dimir builds, but struggle elsewhere. A potential 3/3 for two mana is certainly respectable, and you can usually find a spot to sneak in the extra mana for the mill ability.
- **Vraska's Final Mercy** — Two mana and two life to kill a creature is a solid deal, even if it is at sorcery speed. The other half is basically a Sign in Blood where you have to wait around for the second card. That’s not an awful fallback plan, but this is almost always going to be used as the removal spell.
- **Way of the Cryomancer** — The place where this can really shine is using its ability to copy spells that also Empower Jace. That can start snowballing out of control pretty quickly if you’re able to keep the token alive. The downside is that it does ask you to have the right mix of spells and enough protection to keep the engine going.
- **Way of the Deathbringer** — I’m not going to stoop quite as low as making Mandalorian references for all of these Way cards. Maybe that is the way. I certainly don’t mind upgrading a couple of random tokens into 4/4s.
- **Way of the Healer** — This is where the Empower Jace ability really starts to show its limitations. I’d be significantly higher on this card if you could get all of the value in the same turn. Being able to make two 2/2s with a surveil or a 2/2 with a cantrip would make this a really strong modal card.
- **Way of the Mentor** — I don’t like this nearly as much as some of the other Empower Jace enchantments. It doesn’t add some cool unique ability, so the entire value of the card really comes down to how many counters you can squeeze out of it.
- **Way of the Mind Sculptor** — Remember, we’re grading these based on how they fit into the average deck. At baseline, this is basically a draw two that leaves behind a couple points of Empower Jace. That’s fine, but you’re usually going to want more for five mana.
- **Way of the Necromancer** — You need way too many things to go your way before this generates enough value to be worth the trouble. You need creatures, at least one planeswalker, and this on the battlefield. Then you need those creatures to die while your planeswalker somehow survives through everything.
- **Way of the Paradox** — This is not the way. The real paradox is why this ended up in your deck in the first place. I’m not really interested in a three mana Explore even if it has a little extra thrown in.
- **Way of the Pyromancer** — Must resist cheap Mandalorian references…well, for now. I’m 100% doing them when we hit green, but at least I’ll have the excuse of Grogu flavor when we get there. Oh yeah, the actual card. To quote Mr.
- **Way of the Warlord** — On its own, this can come down, pop a creature and your opponent for two damage each, and still leave you with a surveil afterward. That’s a pretty solid pile of value. The problem is that there’s a huge difference between dealing two damage and three, especially when this also costs one more mana than No Admittance.
- **Way of the Wildspeaker** — Finally, Mando can say the line: “This is the way.” Grogu would probably approve too, although there’s a decent chance he’s just distracted because he stole another cookie. Kids these days, am I right? Empower seven is a massive amount of juice.
- **Winter, Team Player** — Unlike Game of Thrones, Winter is not coming—at least not to my draft deck. He can play for a team, just not mine. Terrible jokes aside, this feels like it would be a perfectly serviceable card at three mana even without convoke.
- **Winter, Tormented Loner** — I initially read this as Tormented Loser and had a much darker writeup prepared for it. Loner is a little better, but still not exactly where one wants to be.
- **Wrath of the Bloodmane** — I guess I just really like a lot of the red removal in this set. I’m a little lower on this one, though, even if it can potentially deal four damage for only two mana. Getting that discount requires you to have a legendary creature around, and an Empower Jace token doesn’t count.
- **Wrecking Gecko** — I guess this is what happens when you ignore the Gecko and don’t save 15% on your car insurance. As a card, this is another chunky piece of top-end filler with a mana sink attached.
- **Yargle, Glutton of Urborg** — Good ol’ Yargle showing up after Yargle Day to try to Yargle your opponent in the middle of nowhere. Honestly, you probably shouldn’t be playing this card very often, and it’s definitely closer to a 1.0 than a 2.0.
- **Yargle, Goliath of Otaria** — I can definitely see the vision of Sir Mix-a-Lot playing a bunch of these. Unless you share his appreciation for rear ends, though, you might be a little less interested. Did I really make two big-butt jokes in one article? Don’t judge me. The material was right there.
- **Yoshimaru, Beloved Companion** — I’m not saying Yoshimaru is a bad dog. He’s a very good boy. He just happens to be a slightly underwhelming Magic card. At two mana, I’d be looking at this as a premium uncommon. At three, I need a pretty specific deck before I’m excited to put him in my 40. That said, card grade aside, he still gets all the pets.
- **Yoshimaru, Scrappy Stray** — Someone needs to rescue this poor pup. We can’t just have Yoshimaru running the streets like this. A fight spell is obviously worse than a bite spell, but getting the effect stapled onto a body for only two mana makes the whole package a lot more appealing.
- **Your Fate Ends Here** — Let’s be honest here: how often are you really pointing a three-mana removal spell at something that costs less than three? Not very. That means this is basically Murder with upside. It can also hit planeswalkers, and you get a surveil tossed in for good measure.
- **Yuriko, Blade of the Mighty** — You never really have to worry about tricks while Yuriko is on the battlefield, so you’re free to attack with a lot more information than usual. That double strike can also be mighty nice when you’ve got an evasive creature or some big chonker with trample rumbling into the red zone.
- **Yuriko, Hope from the Shadows** — This is one of those cards that reads like it should probably be a 1.0 or 1.5, but I have a feeling it’s going to play better than that. I love one-mana tricks, and this one even leaves behind a body. The only real ask is that you have some cards in your graveyard, which shouldn’t be too difficult in most games.
