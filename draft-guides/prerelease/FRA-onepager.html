You don't have to build anything.

Three minutes. Skip the long guide — that one's for building, and you're not building.

There's no drafting. We build all three decks together, hand you one, and you play it.

The hard part of Limited — knowing what's good, reading a card pool, cutting the right stuff — is the part you're skipping entirely.

What the night actually looks like

  1. Everyone opens their six packs. We photograph them so we can split the cards back afterward.
  2. All 18 packs go into one pile and the three of us build three decks. About an hour.
  3. Someone hands you a deck. You play three or four rounds, best of three, roughly 50 minutes each.

Prereleases are the most casual event in Magic. People bring their kids. Nobody has played this set before either — the cards came out today. Everyone at your table is also reading cards for the first time.

Your deck: White/Blue Fliers

Play a cheap creature every turn, get in the air, and keep attacking.

Roughly 16 creatures, a lot of them small fliers, and a couple of tricks. This is the cheapest deck in the set and the one with the most two-drops — you will almost always have something to do on turn two, which is more than most decks here can say.

Surveil comes up constantly: look at your top card, keep it or bin it. Several of your creatures get better on any turn you did it. It’s free, so do it when you can.

You win by getting ahead early and staying in the air. Ground stalls are fine for you — you’re flying over them.

One honest warning: yours has the least removal of the three decks. You win by having the better board, not by killing their stuff. That’s the plan, not an oversight — and it means you face fewer tricky “should I use this now?” decisions than your teammates do.

Three habits to bring

  • Attack the planeswalker. Almost everyone in this set makes a blue Jace token. It looks harmless — it just draws them cards. Left alone it draws four or five. Your fliers are the best thing on the table for killing it, because nothing blocks them.
  • Don't dump your whole hand. Some cards kill every small creature at once, and your creatures are small. If you're already ahead on board, hold one or two back. Going from "way ahead" to "nothing" in a single card is the most common way this deck loses.
  • Play first. When you win the die roll, choose to play. It's almost never wrong.

Two cards worth knowing on sight

5 mana · black · instant Rise of the Deathbringer

All creatures get -3/-3, which kills most of your board at once. Opponent in black, five lands up, and you're way ahead? That's the moment to stop adding creatures.

2 mana · white · instant Prophesied End

Kills any creature. The useful bit: they only draw a card from it if the creature wasn't attacking. So attacking into it is genuinely the cheaper line — don't freeze up over it.

When you don't know something

Ask. Ask your opponent what their card does — they'll tell you, it's expected. Ask us, we're sitting right there. Nobody at a prerelease minds, and reading a card carefully is not cheating.

Unsure whether to attack? Ask yourself: if they block and I lose this creature, am I still fine? If yes, attack.
