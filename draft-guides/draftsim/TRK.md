# TRK — Draftsim Limited Set Review (Star Trek)

Per-card review by **Andrew Quinn**, published on Draftsim 2026-11-02 and captured 2026-11-11. The
reviewer's **0–10** ratings are held in [grades/draftsim_TRK.json](../../grades/draftsim_TRK.json),
rescaled to /5 for the **DS** column; the prose below is that review's per-card reasoning.

⚠ **Pre-release opinion.** Written before the format was played, and the reviewer says so: "this
review is based on my initial impressions of the cards. Many cards will under- or over-perform."
17Lands GIH WR supersedes every call here once TRK is on Arena.

The planet duals, anomaly lands, shock lands and dilemma lands were reviewed as four groups (4, 3, 4
and 5 /10); each land carries its group's grade in the JSON but has no note here. The Stardates
bonus-sheet cards are reviewed under their original card names, which is how they are keyed below.

Notes are the reviewer's own words, trimmed to the first sentences of each entry.

## Card notes

- **70,000 Light-Years from Home** — The plot of Star Trek: Voyager involves the fledgling crew of the U.S.S. Voyager as they end up on the other side of the galaxy, 70,000 Light-Years from Home. So yeah, exiling a creature or spacecraft is a pretty resonant ability for this card to have.
- **A Good Day to Die** — Even just for 1 mana, A Good Day to Die isn’t a combat trick I’m ever happy to play. We’ve seen some defensible versions of this effect in the past, but they all do much better than simply drawing you a card, like leaving a token behind or reanimating the creature. This new version just doesn’t cut it for me.
- **Academy Instructor** — Like most playable Young Pyromancer variants, Academy Instructor is still cheap enough to be worth playing. On top of that, it also triggers with every noncreature spell you cast, not just instants and sorceries.
- **Adversary by Algorithm** — Adversary by Algorithm gives us effectively a reprint of Call of the Herd, except with beam me up. I really like that, and with how good Bestial Incursion was in Reality Fracture, I have no problem thinking this will be very good here, too.
- **All Good Things . . .** — Perhaps not the episode I’d have chosen here. All Good Things. . . was The Next Generation’s final episode. It sees Picard, tasked by Q, attempting to solve the mystery of a spatial anomaly from three distinct points in time simultaneously.
- **Amok Time** — Known to Trekkies as simply “the one where Spock gets horny”, Amok Time is a classic episode where Spock experiences “Pon Farr”. This requires him to return to Vulcan and meet his arranged mate, but it results in him and Kirk having a fight to the death.
- **Andromedan Automatons** — This is going to be a really fun challenge during a booster draft. The first copy of Andromedan Automatons is basically unplayable, but once you find a second one, the first one you play draws you a spell when it enters. That’s pretty good, and if you find a third or even a fourth copy, they get even better.
- **Antagonist Incorporator** — Antagonist Incorporator looks like a great way to swarm the board with additional Borg tokens. Your Borg decks already want to go wide, and not only does this help you to do that, but it also rewards you for it. Plus, the tokens you make also have this ability and can create even more tokens.
- **Anthropological Mission** — Getting to draw a card whenever you play a noncreature spell could be quite powerful. The first two spells only let you loot, but every spell thereafter nets you a whole new card. Anthropological Mission requires a little bit of time to get going, but if you can give it that time, it should be very effective.
- **Arcum Dagsson** — Since there are some expensive noncreature artifacts in TRK that we’d really like to cheat out, such as The Holodeck and Deep Space 9, I could see Arcum Dagsson playing a role every now and again.
- **Armus, Skin of Evil** — In one of TNG’s most unexpected episodes, the crew discover a planet inhabited by only a single lifeform: Armus, Skin of Evil. They try to engage with it and… let’s just say it goes very wrong for them. This card is really powerful, too.
- **Assault Drone** — +1/+0 to your team certainly isn’t nothing, and the more aggressive you are, the more you’ll appreciate it. But Assault Drone only looks average to me, as not buffing toughness or making your Borg drones more resilient in any way is a shame.
- **Assembly Specialist** — Two mana for a pair of 1/1s is always a good deal when we get it. When you add in the fact that the Borg want to be a go-wide aggro deck centered around artifact creatures, Assembly Specialist looks like one of the best ways to start your curve in that deck. I’d prioritize this very early when drafting a Borg deck.
- **Astute Terraformer** — Rootwalla and similar creatures get to use threat of activation to attack past bigger creatures in the early turns of a game. Astute Terraformer doesn’t, as its ability is still too expensive to activate that early in most games.
- **Automated Warfare System** — This card comes from a very evocative episode of TNG. The crew finds a planet whose population was completely wiped out by automated weaponized drones from a weapons company who just wanted to sell more weapons.
- **Bat'leth** — Three mana to give -2/-2 is pretty inefficient by most standards, but the classic Klingon Bat'leth gives you quite a bit more than that, leaving behind a very relevant piece of equipment in addition to the removal.
- **Battle-Scarred Survivalist** — I get that the Klingon deck is going to have a sacrifice theme, but I don’t think Battle-Scarred Survivalist looks that good. We’ve seen Unruly Mob in past sets be virtually unplayable, and this feels like a watered down version of that.
- **Beckett Mariner, Impetuous Ensign** — I’m sure I’m missing something having not watched Lower Decks, but Beckett Mariner, Impetuous Ensign’s card design leaves a little to be desired. It’s basically just a 2/1 with first strike, which is of course a good creature, but it has a lot of extra text that really doesn’t amount to much.
- **Blastoneuron** — This theme of having cards leave your graveyard for value appears to be well-supported. Blastoneuron’s enters trigger isn’t the most useful thing you can do, but having a flying creature sit around and trigger over and over sounds pretty good to me.
- **Boarding Action** — Getting to play Boarding Action on turn 2 and immediately attack is a great way to start a curve. Later in the game, you might even find a good spot to cast this twice with its beam me up cost and get a great attack in with your two new haste creatures.
- **Borg Cube** — One of the things that made the Borg so distinctive was the intimidating design of their ships. They were simply massive cubes or spheres floating through space, and seemingly impossible to penetrate. Borg Cube looks obscenely powerful.
- **Brad Boimler, Eager Ensign** — Brad Boimler, Eager Ensign is one of the main characters from one of the animated series, Lower Decks. While I’m not very familiar with the show, this card is extremely good.
- **Branching Timeline** — Eight-mana spells need to have some guarantee of winning the game for you to want to bother accelerating into them. Branching Timeline does nothing of the sort and is entirely random, which makes it completely unplayable.
- **Brazen Bluff** — I absolutely love the design of this card. Big bluffs like this were a quintessential part of Captain Kirk’s character and style of command. Sadly, the most common outcome of Brazen Bluff is basically a Negate, so it’s not at all playable in Limited. I’m looking forward to playing it in Standard, though.
- **Bristly Bill, Spine Sower** — I just want to say that representing McCoy as Bristly Bill, Spine Sower is a stroke of genius. Also, Bristly Bill is a spectacular card. Putting a +1/+1 counter on something with every land you play is incredible, and in the late game, its activated ability is often better than using your mana to cast a new spell.
- **Bruvac the Grandiloquent** — I absolutely love Bruvac the Grandiloquent. It’s in fact my main commander. But there are literally zero cards in this set that combine with it. Unless you really want to play Zachary Quinto as a Horned Turtle, you should never play this.
- **Cantankerous Captain** — Cantankerous Captain brings back the design we saw on Valor Singer from Forgotten Realms and adds a pretty mediocre bonus once you have 8 power among your creatures. You’re going to be able to find much better creatures for your aggro decks, but this will fill the curve in fine.
- **Captain Benjamin Sisko** — The badass captain of Deep Space Nine, Captain Benjamin Sisko functions kind of like a Hero of Precinct One but for dilemmas. I’ll happily take that. There are so many modal spells and abilities in Star Trek that this seems like a great card to take early and build around.
- **Captain James T. Kirk** — Few characters in science fiction are quite as prolific as William Shatner’s Captain James T. Kirk. As Captain of the Enterprise, he never shied away from getting his hands dirty, and hand-to-hand brawls were common in the original series.
- **Captain Jean-Luc Picard** — Here, we have the best Captain in the whole show (fight me), Captain Jean-Luc Picard. Legendary actor Patrick Stewart brought this incredible character to life through 10 TV seasons (across TNG and Picard) and four movies, and he is one of my reasons for loving this show in the first place.
- **Captain Jonathan Archer** — I never watched Star Trek: Enterprise, but if I had known that Captain Jonathan Archer had a lovely dog, I might have given it a try sooner.
- **Captain Kathryn Janeway** — Captain Kathryn Janeway looks quite strong. Exploring is a fairly random mechanic; you’re not sure what you’re going to get out of it, but allowing every creature you play to explore should help you to smooth out your draws and make sure you’re finding the best spells you can.
- **Captain Michael Burnham** — The adoptive sister of Spock, Captain Michael Burnham of the U.S.S. Discovery is a somewhat contentious figure in the Star Trek universe, but a capable Captain nonetheless. As for the card, this is a pretty solid payoff for this red/green landfall deck.
- **Captain Pike, Fullness of Time** — Most people know who James T. Kirk is, but far fewer will know that in Star Trek’s pilot episode in 1966, Christopher Pike was in fact the captain of the Enterprise.
- **Captain Sisay** — Captain Sisay looks like it should be good in a set with so many legendary cards in it, but this card is just outdated and weak. Four mana for a 2/2 is very bad, and it doesn’t do anything until you get to untap with it. It’s a hard pass from me.
- **Captain's Tutelage** — We’ve seen plenty of cards similar to Captain's Tutelage in the past, and they’ve never really panned out. An effect like this isn’t exactly guaranteed to win a combat for you, and you certainly won’t win the next after it has resolved.
- **Cetacean Ops** — We’ve seen a few 3-mana 2/2s in recent sets that pick up a +1/+1 counter whenever you cast a noncreature spell, and they’ve all been bad. Cetacean Ops being a 4-mana 3/3 actually makes me less inclined to want to play it.
- **Cha'DIch Investigator** — Five-drops tend to be very replaceable these days, yet Cha'DIch Investigator looks surprisingly good for a common, kind of like Girder Goons from Streets of New Capenna. A 4/4 plus a 2/1 haste creature is worth paying 5 mana for, and I can see this being good top-end for the mana curve of your Klingon aggro decks.
- **Class-F Shuttlecraft** — We keep getting sets where the generic 3-drop mana rock is actually playable, and I believe this one is too. Class-F Shuttlecraft can fix your mana in any deck you want, but it also gives you the upside of getting to attack later in the game. It doesn’t do a ton, but it certainly gets the job done.
- **Cleveland Booker, Empath** — Even if they can’t block, creating a free 2/2 token on landfall is an incredibly powerful ability. Especially considering the fact that there are many ways to give yourself additional land drops each turn.
- **Cloak and Phaser** — Cloak and Phaser looks like an incredible payoff spell for strange new worlds. At 6 mana it’s very castable, but a little too expensive. At 5 or less it’s incredible. To accomplish this, you only need three or more different lands, which sounds very doable.
- **Cloistered Telepath** — I’m not quite sure where Cloistered Telepath will fit in this format, but decent triggered ability like this makes it a good option for beam me up spells. It’s quite weak though, so I think I’ll cut this more often than not.
- **Cold-Blooded Crew** — Our final landcycler, Cold-Blooded Crew looks a fair bit worse than its counterparts in other colors, but that’s neither here nor there. You’ll definitely play it as a way to fix your mana, and if you end up casting it as a 6-drop that can’t be chump blocked, there are far worse things you could do.
- **Collation Trimatrix** — The main reason Cryptbreaker was so good back in the day was for this ability right here. Collation Trimatrix may not be able to create any tokens, but getting to draw a card just by tapping three of your Borg is an incredible ability.
- **Collective Drone** — The problem with a 10/2 creature is that as ridiculous as that stat line might seem at first, it still just trades off with other 2-drops in combat. Collective Drone won’t always be that big, but the simple fact that it never gains any toughness makes it fragile.
- **Combadge** — I get the gimmick, I really do, but Combadge feels embarrassingly bad. You have to pay 3 mana to cast it, and then spend 4 mana equipping both copies to your creatures to make them both unblockable. Except that disrupting one of them disrupts the other.
- **Command Decision** — This looks eerily similar to Surgical Precision, which we just saw in Reality Fracture. Command Decision is a decent removal spell, but one that’s quite situational and might not find a target.
- **Command Subadjunct** — Vigilance carries a lot of extra weight in this set as it can allow you to attack with your creatures and then station a spacecraft with them afterwards. Even so, Command Subadjunct feels like it’s only slightly better than a vanilla 2/1 for 1, which is very playable, but nothing exciting.
- **Commander Raffi Musiker** — Most of the time, you’re going to want to investigate with Commander Raffi Musiker. A 3-drop Thraben Inspector is pretty strong, even at the cost of 2 life.
- **Common Goal** — We do prefer these spells to be instants, but letting two of your creatures team up to destroy another means that Common Goal ought be a very effective removal spell in any green deck. It’ll basically always find something to kill.
- **Conquest Mission** — While taking a turn off to play Conquest Mission doesn’t sound too appealing, giving you a free 1/1 every single time you play an artifact sounds absolutely incredible. This clearly rewards you for building your deck to maximize it by filling it with as many artifacts as you can find.
- **Consider the Prime Directive** — Most conditional counterspells get significantly worse in the late game, as the more lands your opponent has, the fewer spells you’ll actually be able to counter. However, Consider the Prime Directive is never dead. Stunning a creature and drawing a card is also a very good mode to have access to.
- **Constable Odo** — The ability I like the most on Constable Odo is naturally the double investigate. Two Clues is the easiest way to turn Odo into card advantage, but having a nice tempo play by stunning a creature for a few turns is certainly going to come up from time to time, too.
- **Crystalline Entity** — The Crystalline Entity was an early “villain” in The Next Generation, an all-powerful entity that the Enterprise crew couldn’t figure out how to stop. It was highly destructive, so seeing it here as a massive creature that wipes the board when it enters seems very appropriate.
- **Culber and Stamets, Star-Crossed** — As I watched Discovery, I felt entranced by the beautiful love story between Hugh Culber and Paul Stamets. They go through so many hardships and yet always manage to find each other again, and it was wonderful to watch. Sadly, Culber and Stamets, Star-Crossed doesn’t look like a very good card.
- **Cybernetic Specialist** — Cybernetic Specialist is quite unassuming. It’s a strictly better version of Sanitation Automaton from Murders at Karlov Manor, which was a huge overperformer. It has synergy in white-black and green-black, and might be a perfectly fine curve-filler for just about any deck.
- **D'Ghor Bloodletter** — Given how common the 2/1 Klingon tokens and the 1/1 Officer tokens are, the ability to give -1/-1 to all creatures sounds like it could be quite devastating.
- **D'Vana Tendi, Optimistic Ensign** — While 3-drop mana dorks aren’t the greatest, D'Vana Tendi, Optimistic Ensign gives you something good to spend your extra mana on. I’d happily play this in any blue/green deck, but I’d fear that the double color pips in its cost might hinder its playability in a deck that’s only one of its colors.
- **D-7 Battle Cruiser** — Creating a temporary 2/1 token with haste every turn isn’t a bad effect in theory, but is it worth the cost for this card? I’m not entirely sure.
- **DOT-7 Repair Squad** — Untapping a creature for 2 mana and only at sorcery speed sounds kind of useless to me, but it can be helpful if you want to station a spacecraft. Aside from that, DOT-7 Repair Squad doesn’t look very good at all and will probably be quite bad.
- **Dahar Master** — Finding the sacrifice fodder for Dahar Master is the only thing you need to do here. If you sacrifice a creature with 2 power, a Shock in exchange isn’t actually a good trade for you. It’s not that this card is bad, but you need to make sure that what you give up is worth what you get out of it.
- **Dal R'El, Augment Orphan** — Having seen just how aggressive the landfall deck appears to be, this card disappoints me. With all of its extra lands, this deck shouldn’t have much trouble with mana, and Dal R'El, Augment Orphan does virtually nothing to advance the aggressive game plan.
- **Daniels, Temporal Agent** — You had me at Gravedigger. Daniels, Temporal Agent is a powerful two-for-one play that should be in any deck that can feasibly cast it, plain and simple. In black/green specifically, its second ability gives Daniels a built-in way to leave the graveyard and trigger all of your payoffs for a second time.
- **Data, New Life** — I’ve been very wrong about cards like this in the past, so I’m being extra careful with how I rate Data, New Life. It naturally grows every turn just from you drawing your card for the turn, but you’ll really want to combine it with other sources of card draw to make Data really shine.
- **Dathon and Picard at El-Adrel** — TNG’s season five episode “Darmok” is probably my favorite in the entire franchise. In it, the Enterprise encounters the Tamarians, a race whose language is seemingly impossible for them to decipher.
- **Dathon, His Heart Open** — Darmok is a work of art. The Tamarian language and culture is all based around telling stories. In one of the best scenes of the episode, Darmok signals to Picard that he’d like to hear a story from Earth, so Picard tells him the story of Gilgamesh and Enkidu. Sadly, Dathon is injured and doesn’t survive the night.
- **David and Carol Marcus, Creators** — We’ve seen plenty of similar 3-drops that accelerate you with an extra land and they’ve always been great, but David and Carol Marcus, Creators even fixes you for every color by giving you a New Planet token. The card’s just great, and you should play it in every green deck you can.
- **De-evolve** — This is certainly an interesting way to word a Pacifism in black. For the most part, this will function the same way, but later in the game you’ll get to exile the creature as soon as the turn starts, which is pretty nice.
- **Deanna Troi, Psychic Counsel** — Deanna Troi, Psychic Counsel in TNG is one of the things that made the show look so ahead of its time, and the card is quite good. Troi’s very good when you cast it early and a lot of your creatures enter with counters, and less good the later you play it.
- **Death Is Irrelevant** — Weirdly enough, Death Is Irrelevant looks basically unplayable in Borg decks. Casting an 8-mana spell in a low-curve aggressive deck simply isn’t going to happen. That said, if you have a deck that can resolve an 8-mana spell, then this will almost always win you the game.
- **Decree of Pain** — For all its different reprints, we’ve not actually seen Decree of Pain in a 1v1 Draft set since it was first printed in Scourge back in 2003. I’m not often in the habit of casting 8-mana spells, but a board wipe that draws a bunch of cards sounds very appealing.
- **Deep Space 9** — Well then. The titular space station of its own show, Deep Space 9 looks like one hell of a big win condition. Potentially four entirely free permanents every single turn is absolutely incredible, and it even spots you the first four as soon as it enters.
- **Deep Space Scan** — If this format is as slow as I suspect it will be, Deep Space Scan could actually be very powerful. However, requiring at least four or five different lands and a slow enough format to accommodate it doesn’t sound like a winning combination to me.
- **Dominion Assault Force** — A 4/4 vigilance creature for 4 isn’t exactly terrible, though I do wish it did a little more than that. A free surveil is definitely nice to have, but not something that elevates Dominion Assault Force beyond being a simple vanilla creature.
- **Dominion Infiltration** — Creating temporary clones like this isn’t a winning play, especially when you need to target your opponent’s creatures with it. You have no control over how good Dominion Infiltration will be when you maindeck it, which is a surefire recipe for a terrible, unplayable card.
- **Dominion Saboteur** — Five mana is a little on the expensive side for a Clone, but that really shouldn’t matter. These types of creatures are always fantastic, and Dominion Saboteur is no different.
- **Dominion Supervisor** — It’s quite funny to see a big, scary Dominion agent being barely better than the adorable Pridemalkin. Dominion Supervisor is a nice card to curve into naturally, and it might be quite good in the late game when you have multiple creatures to gain trample, but it’s kind of average overall.
- **Dr. Beverly Crusher** — An old friend of Captain Picard’s, Dr. Beverly Crusher was the heart of the Enterprise crew for six of the seven seasons of TNG. She was more than just the ship’s doctor; she was often the moral compass that Picard would turn to in his darkest moments.
- **Dr. Joseph M'Benga** — Dr. Joseph M'Benga is interesting, but the main thing to note about this doctor is that it doesn’t do anything by itself. Doubling all your counters is a good ability to have access to, but unless you’re using counters quite frequently, M’Benga basically does nothing.
- **Dr. Leonard "Bones" McCoy** — The final member of our original bridge crew in this review, Dr. Leonard "Bones" McCoy was a brilliant doctor with a good dash of southern charm. His metaphors, hostile personality, and close personal relationship with Captain Kirk turned him into a much-loved character across the whole show.
- **Dr. McCoy, Surly Surgeon** — I could easily be wrong here, but this kind of effect doesn’t seem very good in the first place and even less good on a creature with such weak stats.
- **Drivnod, Carnage Dominus** — There are a few death triggers in this set, but Drivnod, Carnage Dominus doing barely anything else isn’t a great look. At 3 toughness, it really needs that indestructible counter to survive and even then, there are still plenty of ways to answer it.
- **Eject the Warp Core** — Vayne's Treachery was among the best commons in Final Fantasy Limited with good reason. The ability to kill any big creature for just 2 mana, even at the cost of another sacrifice, is very powerful. Eject the Warp Core is exactly the same card, and I have no trouble assuming it’ll be just as good in this setting.
- **Elim Garak, Simple Tailor** — There are so many dilemmas you can face in this set that I’m definitely keeping my eye on any and all dilemma payoffs. Elim Garak, Simple Tailor looks like a very strong one to me.
- **Emergency Holographic Crew** — Spacecraft don’t seem too likely to end up in the graveyard, since you need to station them before they can trade off in combat. That said, getting a sweet two-for-one with Emergency Holographic Crew is hard to pass up.
- **Emergency Medical Hologram** — Emergency Medical Hologram is such an unassuming 1-drop that I think it might actually be good enough. Putting its +1/+1 counter on itself isn’t the best play you could make, as a 1/2 on the board is very weak.
- **Empress Galina** — When Empress Galina was first printed, there were very few legendary permanents in the game. You might have 10 or so in a set to help tell the story. But in a Universes Beyond set, that number is very different. There are 104 legendary permanent cards in Star Trek, and Galina can steal each and every one of them.
- **Encounter the Unknown** — Two damage to all creatures is the base rate on this card, and I think that should be pretty good. We’ve seen a lot of small creatures in the format so far, which leads me to believe it’ll be quite effective.
- **Energize** — This card reminds me a lot of Silent Departure, a very powerful interactive spell in its own right. Like its predecessor, Energize, gives you two potentially powerful tempo spells. They may be sorceries, but resetting a spacecraft, removing an annoying blocker, or whatever else is well worth playing this card for.
- **Engineered Explosives** — Engineered Explosives is a classic Magic card that has been a competitive mainstay for over 20 years. If you have access to more than just two colors, it allows you to destroy just about anything your opponent might put in front of you, which is incredibly flexible for an otherwise colorless removal spell.
- **Ensign Pavel Chekov** — This Ensign Pavel Chekov card has quite a unique design. It might be possible to mill out your opponent if you can combine it with other similar effects, but the most important part of Chekov’s effect is the ability to steal a land from your opponent.
- **Enterprise NX-01** — The Enterprise NX-01 was the ship piloted by the crew from Star Trek: Enterprise. This card certainly has the potential to be powerful, but without knowing how fast the format will be, it’s difficult to evaluate.
- **Erica Ortegas, Plucky Pilot** — Landfall decks often have difficulty with running out of lands to keep triggering their effects, so Erica Ortegas, Plucky Pilot sounds like a reasonable effect to have access to, since it makes it so that you can keep playing lands every turn.
- **Ertai Resurrected** — Even though each ability lets your opponent draw a card, it’s hard to feel too scared of anything your opponent might do if you sit back with an Ertai Resurrected in your hand. Countering or destroying anything at instant speed and putting a 3/2 onto the board is phenomenal. It’s as simple as that.
- **Excalbian Gamemaster** — Okay, this is just absurd. Excalbian Gamemaster is the quintessential “army-in-a-can” creature, giving you a minimum of two 1/1s when it enters and probably a lot more than that if you’ve built around it.
- **Exploratory Mission** — This is a very interesting card advantage engine. It essentially reads as Landfall: Draw a spell”, which honestly sounds really powerful. Like the other cards in this cycle, Exploratory Misson is a 4-mana enchantment that doesn’t affect the board, so its power level is naturally limited.
- **Federation Field Medic** — You had me at “draw a card”. I sincerely hope to always be able to draw a card off of Federation Field Medic. But if we can’t, it’s still pretty good to play it as a 3/2 plus a +1/+1 counter, so this is just a great card for the +1/+1 counters deck.
- **Federation Probe** — Say it with me: “We don’t underestimate 1-drops!” I’m very happy to play Federation Probe on turn 1 and get a surveil along with my flying 1/1, then later in the game it can buff a creature and potentially get a better attack in. But that’s not what makes this card special.
- **Federation Resupply** — A one-off conditional tutor spell isn’t worth spending your mana on, even if what you search for is really powerful. However, Federation Resupply lets you search for two things overall, kind of like a white Mystical Teachings. Is that good enough?
- **Final Frontier** — I’m very much not a fan of 3-mana Rampant Growths, but with the strange new worlds and landfall mechanics, plus the fact that this can fetch a planet dual land, I reckon Final Frontier will be one of the better versions of this effect that we’ve ever seen.
- **First Contact** — The black/green archetype in Star Trek is all about cards leaving your graveyard, and we’ve seen several payoffs that reward you for that. Funnily enough, not only does First Contact fill your graveyard for this strategy, but the card you get back also comes from your graveyard, so it’ll trigger all of these effects.
- **Flare of Fortitude** — While casting this for no mana is quite interesting, the effect isn’t. Flare of Fortitude isn’t something you should put in your deck, no matter how you end up casting it.
- **Free Borg Revolutionaries** — As we’ve seen the last few sets, basic landcycling is a very powerful ability, no matter how bad the creature you put it on happens to be. Free Borg Revolutionaries also happens to be quite a powerful creature when you play it.
- **Fugitive Listener** — The typical Ravenous Rats design has been losing a bit of luster lately, so changing it up and putting that same ability on a 3-drop with a bit more power sounds alright to me.
- **Gabriel Lorca, Mirror Menace** — The surprising villain of Star Trek: Discovery’s first season, played by the brilliant Jason Isaacs, Gabriel Lorca, Mirror Menace is a pretty annoying card. A 5/4 with menace for 5 mana isn’t all that great, but the ability to keep bringing Lorca back whenever you face a dilemma certainly paints it in a new light.
- **General Chang, Cold Warrior** — What usually holds sacrifice decks back is their lack of good, cheap sacrifice outlets. General Chang, Cold Warrior is very cheap both to cast and to activate, but it also gives you an extremely powerful effect, putting counters on all of your Klingons at once.
- **Genesis Device** — As a big fan of Scapeshift in Modern, I’m really liking Genesis Device. The machine capable of turning a lifeless planet into a lush paradise, teeming with life, of course can turn all your lands into new ones. I think the main use of this is simply to ramp you like Final Frontier can.
- **Genesis Effect** — In Wrath of Khan, the Genesis Effect is the process by which a dead planetoid can be transformed into a planet full of life, but it also destroys any trace of life in its path. As such, the effect of this card is a wonderful top-down design.
- **Georgiou, Terran Emperor** — Georgiou, Terran Emperor is a fun and unique design for a 6-drop. As much as I’d love to go down to 0 life and start creating hordes of officer tokens, the fact that you’ll lose as soon as it leaves play makes this strategy a bit delicate. Though if your opponent can’t remove it at all, you’ll inevitably win.
- **Ghost of Illyria** — There are a lot of good enters triggers in Star Trek, so it shouldn’t be too hard for Ghost of Illyria to shine in. Most of the spacecraft also have triggers you can utilize, since this works with any nonland permanent.
- **Gowron, Eager for Battle** — As you’d expect from a rare, multicolored Klingon in this set, Gowron, Eager for Battle is the perfect sacrifice outlet for the deck. By sacrificing a Klingon to Gowron, it gets a +1/+1 counter, trample, and you draw a card.
- **Grand Nagus Zek** — The Ferengi, introduced in Star Trek: The Next Generation, are basically extremist capitalists, where their entire culture and society is built around the acquisition of wealth no matter the cost. So, of course Grand Nagus Zek and the other Ferengi we see here will be Treasure-themed.
- **Gray and Adira, Forever Joined** — The trouble with cards like this is that while it’s supposed to be a payoff for a noncreature spells deck, it requires you to have creatures to use it with. Cards like that have historically not been good.
- **Guidance Failure** — So yeah, we’re back to this design again. Four mana for an instant that puts a target creature on the top or bottom of its owner’s deck. We’ve seen so many variations on Guidance Failure that we know it’s going to be good, especially with the cost reduction to help slot it into more places on the curve.
- **Gul Dukat, Colonial Prefect** — The villainous Cardassian, Gul Dukat, Colonial Prefect, probably won’t do as much damage in Magic as the character did in Star Trek. While the card’s ability is certainly interesting, the fact it costs 7 mana is a significant drawback.
- **Gwyndala, the Progeny** — Gwyndala, the Progeny is a very simple card in Limited. It’s a 2/2 for 2 that leaves behind a very relevant equipment after it’s removed from the field. That’s definitely good, but not too exciting. There isn’t much equipment in Star Trek for its second ability to be of any use in Limited, so it really stops there.
- **H.M.S. Bounty, Unlikely Timeship** — In Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home, Kirk and the Enterprise crew travel back in time to rescue a pod of Humpback Whales and bring them to the 23rd century. The H.M.S. Bounty, Unlikely Timeship is the captured Klingon warbird that they use to do this, making this an incredible top-down design.
- **He's Dead, Jim** — Well, every set worth its salt has one of these. Four mana to destroy any creature at instant speed and give a minor bonus on top is always good, usually one of the best commons in the set, and nothing else needs to be said about it.
- **Hemmer, Fixing What Is Broken** — I was frankly hoping for more out of the signpost uncommon for spacecraft. Perhaps even a powerful spacecraft? Hexproof is a cool ability to give, but Hemmer, Fixing What Is Broken isn’t an exciting enough payoff to move in on this color pair.
- **Highly Illogical** — Four mana Cancels aren’t where we typically want to be, but you can’t cast Cancel on turn 2 to hit a cheap spell. Highly Illogical actually seems like a pretty logical card to play in a blue deck, especially one based around noncreature spells.
- **Hit It!** — Giving all your creatures haste definitely isn’t worth playing a whole card for, so the power of Hit It! has to rely on its other ability. I really dislike how it’s only very likely to ramp you and not simply guaranteed. I just know that small percentage chance will come back to bite me often enough to hate this card.
- **Hive Mind Coprocessor** — If you have successfully “gone wide” by the time you play this card, you should theoretically be able to get an attack in and probably draw several cards for doing so. The fact that you don’t need to pay mana into the triggers from Hive Mind Coprocessor makes it a lot easier to use, too.
- **Holo-Engineer** — Before we even get to the rest of the card, Holo-Engineer starts out life as a 3/3 that creates another 3/3 for just 4 mana. That’s already incredible, and you should play it in every green deck. Now, what can we do with its other ability?
- **Hologram Janeway** — I often talk about the effect of Luminarch Aspirant and how broken it is in Limited. Hologram Janeway has a remarkably similar effect, but with a lot of extra hoops to jump through.
- **Hologram Stalker** — If you were looking for a good payoff for the black/green archetype, look no further than Hologram Stalker. Six mana gets you a good card out of your graveyard, a 2/2, and a 3/3. Incredible! Then, anything else you get out of the graveyard later creates another 3/3.
- **Horta** — Poor old Colossal Dreadmaw, another victim of power creep in the 2020s. Horta is a strong creature if you cast it, but the main reason to put it in your deck is because of its landcycling, just like the other entries in this cycle.
- **Hoshi Sato, Exolinguist** — You don’t need any other creatures on the battlefield for Hoshi Sato, Exolinguist to be worth playing. It counts its own creature types, so you should always be able to look at two cards and pick the best one.
- **Hull Breach** — I think it’s worth main-decking artifact removal in Star Trek, and Hull Breach is certainly good enough for that. If you manage to catch a strong enchantment at the same time, then all the better.
- **Humpback Whales** — Humpback Whales is blue’s basic landcycler, which already makes it quite playable. A 5/6 vigilance creature with ward is certainly more than nothing should you end up casting it, but the fact that you can use Humpback Whales to fix your mana at any point you draw it makes this card extremely useful for any deck.
- **I'm a Doctor, Not a . . .** — It’s great to see another of McCoy’s favorite phrases immortalized on a card like this, but sadly, the card just isn’t very good. It may leave a +1/+1 counter behind, but I'm a Doctor, Not a . . . is still a very mediocre combat trick that I’m not going to want to play very often.
- **Impossible Choice** — The season two episode of Voyager, “Tuvix”, posited one of the show’s best and most well-known ethical conundrums. Tuvok and Neelix end up fused together into one person, and the entire episode is spent debating whether it’s ethical to split them back into their original selves.
- **In the Pale Moonlight** — One of Deep Space Nine’s best episodes, In the Pale Moonlight sees Captain Sisko reflecting on the personal cost he’s paid in attempting to trick the Romulan empire into joining the Federation’s war against the Dominion. This saga tells that story pretty well and is a very strong card to play.
- **Infinite Diversity** — As noble a cause as Infinite Diversity is, it’s a 9-mana spell. It’s pretty close to uncastable in most situations, and it requires a good spell to copy and plenty of creatures in play to fuel it, all of which add up to an unplayable mythic.
- **Interstellar Effigy** — Usually, we like our Man-o'-Wars to be much cheaper than 5 mana, but Interstellar Effigy can still get the job done. It may come down later in the curve, but bouncing a creature while adding to your board is still very powerful.
- **Isolinear Processor** — We don’t get a lot of 2-drop mana rocks for a very good reason; they’re usually too strong. Isolinear Processor may only be restricted to playing artifacts, but there are a lot of good ones to ramp into, and this can be a good way to enable some of the more powerful artifacts in Star Trek.
- **Isshin, Two Heavens as One** — Isshin, Two Heavens as One is a difficult card to get right. In the context of this set, you’re mostly looking at the attack triggers from cards in the red/white archetype, but splashing black in an aggressive Boros deck might not be possible. I’d love to see it happen, but I don’t think it will.
- **Jadzia Dax, Living Legacy** — One of Deep Space Nine’s main characters, Jadzia Dax, Living Legacy is doing a pretty fair impression of a Badgermole Cub. While nowhere near as broken, Jadzia still sits back and creates large amounts of extra green mana for you. You can even use other cards to put +1/+1 counters on it and speed up the process.
- **Jake Sisko, War Correspondent** — Eumidian Terrabotanist is already a fine card, so throw in the ability to investigate sometimes and you have a very nice card. There are a couple ways to enable that second land drop naturally, and you’ll be very happy if Jake Sisko, War Correspondent is able to turn the odd ramp spell into extra Clue tokens.
- **Jett Reno, Problem Solver** — Easily one of the best characters in Star Trek: Discovery, Jett Reno, Problem Solver is quite a nifty little build-around for the dilemmas deck. It’s hard to know how frequently you can trigger it, but I’d imagine that a good version of this deck can do so with relative ease.
- **Jhoira, Weatherlight Captain** — In case you’re unaware, a “historic” spell is any artifact, saga, or legendary spell. Jhoira, Weatherlight Captain is pretty good, allowing you to draw a lot of free cards if you have a deck that can support it, but being a Hill Giant in stats definitely holds it back a lot.
- **Kai Winn, Faithless Leader** — To make the most of Kai Winn, Faithless Leader, you’d want some kind of prior knowledge of your opponent’s hand and/or deck. This card gets a lot better in an open decklists environment, but going in blind? It’ll more often than not be a 3/1 for 2 with very little upside.
- **Kenrith, the Returned King** — No matter which colors you have in your deck, Kenrith, the Returned King should be powerful. Its abilities are expensive, but beyond the red one, any one of them can win you the game if you activate it over and over.
- **Khaaaaaaaaaaaannn!** — This heavily memed moment from Wrath of Khan obviously had to be made into a card of its own, but I don’t have to like it. The card is a fine piece of removal. Khaaaaaaaaaaaannn! will deal 2 damage for 2 mana, 4 damage for 3, 6 damage for 4, and so on.
- **Khan, Despot by Design** — I’m very high on Khan, Despot by Design given the context of set. We’ve seen a lot of creatures with only 1 toughness, including the 1/1 Officer and 2/1 Klingon tokens. That should mean that Khan has a lot of opportunities to come down and devastate a board.
- **Khan, Vengeful Heart** — Khan Noonien Singh is perhaps Star Trek’s most legendary villain. The mythic representing him is quite strong in Limited, but casting Khan from exile isn’t likely to happen too often.
- **Kira Nerys, Firebrand** — Kira Nerys, Firebrand is a very unique and interesting design. While it looks powerful for certain Constructed decks, I can’t think of all that many ways to utilize Kira in Limited, mostly just flash creatures or instant-speed token-makers.
- **Kirk, Commanding Officer** — We’ve not seen a lot of good reasons to care about getting to 8 power’s worth of creatures, despite it being the red/white mechanic. Kirk, Commanding Officer is a good reason though.
- **Kobayashi Maru Scenario** — The Kobayashi Maru Scenario is a test given to Starfleet cadets that have command ambitions. It’s an unwinnable test designed to have the cadet experience fear in the face of certain death. If you hope not to lose to it, you’ll have to find a way to beat it.
- **L'Rell, Fierce Visionary** — Sacrifice decks are always going to want access to as much sacrifice fodder as you can give them. L'Rell, Fierce Visionary is perfect for providing you with an endless stream of Klingon tokens to sacrifice, which should then pair well with the sacrifice payoffs we’ve seen.
- **La'An Noonien-Singh, Security** — Since La'An Noonien-Singh, Security only requires you to attack to trigger, you can often trigger it on the same turn you play it. With at least 4 power already on the board, La’an can come down and allow you to attack profitably past a single blocker.
- **Lieutenant Commander Data** — Perhaps the most beloved character in Star Trek: The Next Generation, Lieutenant Commander Data was a highly sophisticated android whose character arc centered around his quest to better understand humanity. This mythic card does quite a lot if you can enable it.
- **Live Long and Prosper** — You can clearly see what they were trying to do with this card. Doubling your life total means to live long and doubling the cards in your hand means to prosper. I get it, but what if your life total is low and you have no cards in hand?
- **Locutus, the One Who Speaks** — In one of The Next Generation’s best plot twists, the season three finale sees the Borg assimilate Picard into their collective, giving him new life as “Locutus”. Locutus, the One Who Speaks is an incredibly powerful Borg payoff, giving +1/+1 to all your artifact creatures, including itself.
- **Loot, Exuberant Explorer** — Loot, Exuberant Explorer isn’t bad by any means, but it does almost nothing in the early game. A 1/4 is an okay blocker, but you’re not guaranteed to have enough lands to make use of extra land drops.
- **Lore, Positronic Provocateur** — Lore was essentially Data’s “evil twin brother” and a recurring villain in The Next Generation. Lore, Positronic Provocateur isn’t quite as exciting as the character might have you believe. Both abilities are solid, but not exciting.
- **Lt. Cmdr. Geordi La Forge** — Another popular character from TNG, Lt. Cmdr. Geordi La Forge sadly doesn’t have an inspiring card here. Doubling up your station abilities isn’t all that good. I can’t imagine this will be necessary for any blue decks, so even if you do play it, you won’t miss it when you don’t draw it.
- **Lwaxana Troi, Charming Guest** — Lwaxana Troi has Deanna’s mother who could read people’s minds thanks to her Betazoid heritage. As such, she’d often exclaim that Picard or other men around her are thinking about her in a sexual way to embarrass and flirt with them.
- **MACO Strike Team** — I’m sure that MACO Strike Team is going to be incredibly annoying in Constructed, but it seems a bit underpowered for Limited. The fact that it triggers from any of your creatures being targeted is fantastic, but as a 2/1 creature itself, it’s extremely weak and vulnerable.
- **Make It So** — This is a draw spell I really like. Picard’s catchphrase, Make It So, lets you draw two cards and create a 1/1 token, which sounds pretty good for just 4 mana. It also has you face a dilemma, which makes it very desirable for any decks that care about that.
- **Malfunctioning Holodeck** — I love the flavor behind this card. In TNG, the Holodeck would frequently malfunction, trapping its users inside it until the problem could be resolved, so of course this is a Banishing Light variant.
- **Marooned** — The latest sets have had very good variations on this effect in blue, but Marooned is missing the clause of removing the abilities of the thing it enchants.
- **Melkotian Adjudicator** — Since this triggers off not just instants and sorceries, but also some creatures, I quite like it. It’d be easy to compare it to Cetacean Ops, but the fact that it flies makes a big difference.
- **Mikaeus, the Lunarch** — Mikaeus, the Lunarch was an absolute bomb when it was first printed 15 years ago. Nowadays, power creep has settled in a little bit. This feels a fair bit worse than Reality Fracture’s Guiding Hydra, though it’s still strong enough that you should always play it in a white deck.
- **Miles O'Brien, Transporter Ops** — Before Colm Meaney’s Miles O’Brien became the chief of operations on Deep Space Nine, he was the Transporter Chief aboard the Enterprise-D in The Next Generation. Whenever a story involved some sort of transporter malfunction, Chief O’Brien was there to pick up the pieces.
- **Minamo, School at Water's Edge** — Starfleet Academy is situated on the San Francisco Bay, right next to the Golden Gate Bridge. How fitting for it to be here as Minamo, School at Water's Edge. This land is obviously very good, especially with the number of legendary creatures in this set.
- **Mirror Universe Intrusion** — Creating a permanent clone of a creature is one thing, but to have that restricted to only creatures your opponent controls is worrying. Mirror Universe Intrusion has the potential to be a back-breaking card, but if your opponent controls no creatures or only weak ones, it does next to nothing.
- **Mogis, God of Slaughter** — If you can enable the devotion on Mogis, God of Slaughter, then it’s obviously very good. My main concern is that Star Trek is very much not set up to enable that.
- **Moopsy** — I get to say this in every set review, but 1-mana 1/1s with deathtouch are really good. Yet none of them have ever been called Moopsy before, which honestly might be worth an extra point on the grade.
- **Mr. Spock, Science Officer** — Perhaps the most recognizable character in all of Star Trek, Leonard Nimoy’s Mr. Spock, Science Officer is a unique card that can double every single card you draw. Normally, these effects don’t let you draw an additional card in the draw step, but Spock does.
- **Mugato** — I’m not normally in the habit of giving praise to what’s basically a big vanilla creature, but reach and vigilance is such a deadly combination that I think Mugato is actually better than it looks. It’s so hot right now!
- **Munitions Enthusiast** — You don’t need any additional abilities to make a 3/2 trample for 2 mana worth playing. Munitions Enthusiast is a great early play in any red deck, and it trades up extremely well, since the 1 extra point of damage will help it kill bigger creatures in combat.
- **Neelix, Moral Support** — Neelix might be one of the most disliked characters in all of Star Trek; he’s just kind of annoying and often used as comic relief despite none of the jokes really landing.
- **Nog, Ambitious Cadet** — On its own, Nog, Ambitious Cadet can attack as a 3/4 with menace on turn 3 and immediately surveil. From there, if you level it up to its final ability, Nog can easily give you a bunch of free land drops, which also give you the added benefit of having cards leave your graveyard.
- **Nyota Uhura, Communications** — Lieutenant Uhura, played by the wonderful Nichelle Nichols, made waves as one of the first black television characters in a major role in any genre, let alone science fiction. When you play Nyota Uhura, Communications, you should probably name 2 or 3, as they’re the most common mana values of cards in Limited decks.
- **Oberth-Class Starship** — It may take a lot to station this little ship, but the fact that it immediately draws you a card (albeit, a basic Plains) is the key takeaway here. Oberth-Class Starship is a very playable card in any white deck, and there are also some nice synergies to find with it.
- **Obsidian Order Operative** — Facing dilemmas in TRK seems fairly trivial to do. I like having access to a trigger that drains my opponent with each dilemma I face, and a 2/5 makes for a fairly resilient blocker, but Obsidian Order Operative isn’t exciting me like it should.
- **Open Communications** — I love this little card. We’re not typically in the habit of casting 1-mana cantrips without some good reason to do so, but Open Communications will surely act more like a Think Twice. It’s also a great, cheap way to trigger the many cards that care about noncreature spells.
- **Organic Avulsion Unit** — Menace on one creature isn’t a problem. Your opponent can usually deal with it without too much fuss. But menace on your whole team? That’s dangerous. Consider a board where both players have three creatures.
- **Overflow Submatrix** — I don’t think I had a new riff on Lurrus of the Dream-Den on my bingo card for Star Trek, but it does look like that’s what we have in Overflow Submatrix. They may come with finality counters, but the ability to recast any artifact creature in your graveyard over the next few turns is incredible.
- **Pah-Wraith** — Most decks you build will have at least two differently named lands. Namely, the two basic lands for your two colors. As such, Pah-Wraith will basically always attack as a 6/4 with first strike at minimum. On top of that, giving +2/+0 to your team looks pretty good.
- **Pattern Buffer Preservation** — This card reminds me a lot of Final Fantasy’s Resentful Revelation. While Pattern Buffer Preservation doesn’t give you as much card selection, the vastly cheaper beam me up cost means you’ll get to cast both copies of it a lot more often.
- **Peace in Our Time** — This is a cool build-around that might be worth building around, but the idea of letting my opponent draw cards for free doesn’t really inspire me with confidence.
- **Perils of the Past** — First thing’s first, using this as a combat trick is a bad idea. It should probably be what you do around 5% of the time. About a quarter of the cards in this set are artifacts, which is just enough to make artifact removal look like something you want to put in your main deck.
- **Pet Sehlat** — There is really not much difference between Pet Sehlat and a vanilla 3/3 with no downside, the latter of which wouldn’t be playable. Raging Kronch was still pretty good almost a decade ago, and this can turbo-station or hit you 8-power payoffs quickly, so it has merit even when it can’t attack.
- **Phlox, Unorthodox Doctor** — I do love to draw some cards as a reward for doing what an archetype wants, but Phlox, Unorthodox Doctor isn’t quite that simple. Having your creature deal combat damage to a player as well as have a counter on it means there are two ways in which this could go wrong.
- **Photosynthetic Physician** — A 5/5 reach creature for 5 mana is alright, but not exciting. What I actually like about Photosynthetic Physician is its graveyard ability, so I’d rather mill or discard it and then have it leave the graveyard for some value and hopefully trigger one of the green/black payoffs for doing so.
- **Phyrexian Tower** — Lands that can tap for 2 mana should never be underestimated. Phyrexian Tower shouldn’t be randomly put into any deck, but if you have enough sacrifice fodder for it, it should be extremely powerful.
- **Picard, Leading by Example** — While effects like this one are best when you can use them at instant speed, the fact that Picard, Leading by Example starts out by giving +2/+2 means that it can turn just about any creature into a legitimate threat.
- **Planetary Bombardment** — One mana for 4 damage is truly an absurd rate, which is why Flame Slash still sees play in Vintage Cubes to this day.
- **Planetary Patrol** — Assuming you hit your land drops, at least for the first few turns of the game, Planetary Patrol will basically be a 3/3 for 2 mana whenever it attacks. That’s pretty good, and the base of a 2/2 for 2 is perfectly fine. I don’t know if I necessarily want it outside of a landfall deck, but it does look solid.
- **Plasma Cascade** — Plasma Cascade isn’t exactly Lightning Strike, but it’s still a good deal for 3 mana. I love the extra bonus that’s been tacked onto it too, as trading an excess land for a better card is very close to just drawing a card.
- **Primary Unimatrix Adjunct** — Double strike is an extremely powerful ability, so giving it to your whole team should be pretty good. My main problem with Primary Unimatrix Adjunct is just how vulnerable it looks.
- **Prosper, Tome-Bound** — Prosper, Tome-Bound is one of the most popular commanders in my local scene, and for pretty good reason. Having it sit there and effectively draw you an extra card every turn is very powerful. But not only that, your opponent simply can’t attack a lot of creatures into a 1/4 with deathtouch.
- **Pure Energy** — I was a fan of Predictive Preparations in Reality Fracture, though it didn’t really perform well, but I’m not a fan of Pure Energy.
- **Q, Stacking the Deck** — John De Lancie’s mysterious “Q” was a recurring villain from The Next Generation who also showed up in Deep Space Nine and Voyager, a seemingly omnipotent being who would act as a constant annoyance to Picard and the rest of the Enterprise crew. And this weird card design really does suit him.
- **Quark, Humble Proprietor** — I really don’t know what to make of Deep Space Nine’s humble Ferengi barkeep, Quark, Humble Proprietor. It’s a really unique design that basically requires you to sacrifice it at some point to accomplish anything at all. But even when you do, your only reward is a bunch of Treasure tokens.
- **Ravenous Cosmozoan** — While the dream is to cast this for just 1 mana, even at 3 or 4, Ravenous Cosmozoan looks like a great card to cast. Flying appears to be at a premium in this set, so a 3/3 flier will go a long way. It won’t take much to set this up to be playable and a really good spells deck might get to utilize it even more.
- **Reckless Impulse** — Unlike most draw spells, Reckless Impulse isn’t great to play early in a game. It is, however, a very nice card to draw later in the game, as you’re far more likely to have enough time and mana to play both cards you hit with it.
- **Red Alert!** — Okay, Red Dwarf is the first thing I think of when I hear “Red Alert!”, not Star Trek, but so be it. This card is crap anyway. Trumpet Blast is basically never playable and getting a cost reduction on the worst turn to play it isn’t even an upside.
- **Relentless Drednok** — It may not have evasion, but getting to play a Relentless Drednok on turn 1, especially in a Borg deck, is a great way to start off your mana curve. Lifelink is very powerful, even on small creatures, and getting to turn it into a free card later in the game makes it a great card to sacrifice, too.
- **Replicator** — I’ve been playing Pauper a bit recently and am learning how powerful Campfire can be, simply because it’s a cheap artifact that sits in play and gains you life every turn. Replicator has a lot of that same energy going on, as you can just copy the Food token it gives you every turn and use them to stay alive.
- **Resistance Is Futile** — We don’t see a lot of clean 2-mana removal spells anymore. While Star Trek does have a considerable number of artifact creatures, including all of the spacecraft, Resistance Is Futile still answers a lot of creatures that need to be dealt with.
- **Riku of Many Paths** — When it was announced in the late 2000s that there would be a new Star Trek movie with a brand-new recasting of the original Enterprise crew, fans were understandably excited.
- **Rishkar, Peema Renegade** — All the value you get from Rishkar, Peema Renegade is given to you upfront. You don’t need to wait around; you just immediately get your two +1/+1 counters, one probably going on Keenser and the other on your 2-drop. That’s simply excellent, and it gives you quite a sizable early lead in any game in which you play it.
- **Rogue Artificial Intelligence** — Just like the other landcyclers, the fact that this is mana fixing is the main reason to play it. When you do play it on 6, Rogue Artificial Intelligence will have a significant impact on the board no matter which mode you choose, while also being an artifact creature for the Borg and a dilemma for blue/black.
- **Rok-Tahk, Budding Scientist** — I really don’t get this card. Even at 4 or 5 mana, I don’t think it’d be good, so at 6 mana I’m baffled. Rok-Tahk, Budding Scientist is simply over-costed and doesn’t do something that a 6-drop wants to do. The indestructible Murf token is kind of interesting, but it’s such a weird reward for casting a 6-mana beater.
- **Romulan Bird-of-Prey** — Two mana for a 4/4 with flying is obviously an excellent deal, but you have to go through a few extra steps to get there with Romulan Bird-of-Prey. You could station it if you wish, but you can also get there just by casting noncreature spells as you normally would.
- **Rules of Acquisition** — For the most part, Rules of Acquisition looks unplayable to me. Stealing an opponent’s creature is an extremely powerful effect, but allowing your opponent to draw three cards in exchange feels like a trade I never want to make.
- **Rutherford, Upgraded Ensign** — Getting to look at your top six cards means that if you’ve built your deck correctly, Rutherford, Upgraded Ensign will hit an artifact very often. Let’s say there are 10 artifacts left in your deck when you play Rutherford on turn 3. That gives you a 92.63% chance to find at least one artifact.
- **Saavik, Stoic Student** — One could look at Saavik, Stoic Student and see a white Flametongue Kavu. While that may be the case, Saavik is far more situational and quite inefficient.
- **Salt Vampire** — We really have come a long way since the days of Mesmeric Fiend. We don’t see new versions of this effect very often, but when we do, it’s often an upgrade on the previous one. Salt Vampire is an excellent play on turn 2.
- **Sarcophagus, Ship of the Dead** — A lot of the value of Sarcophagus, Ship of the Dead is given to you up front, as even before it’s stationed, it’s basically an Orcish Oriflamme. Of course that’s not enough to justify the whole grade; that +1/+0 boost not only helps you in combat, but also helps you to station.
- **Sarek, Federation Ambassador** — Sarek was a recurring character in multiple Star Trek projects as both Vulcan’s ambassador to Earth and Spock’s father. Sarek, Federation Ambassador simply asks you to control five or more creature types to draw a card.
- **Saru, Unthreatened** — As the moral compass of the U.S.S. Discovery, whenever Michael Burnham strays, Saru is there to set her back on course. Therefore, it’s very appropriate to see Saru, Unthreatened as a creature who saves another creature from danger.
- **Saurian Explorer** — Landfall decks typically suffer from running out of lands and not being able to trigger their abilities anymore.
- **Savvy Profiteer** — Savvy Profiteer is mostly just a 2-drop mana dork in red, which is solid on its own. If you have the time, creating Treasure tokens will allow you to build up your mana over a few turns to cast something really big, or it could just give you some stuff to sacrifice.
- **Scotty, Miracle Worker** — His Scottish accent may have been questionable at best, but James Doohan’s Scotty, Miracle Worker became an integral part of sci-fi history as the Enterprise’s chief engineer.
- **Sela, Tal Shiar Paradox** — One of the most memorable plot twists in TNG was the reveal of this Romulan character, played by Denise Crosby, whose own main character on the show, Tasha Yar, had been killed off a few seasons prior. Sela, Tal Shiar Paradox is first and foremost an excellent payoff for the black/green graveyard deck.
- **Sen Triplets** — Sen Triplets is, as I understand it, a much-maligned commander. Getting to play cards from an opponent’s hand in a 4-player game can be pretty nasty. In 1v1 Limited, it’s just not as good.
- **Set Phasers to . . .** — Looks like we’ll have to Set Phasers to . . . kill most of the time. One mana for 3 damage is a very efficient rate for a removal spell, even if you can only use it in combat. And every now and again, picking stun will let you interact favorably with a bigger creature.
- **Seven of Nine** — You can’t ask for much more than drawing a card whenever you face a dilemma. We know from past experiences that this is among the best kinds of payoff for a Draft archetype, and dilemmas are extremely well supported in Star Trek. Seven of Nine is an excellent build-around for blue/black.
- **Seven of Nine, Unassimilated** — Seven of Nine is without a doubt one of the most interesting characters in the whole franchise. Rescued from the Borg by the crew of Voyager, she embarks on a journey to reclaim her humanity and individuality.
- **Sheoldred, the Apocalypse** — I have… thoughts about this. Benedict Cumberbatch aside, Sheoldred, the Apocalypse is an unbelievably powerful card. While it provides no card advantage whatsoever and might simply die to removal, the simple fact that it punishes your opponent for trying to find that removal is the most crippling part about it.
- **Shields Up!** — Just like Take Up the Shield before it, Shields Up! should be a highly effective combat trick that protects a creature against just about anything. While costing 2 mana isn’t ideal, hexproof and indestructible covers pretty much all your bases and makes it worth playing.
- **Shizo, Death's Storehouse** — Much like Minamo, Shizo, Death's Storehouse is a strict upgrade over a Swamp and an additional land name to enable the strange new worlds mechanic, so you should always play it. Fear is a strong evasive ability too, so you should definitely be able to use this to break some board stalls every now and again.
- **Shuttle Ace** — I don’t see Shuttle Ace being very playable. It’s a very replaceable 5-drop, and it gives very little value when you play it. It’s just very unexciting, and I think we can move on.
- **Sickbay** — Five mana is a lot for this effect, maybe too much. Sickbay at least gives you a +1/+1 counter on the turn you play it and then gives you two counters on any turn in which you played a creature. Food tokens turn into three additional counters, and playing the planet duals also rewards you with extra counters.
- **Silicate Surveyor** — Just about any creature that “draws” a card when it enters is worth playing. Silicate Surveyor really pushes that idea to its limit, since a 4/4 reach creature for 5 mana is very far away from something I’d be happy to play otherwise. It’s a fine card, but when it gets this expensive I wonder if we can do better.
- **Sisay, Weatherlight Captain** — If you’re able to activate the ability of Sisay, Weatherlight Captain, it can definitely be very powerful. But all five colors is a big ask, so I don’t think it’s very likely to happen. The Strange New Worlds archetype might be able to make this work, but it’s a long shot at best.
- **Species 8472** — The trouble with playing any 6-drop is the massive loss of tempo you’ll feel if your opponent is able to remove it as soon as it hits the board. But if your 6-drop gains a bunch of life when it enters, that helps to offset that tempo loss.
- **Spock, Always Your Friend** — We’ve seen a lot of creatures similar to this in the past, like Augur of Bolas or Wandering Mind. They’re typically very good, and Spock, Always Your Friend isn’t only a good rate for this, but also lets you pick up artifacts and enchantments, giving you a much wider range of available options.
- **Stable Wormhole** — Stable Wormhole isn’t a card I’d want to put in an average green deck. On balance, it’s likely just worse than Final Frontier. However, the landfall deck in particular is an aggressive one that really wants to hit more land drops, and this does seem like a good way to enable that turn after turn.
- **Starfleet Academy** — For Starfleet Academy to net you any mana, you need to have at least seven different creature types in play. While that’s not impossible, it doesn’t sound very likely at all, and even when that happens you’d need mana sinks to make it matter.
- **Starfleet Security** — The death trigger is limited to only your turn, but Starfleet Security looks an awful lot like a creature that draws a card when it dies. I don’t make much of it being a 1/1 with haste, but it’s clearly a useful 1-drop that will shine alongside some ways to sacrifice it.
- **Starfleet Spanner Jockey** — On top of being an excellent early blocker, Starfleet Spanner Jockey is quite good at stationing spacecraft. Not being able to attack most of the time might hinder it somewhat, but a 3/3 for 2 mana in Star Trek still has plenty of uses.
- **Stella Lee, Wild Card** — We’ve never had Stella Lee, Wild Card in a Draft set before, but it looks really good. Blue/red’s archetype is based around noncreature spells already, and there are a lot of cheap cantrips that help to enable this.
- **Storied Veteran** — This aggressive Klingon card does a few things on its own. Firstly, the fact that it can come back makes it an excellent creature to sacrifice. Bringing it back of course causes it to leave the graveyard, so it’s also good in black/green.
- **Stray from History** — I’m never a big fan of Coercion effects, but Soul Salvage is definitely something I can get behind. Stray from History looks like a very solid common that I’ll probably always want at least one copy of in a black deck.
- **Subcommander T'Pol** — Subcommander T'Pol is quite a powerful creature. I’d already be very happy to have a Frost Lynx with flash, effectively giving you an additional turn of that creature being stunned.
- **Sulu, Space Swashbuckler** — Oh my, indeed! The legendary George Takei brought Mr. Sulu to life, the pilot of the Starship Enterprise. Sulu, Space Swashbuckler recreates one of the character’s most iconic moments from the show with a heck of a lot of style (editor’s note: Yes, we know that “Oh, my!”
- **Support Mission** — I hate playing cards like this. Support Mission does nothing to affect the board on the turn you play it, and that’s the key problem here. While all the mission cards in this cycle have that drawback, they all make up for it by having considerably better effects than this does.
- **Suurok-Class Science Vessel** — It’s 3 mana and it draws a card. I’m sold. While I doubt this will be as good as cantrip creatures of the past, Suurok-Class Science Vessel still has all the hallmarks of a brilliant common for blue.
- **Suus Mahna Practitioner** — “Tapper” creatures tend to appear in white, so this card is throwing me off somewhat. The first two land names are pretty trivial, so Suus Mahna Practitioner should never cost more than 3 mana to activate.
- **Sylvia Tilly, Joy of Discovery** — Tilly was, by far, my favorite character in Discovery. She was so sweet and pure, and her struggles with anxiety and self-doubt were inspiring to me at a difficult time in my life. Her card, Sylvia Tilly, Joy of Discovery, has a few things going on. It’s primarily a payoff for the graveyard deck.
- **Syndicate Liquidators** — Giving two +1/+1 counters to any target creature is a really nasty effect to have on this creature.
- **Tactical Officer** — As a 2-mana 2/1, Tactical Officer really needs to make use of its ability. Unfortunately, that ability isn’t even active until you have a board full of creatures, so I don’t think this is something you want to play unless you just really need a 2-drop.
- **Tal Shiar Analyst** — Thermo-Alchemist was one of my favorite spells build-arounds from the past. Tal Shiar Analyst, despite costing an additional mana, is practically the same card.
- **Tal Shiar Operative** — This creature essentially attacks as a 2/2 with deathtouch, which is going to be pretty hard for a lot of opponents to block profitably. As such, Tal Shiar Operative is a simple enough common that can provide you with a repeatable source of dilemmas. That’s definitely useful to some decks.
- **Talon Gates of Madara** — It’s pretty cool to be able to hide a protection spell in your land base like this. Talon Gates of Madara looks pretty strong to me. We’re already happy playing it as Unknown Shores with upside, and sometimes it’ll be really powerful when you get to use its ability at the right time.
- **Targ Wrangler** — Unlike the Pet Sehlat, Targ Wrangler looks extremely good for an oversized 3-drop with a downside. The thing is, we’ve seen a lot of cards act as sacrifice fodder so far, which means it shouldn’t be too hard to negate this drawback completely.
- **Tasha Yar, Out of Time** — Tasha Yar, Out of Time is a fast, aggressive creature with a great death trigger, and you can bring it back later for one final hit. Tasha gives you quite a lot of advantage for a single card; you just have to find ways to make the most of it.
- **Tatyova, Benthic Druid** — Tatyova, Benthic Druid is one of my favorite “mythic uncommons” of the past. The fact that it draws you a card every time you play a land means you naturally draw into more lands and can keep triggering it every turn.
- **Tea, Earl Grey, Hot** — Captain Picard’s go-to drink is well known among Trekkies. Tea, Earl Grey, Hot are words uttered in countless episodes of TNG. The card is really good too, you’re probably always happy with a 2-mana artifact that draws a card in any deck that cares about noncreature spells or artifacts.
- **Tell Me What You See** — In this classic TNG episode, Picard is captured by the Romulans and is subjected to psychological torture through gaslighting. His captor keeps insisting that he’s being shown five lights when there are only four, but our intrepid Captain never compromises, always repeating that there are just four lights.
- **Temporal Conqueror** — We’ve seen this exact card in previous sets between Temporal Conqueror, Eccentric Farmer, and Ostrich-Horse, which all varied in power level. In Star Trek, getting the land back from your graveyard triggers gravebreak effects, allowing it to be an even better card later in the game.
- **Tenacious Drone** — While I’d prefer the stats of this creature to be the other way around (a 1/1 that dies into a 2/1), Tenacious Drone is still a high quality 2-drop that doesn’t mind being sacrificed. It clearly should work in the Borg deck, but it’s probably good enough for the Klingon sacrifice deck, too.
- **Test of Conviction** — Symmetrical draw spells, even ones that aren’t entirely symmetrical, are never very good. As such, we’re really looking at Test of Conviction as a spell that distributes +1/+1 counters, which frankly isn’t very good either.
- **Test of Courage** — While I think artifact removal is worth main-decking in TRK, one as restrictive as this doesn’t seem good enough. Test of Courage feels like a solid sideboard card and nothing more.
- **Test of Identity** — Some decks are going to be perfectly happy casting this as Thrill of Possibility, while Klingon decks are going to want it as a cool combat trick that you can essentially cycle. Test of Identity looks like it hits enough notes that it’ll see play, but it’s not something you should go out of your way to pick up.
- **Test of Loyalty** — Given the black/green theme, I can definitely see wanting there to be a Raise Dead in this format, and here it is. You’ll use Test of Loyalty most often simply to get a creature back, as guaranteeing yourself a castable spell is pretty much always better than drawing a random card.
- **Test of Priorities** — Instant speed flicker spells aren’t printed too often anymore, so it’s nice to see this one here. For the most part, Test of Priorities is a cheap cantrip for both the dilemmas deck and the noncreature spells deck.
- **Thada Adel, Acquisitor** — Blue decks in TRK Limited are bound to have a few artifacts in them, so Thada Adel, Acquisitor is likely to be an unblockable 2/2 that rips apart your opponent’s deck, potentially giving you a bunch of free cards too.
- **The Caretaker** — Facing dilemmas seems remarkably easy to do in Star Trek, and The Caretaker has got to be the best payoff for them. You can trigger it in every end step, so instant-speed modal spells might be able to add up to two triggers every turn cycle.
- **The City on the Edge of Forever** — Widely regarded as the best episode of the original series, and I’m inclined to agree, The City on the Edge of Forever sees a deranged McCoy time travel back to the 1930s and inadvertently alter the present so that Starfleet never existed.
- **The Destruction of Praxis** — Board wipes are very powerful in Limited, but the biggest downside they have is that your opponent gets the initiative to make the first play after the dust settles. If you have six different lands in play, sweeping away all creatures and then creating a pair of 2/1s with haste is an absurd effect.
- **The Doctor, EMH** — At the bare minimum, The Doctor, EMH represents a 3/3 and two +1/+1 counters for 5 mana, which is exactly what I’d expect for that mana cost. If you have any other +1/+1 counters in play though, you can get so much more out of it.
- **The Duras Sisters** — A 3/2 with a very good death trigger for just 2 mana is really good. Better yet, having a hybrid mana cost means that you’ll never fail to cast The Duras Sisters on turn 2 in a black/red Klingons deck. This is just the perfect aggressive 2-drop for that deck, and something I’d prioritize early.
- **The Holodeck** — The Holodeck was probably one the coolest innovations for The Next Generation as it allowed the writers to tell a greater range of stories in the series.
- **The Inner Light** — Another of The Next Generation’s greatest episodes, The Inner Light sees the Enterprise encounter a probe in deep space that forces Picard to live out a lifetime as a member of a long dead alien race. It’s a beautiful story, and it makes for a very powerful saga.
- **The Measure of a Man** — This iconic TNG episode is one of my favorites by far, but sadly the card version leaves much to be desired. The Measure of a Man would be great if you had a way to bounce or sacrifice it before the final ability goes off, but I don’t think TRK has much of that.
- **The Naked Time** — The Naked Time was the fourth episode of the original series of Star Trek. In this episode, an infection spreads throughout the crew leaving them feeling intoxicated, leading to all sorts of weird incidents. This card, unfortunately, doesn’t look very good.
- **The Needs of the Many** — In the climax of The Wrath of Khan, Spock sacrifices his own life to save the Enterprise crew from Khan’s final gambit. With his final, dying breath, he simply reminds Kirk that “The Needs of the Many outweigh the needs of the few, or the one.” This card isn’t particularly good.
- **The Traveler** — I really hoped for more out of the blue/green signpost uncommon. The Traveler has the potential to have a lot of power, but that 3 toughness means you’ll find it difficult to put that power to use, as it trades off with anything that has 3 power of its own.
- **The Wrath of Khan** — I’ve mentioned it a few times already, but 1982’s Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan is believed by many Trekkies to be the best Star Trek project ever produced. This card does a reasonable job of telling this story, and it’s quite powerful, too.
- **There's Coffee in that Nebula** — In only the fifth episode of Voyager, the crew find that their energy levels are critically low, meaning Janeway can’t use the replicators to make a good cup of coffee.
- **Those Old Scientists** — Those Old Scientists is a fine draw spell if you have something to get back with the second chapter, but it loses you card advantage if you don’t. I don’t think a lot of decks will be interested in playing this, but it’s certainly not out of the question in a good noncreature spells build.
- **Torbran, Thane of Red Fell** — The triple red on Torbran, Thane of Red Fell is definitely a downside, but the effect you get is incredibly powerful. I especially like how this will buff the damage from both the 1/1 Officer tokens and the 2/1 Klingon tokens.
- **Transporter Malfunction** — I always try to pay attention whenever I see a removal spell that I can cast twice. There are so few removal spells with flashback in the game, let alone ones that are this efficient. You’d happily play Transporter Malfunction even if it didn’t have beam me up, so the fact that it does is a huge boon.
- **Tricorder** — Short Sword variants crop up time and time again, and they’re usually only slightly outside the realm of what’s playable. I believe Tricorder gives you enough upside on top of your usual +1/+1 to play this in a few decks, though I’d also cut it from plenty of others.
- **Troubling Tribbles** — The tribbles are a weird creature in Star Trek lore that multiply exceedingly quickly. Troubling Tribbles does exactly that, but they’re next to useless when they do.
- **Type-2 Phaser** — I’m not sure we’ll be setting our phasers to stun any time soon. While it’s cool that this stuns a creature when it enters, I can’t help but be put off by that expensive equip cost. Type-2 Phaser does some good things, but 3 to equip when it doesn’t give any boosts to toughness feels a bit too much.
- **U.S.S. Cerritos, Following Up** — I laughed at S.H.I.E.L.D. Helicarrier and called it unplayable. Players were so desperate for flying creatures in Marvel Super Heroes that it actually performed fine, but I still maintain that it was awful.
- **U.S.S. Discovery, at Black Alert** — When designing the card, U.S.S. Discovery, the Black Alert, of course the discover mechanic made sense to use. Getting to discover 5 when Discovery enters or attacks is huge, and it’s even one of the easier spacecraft to station!
- **U.S.S. Enterprise-A, Free Spirit** — The U.S.S. Enterprise-A, Free Spirit has to be one of the strongest spacecraft we’ve ever seen. It’s essentially a Baneslayer Angel that also creates three 1/1s on entering. It only requires six charge counters to become a creature, which the three 1/1s can provide over the course of two turns.
- **U.S.S. Enterprise-D, Galaxy-Class** — The legendary ship captained by Jean-Luc Picard, the U.S.S. Enterprise-D, Galaxy-Class is one of the more powerful spacecraft in this set. It essentially gives you an impulse draw the first time you station it in a turn, which is something you can easily control.
- **U.S.S. Excelsior, Cutting Edge** — Destroying a tapped creature isn’t always going to be something you can do, but when you can, the U.S.S. Excelsior, Cutting Edge will be a strong two-for-one. While it will be hard to station, as long as you get to remove a creature, you don’t even need to do so right away.
- **U.S.S. Reliant, From Hell's Heart** — The Reliant is the ship in Wrath of Khan that Khan commandeers and takes into battle against Kirk and the Enterprise. U.S.S. Reliant, From Hell's Heart looks like it has a lot of potential.
- **U.S.S. Voyager, Lone Wanderer** — The classic U.S.S. Voyager, Lone Wanderer of course has to do with lands. The Voyager crew became the first in Starfleet to explore the uncharted Delta Quadrant on their way home to Earth.
- **Una Chin-Riley, Number One** — This kind of effect isn’t bad in theory, but costing 5 mana is just far too much for Una Chin-Riley, Number One to be something I’m ever particularly happy to play.
- **Unexplored Territory** — While you get access to some mana on the turn you play it, on balance Unexplored Territory is simply worse than an Evolving Wilds, which we have a precedent for in how poor Shire Terrace was in The Lord of the Rings. That’s not to say it’s bad or unplayable, but it doesn’t feel like the easy slam dunk that it could be.
- **V'Ger, the Intruder** — This is one hell of a big payoff for a ramp deck. V'Ger, the Intruder does everything I want out of a 7-drop. As a 5/7 flier, it should close out games relatively quickly.
- **Vash, Freelance Archaeologist** — I think the archetype that impresses me the most in terms of payoffs is the black/green one. We’ve seen several good ways to have things leave your graveyard for profit, and Vash, Freelance Archaeologist is just another great reward for that. There’s not much else to say.
- **Vengeance Mission** — Building this up to seven quest counters sounds like quite a lot. It’s worth noting that creatures trading off in combat will of course trigger this twice, so maybe seven counters isn’t too difficult, but I’d still view Vengeance Mission as only hitting your opponent for 1 each time it triggers.
- **Vexing Drone** — Tapping an opponent’s creature is a pretty nice ability to have in an aggressive deck, and Vexing Drone has the magic stats of a 2/2 for 2, meaning we’d probably play it regardless. This is a solid creature for the Borg decks that actually gets better later in the game.
- **Vulcan Mind-Meld** — The Vulcan Mind-Meld is a skill whereby Vulcans can share their thoughts with other living beings and vice versa.
- **Warp Core** — Using Warp Core to find a basic land makes it playable in a lot of different decks. I’m intrigued by its ability to station your spacecraft though, as doing so with any excess mana you have will take a lot of pressure away from your creatures.
- **Warp Speed** — At first glance, Warp Speed seems like it’s doing a lot. It fetches you a land, it guarantees your lands all enter play untapped, and it can turn itself into two extra cards in the late game. The problem is that it costs 3 mana to not affect the board and to not accelerate your mana, and that is not good.
- **Wesley Crusher, Starfleet Cadet** — “Shut up Wesley!” – Captain Picard Perhaps one of the most divisive characters in Star Trek, Wesley Crusher was a young upstart boy genius who aspired to become a full Starfleet officer. I personally loved the character, but there we go. Wesley Crusher, Starfleet Cadet has a lot of possible options going for it.
- **Will Riker, First in the Field** — As a frequent member of landing parties, Will Riker, First in the Field is all about landfall. While having no landfall triggers itself, Riker creates two landfall triggers every turn.
- **Worf, Honor-Bound Warrior** — I’m sure Worf would be the one to tell you that a warrior is patient and waits for the right moment to strike. Worf, Honor-Bound Warrior is perfect for sitting back, building up counters, and then striking when it’s big enough to attack profitably into your opponent’s blockers.
- **Worf, Intelligence Contractor** — Worf was the first Klingon officer to serve in Starfleet, and he’s the most used character in the entire franchise. This card, Worf, Intelligence Contractor leaves quite a bit to be desired.
- **Xenobotanist** — Yep, a 2-drop mana dork in green. Xenobotanist is a great card. It fixes your mana, and you should take it very highly.
- **Xindi Surveyors** — I’d call this virtually unplayable in many sets. But in Star Trek, landfall is surprisingly well supported. A turn-3 Xindi Surveyors might end up a 6/5 on turn 4, so I could definitely see this being playable in that deck. But I wouldn’t want to play it elsewhere.
- **Year of Hell** — Certainly my favorite episode of Voyager, this two-parter sees Janeway and the crew navigating through the space of a hostile race with temporal manipulating weapons for an entire year. These people have used their weapons to erase entire enemy races from history, and now they’re trying to do the same to Voyager.
- **Zefram Cochrane, Warp Pioneer** — Played by the brilliant James Cromwell in Star Trek: First Contact, Zefram Cochrane, Warp Pioneer was the inventor of warp drive who made first contact with the Vulcans, ushering in an era of peace for the whole world.
- **Zero, Incorporeal Sensate** — As far as annoying little artifact creatures go, Zero, Incorporeal Sensate is certainly up there. It’s essentially an uncommon Elite Spellbinder that also works with all of your Borg payoffs, despite not being a Borg itself.
