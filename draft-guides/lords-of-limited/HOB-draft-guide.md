# HOB — The Hobbit (Lords of Limited)

## ⚠ Recency rule (read first)

**Two episodes, and the second one is the real guide.** The 2026-08-11 crash course was recorded on Arena release day with **no drafts played** — Ben Morning and Ethan Sachs skipped previews, skipped first impressions, and neither prereleased (*"we're kind of going in blind"*). The 2026-08-17 episode is their **week-one post-play info dump**: Ethan drafted heavy blue to mythic, Ben got shoved into red-black repeatedly and won a Collector Booster box, both are ~10 drafts deep, best-of-one, pick-one only.

**Where the two conflict, 2026-08-17 wins.** The crash-course grades were predictions; the week-one reads are from games.

## Supersessions

Crash-course take → week-one take, and the verdict.

- **Colour rankings: blue jumped from #3 to co-#1.** Crash course: *Black > Red > Blue > White > Green*. Week one: **Black 1, Blue 2 (clear), Red 3, White 4, Green far last** — Ben *"almost wanted to hot-take blue at number one."* Both now describe blue and black as *"head and shoulders above the rest."*
- **The format is colours, not colour pairs.** The single biggest reframe of the episode. Ben: *"In my head, this format is not color pairs. It is colors."* Ask **am I base blue or base black**, then ask what comes along for the ride. Decks routinely end up 11 Islands / 11 Swamps plus a handful of a second colour, because a small set means the redundant pieces keep coming. Red and white are **support colours**; green is a non-colour without rares.
- **G/U fell from #3 to last.** Crash course ranked Elves third on *"sweet-looking signposts."* Week one: neither host will touch green — *"I don't want to touch green with a ten-foot pole"* — and the new order is **B/R > blue-X > U/W > B/G > R/W dwarves > U/G**. This matches 17Lands, where GU is the worst supported pair.
- **Crude Bent Blade was a sleeper; it is now the format's defining card.** Crash course had it as a notable common with *"diminishing returns in multiples."* Week one it is format truth #1: *"how your cards and deck match up against this card on turn three is the crux of the speed of this format."* They no longer diminish it in multiples — three or four is normal.
- **Goblin Plate Mail is the #1 common, over Pinecone Strike.** Crash course had Pinecone Strike as broken and possibly the best common. Ben's week-one top five: **Goblin Plate Mail > Pinecone Strike > Bilbo Baggins Burglar > Crude Bent Blade > Rage into the Valley**, and he takes Plate Mail over all of them — including over Settle the Wreckage pack-one-pick-one.
- **Bilbo Baggins, Burglar may not even be blue's best common.** Ben talked himself into **Lakeshore Apothecary** as the more load-bearing card: Bilbo is replaceable, the Apothecary is the game plan. He still takes Bilbo first because it wheels less predictably.
- **Velvetwing Butterflies: Ben was right, Ethan concedes.** Ethan's crash-course top white common is a week-one **mover down** — *"not a card as far as I'm concerned."*
- **Wood Elves: Ben was wrong.** His *"stinker"* is now specifically good — it is the green card that survives Crude Bent Blade, and *"extremely annoying"* to play against.
- **Gathering of Darkness is worse than it reads.** Unintuitive week-one reversal: red-black runs only 8–10 creatures and much of the format's removal exiles, so the gravedigger half misses. **Rage into the Valley is now firmly better.**
- **Not a splashy format — confirmed and hardened.** *"You're not supposed to splash in this format."* Only splash a card that does the specific thing you need (removal), and cut tap lands down to **one, sometimes zero** — stumbling on a tapped land loses games outright.
- **Blocking: still unresolved, now with sides.** Ben (red-black): blocking is effectively illegal, play defence with removal, always be attacking. Ethan (blue): on the draw he takes every trade offered, and thinks refusing to trade is *why* the play/draw gap is so large. Both agree the gap is real — the Discord figure quoted is ~56% on the play vs ~44% on the draw.

## Format speed / meta read

**Ben's five format truths:**

1. **HOB is Lord of the Rings 2.0 on colour power.** Black > Red > Blue > White > **Green last**. His own caveat: he *secretly* thinks blue is better than red and black because it is the deepest at common.
2. **Archetype power rankings: B/R (strong first) > W/U > G/U > R/W > B/G.** R/W moves up if the format turns out hyper-aggressive.
3. **[Pinecone Strike](https://scryfall.com/card/hob/107/pinecone-strike) is broken** — *"as good here as Lightning Strike was bad in Marvel Super Heroes."* It answers nearly every top uncommon: *"Silvan, you thought you were going to recur that? Nope. You tapped out on turn four for the Chief Warg hoping for a card? Nope."*
4. **Blackjack theory — you want your deck hot.** Count cards as +1 (premium), 0 (fine in its home), −1 (doesn't belong). HOB has an unusual number of bad and meh cards, so the win is in **avoiding the −1s**, not finding the +1s.
5. **Off-colour decks are live, especially U/R and U/B**, because the Grixis cards are so juiced. Structurally: each pair gives up only **two hybrid cards**, and outside the black-red one they're not great — so the cost of ignoring a signpost is low. Contrast Secrets of Strixhaven, which had ~10 gold cards per pair and made off-pairs impossible.

**Ethan's five format truths:**

1. **HOB will be a resource war.** Two-for-ones are both abundant *and* impactful — equipment that arrives with an amass token, creatures that draw. From his MTGO games: *"every time someone did it, I was like, yep, that feels real."*
2. **You can't stop storied, so don't try.** Assume both R/W and W/U players get there. Don't pick a legend out of a pack to deny it; only kill one you already wanted dead.
3. **Looting is great, 1/1 tokens are not.** See the recruit disagreement above.
4. **The format is more about creature types than the commons let on** — the payoff for tribal sits at rare, so it will *feel* tribal all the time even though the commons don't read that way.
5. **Grindy black decks vs. assertive white decks** — revised live toward *"or is it grindy black vs. powerful blue?"* Ben pushes back that black isn't grindy at all: *"black gets to do it all"* — best early game **and** best late game, like green-black elves in Eclipsed.

**Other structural reads:**

- **A Grixis format at common.** Both land on blue-black-red being the depth, and the episode closes with both hosts declaring they'll **force U/B or U/R on day one**.
- **Removal maths.** Ben relays Sierkovitz's analysis: HOB has an outlier **glut of X/2s**, few X/3s, and a bump of X/4s, so you trade down on removal often. Ben doesn't fully buy the conclusion but agrees **cheap interaction will be great**.
- **Removal is matchup-dependent.** One-for-one removal is excellent against the bomb-and-engine decks and poor against the trinkety recruit/amass boards — *"I don't want to point anything at those."* Blue-white specifically plays resiliently against targeted removal.
- **Not a splashy format.** Fixing exists (treasures, the halfling-cycling Evolving Wilds) but the power sits **low on the curve**, and you don't splash efficient two- and three-drops. The fixing's real job is getting you into the five *unsupported* colour pairs, not into three colours.
- **Excitement level: lukewarm.** Ben expects a shallow format — *"pretty Core Set-ish, the good cards are pretty obviously good"* — with the off-colour decks as the most interesting thing in it. Both are drafting **pick-one, not pick-two**.

### Week-one format truths (2026-08-17, supersedes the pre-format truths above)

1. **Crude Bent Blade is a defining feature of the format.** Build and mulligan with turn-three Crude Bent Blade in mind; carry a spare body as *"Crude Bent Blade insurance"* (Goblin-town Flunkies in the mirror, Wood Elves in green, a second recruit body in blue).
2. **Getting deep into blue or black is the best way to start a draft.** Both colours check every box on their own — payoffs, enablers, interaction, card advantage — so you can be happy with commons.
3. **Deck building is where the edge is, not the draft.** You will end with 30+ playables; the last five slots are the puzzle. Cut cards that don't line up against Crude Bent Blade, and ask what the deck looks like **on the draw**.
4. **Always be attacking.** In the red-black mirror, planning to block from behind is *"signing up to almost 100% of the time lose the game."*
5. **Mulligan more; no loose keeps.** *"Phyrexia: All Will Be One levels"* of needing a turn-two play. Three lands + one castable spell is not a keep.

**Other week-one structural reads:**

- **Play/draw is a big deal** (~56/44 by the numbers quoted). Build the deck that still functions on the draw.
- **The games start early and snowball hard**, but blue can out-grind red-black if it survives turns three to five — the same arc as original Lord of the Rings, which this format resembles closely.
- **Five-drops need to be special; six- and seven-drops mostly aren't playable.** Anything that needs to untap before it does something is too slow.
- **Blue's own removal is weak** (Enchanted River's Grasp, Uneasy Partings are just fine), so blue decks want **single-pipped white interaction** — Magnificent End and Celebrate the Mountain-king — as the second colour.
- **The 17Lands disagreement, stated on air.** Sorting by archetype win rate on 2026-08-16 put both blue pairs *last*, which Ben calls *"so counterintuitive to my experience"* — the commons leaderboard is all Grixis cards. Their hypothesis: heavy-blue decks get bucketed as mono-blue or as an off-pair, and blue is bad in any deck **not** built on Lakeshore Apothecary. Treat blue as a base-colour read, not a colour-pair read.

## Archetypes

**Week-one ranking (2026-08-17): B/R > blue-X > U/W > B/G > R/W dwarves > U/G.** Ethan's ranking of what to pair with base blue: **white > black > red >> green**. Ben's caveat on U/B specifically — *"two great tastes that don't taste great together"*: blue wants to grow Apothecaries and grind, black wants to amass and pressure, and the deck often can't decide which it is.

**Red-black quick hits (Ben, ~10 drafts).** Identify early whether you are **aggro** or **midrange** — the aggro build doesn't want Smaug, the Great Calamity or Bolg of the North at all. Shove the curve low, take Tidings of War for the turn-one amass, never cut Rage into the Valley, prioritise cheap removal by efficiency (Stir Up Trouble > Bilbo's Deadly Slice, Crude Bent Blade over both). Sacrifice targets for Stir Up Trouble, in order: a spent equipment, the Goblin-town Flunkies token, the Stony-Voiced Goblins body. The sweet spot is throwing a **4–5 power menace army** at them over and over, so they have to spend a card each time.

**Blue quick hits (Ethan, mythic).** The deck is predicated on a **critical mass of Lakeshore Apothecary and Master's Conciliator — four or more**, so you can reliably play one on two. Core curve: Bilbo adventure on one → Apothecary on two → Bilbo on three → double two-drop or Long Lake Nuisance on four, then draw two cards every turn thereafter. **Trap:** thinking you want spells that are good from the graveyard. You don't — pitch real spells to recruit; Plunder the Trollshaws is the one card you actively want to discard. Second trap: playing bad cards because recruit makes them *"free."* Master's Conciliator can genuinely mill the blue mirror out.

Ranked by Ben's crash-course power rankings (pre-format, superseded above).

**1. B/R Goblins — amass + sacrifice.** *"Extremely juiced."* Both hosts agree it gets the best cards at every rarity. The refrain: *"if we look at everything red-black we're like yes, yes, yes, and every other colour we're like okay, maybe."* Black is the best colour, red's commons want to be here rather than in R/W, and the pair gets the only hybrid card worth splashing.

**2. W/U Humans — recruit + draw-two.** Ethan's pick and the deck he likes. Functional, cheap, efficient, with a mechanic that is *"good and smooth."* Ben's structural point in its favour: even where a signpost only half-supports the archetype, W/U survives it because the underlying deck works.

**3. G/U Elves — landfall + ramp.** *"Some sweet-looking signposts."* Both rate Silvan Reveler and Thranduil highly. The concern is tempo: doing nothing on turn three casting Silvan Rally *"is asking for a boatload of trouble"* against a black-red deck.

**4. R/W Dwarves — storied + equipment.** Moves up if the format is hyper-aggressive. Ben on the signposts: *"the red-white signposts do not look good to me"*, and Ethan agrees. Storied itself is trivially easy to turn on and lives almost entirely in the Jeskai colours.

**5. B/G Wolves — ferocious. Ben ranks it last and is *"not a believer at all."*** The thesis: green and black want opposite games. Black wants goblins, sacrifice and grinding value; green wants elves, landfall and ramp. *"I don't think black's best cards want to get paired with green's cards, and I don't think green's best cards want to get paired with black's."* Ethan's concession: it'll look like **either** a green elves deck with black removal **or** a Rakdos deck with green support — not a fusion — *"which is not a great sell, because then the one colour you're primary in has to be extremely open."* Both flag that [The Chief Warg](https://scryfall.com/card/hob/150/the-chief-warg) not having four power itself is a design miss.

## Card notes

### White

- **Celebrate the Mountain-king** — both hosts' #1 white uncommon. "Absolutely premium, one of the best uncommons in the set." Removal plus a recruit.
- **Eagle of the Great Shelf** — Ben's #2 white uncommon pre-format; week-one **mover down** — another five-drop that only pays off if you untap, and five-drops need to be special.
- **Iron Hills Blacksmith** — Ethan's #2 white uncommon. Two pieces of cardboard, wants to wear equipment, and is a storied enabler that's hard to remove.
- **The Mountain-king's Return** — honorable mention, left off both lists. Loot plus a reanimated three-drop plus a counter is "a lot of juice from a three-mana play", but it's "a disaster if you don't have a three-drop in the graveyard".
- **Magnificent End** — white's only common removal, "full stop". Week one: **excellent, but in blue decks, not white ones** — two-mana instant-speed removal a heavy-blue deck holds up alongside Plunder the Trollshaws. Same logic makes Celebrate the Mountain-king blue's other wanted splash.
- **Velvetwing Butterflies** — week-one **mover down**, Ethan concedes: *"this card does not matter."*
- **Dwarven Provisioner** — the best of white's three common team-pumps and "a real card". The open question isn't whether white's go-wide plan is supported, it's whether it can compete with what red-black does.

- **Lake-town Lookout** — Ethan tried to defend it as Crude Bent Blade insurance; Ben's rule wins — *"don't play bad cards to make their good cards worse."*
### Blue

- **Bilbo, Luckwearer** — both hosts' #1 blue uncommon. "They finally made Switcheroo playable" by stapling it to a looter; the joke line is trading your recruit token for their best creature.
- **Old Fat Spider Can't See Me** — *"in the data dumpster,"* and both hosts are massive fans anyway. It neuters an amass army without letting red-black rebuy the trade, and the free chapter-three/four draws trigger blue's draw-two payoffs without spending mana. Ethan's heuristic reversed from *"hold it"* to **get it down as early as possible**.
- **Bilbo Baggins, Burglar** — top-three common, but week one it is **not clearly blue's most important card** — Lakeshore Apothecary is. Take Bilbo first anyway; the Apothecaries are the ones that wheel.
- **Plunder the Trollshaws** — "the full package". Pitch it to recruit, then have a draw-two sitting in the graveyard.
- **Lakeshore Apothecary** — the crash-course hot take is now the whole blue deck. Ethan wants **four-plus** copies of the draw-two two-drops so one lands on turn two every game; he has had 5/5 and 4/4 Apothecaries in separate decks. Blue without Apothecary as the plan is *"horrible."*
- **Uneasy Partings** — good, but Ethan left it off: a little expensive for the threats you want to answer, and it doesn't remove them permanently.
- **Gandalf, Wandering Wizard** — both hosts out. "They did him dirty." Five mana for a 4/5 ward 3 with a six-mana bail-out clause, and no archetype actually wants him.
- **Enchanted River's Grasp** — one of Ben's two notable omissions. Too much bounce, self-bounce and sacrifice running around for an aura to stick.
- **Thranduil's Decree** — Ethan was initially high on this six-mana counter, then never wanted to pick it in the practice draft: "that must not be very good if I don't actually want to pick it out of a pack."

- **Master's Conciliator** — the second half of blue's two-drop core. Grows to a 5/3 vigilance quickly, and multiples genuinely mill out the blue mirror. Fill your own graveyard to seven first.
- **Confiscate and Bother** — a draw-two trigger you can hold up on their turn; part of why the blue deck plays at instant speed.
- **Thrór's Map** — mostly a **blue** card: a repeatable way to trigger draw-two. Never your turn-two play.
### Black

- **Gollum the Abandoned** — Ethan's #1 black uncommon. "Hits like a truck on turn two" — the drain happens even when there's nothing to exile, and exiling flashback cards off a recruit is oppressive. The threat of recursion is real.
- **Gnashing of Teeth** — Ben's #1 black uncommon. "Absolutely premium." Sweeps the 1/1s and X/1s or finishes something post-combat: "why does black get to wreck all of blue-white's work in one card for free?"
- **Down, Down to Goblin-town** — both hosts' #2 black uncommon. "Torment of Hailfire plus" — hand disruption, a body, and a double drain.
- **Desolation Prowler** — the Putrid Leech reprint; both say it could easily have made their top black uncommons list.
- **Rage into the Valley** — **never cut it** (week one). Ben's theory on why it wheels: people read *"three mana, draw a card, lose a life"* and stop before amass 2. He no longer takes expensive uncommons over it, and it is now clearly ahead of Gathering of Darkness.
- **Stony-Voiced Goblins** — Ben's notable common. Flashback in the set makes discard worse, but relevant creature types, the sacrifice theme, and equipment to carry outweigh it.
- **Crude Bent Blade** — **the defining card of the format** (week one). Two mana that both kills and grows, and it makes every innocuous body a threat. Three or four copies is normal, not diminishing. Build against it: keep a spare body on board so you never sack the card you care about. Green without Wood Elves *"really struggles"* against it.
- **Bilbo's Deadly Slice** — Ethan listed it reluctantly, Ben cut it entirely: "Murder's washed."
- **Front Porch Sentries** — "eats recruit boards for breakfast": trade with an X/2, then pick off a 1/1 with the death trigger.
- **Stir Up Trouble** — confirmed a banger. One mana to kill anything is better than Bilbo's Deadly Slice at four, and the tempo swing lets you develop the same turn. Sack a spent equipment first, then a token.
- **Gollum, Silent Slinker** — Ben cut it from a trophy deck; Ethan calls it replacement level. Playable, not a card that pulls you anywhere.

- **Gathering of Darkness** — **week-one mover down.** Red-black plays only 8–10 creatures and much of the format's removal exiles, so the gravedigger half whiffs. Rage into the Valley is just better.
- **Ravening Warg** — the filler two-drop to prefer when your deck is short on point-and-click removal: a death-touch body your opponent must answer beats Front Porch Sentries' death trigger.
### Red

- **Pinecone Strike** — Ben's format truth #3: broken. Best or second-best common in the set, and it lines up against nearly every premium uncommon in the format.
- **Bothersome Noisemaker** — Ethan's #1 red uncommon pre-format; week one Ben would take it *"over every common and uncommon"* in red-black, because that deck's good cards are almost all noncreature spells.
- **Misty Mountains Raider** — Ben's #1 red uncommon. Worse than past versions of this template because it lacks haste, so it can't enable itself, but still strong.
- **Snowslope Hunter** — both hosts' #2 red uncommon.
- **Goblin-town Flunkies** — *"insanely good Crude Bent Blade protection in the mirror"* — the spare 1/1 keeps your army safe. Also the cleanest Stir Up Trouble fodder.
- **Tidings of War** — both hosts' #1 sleeper common pre-format, and week one Ben's engine card for red-black **aggro**: turn-one amass, flash back for three more, curve into Goblin Plate Mail and Rage into the Valley. Ethan is cooler on it and thinks it's much worse in the midrange builds.
- **Smaug, the Great Calamity** — week one both hosts are **off it** as a high pick. Fine in red-black midrange, actively wrong in the aggro build. Ben also notes the visual trap: it looks like the deal-three, and Pinecone Strike is the deal-three.
- **Dori, Bearer of Friends** — two-thirds of the way to storied on its own, since it's a legend that makes a treasure. Two-drop into Dori and you have an enduring story.

### Green

- **Troll Negotiations** — Ethan's clear #1 green uncommon and "probably the right top uncommon". Ben is scared to cast four mana into open mana; the case for it is casting it on turn four while they're tapped out.
- **Beorn's Hospitality** — Ben's #1 green uncommon. Landfall counters, and a seven-mana mode that turns it into a land-count creature. "Fine — don't love it, don't hate it."
- **Old Fat Spider** — Ben's #2 green uncommon. "This could be a common by today's standards." Once it sticks on six mana it's very annoying.
- **Woodland Weavemaster** — Ethan's #2 green uncommon, graded "C+ at best". Never your reason to draft elves, but it enables explosive double-spell turns.
- **Quarrel** — on both hosts' common lists; green's interaction.
- **Wood Elves** — week-one **mover up**. Ben, who called it a stinker, now names it as the reason green survives Crude Bent Blade: *"extremely annoying against me as the person with the Crude Bent Blades."*
- **Warg Tactics** — Ethan's sleeper. Two-mana modal: destroy a flier, or a +1/+1 counter with trample and hexproof. "When you need it to be Plummet and it kills a Smaug, that seems very strong."
- **Boughside Wanderers** — Ben's sleeper, low confidence. Fine top end and a two-for-one at common, but black and blue get their two-for-ones at three mana instead of six.

- **Wilderland Scrounger** — week-one **mover up**: a single-pipped 3/6 ferocious body that has bodied both hosts. The green card to play if green is genuinely your second colour.
### Multicolor and hybrid

- **Bolg of the North** — Ethan's #1 multicolour and "one of the best, or maybe the best, uncommon in the set".
- **Goblin Plate Mail** — "This card is dumb. This card is bonkers good." One of the best commons in the set: a two-mana 2/1 menace that leaves an equipment behind, and menace makes a big army very hard to chump.
- **Large Bear** — Ben's #1 multicolour, and the episode's namesake. "Large and in charge", "hits like a truck". Ethan would like it more at three black-black than at Golgari hybrid.
- **Silvan Reveler** — Ethan's #2 multicolour. "Insanely strong", Rogue Refiner vibes; slow, but it grinds through the late game and can be pitched to recruit and rebought.
- **Thranduil, Sindarin Liege** — Ben's #2 multicolour, an elf lord that makes elves on landfall. Ethan's caveat: "seems like Pinecone Strike bait to me."
- **Thorin Oakenshield** — the R/W signpost and the storied example card. Ward 1 on your whole board once you have an enduring story.
- **Bifur, Melodic Rider** — "No, this is terrible." Six mana for a 4/5 that doubles dwarf triggers, in a deck that needs to go fast.
- **Nori, Teller of Tales** — Ethan likes it as a two-mana 2/2 first strike legend in the colour pair that wants legends; Ben grades it "a C−".
- **Fearsome Goblin Pair** — amass Goblins 4 on death; "amass four is large".
- **Duskwatch Hunter** — week one: *"replaceable filler plus."* Getting the counter up front (rather than on a death trigger, as black usually does) is a lot of stats for two mana.
- **The Chief Warg** — triggers on *any* attack, not just its own, so it can trigger the turn it lands. Both flag that it doesn't have four power itself as a real design miss for green-black.
- **Patient Instructor** — clean and straightforward, but despite being hybrid it really wants to live in blue-white specifically: "you're not playing this in your blue-green elves deck."
- **Bard the Bowman** — Ethan grades it "a B−" and mostly likes it because he likes blue-white. Ben thinks it's too understated and anemic, and wishes it could recruit itself.
- **Eagle's Rescue** — the dream is pitching it to recruit, then reanimating it onto a 1/1 token. Ben: "that's a lot of work for a four-mana 3/3 flier." Ethan: "yeah, but you also got to draw a card."
- **Mirkwood Nurturer** — Ben doesn't think it's a good card but concedes the juice is real: picking up Crude Bent Blade, a Stony-Voiced Goblins body, or a saga. The question is whether you survive to get it.
