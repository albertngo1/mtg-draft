## Format speed / meta read

- **Slow format. ("The sword.")** All three episodes converge here, confirmed in the final ep. Games go long, board stalls are common, missing land drop 3 is "an auto-lose" (mulligan accordingly). Flooding hurts less than screwing.
- **Power level is "scrappy," not low.** Power comes from **synergy, not raw card quality**. "It's not that two-for-ones are back, it's that five-for-ones are gone." Commons make up the bulk of good decks; rares are mostly modest two-for-ones. Compared repeatedly to the Lords of Limited cube and Kaldheim/Kaladesh-Remastered-style "feel-it-out synergy."
- **The central tension:** you must be *doing something synergistic*, but you must NOT jam bad cards to do it. "Synergy doesn't mean making bad cards good — it means making good cards better, and choosing the right last 7–8 cards." The best synergy cards (Mr. Fantastic, Echo, H.E.R.B.I.E.) require *no work* — they're good cards that happen to combo.
- **Curves are atypical.** It is trivially easy to fill 3- and 4-drop slots (the 4-drop slot gets badly clogged with good cards) and **hard to fill the 2-drop slot**. Do NOT play bad filler two-drops — you "0-for-1 yourself." Land-cyclers count as your two-drop (cycle on 2, play a 3-drop on 3). Typical builds: 3–5 real two-drops + 2–3 land-cyclers; 16–17 lands; creature counts run high (17–18 common) because so many engines are creatures.
- **4 toughness / 4 mana value is THE magic number.** You need to attack through 4-toughness bodies and to kill them. This is why **Lightning Strike (deal 3) is underwhelming** — it misses the cards that matter.
- **Removal is scarce — prioritize it and hold it.** Spend high picks on interaction; the "catchall" removal (exile / destroy outright) is what matters. Patience with removal (waiting for the creature that matters) is a key play pattern. The black instants and white exile spells are the premium removal.
- **Heroes (white-leaning) vs. Villains (black-leaning).** White = heroes-matter, black = villains-matter, the other three colors mix. Doing the *hero* thing is easier and better-supported than the *villain* thing.
- **Draft navigation is back.** 10 archetypes, real signal-reading, you can get deep into a color and pick your second color late. This is the single most praised feature. Unlike recent guild sets, you are NOT on rails. **Draft with preferences again** — if you find an underrated package, you can re-draft it repeatedly.
- **Breaking board stalls:** flying and trample close games; resource-advantage engines (the uncommon lands, card-draw engines) win the long grind. Both paths are real. **Retro (2026-08-03) adds: flying is straightforwardly good in this format** — a take the hosts realised they had never stated outright.

**Settled retrospective reads (2026-08-03) — these outrank everything above:**

- **It is a good-cards-matter set wearing synergy clothing.** Power level gaps, not synergy, decided games. Roughly a third of the card file is cards you hope never to play, and most of them are the "ooh, that looks cool" build-around cards. Synergy that costs you nothing (Mister Fantastic, Echo, H.E.R.B.I.E.) is real; synergy you pay card quality for is a trap.
- **Slow — Ethan finally "dared to say the S word."** Confirmed with no hedging. This is why the uncommon lands and mana sinks were so strong.
- **Mana base: 16 lands + 2–4 land cyclers was ~90% of decks.** Never 17 after the first few days; never 15 (curves are too high). Land cyclers are the flood/screw insurance that makes 16 correct.
- **Four is the number you cannot miss on land drops**, and 4 is also the magic toughness *and* the clogged mana-value slot. A pack-1-pick-1 four-drop genuinely constrained the rest of your draft — "I can't take a four for a while."
- **Two-for-ones are back to mattering**, after Secrets of Strixhaven's six-for-one insanity. Four two-for-ones vs. your opponent's two is a felt advantage. This is the context that rescues cards like Bold Biochemist and Hero in Training.
- **The set had no format-defining mechanic.** Land cyclers (or "the 4-drop slot") are what you will remember it by. Power up is just exhaust/kicker; connive is a clean hand-smoother with minor draw-two and +1/+1 overlap; **teamwork was under-supported** — on every card you either always paid it or never did, nothing rewarded being tapped for it, and only Agent Maria Hill cared.
- **Villains-matter was a trap; heroes won on raw card quality.** White and blue are the hero colors, so the hero side simply had better cards. "There are some villains in green, but the villain payoffs were bad."
- **The uncommon land cycle (Baxter Building, Avengers Tower, Villainous Hideout) were the most powerful utility lands in a long time** — mana sinks that break the stalls the format produces.
- **The army-in-a-can uncommon cycle was single-pipped, and that is why the format was soupy.** Okoye, Falcon, Madame Masque and Ka-Zar were all splashable on one pip; red conspicuously got none. If they had been double-pipped the format would have been far more disciplined.
- **The format was out of balance across its 10 pairs — and the hosts call that a feature.** Realistically 6–8 pairs were live. The skill it rewards is setting yourself up for the best deck and pivoting cleanly when it is not open. Note this rewards *paper pods* far more than Arena, where you cannot read eight functional drafters.
- **Red was the consensus worst color and still slightly underrated.** It has real uncommons (Jessica Jones, Private Eye; Photon Blast Barrage; Loki Laufeyson) and a disproportionate share of the good gold rares. It is "pick your spots," not "never draft red." A red-black deck 3-0'd the paper pod Ethan drafted in.
- **Verdict: best set of 2026 so far — "that's not saying much."**

## Archetypes (with explicit archetype-hierarchy ranks)

Ranked by the **2026-06-23 early-access episode**. The hosts gave an explicit two-tier split and a color ranking of roughly **Blue > White > Green ≈ Black > Red**. **These are predictions.**

> **Settled retro overlay (2026-08-03):** the official final color ranking is **Blue #1 · White #2 · Green #3 · Black #4 · [gigantic gap] · Red #5** (Ben dissents for White #1 and would put the gap after Green). **Blue-White is the best deck in the format** — it plays a normal interactive curve with no effort and has by far the deepest commons (white: Web Up, Murdock's Crusade, Hero in Training, Raft Security Officer; blue: S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drone, We Say Thee Nay!, Trickster's Stratagem, Frozen in Ice). Black and red have "a couple removal spells" and, in red's case, essentially no good commons. **Blue-Red artifacts is confirmed the poster child for why synergy failed** — there simply were not enough good cards in it, and you could reach the same power level with uncommons that needed no work. Below-tier pairs are still functional in a real eight-person pod.

## Card notes

Later episodes supersede earlier. **All takes are pre-data predictions.** `(?)` marks low-confidence readings. Every card name below matches the printed/Arena name in `cards_MSH.ndjson`.

### White

- **Agent 13, Sharon Carter** — Both hosts' #1 white uncommon. 2W 3/2; attack alone → investigate. Two-drop into Agent 13, attack, make a clue, opponent is far behind. Value engine, not purely aggressive.
- **Okoye, Dora Milaje Leader** — Army-in-a-can; 3W 3/2 that makes two 1/1 soldiers, attacking tokens get first strike. Audibled UP to a top-2 white uncommon in both the crash course and the final ep. "Point to you on this one" — wasn't on Ben's radar without Ethan.
- **White Widow, Free Agent** — 3W 2/3, choose: counters on up to two creatures, or return an artifact/enchantment from graveyard. Flexible two-for-one; Ben's #3 white uncommon.
- **Agent Maria Hill** — W 2/1; tapped to pay a teamwork cost → +1/+1 and draw. Looked broken in preview as a secret blue-white gold card; **cooled in the crash course** because teamwork spells are thin. Still good in a real teamwork deck. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **Down further.** Teamwork was under-supported and Maria Hill was possibly the *only* card that rewarded paying a teamwork cost. Ethan's gripe list explicitly names "thinking you could take Agent Maria Hill over Cruel Alliance pack 1 pick 1" as crazy.
- **Patriot, Shield Wielder** — 1W 2/2; 2,tap give another creature +2/+0 and hexproof. Flag-bearer style protection; "great" if you can reliably hold it up in a tricky blue-white shell.
- **Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.** — 2W 2/4; attack-alone creatures get +1/+1. Ethan's favorite sleeper — "basically a 2/4 exalted," good in multiples. The format wants you attacking with large single threats.
- **Hero in Training** — 2W 2/2; ETB draw a card, +2 life if you control another hero. "Absurd" common — a cantrip body that's a great counter-target. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **Ethan's #1 white common** ("a three-mana two-for-one"), and he rates it above Klaw, Sonic Subjugator. Ben takes his first Web Up over it because interaction is what you can't manufacture. Both agree it is a top-2 white common.
- **Murdock's Crusade** — 1W sorcery, teamwork 4; exile a creature toughness 4+ (and/or exile an enchantment MV 4+). **Moved UP in the final ep** — unlike Lightning Strike it actually kills the enginey 4/5-drops that matter. Real removal. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **Winner of the Imperial Oath Award** for clunky-looking overperformer — the first removal spell to ever win it. Everyone filed it as a sideboard card or reluctant 23rd; it ended up doing everything, because removal was scarce, power up turned small creatures big, bombs had to leave the battlefield, and exile mattered. Named one of the two biggest movers up in the format.
- **Web Up** — 2W enchantment; O-Ring (exile a non-land permanent). The white catch-all removal; Ben specifically praised including it. Note: Guerrilla Gorilla can blow it up. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **Ben's #1 white common**, over Hero in Training — "I've got to have a couple Web Ups in my white deck; I can put another three-drop in the three-drop slot." Together with Murdock's Crusade it is why white is a top-2 color.
- **Take Up the Shield** — 1W instant; +1/+1 counter, lifelink + indestructible EOT. Ethan: "Biosynthetic Burst 2.0," likes it; Ben left it off his list. Quality hinges inversely on how good your hard removal is.
- **Super Villain Lockup** — 1W enchantment, flash; exile a *tapped* opponent's creature. Good (removal is good), but can never hit something that never attacks (e.g. Mr. Fantastic).
- **Helicarrier Strike** — W instant, teamwork 2; deal 2 (or 4 with teamwork) to an attacker/blocker. Ethan graded it ~0 (bad if white is purely aggressive); came around once mana-efficiency + "4 is the magic number" mattered. Improves white's curve. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **Live disagreement, unresolved.** Ethan: "justice for Helicarrier Strike — that card is so efficient." Ben plays around it constantly and thinks it only beats opponents who don't know it exists. Treat as good against unknown fields, weak against strong ones.
- **Panther Pounce** — W instant; target player investigates, target creature +2/+0 (?) gains flying, untap it. "A lot for one mana" — can't get blown out (you keep the clue), and triggers token/artifact-ETB synergies. Sleeper.
- **Borough Backup** — 4W sorcery; make two 3/2 vigilance hero tokens, basic land cycling. Imperial-Oath-flavored; great with token payoffs and as a flood insurance / splash enabler.
- **Brave Brawler** — W 2/1 lifelink, power up 4W (+2 counters). Watch-list two-drop (Ari Lax / Mark Anderson liked it). Plays as a turn-2 lifelinker that later becomes a 4/3 problem.
- **Political Triumph** — W plan; creature ETB → scry 1 + plan counter; at 4 counters draw + put a +1/+1 on each creature. The least-bad plan; "nutter butters" on turn 1, terrible topdeck. Hosts remain wary of plans overall. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **Confirmed the only plan worth playing** — "plans were largely terrible with the exception of Political Triumph."
- **Captain Marvel, Earth's Protector** — Greater-or-garbage: **garbage** (preview). A 4W 4/4 flying vigilance Serra Angel with conditional flash — too many vanilla 4/5-drops with no ETB. Possible sleeper if it survives.
- **Crowd of True Believers** — W 1/2; tap an attack-alone creature for +1/+0 and 1 life. **Literal F — do not play it.** Named explicitly as unplayable. **Retro 2026-08-03:** named **white's top synergy trap common** in the retro. Ben allows that "there are true believers out there"; Ethan does not.
- **Raft Security Officer** — White's third piece of interaction at common (a tapper). Retro 2026-08-03: the card that best illustrates the format — "this is why cards like Raft Security Officer, the tapper, or Frozen in Ice, which might sometimes not be great, ended up being pretty good." When removal is scarce, anything that answers a creature counts. **If you finish a draft with only two ways to interact, you will get pinched.** Be willing to splash removal.
- **Okoye, Dora Milaje Leader** (army-in-a-can, retro re-rank) — see the white uncommon entry above. Retro 2026-08-03 slots it **last of the four army-in-a-can uncommons** — "still great, but it's the one I don't want to *splash* for," whereas Falcon, Madame Masque and Ka-Zar are all worth splashing off their single pip. Ben prefers it over Madame Masque; Ethan does not.
- **Super-Soldier Serum** — W aura that turns its target into Captain America. Retro 2026-08-03: **do not play it.** "A little fragile aura that gets two-for-oned" — a massive flavor fail on the set's signature transformation.
- **The Sentry, Golden Guardian** — W rare. Retro 2026-08-03: **high risk, high reward, but mostly just high risk.** Ben's instinct was to jam it and he credits the wider Magic community with talking him out of it. You *can* lose to it, but there are a lot of answers.

### Blue

- **Justice, Vance Astrovik** — Both hosts' #1 blue uncommon. 1U 2/2 flyer; ETB bounce a non-land permanent, grows when your non-lands get bounced. "Incredibly pushed" — value mode (3/3 flyer) or tempo mode, plus +1/+1 synergy. The "year of mana wars continues." **Retro 2026-08-03:** **confirmed broken** — "Justice just kept the busted Man-Thing train rolling" (after Invasion Submersible), and the hosts genuinely don't know how design keeps finding better versions of the card.
- **Mister Fantastic, Reed Richards** — 2U 2/4 reach; whenever one or more tokens you control enter, draw. Final ep: "phenomenal," worth pivoting for, and crucially requires *no work* — it's just good when you draft good token-makers. Member of the Fantastic Four infinite combo (see below). **Retro 2026-08-03:** **Guide-vs-data conflict, unresolved — trust the data by default.** Both hosts still rate it a top-2/3 blue uncommon and stress that it is *not* once-per-turn (Madame Masque token → draw → future tokens all trigger again) and that the **reach** blanks opposing S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drones. But they pulled up 17Lands live on air and found it below Kid Loki and Attuma, Atlantean Warlord, and *worse* filtered to top drafters. Their theory: people fail to cut it from decks with too few token-makers, and fail to make the small draft concessions (Borough Backup, token uncommons) that switch it on.
- **Falcon, Winged Wonder** — 3U 3/4 flyer; makes Redwing (1/1 flyer that surveils on attack). Pure good-stats two-for-one; "single-handedly won a game surveilling through a board stall." A top-3 blue uncommon for both by the final ep.
- **Echo, Perceptive Prodigy** — 2U 1/4 vigilance; 1,tap copy an activated/triggered ability from a creature you control. "You do NO work to make this good" — the ideal MSH card. Doubles Killmonger ETBs, etc. Build-around-adjacent but free.
- **S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drone** — 2U 2/2 flyer that brings a 1/1. "Bread and butter, just great" — a real two-for-one that's also an artifact and a token-maker. Every blue drafter takes it.
- **We Say Thee Nay!** — 1U instant; Quench (counter unless pay 2), teamwork 2 → pay 4 instead. A scalable counter that's a premium card; great with the clogged-4-drop turn (opponents tap out for their many good fours). Holds up alongside a land-cycler — "chef's kiss."
- **Trickster's Stratagem** — 3U sorcery; put an opponent's creature 2nd-from-top or on bottom, then your creature connives. Synergistic removal — triggers draw-two and counter payoffs. Sorcery speed is a downside but the card's strong enough.
- **Iron Lad, Diverging Destiny** — 2U 2/2 flying vigilance artifact; tap to draw if top card is an artifact. **Cooled in the crash course** as the hosts soured on artifacts; only good with ~15+ artifacts.
- **Atlantis Attacks** — 5UU sorcery, teamwork 4; make a 6/5 hexproof Leviathan and/or bounce 1–2 non-lands. "Sleeper great" — game-ending if cast; pitch it to connive when clunky. Sweet spot is one copy; a blue-green ramp target.
- **Frozen in Ice** — 2U aura; tap + lock + strip abilities from a creature. Sleeper removal — blue needs to get things dead, and *not* sending them to the graveyard is a bonus vs. graveyard decks. (Caveat: Web Up / exile is even better.)
- **Futurist Forge** — 1U artifact; ETB draw, then 3U sac → draw two. Card-advantage in installments; deep-blue common.
- **Wiccan, Rising Magician** — 4U 4/4 flyer; cast a non-creature spell → flicker another non-land non-token permanent. Genuine disagreement: Ethan high (flickers your busted ETBs, foils plans), Ben "garbage" (mostly a vanilla 5-mana 4/4). Unresolved.
- **Bold Biochemist** — 1U 1/3, power up 6 (+1 counter, draw two). Ethan: a fine relevant-late two-drop ("Mulldrifter/Landscape Painter back"). Ben: "stinks." Live disagreement; lean Ben given the slow, high-toughness format. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **disagreement resolved in Ethan's favour — lean Ethan now.** Ben's Secrets of Strixhaven comparison (the two-mana body with a five-mana draw-two attached, a nothingburger there) fails because context differs: in SOS two-for-ones were irrelevant next to six-for-ones, in MSH they decide games. A turn-2 Bold Biochemist is a card your opponent probably cannot afford to spend one of their ~4 removal spells on, and it converts every late-game top-deck into a two-for-one.
- **Stature, Size Shifter** — U one-drop; "the blue fireball." Retro 2026-08-03: **impressed both hosts more than expected.** A one-drop that must be answered by a removal spell, in a format where removal is pinched. The only way it is bad is if you have it in your opener but never apply enough pressure to make it matter — back it with real pressure and it demands an answer. The general principle: **the lower on your curve you can put a card that makes the opponent ask "should I answer that?", the more juice you get out of your deck.**
- **Hydraulic Helper** — U common that taps for mana. Retro 2026-08-03: **blue's top synergy trap common.** Both hosts agree, with the only concession being that it is still better than Crowd of True Believers.
- **A.I.M. Scientists** — the blue land cycler. Retro 2026-08-03: **#3 of the five-card land cycler cycle** on the official ranking (Borough Backup > Savage Land Dinosaur > A.I.M. Scientists > Kree Sentinel > Roxxon Brutes). Think of these like the Hour of Devastation deserts — the ranking is "happiest to take this as my first card of that colour." All five are real cards on rate, and land cycling is the format's flood-and-screw insurance.
- **Super Suit** — U flash equipment. Retro 2026-08-03: **not good.** Ben never played it, Ethan's verdict is a flat "no" — you weren't playing them for the equipment and there wasn't enough to do with them once they resolved.
- **Aerial Doombot** — U 1/1 flyer, power up 5U (+3 counters). Scrappy hyper-aggro-in-the-air filler. "Questions about it," but one of the cards you scrap out with if forced low.
- **Super Intelligence** — U aura; enchanted creature's controller draws each upkeep. Greater-or-garbage: **garbage**, but Ethan will "absolutely" try a meme deck. Cute with Speedball (hexproof-plus) or Patriot protection.

### Black

- **Dark Deed** — Both hosts' #1 black uncommon and possibly the **best removal in the format, full stop**. 1B instant, −4/−4. Efficient, kills the 4-toughness bodies that define the format.
- **Klaw, Sonic Subjugator** — 1B 2/2; ETB reveal (1 + creatures in your yard) cards, you pick one to discard. "Insane" — Thought-Stalker-Warlock vibes; at worst a 2/2 that strips a card, at best Thoughtseizes their hand. Can't hit on curve. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **a genuine pull into black** — the floor (a 3-mana 2/2 Ravenous Rats stripping a land) is better than it reads, the ceiling (look at their hand and take the best card) "felt broken." Both hosts rate it **below Hero in Training**, and Ethan draws the line at splashing it.
- **Madame Masque** — 4B 3/2; ETB connives (brings a 2/1 menace token via its own draw-two), then each draw-your-second-card makes another 2/1. "Broken," significantly better than Moonstone. **Ben omitted it in the crash course by misreading it** — corrected to a top-2 black uncommon. **Retro 2026-08-03:** one of the four **army-in-a-can uncommons** (with Okoye, Falcon and Ka-Zar) that the hosts single out as the clearest case of raw card power beating synergy. Ethan loves it and rates it above Okoye; all four are **single-pipped, which is the main reason the format played out soupy** — you open Madame Masque while green-white and just find a way to cast it.
- **Cruel Alliance** — 2B sorcery, teamwork 2; exile a creature MV 3 or less (with teamwork: exile anything + gain 3 life). Ethan's pick for **best removal in the format** — flexible, exiles, life buffer. Sorcery speed barely matters given the play patterns. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **confirmed black's #1 common, unanimously ("Cruel Alliance all day").** The nicest detail is the teamwork sweet spot — sometimes you exile a two-drop and still pay teamwork just to gain the 3 life. It is a correct pack-1-pick-1 over Agent Maria Hill and it is not close.
- **Project Deathlok Soldier** — B common. Retro 2026-08-03: **synergy trap.** Ethan calls it a dead-heat with Agents of HYDRA for black's worst trap ("a piece of garbage cardboard to connive away and then pay three mana to get back"). Ben will play exactly one as sacrifice fodder if he has the 2/3 lifelinker to build an engine with — "but I'm not excited about it."
- **Agents of HYDRA** — B common. Retro 2026-08-03: **black's top synergy trap common** on Ben's ruling. Ethan played it exactly once, in a "unicorn draft" black deck with both black plans plus The Masters of Evil, where literally anything that said *villain* was correct. Outside that deck it is unplayable.
- **Roxxon Brutes** — the black land cycler. Retro 2026-08-03: **#5 (last) of the five land cyclers.** Still a real card; just the one you are least happy to take as your first black card.
- **Stolen Stark Tech** — B flash equipment. Retro 2026-08-03: **not good, but the better of the two flash equipment.** Black has the tools to turn it into an actual two-for-one — use it as a trick, then move it somewhere else. Ethan still would not play it.
- **Hour of Defeat** — 3B instant; destroy a creature, surveil 1. Solid catch-all; both rate Cruel Alliance slightly higher.
- **Arnim Zola, Bio-Fanatic** — 2B 2/3; 3,tap make a 2/1 menace villain token if 2+ creatures in your yard. "Good" repeatable token engine in graveyard-black.
- **Yellowjacket, Heartless Marauder** — B 1/2 flyer; when another villain enters, +2/+1 (?) and lifelink EOT. A premium-ish two-drop in a format starved for them; a 2/2 flying lifelink in the right deck. Caveat: don't force villains to enable it.
- **Baron Strucker, HYDRA Overlord** — 2B 2/2; villains cost 1 less, your villains connive (once/turn). **Moved DOWN** — Ben defended it as a manalith-plus-connive in the crash course; final ep "not that good, not even a reason to draft villains." A 3-mana 2/2 that dodges combat is fragile.
- **Moonstone, Harsh Mistress** — 3B 2/4 flyer; discard a card → exile-and-play it through your next turn. Connive payoff; "premium if you're conniving, but you don't *need* it if you're already popping off." Worse than Reed Richards in the 4-mana-2/4-that-draws slot.
- **HYDRA Troopers** — 2B 3/2; ETB make a tapped 2/1 menace if 2+ creatures in yard, else mill 2. Hyped as "best creature in the format if enabled," then **walked back**: bad on turn 3 unenabled, irrelevant late, can't enable itself, NOT a reason to be in black-green. Play it in any black deck, but it's "as good as your deck is." **Retro 2026-08-03:** **named the single biggest mover DOWN of the entire format** — "even when you did the thing, it wasn't that good." It failed for the same structural reason Lightning Strike did: creatures on the ground do not matter much when 4 toughness is the magic number. Settled.
- **Robot Domination** — 3B plan; cards to your yard from anywhere → draw, lose life, plan counter; at 3 counters make three 2/2 robot villain tokens. The best plan with white's — but you want your *own* sac outlets (Kingpin's Enforcers, Project Deathlok Soldier, Bullseye) to trigger it at will, not rely on combat. **Retro 2026-08-03:** plans stayed bad, but Ethan keeps **a soft spot for the two black plans specifically** (this and Doom Reigns Supreme) alongside The Masters of Evil, in the one all-villain deck archetype where playing bad villains is correct. That deck exists; it is a unicorn, not a plan.
- **The Masters of Evil** — 5B 5/6; other villains +2/+1, can discard it to tutor a plan. "Great, no notes" — anthems a board and is castable if you have one.
- **Red Room Recruit** — 1B 1/2; ETB connive. Ethan "great" sleeper (free yard fuel for black); Ben "too small-ball." Live disagreement.
- **HYDRA Infiltration** — 3B enchantment; ETB opponent discards two; attack-alone → drain 1. "Definition of a sleeper but I feel like I'm in a coma reading it" — Ethan likes the white-black grind plan; Ben skeptical.
- **Ninja of the Hand** — 2B 2/2 deathtouch, power up 4B (each opponent discards, +1 counter). Ethan still "buying stock"; Ben "too clunky, telegraphed." Lean Ben.
- **Decoy Ploy** — 1B (?) instant; return a villain and/or hero from your graveyard. Recursion glue for hero/villain decks.

### Red

> Red is the consensus **worst color** — its cards "fall off a cliff after the top two," it gets pulled in four disparate directions without good cards for any of them, and it's the only color with no army-in-a-can uncommon. The bright spot is the self-targeting / non-combat-damage package, but it's fragile.

- **Iron Fist, Living Weapon** — 2R 2/3; cast a spell targeting your creature → Iron Fist gains "tap: deal damage equal to power to any target." "Rare power level at uncommon," the best red card. The payoff that makes self-targeting work. Must be answered the turn it lands.
- **Truck Toss** — 2RR instant (2 less with a vehicle); deal 4 to any target. Ben's #1 red uncommon — the biggest burn chunk, and "4 is the magic number." (May get edged by the red creatures on a holistic re-rank.)
- **Hawkeye, Young Avenger** — 3R 2/4 reach; your non-combat damage to opponents/their permanents gets +Hawkeye's power. Ethan's favorite red card — a real build-around (Lightning Strike, Truck Toss, Hawkeye's Bow all go face). Risk: legendary, dies to removal and you're done. With Hawkeye out, Repulsor Blast's teamwork mode domes for 4. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **it does unlock a lot of red's damage potential and Ethan still loves it — but it asks you to play a lot of bad cards** (HYDRA Assault Robot, Hawkeye's Bow) to get there. Ethan kept passing it even while in red because there was always something else to do that didn't skew the deck.
- **Loki Laufeyson** — 1R 2/1; 1,tap copy your next instant/sorcery MV ≤ Loki's power; power up 4R (+2 counters). Pop-off potential — doubles Lightning Strike the turn you untap.
- **Photon Blast Barrage** — XR sorcery; copy X times, each deals 1 to a creature (split as you like). Ben reframed Ethan's evaluation: it's better than "3-for-1 / 4-for-2" math suggests. Bad vs. high toughness; great at mopping up tokens. Combos with **Red Hulk** (enrage) for ~15 damage off an X=4 cast.
- **Crimson Operative** — 3R 3/2 artifact prowess; ETB impulse-draw (play top card through next turn). "A two-for-one in a desolate landscape of one-for-ones." Notable red common; Ethan prefers the 3/2 printing.
- **Lightning Strike** — 2R instant, deal 3 any target. **Consensus hot take: not that good** (~C+). Misses the 4-toughness cards that matter. Would be better if red were better. Still your stock burn. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **it became the namesake of a new Lords of Limited award** — "the Lightning Strike in Marvel Super Heroes award, for common removal spell that fell off a cliff" (prior would-be winners: Last Gasp in SOS, Skulduggery in OTJ, Galvanize in MKM, Plasma Bolt in EOE). Not even a top-3 red common. Ethan's own summary: telling Crash Course Ethan that Lightning Strike and HYDRA Troopers would miss his top-commons list would have been unthinkable.
- **HULK SMASH!** — R common. Retro 2026-08-03: **Ben's pick for the best red common, over Lightning Strike**, and Ethan likes the take. It gives red an actual removal spell that *also* triggers the self-targeting package, and it can blow up equipment — which came up far more often than Murdock's Crusade's enchantment mode did. Worth splashing when you need interaction. The sweet spot for the self-targeting deck is red-green: HULK SMASH! + Go Nuts! + the green uncommon bite spell, then just play good cards. You get into trouble when you start counting Panther Pounces and Blazing Crescendos.
- **Stark Industries Executive** — R 1/2 that taps for two mana to make a Treasure. Retro 2026-08-03: **"Undercover Skrull at home" — a good role player.** A one-drop that ramps you for two is close enough to green's best common, and the repeatable token generation is relevant in a color that cares about having artifacts to sacrifice and about fixing for splashes.
- **Hawkeye's Bow** — R equipment. Retro 2026-08-03: **red's top synergy trap common.** Both times Ben "got worked over" by it, Hawkeye, Young Avenger was on the battlefield making it deal 3 — without Hawkeye it does not get there, and the decks that beat you with it were usually beating you with something else.
- **HYDRA Assault Robot** — R common. Retro 2026-08-03: **a bad card** whose only use is as a Hawkeye, Young Avenger enabler (it deals 3 with Hawkeye out). Do not build toward it.
- **Kree Sentinel** — the red land cycler. Retro 2026-08-03: **#4 of the five land cyclers.**
- **The Ruinous Wrecking Crew** — a red-leaning gold rare, named alongside Monica Rambeau and the Superhero Civil War saga as evidence that **red's card quality at rare was genuinely good** even though its commons were the worst in the set. "Red had good card quality; you just had to pick your spots when to get into it."
- **Monica Rambeau** — a flip rare that becomes a hexproof threat. Retro 2026-08-03: cited in the same breath as The Ruinous Wrecking Crew as one of the good rares propping red up. (Colour reading uncertain from captions — the hosts called it red; the card data lists it as white (?).)
- **Repulsor Blast** — 3R sorcery, teamwork 2; deal 5 to a creature (+2 to its controller with teamwork). "Closer to 4-mana-kill-anything than not"; red needs to kill bigger things.
- **Blazing Crescendo** — 1R; target creature +3/+1 EOT, impulse-draw. Sleeper — cantripping pump that targets your own creature (turns on Iron Fist / Colleen Wing), and it replaces itself.
- **Jessica Jones, Private Eye** — 2R 2/3; tap + stun her, impulse X = her power. "A good card that might be homeless" (crash course). Final-ep softening: maybe red-white wants it (pump her power → exile 4–5 cards), draws two-ish every other turn on her own.
- **Misty Knight, Hero for Hire** — 1R 3/1; 2,tap discard a card, draw for each card discarded this turn. Cool with connive/land-cyclers. "If it weren't red, absolutely" play it — held back by being red.
- **Machinesmith Automaton** — 2R 2/2 artifact trample; grows when another artifact enters. Comes down turn 3, gets big in a long game. Not a reason to draft blue-red; one of the less-embarrassing artifacts.
- **Hire a Crew** — 2R instant; make a 2/1 menace villain, your creatures +1/+0 EOT. Ethan pitched it (token + buff + villain); Ben "every box it checks is too small." Lean Ben.
- **Kree Commandos** — 2W (white, listed here for the aggro-air discussion) 2/1 vigilance flying prowess. Ethan's late add for low-power blue-white prowess-tempo. (Note: this is a *white* card.)

### Green

> Green's top uncommons are bangers (Ka-Zar especially) then "fall off a cliff." Both of green's removal spells can cost a single mana — a huge plus. Hard to get *into* green unless you see a top uncommon early.

- **Ka-Zar of the Savage Land** — 4G 3/2; play lands from top of library + makes Zabu (legendary 2/2 cat that grows on landfall). Both hosts' #1 green uncommon, "bonkers/ridiculous" — Oracle of Mul Daya stapled to a body and a token. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **confirmed the best green uncommon**, and one of the four single-pipped army-in-a-can uncommons worth splashing. It is also the benchmark the trap build-arounds fail against — if your build-around's *ceiling* is roughly "cast Ka-Zar," don't do the work.
- **White Tiger, Ava Ayala** — G 2/2, power up 5G (+1 counter, make the Tiger God, a legendary 4/4 that can't be blocked by more than one). Excellent two-drop that stays relevant — cast on 6 as a 3/3 + 4/4. A top-2 green uncommon.
- **Punishing Punch** — 2G instant (2 less if 2+ creatures in yard); your creature deals 2×power to a creature. Single-green kill-almost-anything at instant speed in green-black. Premium, very efficient.
- **Wakandan Royal Guard** — 4G 4/4 vigilance; ETB +1 counter (or +2 if the target is another hero). Ethan's bump-up; turns the blue mana-war (Justice) into a 4/4 flyer, or makes a 6-power haste-y board. Ben thinks it's out of place vs. the other notable commons.
- **Go Nuts!** — G sorcery, teamwork 3; +1/+1 counter and/or your creature fights theirs. "Has been nuts" — one-mana interaction, hugely tempo-positive when you double-spell. **Caveat the hosts learned the hard way:** you must already have 3 power on board to pay teamwork (you can't count the buffed creature). Green's removal being one-mana is "big game." **Retro 2026-08-03:** still excellent, but **demoted to green's #2 common behind Undercover Skrull**, and it heads Ethan's "what grinds my gears" list — "the sublime epiphany of Marvel Super Heroes": one green open, hover-hover-hover, click-click-click, tap, figure it out. Slow to resolve, fully telegraphed.
- **Pet Avengers** — 3G 4/4 reach, power up 6G (+1 counter, make a 3/2 vigilance hero). Genuinely good stats, but **both flagged it as a possible non-mover**: stuck in the clogged 4-drop slot, low ceiling, "makes the board stall rather than break it." Best in a green deck that *wants* to stall. Not a reason to go green. **Retro 2026-08-03:** the one genuinely interesting power-up decision in the set: **do not pay the power up on exactly six mana.** Either cast it on four, or hold until you have seven and can do something else with the odd mana — dumping 11 mana into it when you don't have to feels terrible.
- **Undercover Skrull** — 1G 1/1; +2/+2 and all creature types if 2+ creatures in yard, taps for any color. "Really good" — a two-drop that ramps 2→4 and becomes a 3/3 late. Rare praise for a green manalith. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **green's clear #1 common for both hosts, over Go Nuts! — a reversal.** Pre-format both would have said Go Nuts!. Going 2→4 on mana, fixing your splash, and still being a relevant body late beat one-mana interaction, and it carries useful types in both directions (a two-drop villain early, a shapeshifter once you have two creatures in the yard).
- **Guerrilla Gorilla** — G common that destroys a non-creature, non-token permanent. Retro 2026-08-03: **one of the two biggest movers UP in the format**, alongside Murdock's Crusade. Ben's arc was "sideboard card only → sideboard card only → okay, I'm just playing this." Blowing up a Web Up or an equipment is the dream, but the floor is the real story: the format is full of 3/2 flyers, so blocking a S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drone and leaving them with just the 1/1 already feels fine (?). Annoying to play against, which is the tell.
- **She-Hulk, Jade Defender** — G uncommon; a Flametongue-Kavu-style ETB removal creature. Retro 2026-08-03: **green's #2 uncommon, "not far behind Ka-Zar," and Ethan hates playing against it.** The complaint is a design one — green, which is not one of the better archetypes, gets an unrestricted point-and-click answer ("Guerrilla Gorilla at least has to hit a non-creature, non-token; She-Hulk is just *what have you got*"). It blows up the Iron Man you built your deck around.
- **Powerful Broker** — G common. Retro 2026-08-03: **green's top synergy trap common.** "What if you could play a Centaur Courser that had to tap itself at sorcery speed — to do nothing?"
- **Savage Land Dinosaur** — the green land cycler. Retro 2026-08-03: **#2 of the five land cyclers**, behind Borough Backup. A ~7/6 trampler for six (?) is a genuinely fine rate even before you count the land-cycling mode.
- **Heroic Feast** — G enchantment. Retro 2026-08-03: **a "high ceiling" trap.** You can make it work, but making it work means filling your deck with Rapid Rescues, and the ceiling you reach is roughly *the same as just casting Ka-Zar of the Savage Land*. Emblematic of the third of the card file you hope never to play.
- **Ant-Man's Army** — 2G 3/2; ETB make a food or treasure. "Really good" — low ceiling, very high floor; checks artifacts/ramp/fixing/life-gain/tokens boxes. Better than Pet Avengers per the final ep (3 mana, not 4).
- **Rapid Rescue** — G instant; mill 2, put a permanent from them into hand, gain 2 life. (Seed of Hope reprint.) Ethan "very good, very hard to miss"; **Ben whiffed on it twice on stream and swore off it** — genuine live disagreement on the permanent-count tension. Smooths green-black into a turn-3 HYDRA Troopers.
- **Hulkling, Burgeoning Bruiser** — 2G 2/3 vigilance hero; grows when a bigger-statted creature enters. A "counter factory" engine — pairs with Mister Hyde, Monster Within to build a draw loop (add counter / pull counter to draw). Not great alone; great in a +1/+1 home.
- **Reptil, Dinomorpher** — G 1/2; pay to become a 3/5 (3 mana) or 6/6 trample (6 mana) until EOT. Greater-or-garbage: **garbage.** Unique but a lot of mana for a one-turn effect.
- **Hellcat, Undying Vigilante** — GG 2/2 haste; dies → returns with a +1/+1, loses abilities, gains haste. Greater-or-garbage: **garbage** as a pick — needs ~11+ Forests; only a reward in a heavy-mono-green draft, never a card you warp toward.
- **Tigra, Feline Fury** — 1G 2/1 (?) flash trample; +1/+1 whenever you gain life. Needs ~2 life-gain sources and a 3rd toughness before it's exciting; off-theme. "Please do this thing" printed on it but the support isn't there.

### Multicolor (gold / hybrid)

- **Killmonger, Scourge of Wakanda** — BG 3/3; ETB optionally sac a creature → destroy a non-land permanent; +2/+1 if 2+ creatures in yard. **The best gold uncommon by far — "two gradations ahead of the pack."** Predicted top of the 17Lands power rankings. Removal + body + yard-enabler; worth pivoting for.
- **U.S.Agent, John Walker** — Orzhov hybrid 3/2; ETB make & auto-equip "Sturdy Shield" (+1/+2, equip 2). "Great" — flexible mana value, token + power-buff to punch through, auto-equips so no tempo loss.
- **Captain America, Living Legend** — WU 3/4 vigilance; first time each of your creatures taps on your turn, untap it. "A great monster" — well-statted, enables teamwork-then-attack and **crews S.H.I.E.L.D. Helicarrier then untaps your crew**.
- **Black Widow, Double Agent** — WB 3/2 deathtouch; attack-alone → first strike + menace. "Extremely oppressive from the white-black deck" — effectively unblockable when attacking alone.
- **Iron Man, Master of Machines** — UR 1/4 flying vigilance artifact; +1/+0 per other artifact, draws on attack if an artifact entered. The blue-red signpost. Good "if there have to be artifact decks," but only draft it when nobody else is fighting for it ("blue-red otter in Bloomburrow"). Kill it and the deck does nothing. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **the poster child for why synergy didn't work in MSH.** The trap is thinking "I must pack my deck with as many artifacts as possible"; the correct play is to pack your deck with as many *good* cards as possible and play Iron Man only if some of them happen to be artifacts — "reverse synergy." Mister Fantastic, Reed Richards was simply an easier card-draw engine to enable at the same power level.
- **War Machine, Legacy of Iron** — Boros hybrid 1/3 flyer artifact hero; at combat, another creature gets +War Machine's power. Good in an aggressive Boros build — buff it once and it's a 2/3 flyer handing out +2/+0 each turn.
- **Abomination, Terrifying Titan** — Gruul hybrid 4/4 trample, power up 5 (+1 counter, fight a creature). Big, gets bigger, potential two-for-one. "Love this card."
- **Hulk, Gamma Goliath** — RG 6/5 reach trample (5 mana), other power-ups cost 3 less, power up 6RG (+5 counters). Huge; punishes you for not killing it (a 9/8 trampler the turn after). Gruul's headliner beater.
- **Kang, Temporal Tyrant** — UB 3/4; connives on attack, drain 1 on your second-card-drawn. The blue-black signpost; accrues value but is "just a 4-mana 3/4," emblematic of the set's understated signposts.
- **Madame Hydra** — BR 2/3; cast a villain → make a 2/1 menace. **"Terrible."** Doesn't meet a 2026 power threshold (4-mana 2/3 do-nothing). The reason red-black is weak.
- **Ant-Man, Colony Commander** — GU 2/2; attack + pay 1 → +1/+1 on a creature; putting a counter on a creature makes a 1/1 insect (once/turn). Understated 3-mana 2/2 that asks for a lot of mana and work for a minor payoff. Both lukewarm.
- **Beast, Erudite Aerialist** — GU (Simic hybrid) 3/3; has flying if you put a +1/+1 on it this turn, draws on combat damage. 4-mana 3/3 that does a lot of work for a small payoff. Both lukewarm.
- **The Serpent Society** — BG rare 3/4 deathtouch ward, 5 poison counters; when another deathtouch creature you control dies, each opponent sacs a non-token creature. Saw it "absolutely savage" people via instant-speed deathtouch (Widow's Bite). Strong build-around.

### Colorless / Artifacts / Equipment

> General read: the **equipment theme is dead** ("so much garbage equipment," three black uncommons wasted on it are "F-tier zeros"). The good artifacts are just good cards on their own.

- **Captain America's Shield** — 2-mana rare equipment, indestructible; +8/+0, vigilance, taps a blocker on attack, equip 2. **"Absurd," moved way up** — on a flyer it's an oppressive recurring threat. Only beaten by exiling the carrier.
- **H.E.R.B.I.E. Scout Unit** — 2-mana artifact 2/1 flyer that cantrips (per the namesake discussion). High-floor two-for-one flyer that also ramps; C++ but doesn't always fit the clogged 4-drop slot. Best in lower-ceiling / aggressive decks (the higher your deck's ceiling, the less you want it). **Retro 2026-08-03:** **winner of the Golden Egg Award for the common that glued the most archetypes together.** "In a world of two-for-ones and raw card quality, it wasn't busted — it just did enough, in a lot of spots." Sometimes you want the draw-two, sometimes the artifact, sometimes the token; it never asks you to do anything for it. Would you take other cards over it? For sure — that's the point.
- **S.H.I.E.L.D. Helicarrier** — 4-mana 4/5 flying vehicle; ETB make two (?) 1/1 soldiers, crew 6. Ben thought "terrible," **revised to "pretty good"** — broken with Captain America (crew, then untap your crew). Almost always a blue-white card. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **partly re-reversed: "crucible six was in fact too much."** Ethan's final verdict is that costing four *and* crewing six was one cost too many — at three mana it would have been way too good, at four with crew 6 it is a cool card you rarely deploy. Ben still likes it. Treat it as a Captain America, Living Legend payoff, not a standalone.
- **Falcon's Wing Harness** — 1U equipment; ETB attach, equipped creature +1/+1, flying, ward 1, equip 2U. "A good card" — auto-equips, grants evasion. "Would have lost to this if I didn't have Web Up." Maybe best in blue-green (make big chunkers fly). One-of, not an equipment theme.
- **Avengers Tower** — uncommon land; taps for hero-only any-color, 4,tap dig 3 for a hero. Greater-or-garbage: **great.** "Everything is a hero or a villain." Impressive at grinding out board stalls. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **the highest-ceiling of the three uncommon lands** — the heroes are the busted cards, so "dig to Captain Marvel and win the game" is a real line. The cycle as a whole is called **the most powerful utility lands limited has had in a long time**, and the reason is the format's slowness.
- **Baxter Building** — uncommon land; 4,tap draw a card if you control a 4-toughness creature (trivial in this format). **The most impressive stall-breaker** in early access — effectively "4, tap, draw a card." (Also can filter mana toward bombs like Kang the Conqueror's take-an-extra-turn.) Preview greater-or-garbage was "garbage"; **revised up** in early access. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **the most fun of the three, and the one that makes greedy splashes castable** — a couple of Baxter Buildings is what lets a triple-black Undercover Skrull-style splash actually happen.
- **Villainous Hideout** — uncommon land; 3,tap a villain you control connives. The least-good of the three uncommon lands, but still a real card-selection/stall engine. **Retro 2026-08-03:** confirmed the weakest of the three, but rummaging away a land in the late game is exactly what the slow, settled board states of this format want.
- **Borough Backup** (land cycler ranking) — see the white entry above. Retro 2026-08-03: **the #1 land cycler of the five-card cycle.** Two 3/2s for five mana is a fine rate on its own, and the land-cycling mode means you never have to play the card as a card. Think of the cycle like Hour of Devastation's deserts — this is the one you are happiest to take as your first card of a colour.
- **S.H.I.E.L.D. Spy Kit** — W equipment; +1/+0, equip 1, attack-alone → untap + scry. "Questions about it," but a fine way to scrap out tempo in an aggressive white deck. Not a high pick. **Retro 2026-08-03:** **Moved up — "one of the best tools for an underpowered draft," and Ben's refinement is that it's just good in white full stop.** Every piece of it earns: the +1/+1 is what lets an aggressive creature attack into a 2/4, the vigilance makes racing miserable for the opponent, and the scry on attack is free value. Especially strong on a flyer.
- **A.I.M. Synthoids** — 2-mana 1/3 robot villain artifact; ETB surveil 2. Looks like it does everything (artifact + villain + yard-filler) but **"too low impact" — not the golden egg.** Predicted to rise in Pro-Tour pod play where good cards are scarcer.

### Rares & bombs

**The Fantastic Four infinite combo:** Human Torch, Johnny Storm + The Thing, Ben Grimm + Invisible Woman, Sue Storm + Mister Fantastic, Reed Richards in play together loop on your draw step and win on the spot. A real (if rare) win condition; the hosts flagged it as a "first on the LoL achievement list." Don't draft *for* it, but be aware of it as opponent and as upside.
- **Kang the Conqueror** — 2UU mythic 4/5 flyer villain; power up 5UUU (+1 counter, take an extra turn, power-ups locked during it). Mostly a great 4-mana 4/5 flyer that threatens to win if it reaches 8 mana; an opponent "savaged" Ben by filtering triple-blue with Baxter Building into the extra turn.
- **Red Hulk** — 4RR 6/7 reach trample, enrage (dealt damage → +1 counter, then deals that many to any target). The Photon Blast Barrage combo piece (~15 damage). Big standalone threat.
- **Mjölnir, Hammer of Thor** — 3R mythic legendary equipment; ETB deal 4 to a creature, double equipped creature's damage, equip "worthy" (legendary non-villain red/white), plus 2R discard → deal 2 to each creature. Ben's initial "shoehorned" reaction reversed to "this is good" — flavorful and powerful.
- **Thor Odinson** — 3RW 4/4 flying vigilance prowess legendary. "A juiced Serra Angel" — the biggest baddest thing if it dodges black removal / blue bounce.
- **Doom Reigns Supreme** — 1B rare plan; villain ETB → drain 1, plan counter; at 5 counters exile opponent's top 5 and cast up to two free. Preview "B"-ish; needs an all-villain deck and 5 triggers — the jury (and the format) soured on plans generally.
- **Titania, Rugged Rumbler** — 2BG 5/5 villain; additional cost discard-or-pay-2, ward discard-or-pay-2. A 3-mana 5/5 ahead of schedule, but "are you ever going to want to discard?" and ward 2 is the only protection. Removal-dependent evaluation; both lukewarm.
- **Leader, Super-Genius** — U mythic. Retro 2026-08-03: **#1 on the official "why aren't these mythics" power rankings — flatly broken.** It triggers on *every* instance of connive rather than once per turn, so an opponent can play it on four, you can answer it, and you have probably still lost the game.
- **The Super Hero Civil War** — WR rare. Retro 2026-08-03: **#2 on the "why aren't these mythics" rankings**, though the hosts find it more beatable than Leader, Super-Genius — "everybody just gets a colourless rare bomb," and if you are packing She-Hulk, Jade Defender and Guerrilla Gorilla you can fight through it. It also lands on the clogged four-drop slot, which helps.
