# FIN — Rough Drafts notes

> Source: **Rough Drafts** (Sam Pratt) — 7 Final Fantasy episodes. Distilled 2026-08-20 from YouTube
> auto-caption transcripts; card names corrected where confident, uncertain readings marked `(?)`.
>
> This channel is structurally different from the other four in `draft-guides/`. Most episodes are a
> deep dive on a **single card**, used as a lens onto a broader format or design concept — its value
> is depth and framing rather than card-by-card grades. Sam positions his content as a complement to
> the full set reviews rather than a replacement.

## Format speed / meta read

- **Blue is the best colour, and Sam reasoned it from the archetype data rather than from card
  evaluations.** His argument (Ep 12): two-colour decks aggregate around a 58% win rate, and **all
  four blue pairs clear that bar** — which is not true of any other colour. He is careful not to
  overstate it: Play Boosters have compressed colour power levels and FIN is a balanced format. He
  also notes that formats where blue is genuinely the best colour feel distinctive precisely because
  **blue is usually under-drafted and mis-drafted**, so the reward for noticing is larger than usual.
- **The interaction-versus-creatures pendulum has swung back toward interaction, and FIN sits on the
  interaction side** (Ep 16). His account: through roughly 2022–24, cheap value creatures and
  multi-body commons were pushed hard while the removal on offer was mediocre, so aggro dominated.
  Since then, cheap interaction has gotten genuinely good while cheap creatures have been powered
  down and largely moved off common. FIN is a clear case — **Thunder Magic plus black's three strong
  removal commons (Cornered by Black Mages, Vayne's Treachery, Sephiroth's Intervention)**.
- **Games are not longer, but the critical turn moved later.** Average game length has not shifted
  much, because powerful cards still end games — but the power now sits in the more expensive cards,
  formats are **less snowbally**, and the decisive combat has drifted from around turn four or five
  to **turn six or seven**. Practical upshot: you are punished less for a slow start than instinct
  from a few years ago suggests, and rewarded more for having interaction at the critical turn.
- **Lane-finding is the core skill, because everything works.** Ten pairs, eight drafters — something
  is always open, and since the decks are synergy-based within their pair, finding the open lane is
  worth more than forcing a strong one.
- **Splash very little.** Sam's settled position (Ep 15) is that he **barely splashes in FIN**. The
  synergy decks work inside their colour pair and get less out of off-colour cards than they would in
  a goodstuff format — his example is that Black-Red does not gain much from splashing blue, because
  a good Black-Red deck is already available within Black-Red.
- **Power versus synergy is the right frame for deckbuilding here.** Some decks want half a dozen
  genuinely powerful cards with the rest set up to play normal Magic around them; others want the
  synergy. Deciding which one you are building is the decision that matters.

## Card tips

- **White Auracite** — the format's best common by win rate, and the subject of Ep 16's broader essay
  on why cheap interaction is well-positioned right now.
- **Sahagin** — the anchor of the blue-is-best argument; a cheap blue common whose four toughness is
  load-bearing in a format where the cheap removal tops out at three damage.
- **Sorceress's Schemes** — *"something that I never would have tried without the data."* Sam flags
  it as roughly the third-best red uncommon by opening-hand win rate, just behind Thunder Magic, and
  notes it climbs further when filtered to strong players. Notable because a card that does nothing
  in the wrong deck usually posts a poor aggregate number and this one does not. **Best in UR and BR.**
- **Thunder Magic** — the tiered shock variant, and his benchmark for a strong red common.
- **Black Mage's Rod** — discovered by losing to it. He saw it twice in scrappy Blue-Black decks made
  almost entirely of commons, and the insight is that the deck wanted to **attach it to creatures
  rather than cast more spells**, backed by cantrips like Dreams of Laguna.
- **Dreams of Laguna** — the two-mana surveil-and-draw with flashback that supported the Black Mage's
  Rod decks above.
- **Summon: G.F. Ifrit** — he is explicit that **the win-rate data on this is bad** and mostly agrees
  with it. The nuance is where *not* to play it: **not in UR or BR**, because those decks already get
  their card filtering from the non-creature spells they actually want to cast. He will play it in
  **RW** sometimes, where that filtering is otherwise missing.
- **Adventurers' Airship (?)** — a card he wants specifically in **RW or WG**, decks that otherwise
  have no way to see extra cards. Same logic as the Ifrit note: card selection is a role, and which
  colours need it filled differs.
- **Reach the Horizon** — the Ep 15 lens for splash discipline rather than a card recommendation.

## Deep dives

### Ep 12 — Sahagin: what it means when blue is the best colour
Two parts: a history of Arena-era formats where blue was the strongest colour, and then FIN's blue
cards specifically. The generalisable point is the method — he reads colour strength off the
**archetype** win rates rather than off card evaluations, and checks whether *all* of a colour's pairs
clear the aggregate bar.

### Ep 13 — Sorceress's Schemes: when the data says a narrow card is good
A card that recurs instants and sorceries from the graveyard "should" have a poor aggregate win rate,
because it is dead in the wrong deck. It doesn't. The episode is about taking that kind of signal
seriously — when the aggregate likes a narrow card, there is usually something real there.

### Ep 14 — Black Mage's Rod: equipment as a spells-matter payoff
Built from two games he lost to the card at MagicCon Vegas, both against scrappy commons-and-uncommons
Blue-Black decks. The lesson is that the equipment rewarded a deck that attached it and attacked,
rather than one that kept casting spells.

### Ep 15 — Reach the Horizon: when to splash, and when not to
His most transferable FIN episode. Walks the tiers of splashing — one card, a couple of cards, a full
multicolour pile — and concludes that FIN mostly does not want any of it, because the pairs are
self-sufficient. Introduces the power-versus-synergy framing for deckbuilding.

### Ep 16 — White Auracite: interaction versus creatures in modern Limited
A design-history essay: how pushed commons and multi-body creatures made aggro dominant for a few
years, how cheap interaction got better and cheap creatures got worse, and why that has slowed the
*critical turn* of a game without lengthening the game itself. FIN is his worked example.

### Ep 17 — Summon: G.F. Ifrit: role-filling and where a bad card is good
Argues from the card's poor aggregate number to a narrower claim — that card filtering is a *role*,
and the colours that already fill it (UR, BR) should not pay for it again, while RW and WG sometimes
should.
