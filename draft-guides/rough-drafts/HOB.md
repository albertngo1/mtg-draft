# HOB — Rough Drafts notes

> Source: **Rough Drafts** (Sam Pratt, "Samp") — 5 The Hobbit episodes/streams. Distilled 2026-08-23
> from YouTube auto-caption transcripts; card names corrected against the printed HOB list where
> confident, uncertain readings marked `(?)`.
>
> **The whole HOB run is a deliberate blind experiment.** Samp went into the set having seen almost
> no previews, refused all outside discussion, and — crucially — **did not look at 17Lands data for
> any of the material below**. That makes this the one source in `draft-guides/` that is genuinely
> *decorrelated* from the numbers the card reference already shows. It is also the one source most
> likely to be wrong on any individual card, and he says so repeatedly. Read it as a well-argued
> structural read on the format, not as card evaluation.

- **Episode 73 (Hot Topics) is not a preview in the usual sense.** It is a reaction to the Wizards

- **Episodes 74 and 75 are the argued reads** — ~6 drafts in and ~2 weeks in respectively. Ep 75 is

- **Episode 76 (~2026-08-27) is the data reveal and is NOT downloaded.** That is the episode where he

## Supersessions (newer take wins)

- **Blue: "kind of cracked" → good cards, badly positioned.** Day 2: *"The blue cards are kind of
  cracked. I feel like blue is really good. I've liked blue white a lot."* Ep 74 doubles down —
  Plunder + Bilbo at common *"is really raising my esteem of blue in particular."* → **Ep 75 walks it
  back:** *"I was really high on blue last week and I am lower now that I've seen exactly what black
  can do."* Cause is explicit: laddering up meant facing better and better BR curve-outs, which blue
  is not built to answer. **Verdict: blue's card quality is real, its positioning is not.**
- **Plunder the Trollshaws: top-common candidate → dampened.** The ep-74 namesake was one of his
  *"pulls for top commons in the set"* and *"generally just better than Think Twice."* → **Ep 75
  explicitly names it as a card his raised BR expectations have "dampened."** Note the shape of the
  reversal: the card did not get worse, the format got faster.
- **Crude Bent Blade: ignored → above Murder.** Day 1 chat told him it was good; *"it was day one so
  I didn't put much stock into it."* Day 2, drafting BR himself: *"This card owns. It's pretty sick."*
  → **Ep 75 rates it, with Stir Up Trouble, above the format's Murder reprint.** *"This took me a
  while to come around to."*
- **Long Lake Nuisance / Esgaroth Garrison: "garbage" → rock-solid.** He called both garbage on first
  read, then reversed once recruit clicked as a bring-a-buddy effect. Garrison settles at *"C level,
  maybe C++… somewhere in the range of C− to C++"* — an honest hedge, not a promotion.
- **Storied as the WR identity → WR is an equipment deck.** Ep 73 spent real time guessing what
  `storied` would be. By the end of Day 1: *"the Wizards archetype description… but the dwarfs deck
  is like an equipment deck,"* and his trophying RW deck had **one** card that said storied on it.
- **Green: "very unfocused" (ep 73) → "green looks a little bit scattered" (ep 74) → "Green is good.
  I haven't lost to green much" (Day 2 aside).** The most unresolved thread in the run — he never
  drafted a green deck, so all three takes are opponent-side observations. Do not lean on any of them.
- **Uneasy Partings — he corrects himself on air.** He noticed on Day 1 that the discount reads
  *nontoken*, forgot it, then re-read it live in ep 75: *"I'm just reading that — that says nontoken
  creature, so this doesn't even work against armies like I thought it did."*

## Format speed / meta read

**The thesis: HOB is a small-game, tempo format, black is the best colour, and menace is the reason
black wins — not amass by itself.** Everything below is that argument.

- **"The Hobbit is the best menace has ever been in a limited format."** This is his headline claim
  and it is a claim about *critical mass*, not card quality. Goblin Plate Mail and Gollum, Silent
  Slinker are individually only *"kind of like a 1.5-for-1 sort of card that's just a decent rate"* —
  but they are at common, and **this is a small set, so a draft table opens more copies of each
  common than normal.** The result is that a black deck routinely assembles two or three menace
  attackers, and the opponent needs **six blockers to take zero**.
- **Amass free-rolls the first eight points of damage.** An amass army scales to a 4/4 or 5/4 within
  the opening turns. *"Your opponent can't really block a 4/4 in the first couple turns of the game,
  or especially a 5/4 menace."* They can answer the army on turn four or five — but by then they are
  eight down and *"in quite the hole that makes it really hard for them to turn around."*
- **Amass is stealth haste, and this is the single most transferable play insight in the run.**
  Adding counters to an *existing* Army means the new stats attack immediately. He found this himself
  mid-draft (*"there was an aha moment… oh, that thing gives haste"*), then built around it: trade the
  army off, rebuild it post-combat so the bigger army swings the following turn. He calls Tidings of
  War *"pretty sick as a haste enabler"* and reads Gollum's adventure as *"amass in disguise — it's
  like a +1/+1 counter with haste."*
- **Menace also invalidates the other half of the format.** Recruit hands the WU deck a stream of 1/1
  chump blockers; menace turns them into nothing. *"Plate Mail makes it really hard to chump block
  like a 7/6 army."* The number of *creatures* your opponent has — not the size of their board — is
  the resource that actually matters.
- **This is a textbook small-game format, and the first good one of 2026.** He is explicit that this
  is the framing to use: keep the battlefield small, and every marginal 1.1-for-1 edge compounds.
  *"If the battlefield is actually empty, that 1/1 token gets to attack turn in, turn out."* Black's
  good cards all do something on the board **and leave something behind** — Goblin Plate Mail buffs
  the army and leaves an equipment; Crude Bent Blade strips a blocker and leaves an equipment. Prior
  examples he names as the same shape: WB in Final Fantasy, black in Edge of Eternities.
- **Black sits on the *tempo* side of tempo/attrition.** The A-plan is killing the opponent *while
  they still hold cards*. The menace + equipment suite is the fallback grind plan for when the fast
  start doesn't finish, which is why the deck has a real second gear.
- **It is a colour read, not a colour-pair read.** He says "black red" throughout but the argument is
  entirely about black: *"Mostly black positions it, in my opinion, very very strongly in this
  format."* Red is largely the hybrid/amass overlap.
- **Five archetypes, not ten, and that structurally hurts blue.** UB and UR are unsupported, so
  blue's only homes are UW and UG. He has drafted UR successfully anyway and says the pair support
  *"doesn't matter too much"* on its own — the real problem is that BR's curve-outs are exactly what
  blue is bad against. *"Black red is really good, blue is really good, but they're sort of opposing
  forces."*
- **It plays small — and he means that as a criticism.** *"It's already starting to feel like a small
  set… I already feel like I'm starting to explore most of what there is to explore on day three."*
  He knew every common in the set after his second draft and every uncommon after his sixth.
- **Little evasion outside menace**, and by his read **the rares may be weak** — he drafted several
  decks with zero rares and asked out loud *"are the rares in this set just bad?"* He also flags that
  not knowing per-colour rare quality is the biggest thing the blind experiment cost him.
- **Adventure barely registers.** Ep 73 predicted it might dominate card quality; in practice
  *"there's really not that much adventure in this set."*
- **Recruit is the best-designed mechanic here** — *"an awesome mechanic, would really love to see
  that come back in a full-size set"* — but designing well is not the same as winning.

## Archetypes

Ranked by the ep-75 (pre-data) read. Note he never drafted a green deck at all, so BG and UG are
opponent-side impressions only.

| Pair | Theme | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **BR — Goblins / amass** | amass + sacrifice + artifacts | **The best deck, by a distance.** Fast, coherent, best curve-outs in the format. Black is doing the work. First BR deck he built he rated *"an eight out of 10… definitely doing the thing"* — with the caveat that he would not go higher without several strong rares. |
| **WU — Humans / recruit** | recruit, draw-your-second-card, token defence | Strong cards, weak position. Its 1/1 tokens are supposed to double-block through menace and mostly don't. He trophied with it on Day 1 (four Plunder the Trollshaws) and liked it a lot before the ladder caught up with him. |
| **WR — Dwarves / storied** | legends + artifacts + sagas | **In practice an equipment deck, not a storied deck.** He trophied his very first blind draft with it. Storied is a low-density afterthought; build it the way you build any equipment deck — pair big power boosts with evasion, especially menace. |
| **BG — Wolves / ferocious** | power 4+ matters | Untested by him and skeptical from ep 73 onward: ferocious normally lives in RG because red gets four-power bodies at a better rate than black. He saw BG decks beat down against him and *wanted* to draft "green-black stompy" but never did. |
| **UG — Elves / landfall** | ramp to late-game payoffs + landfall | **Worst.** *"Blue green has not looked impressive and I don't really see the vision."* Ep 73 called it early on structural grounds: landfall has underdelivered everywhere except original Zendikar, and "late-game payoffs" sitting next to "keep the pressure on" is the classic throwaway blurb. He also never found a reason for a blue deck to want green's expensive top end. |

## Card tips

Confidence is preserved as stated. Where he hedged, the hedge is the content.

**Black**

- **Goblin Plate Mail** — {1}{B/R} equipment; ETB amass Goblins 1 and auto-attach; +1/+0 and menace;
  equip {4}. Rate is only *"kind of like a 1.5-for-1"* — the point is that the menace is **movable**.
  Stack two or three and the opponent needs six blockers to take zero. *"At a certain point I just
  stopped passing this."* Both a card he built around and a card he lost to (*"I don't think we can
  beat this menace equipment"*).
- **Crude Bent Blade** — {2}{B} equipment; ETB opponent sacrifices a creature; +2/+1; equip {2}.
  **Rated above the Murder reprint.** Matters on both ends: menace taxes their creature *count*, so
  edicting even a worthless token hurts, and a real equipment stays behind. He kept forgetting it was
  only equip {2} — *"that's a lot of stats."*
- **Stir Up Trouble** — {B} sorcery, destroy target creature, additional cost sacrifice an artifact or
  creature or pay {4}. The Bone Splinters variant. **Also rated above Murder.** Note the natural
  synergy: black is constantly trading armies off and rebuilding them.
- **Bilbo's Deadly Slice** — {1}{B}{B} destroy target creature; the functional **Murder** reprint he
  spends the run calling "Murder." Fine, but he'd take Crude Bent Blade, Stir Up Trouble, **and**
  probably Rage into the Valley over the first copy.
- **Rage into the Valley** — {2}{B} sorcery; draw a card, lose 1, amass Goblins 2. The Phyrexian
  Rager. **The most honestly hedged card in the file:** *"I have this as sort of middle-of-the-pack
  black… it could be the third best black common, it could be down to like sixth best. There's a
  range there that I don't really have a good grasp on."* He suspects this is exactly the card class
  blind evaluation misses (his comparison: how long it took the field to notice Thraben Inspector).
  He'd take it over the first Bilbo's Deadly Slice. He happily played three copies.
- **Gollum, Silent Slinker** — 4-mana 4/3 menace with a {B} adventure (+1/+1 counter on a creature,
  target player gains 2). The adventure is *"amass in disguise… a +1/+1 counter with haste."*
  Confirmed good — this is the one card he got spoiled on mid-experiment, off a Limited Level-Ups
  thumbnail.
- **Nighthowl Pursuer** — {B} 1/1 menace; ferocious +2/+2 when attacking alongside a power-4 creature.
  Uncommon. *"Just like a really strong card."*
- **Desolation Prowler** — {1}{B} 2/2, pay 2 life for +2/+2 once per turn. The mono-black Putrid
  Leech, and one of two cards he was spoiled on before the set. Same function as an early army:
  arbitrarily large before the opponent has an answer. He notes flatly on Day 1: *"I can't beat
  Putrid Leech."*
- **Reverent Howl** — {2}{B} instant; draw 2 and lose 2, **or** +2/+2 and lifelink. Flagged as a card
  he had *not yet played* and wanted to. *"I feel like I've never really seen a card that combines
  these two modes."* Compares it to a Brothers' War lifelink trick that was never a top common but was
  an important piece of that format's black decks. Explicitly speculative.
- **Front Porch Sentries** — {1}{B} 2/2; dies-trigger −1/−1. Reasonable curve filler; the trigger
  caught him out in a race on Day 1.
- **Stony-Voiced Goblins** — {1}{B} 1/1, ETB each opponent discards. The Burglar Rats. Live enough
  that he calls HOB *"a Burglar Rats format"* and lets it change whether he holds cards.

**Blue**

- **Bilbo Baggins, Burglar** — {2}{U} 2/1, ETB draw a card, with a blue sorcery adventure that scries
  2. *"Phenomenal. I cannot imagine that this card is medium at all."* The scry-2 front half widens
  your keepable opening hands substantially — he kept a one-lander off it. **Still rated phenomenal in
  ep 75 even as he cools on blue overall**, which is the point: the blue downgrade is positional, not
  card-by-card.
- **Plunder the Trollshaws** — {1}{U} instant, draw a card; draw two instead if cast from the
  graveyard; flashback {3}{U}. He almost never cast the front half. Four copies anchored a Day 1
  trophy deck. *"Better than Think Twice, which is a really impressive bar for a common to hit."*
  ⚠ **Dampened in ep 75** — see Supersessions.
- **Uneasy Partings** — {3}{U} instant, put a creature on top or bottom of library; costs {1} less
  against an attacking **nontoken** creature. **The discount does not apply to amass armies** — he
  misread this for a week and corrected himself on air. Still fine at full price against an army.
- **Bilbo, Luckwearer** — 2-mana 1/1 unblockable that loots on damage, with a 5-mana adventure
  exchanging control of two nonland permanents sharing a card type. The adventure massively
  overdelivered — swapping a 1/1 recruit token for the opponent's giant Army came up repeatedly, as
  did stealing things stuck under blue's enchantment removal. *"You get a lot more out of that mode
  than I initially thought."*
- **Lakeshore Apothecary** — {1}{U} 1/2 vigilance that grows on your second draw each turn. Vigilance
  is the load-bearing word: *"it brick walls a ton of stuff."* Bad top-deck, but clearly better than
  the other blue two-drops he saw.
- **Long Lake Nuisance** — {3}{U} 3/1 flier, ETB recruit. *"I called this garbage the first time I
  read it"* → a rock-solid common once he reframed it as a bring-a-buddy flier (he compares it to an older "cloud guard" (?) common).
- **Sound the Trumpets** — {2}{U} counter target spell, recruit if MV ≤ 2. The "Cancel" he kept
  taking. *"I've come way up on Cancel in limited"* — with the caveat that his opponents had clunky
  curves and he was punishing them hard. He wanted two, not three.
- **Thranduil's Decree** — six-mana counterspell that exiles a countered permanent and lets you cast
  it free. *"Pretty funny. I like this card."* Held it up successfully; a real late-game blue payoff.
- **Great Gilded Boat** — blue rare. He could not beat it. *"The boat is just ridiculous… we're
  getting owned by the boat. Can't beat the boat."*
- **Gandalf, Wandering Wizard** — 5-mana 4/5 ward {3}. *"Ward three on Gandalf, kind of sick."*
  Reasonable, not exciting.

**White**

- **Esgaroth Garrison** — {4}{W} */5 (power = creatures you control), ETB recruit. Called garbage on
  first read; settled at *"C level, maybe C++… somewhere in the range of C− to C++. Pretty good for a
  five drop."* It stabilises well and chump blockers are genuinely valuable against large armies.
  Excellent target for the WU lifelink signpost.
- **Magnificent End** — {4}{W} instant, deals 5, costs {3} less targeting a **tapped** creature.
  He expected to never play it and *"ended up playing it a decent amount, and I thought it was fine."*
  Whelmed, not happy — but a real playable.
- **Dwarven Shortsword** — {3}{W} equipment that brings its own 2/2 Dwarf. A solid body-plus-equipment
  package for the WR deck.
- **Settle the Wreckage** — the straight reprint, one of two cards he got spoiled on. *"I don't think
  Settle the Wreckage is that good in limited."*
- **Belladonna Took** — white rare; pays off every token entering. He lost a lot of incremental life
  to it in one game and rated the effect highly.

**Red**

- **Ragged Short Spear** — {1}{R} equipment; ETB may discard a card to draw two; +2/+0; equip {3}.
  *"Very impressed."* A Tormenting Voice stapled to a Bone Splitter, and he notes this class of card
  (Witch's Mark, and a second card the captions render as "melded Moxite" (?)) has consistently outperformed how it reads. Pairs specifically with
  menace: a big power boost plus any evasive keyword is the format's damage engine.
- **Tidings of War** — {R} sorcery, amass Goblins 1, amass 3 instead from a graveyard, flashback
  {3}{R}. Rated primarily as a **haste enabler**, not a body: trade the army off, flash this back
  post-combat, attack with the rebuilt army next turn. *"Tidings is pretty sick as a haste enabler."*
- **Bothersome Noisemaker** — {1}{R} 2/2 that amasses on every noncreature spell. *"Obviously a very
  good card"* / *"so sick"*. He ran three alongside a pile of equipment and it *"worked very well."*
- **Óin the Brave** — {1}{R} 1/3 legendary Dwarf; storied gives +1/+0 and haste; {1},{T}, discard:
  draw. *"There is a lot going on with this card for a common."* Not a top common by his read, but he
  was decently impressed. Note storied timing matters — sagas leave, so your story count is not
  permanent.
- **Pinecone Strike** — {1}{R} 3 damage with exile, and/or destroy an artifact token. Clean removal;
  he flags there are only ~two token types worth killing (Treasures and the Dwarf-made axe tokens).
- **Smaug, the Great Calamity** — 7-mana 5/5 flier with a deal-5 adventure. **His most confident
  negative prediction:** *"This card is going to be the most overrated card of all time… people are
  going to point to that card when they win and not blame it being stuck in their hand when they
  lose."* He specifically tracked opponents casting the adventure and still losing.
- **The Misty Mountains Cold** — *"There's no way this card is good, right?"* A red rare he refused
  to take.
- **Gundabad Opportunist** — {3}{R} 4/2 impulse-draw. Playable filler; nothing more.

**Green** (all opponent-side — he never drafted a green deck)

- **Wood Elves** — {2}{G} 1/1 that fetches a Forest. **The card he singles out as a possible reason
  green struggles.** *"My opponents cast this a lot and it was not scary at all… I think this is
  probably being overrated by a lot of people because it is popular in Commander."* If Wizards seeded
  it as a strong green common, that is a problem.
- **Boughside Wanderers** — {4}{G}{G} 4/4, ETB dig four for a permanent, landfall +2/+2. *"A giant
  cantripping creature is pretty enticing."* But he sees no reason a blue deck wants it — *"what is
  green really offering to the blue deck?"* — so he reads it as a **base-green midrange** card, most
  likely BG.
- **Nasty Little Rabbit** — green uncommon; a Warren Elder variant. It got enormous against him more
  than once. *"The gold gamers love the Nasty Little Rabbit."*
- **Attercop** — 2-mana reach/deathtouch with landfall. Annoying blocker; nothing he rated highly.

**Multicolour / colourless**

- **Mirkwood Nurturer** — {2}{G/U} 3/2, ETB bounce a permanent you control for a +1/+1 counter. A card
  he flagged as **unresolved and interesting**, not good or bad: *"I don't know what this does and I'm
  looking forward to figuring out what it can do."* Bouncing your own saga or ETB creature is the
  obvious angle; he never got there.
- **Bolg of the North** — {3}{B}{R} 5/5; sacrifice a creature to fling its power, and excess damage
  amasses. *"That seems fine. I have no read on this whatsoever."*
- **The Great Goblin** — BR rare that pings the opponent whenever counters land on an Army. He lost to
  it while running a removal-light equipment deck; called it out as the card that beat him.
- **Fearsome Goblin Pair** — {2}{B/R} 1/1 that amasses 4 on death. *"Oh, that card's unbeatable"* on
  first contact — a chump blocker that leaves a 4/4 behind is exactly what the menace plan wants.
- **Dwarven Mattock** — {2} equipment, auto-attaches to a Dwarf, +2/+2 and ward {1}, equip {3}. He
  wanted **three copies** in his all-Dwarf deck and got them. **But he flags his own bias in the same
  breath:** *"I don't think this card will be a high win rate card. I would be surprised to see that…
  this is my bias of wanting to predict the win rate instead of just talking about how it actually
  played."* He was reasonably happy with it in play. A rare, explicit split between "played well for
  me" and "expect it to grade well."
- **Well-Worn Spatula** — {1} equipment, ETB gain 2, +1/+1, equip {1}. Short Sword plus two life.
  His conclusion: **it is not what a grindy equipment deck wants** (those want raw power), and it is
  more at home in a blue deck putting it on a flier.
- **Thrór's Map** — legendary-permanent rummager; a storied enabler he was mildly into.

## Pitfalls

1. **Don't read "BR is good" as "draft the pair."** The argument is that **black** is the strong half.
   Red is the hybrid/amass overlap and a source of equipment.
2. **Count blockers, not board size.** Menace is the format's real resource tax. Recruit tokens, chump
   blockers and go-wide boards do not solve a menace attacker — they solve exactly nothing against
   two of them.
3. **Blue's card quality is a trap relative to its position.** Bilbo and Plunder are genuinely
   excellent; that does not make the deck good against a BR curve-out at high rank. This trap only
   revealed itself to him as he laddered up.
4. **Don't build WR around `storied`.** Build it as an equipment deck. His trophy deck ran one storied
   card. Storied is a threshold you cross incidentally, and sagas falling off means the count is not
   stable.
5. **Beware his own stated blind spot: value creatures and cheap card-advantage commons.** He says
   directly that cards like Rage into the Valley are the hardest class to place without data, and
   names Thraben Inspector as the archetypal miss. Where this file hedges on a value common, weight
   the 17Lands number over the prose.
