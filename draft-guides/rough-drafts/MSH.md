## Supersessions (newer take wins)

- **Removal first → value creatures first.** Ep 66 built its pick order around scarcity: only ~19
  removal spells below rare (vs 29 in Avatar), therefore "the removal that is good [is] even more
  premium… these are going to be like top of my pick orders" (Dark Deed, Lightning Strike, Web Up,
  Cruel Alliance, Go Nuts!, We Say Thee Nay!). **Ep 71 reverses the principle outright:** Wizards is
  "overvaluing how good good removal is versus value creatures," and he retells the Cloudkin Seer vs
  Murder graphic to make the point — the color with the value creature beats the color with the kill
  spell, because you two-for-one the Seer when you Murder it. *"When your opponent's playing a Hero
  in Training and a S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drone, that's going to be what matters."* **Verdict: the
  removal scarcity was real, the pick-order conclusion drawn from it was wrong.**
- **"Micro-synergy format, lots of upside" → the pockets are traps.** Ep 66 was excited that the set
  is full of small one-off themes (artifacts, equipment, heroes, villains, +1/+1 counters, draw-your-
  second-card, self-targeting) and predicted decks would look different draft to draft. **Ep 71: those
  themes "were just kind of there and they were just not worth doing unless they fell into your lap,"
  because no mechanic pushes you toward any of them.** The artifact theme exists "because it's Iron
  Man… there's no mechanical reason," which is why it sits on an island. **Verdict: never bend your
  pick order for a pocket synergy in this format.**
- **Basic land cyclers "probably worse than they appear" → the backbone of the format's splashing.**
  Ep 66 downplayed them. **Ep 68 calls the colorless, any-basic cycling cost "a huge step up"** over
  Topiary Panther-style green-gated versions and makes them the mechanism by which base-green decks
  splash high-impact gold rares; Lenz (ep 69) takes the red one "very highly." **Verdict: reversed.**
- **His own home colors: blue → green.** Ep 67 he had drafted blue in 10 of 10 drafts and said so
  explicitly ("kind of warps my perception"). By ep 68 green is his engine; by **ep 71: "basically
  this entire format I've been drafting green… I take Undercover Skrull over basically every common
  except Hero in Training."** Not a reversal of the *color rankings* (white and blue stay the best
  colors) — a reversal of where the best *deck* is once white and blue get contested.
- **Trickster's Stratagem: "very hard to evaluate" → blue's best common.** Ep 66 hedged hard on a
  four-mana sorcery-speed tuck spell and started "a little bit optimistic." Ep 67: *"This card is
  fantastic. It is perfect for this format… if you are not on board with this card, I would highly
  recommend taking this card really highly."* Ep 71: *"blue's probably best common."*
- **Red is the worst color → red is the worst color AND the best lane.** Both halves survive. Ep 66
  ("red looks really bad") and ep 67 (data: distant fifth) stand, and ep 71 confirms red and black
  just didn't get good commons. But ep 69's guest **Justin Lenz** reports 11 base-red decks out of 30
  drafts with 7 trophies (~80% match win rate) precisely *because* the field has read the same data
  and abandoned the color. **Verdict: the color grade was right and the drafting conclusion was
  incomplete — see Archetypes.**
- **Format verdict: optimistic → a mess.** Ep 66: a Dave Humphreys set, open-ended, "lot to look
  forward to." **Ep 71: *"I just don't really like this format"* and *"this set limited design-wise is
  a little bit of a mess."*** He would put all five non-Spider-Man 2025 sets above every 2026 set.
  The gameplay he still rates highly; the *design* he does not.

## Format speed / meta read

**The thesis: MSH is a raw card-quality format that produces big, cluttered boards. The set is a pile
of legendary characters with half a dozen themes and no mechanic pushing you into any of them, so the
only reliable plan is to take the best card in every pack, start in white or blue (or green for the
fixing), and never pay a card-quality tax for a synergy.**

- **The color balance is the most skewed in years.** Ep 68 states it flatly: white is extremely good
  at common, uncommon *and* rare; blue is a touch behind and also very deep; green is solidly in the
  middle; black is closer to red than to green; red is "really, really lacking." The load-bearing
  claim: **"the blue and white commons are better than everything you get in black and red" —
  taken as a whole, blue/white at common beat black/red at uncommon.** So he starts every draft
  assuming white or blue, and says you get there anyway just by taking the best card for eight picks.
  He notes he does *not* normally draft this way even in imbalanced formats; the gap here is too wide.
- **Boards clog. That is the format's structural signature.** Ep 67's real argument: big board stalls
  are rare in 2020s Magic, and MSH brings them back. Games go long, the ground gums up, and the game
  resolves around whatever can attack over the top. Hence **flying is genuinely good here even though
  the flyers themselves are mostly C-level cards** — the *environment*, not the rate on the flying
  bodies, is what makes evasion strong. He is explicit that he does not love the individual flyers.
- **"Big game" cards are what create the clog.** Ep 67 borrows Sam Black's big-game / small-game
  framework (the real Sam Black — see Caption garble). Cards that add resources without removing
  anything from the battlefield — Hero in Training, Bold Biochemist, Raft Security Officer — generate
  big games. That is exactly why blue and white are the best colors: they clog the board *and* draw
  cards, and then win in the air. Ep 71 reuses this to explain why token-making black cards and Alien
  Invasion underperform: **on a clogged board, small tokens do not matter.**
- **Whoever runs out of things to do loses.** Lenz's line in ep 69, agreed to by Samp: the games are
  long, so top-end and card advantage matter more than he had been used to. A treasure you make every
  turn and never spend is not advantage.
- **Removal is scarce, especially at uncommon.** Ep 66's count: ~19 removal spells below rare (13
  common, 6 uncommon) vs 29 in Avatar. Missing: a white pacifism, a one-mana burn spell, the
  Ride's End variant. Ep 67 confirms from play — *"I often am finishing the drafts with just like a
  couple pieces of removal short of where I am comfortable."* **Consequence: you cannot build a
  control deck here**, and a critical mass of individually-relevant creatures (flyers, reach, burn
  reach) taxes the opponent's few answers.
- **Fixing is exceptional and it changes the pick order for gold rares.** 28 gold rares and mythics —
  as many as a faction set like Secrets of Strixhaven, unprecedented for a 10-pair set. Combined with
  colorless any-basic land cycling in all five colors, Undercover Skrull, Ant-Man's Army treasures
  and gain lands, **very strong gold rares table** because the blue-white seats can't cast them.
  Ep 71: *"The fixing's great in this format. You don't have to worry too much about not being able to
  cast your cards, especially when it's a powerful rare."* Ep 68's method: get **heavy** base one
  colour (9–10 sources), then add one basic per splash colour (two for a double pip).
- **"Nephews."** Ep 66's coinage-by-adoption (from the Limited Level-Ups Discord, credited to user
  Lava Axe): **N**o **E**nters, **P**rotection, **H**aste — the four- and five-mana creatures with no
  ETB, no protection and no haste that you have to untap with. Almost every signpost uncommon in MSH
  is one. Samp's structural worry going in was that the set contains so many that you'd be forced to
  play them. In practice the good blue/white/green commons meant you mostly did not have to, and the
  hedge he offered — that the format might "even out" because everyone plays them — is the part that
  did not need testing.
- **Rare texture:** good rates on creatures, not game-invalidators; power correctly concentrated in
  mythic rather than rare. He compares this favourably to Tarkir Dragonstorm and says it makes the
  format play like Avatar — the rares are beatable if you get on board and pressure.
- **Why the set failed as design (ep 71).** Power up, teamwork and connive don't beget archetypes the
  way Final Fantasy's job select / tiered spells / saga creatures did. The conceit that every creature
  uncommon-and-above is a legend crowded out mechanical design space, so the format ended up being
  about individual card power rather than about its mechanics. His own suggested fix: a mechanic that
  actually cares about legends, or about hero/villain counts. He rates the *gameplay* highly — the
  card-quality, who's-the-beatdown texture clarifies decisions — and the *design* poorly.

## Archetypes

Colors, not color pairs. Ep 66 predicted it ("the decks are more defined by the colors than the color
pairs… I'm going to get really deep into white and then choose my secondary color a lot later") and
the whole run bears it out.

1. **Base green, splash everything — Samp's settled home.** Ranked first *for him*, not first in the
   abstract: green's commons are weaker than white's or blue's, but the fixing (Undercover Skrull,
   Ant-Man's Army, Savage Land Dinosaur, gain lands, Villainous Hideout) lets you play the best gold
   rares at the table. **Critically, this is not a soupy converge control deck** — he says so twice.
   It is a proactive midrange curve with mana acceleration that happens to cast off-color bombs.
   Template from ep 68: 10 forests, 3 plains, 2 swamps, 2 duals, two Undercover Skrulls, two
   Ant-Man's Armies, a Dependable Quinjet and a land cycler, casting a double-black The Coming of
   Galactus and five white cards.
2. **Blue-white / white-x value and flyers — the format's actual best deck, and the most contested.**
   Cheap value creatures clog the ground, card advantage stacks up, flyers close. Lenz: *"if blue
   white is open, I will happily play blue white every single draft and trophy."* White also has the
   most reason to splash — its mythic MDFCs plus the strongest land cycler (Borough Backup).
3. **Red-white or blue-red grindy treasures (Lenz's lane).** Not an aggro deck — the *opposite*. Red's
   aggressive commons are the trap that tanks the color's numbers. Instead: Stark Industries
   Executive on one, convert treasures into cards (K'un-Lun Warrior, Vision of Love, Bullseye), pair
   with white's premium removal or blue's counterspell, splash red's very strong gold rares, and win
   with Photon Blast Barrage / Hawkeye, Young Avenger / Red Hulk inevitability. Everything but the
   first few picks comes late because nobody at the table wants red.
4. **Black-red villains.** Real only when the bombs flow — Avengers: Under Siege performs in the A
   range on 17Lands despite the color pair, and Samp would first-pick it out of most packs. Lenz: *"I
   think I only end up there when the bombs are really flowing."*
5. **Green-white / blue-green midrange.** The straightforward card-quality pairs; green is a very good
   support color for whichever of white or blue is open.

**Not decks:** the artifact "blue-red" theme (only a handful of cards actually care, no mechanic
pushes it), the equipment theme (three black uncommons and a WB rare pointing at bad common
equipment), the white-black attack-alone theme, and any dedicated self-targeting build. Ep 66's one
live idea here — that **blue-black** is a better equipment home than white-black because Falcon's
Wing Harness on evasive bodies beats suiting up ground creatures — was never revisited and should be
treated as an untested preview take.

## Card tips

Hedges are the host's own. Where he said "I have no idea," that is the note.

### White

- **Hero in Training** — **the best common in the set** (eps 67, 68), and one of only two commons
  Samp takes over Undercover Skrull. Three-mana 2/2, ETB draw a card, gain 2 with another hero. The
  archetypal big-game card: mediocre on both offense and defense, but it clogs and it replaces
  itself. Ep 66 hedged that it is "a little close to Inspiring Overseer for comfort" and rated
  Overseer clearly better for having flying; play showed the hedge didn't matter. *"I would really
  recommend not passing this at all."* Splashable — he has cast it off zero Plains.
- **Web Up** — Banishing Light at common. Premium hard removal, and the type he is happy to splash
  because it answers cleanly late rather than trading tempo.
- **Murdock's Crusade** — premium white removal (Lenz): exiles a creature with toughness 4+, i.e.
  hits exactly the threats Lightning Strike and HULK SMASH! cannot. A big part of why red pairs
  better with white than with blue.
- **Borough Backup** — the white land cycler, and the best of the cycle in both ep 66 (prediction) and
  ep 67 (play): five mana for two 3/2 vigilance heroes. Six power over two bodies is a real teamwork
  enabler, and vigilance means they clog the ground and still attack. Single-pipped, so splashable.
- **Take Up the Shield** — *"This card's awesome."* Reads as an aggro trick, plays as a midrange card:
  he would have to be very creature-light not to play the first copy. Two uses that matter here — win
  the combat, or just staple lifelink onto your biggest thing so the opponent cannot kill you on the
  backswing and you get the extra turn to close in the air.
- **Raft Security Officer** — two-mana 1/3 tapper. "A very good two-drop that I've been playing a
  lot"; the 1/3 body is the point (see Bold Biochemist).
- **Agent 13, Sharon Carter** — best of the attack-alone cards; three-mana 3/2, investigate whenever a
  creature attacks alone. Good specifically because your lone attacker is usually a flyer.
- **S.H.I.E.L.D. Spy Kit** — one-mana equipment, +1/+1, untap and scry when the equipped creature
  attacks alone. Same logic as Agent 13.
- **Brave Brawler** — two-mana 2/1 that Lenz pairs with HULK SMASH! for double-digit life swings in
  one combat.
- **Panther Pounce** — *"I don't think it's a good card in the aggregate"*, but a genuine engine with
  Iron Fist, Living Weapon (untap to re-fire the ping). Preview take, never revisited.
- **Super Villain Lockup** — ep 66 predicted a **heavy underperformer**: a Seal Away reprint that only
  hits tapped creatures, with the Dominaria data showing it was drafted at rare-level ASL and played
  badly. Never revisited, so this is still just a prediction.
- **Avengers Assemble!** — busted white mythic that cares about heroes; an excellent splash target off
  a green base as long as you have the hero count.
- **The Mind Stone** — two-mana indestructible legendary rock that taps for white; ep 71 *"I think
  this card is just good. I would not take it above Hero in Training, of course."* A two-mana rock is
  worth more in a format this slow, and the six-mana flicker mode is real — Red Guardian,
  Super-Soldier is the fun target, and power up gets discounted by the re-entry.

### Blue

- **S.H.I.E.L.D. Deployment Drone** — a Preening Champion upgrade with an artifact type line. *"This
  card's fantastic"* — 3/3 worth of stats over two bodies, with the power in the air and a body left
  behind to hold the ground. **Note the calibration:** he expected it to be the best common and the
  data had it around third, which surprised him.
- **Trickster's Stratagem** — see Supersessions. Four-mana sorcery tuck plus a connive. Ep 71 rates it
  blue's best common. It wins races by removing the opponent's single reach/blocker while growing
  your clock, and turning a three-turn clock into a two-turn clock is the whole game when you're
  winning in the air. Also the enabler that makes Ms. Marvel worth playing.
- **Bold Biochemist** — two-mana 1/3, power up 6 for a counter and two cards. He *generally hates* the
  two-mana 1/3 stat line (it doesn't deter attacks) but says it is perfect *here*, because the card's
  job is to survive to the late game and it blocks well into double blocks. Compare Landscape Painter
  in Secrets of Strixhaven, whose 2/1 body could not participate in combat and keep the payoff.
- **Justice, Vance Astrovik** — three-mana 2/2 flyer, ETB bounce a nonland nontoken permanent, grows
  on your own bounces. *"Absurd on rate… just stop passing this, just never pass it."* Note the one
  real knock from ep 66: it cannot bounce tokens, and bouncing tokens is a lot of what makes
  Man-o'-War good.
- **Atlantis Attacks** — seven-mana teamwork-4 sorcery: a 6/5 hexproof leviathan and/or bounce one or
  two nonland permanents. Ep 66 could not evaluate it ("I don't know how this card is going to
  play… seven is very, very expensive"). Ep 67 settles it: **the best spell in the format for
  invalidating the ground**, an instant stabilizer that also blanks black's menace tokens. Pairs with
  Baxter Building (the hexproof body is a toughness-4 creature the opponent cannot remove).
- **We Say Thee Nay!** — quench with teamwork 2 (counter unless they pay 4 if teamworked). Ep 66's
  most optimistic hedge: *"could just be one of the best commons in the set."* **Never confirmed** —
  it reappears only in ep 69 as a good card to hold up alongside a Stark Industries Executive
  treasure. Treat the ceiling as unverified.
- **Falcon's Wing Harness** — the best equipment below rare; snap-on flying.
- **Aerial Doombot** — one-mana 1/1 that powers up into a 4/4 flyer; the cheap evasive body that
  carries the attack-alone payoffs.
- **Ms. Marvel, Kamala Khan** — powerful, and ep 71's **cautionary tale about pick order**: three-mana
  1/4 vigilance reach whose power equals your hand size and who draws a card on every spell targeting
  your creatures. *"That's not a card I want to take early and build around. That's a card where I
  want to have two Trickster's Stratagems in my pool, get passed a Ms. Marvel, and say, oh cool, I'll
  do that now."* Building around it pushes you into bad cards.
- **Pym Particles** — one of the bad cards Ms. Marvel tempts you into. Named explicitly.
- **A.I.M. Scientists** — the blue land cycler; third-best of the cycle behind green and white.
- **Futurist Forge** — a good treasure sink for blue-red grindy builds.

### Black

- **Dark Deed** — ep 66's headline card: *"literally just strictly better Last Gasp"*, two mana
  instant −4/−4, and it answers nine of the ten signpost uncommons (all but Hulk, Gamma Goliath).
  *"It is kind of an outrageous card to print in a set like this."* Caveat: this was the centrepiece
  of a pick-order argument ep 71 walked back — the removal is still good, it just isn't ahead of the
  value creatures.
- **HYDRA Troopers** — three-mana 3/2 that makes a tapped 2/1 menace villain if two creature cards
  are in your yard, otherwise mills two. *"Kind of an outrageous rate… plays really, really well in
  multiples."* Ep 67 supplies the counterweight: black's whole payoff class is 2/1 menace tokens, and
  **on a clogged board those tokens are exactly what blue/white blanks** — one of his stated reasons
  black underperformed.
- **Widow's Bite** — predicted underperformer, *"I don't really see the vision on this card"*; −2/−2
  hits a much worse slice of the format than Last Gasp's −3/−3 ever did.
- **Cruel Alliance** — three-mana teamwork-2 exile with lifegain. "Probably the least good" of the
  premium commons he listed, but still fine.
- **Ronin, Shadow Stalker** — the equipment payoff. The theme it points at never materialized.
- **M.O.D.O.K.** — **his favorite rare of the set.** 3BB 2/2 flying lifelink, pay 3 life to connive on
  your turn, opposing creatures get −1/−1. Very powerful and genuinely open-ended: a Necropotence-
  style puzzle of how much life to convert.
- **Ninja of the Hand** — three-mana 2/2 deathtouch with a power-up discard. Matters as a *tool for
  forcing a small game*, which is the only way the slow token engines get to matter.

### Red

- **Stark Industries Executive** — one mana 1/2, tap at instant speed for a treasure. **Lenz's #1 red
  common, with his own hedge attached: "maybe overhyped on my part, but I do take it the highest."**
  It is not a rate card; it is a key that unlocks cards nobody else at the table wants (K'un-Lun
  Warrior, Vision of Love, Bullseye) and pays for splashes. Instant-speed activation means you never
  go shields-down. Bad top-deck late, so it needs a deck that mitigates that. Happy to play multiples
  (the extra one connives away); happy to take it 13th. Also good sacrifice fodder for Killmonger,
  Scourge of Wakanda and The Ruinous Wrecking Crew.
- **Photon Blast Barrage** — X-spell that copies itself; *"it kind of is Plague Wind."* Lenz holds it
  until it kills four. **Goes criminally late.** With Hawkeye, Young Avenger it is a board wipe; with
  Red Hulk at X=5 or 6 *"the game is usually just over."* Lenz's own read is that this is the card
  that ends his Stark-exec era once people catch on.
- **Jessica Jones, Private Eye** — **his favorite uncommon of the set** (ep 71). Three-mana 2/3, tap
  and stun for two-plus cards of exiled-and-playable value. *"You don't want to attack with Jessica
  Jones"* — she is proof red's good cards want a grindy game, not an aggressive one. Ep 66 had her
  filed under "I really don't know how to evaluate."
- **Lightning Strike** — the reprint. Ep 66 flagged the limitation up front: a lot of toughness-4
  creatures in this format, so it doesn't trade cleanly with the signposts the way Dark Deed does.
  Lenz prefers it over HULK SMASH! specifically in blue-red, where you want to hold up We Say Thee
  Nay! and a two-mana answer at the same time.
- **HULK SMASH!** — instant-speed punch, or teamwork-4 to destroy a noncreature artifact. Lenz: *"I
  think I might"* prefer it to Lightning Strike, in red-white or red-green. Artifact destruction is
  live more often than usual here.
- **Crimson Operative** — four-mana exile-the-top-card. Good, competing at a crowded mana value;
  happy to play multiples.
- **K'un-Lun Warrior** and **Vision of Love** — abysmal stats, *"really bad when you discard a card and
  really quite good when you're sacrificing a treasure."* Only playable behind Stark Industries
  Executive; with it they build your own Hero in Training.
- **Bullseye, Death Dealer** — *"kind of a bad card that I've had a lot of success with"* (Lenz). Turn
  three it is a 2/3 that blocks; late, with an exec, it is five mana instant-speed deal 2 every turn,
  and with Hawkeye, Young Avenger it is deal 4 and locks most opponents out.
- **Hawkeye, Young Avenger** — buffs noncreature damage by its power, which catches punch and fight
  spells and other things you wouldn't file as burn. Samp has been blown out by fighting it.
- **Hawkeye's Bow** — the community's pick for a top red common. Ep 69 quietly declines to endorse the
  ping plan; Lenz's whole thesis is that the aggressive red commons are the trap.
- **Loki Laufeyson** — two-mana 2/1 that copies your instants and sorceries. Ep 71: *"I've lost to a
  bunch of Loki decks that are just playing a ton of interaction and are able to copy that
  interaction consistently."*
- **Red Hulk** — six-mana 6/7 that counters up and pings when dealt damage; see Photon Blast Barrage.
- **Fin Fang Foom** — copies your instants/sorceries that target an artifact **or a land**, including
  your own permanents, and grows on each copy. He initially misread it as land-destruction-only. Best
  trick: copying Avengers Disassembled doesn't kill it, because the counters land first.
- **Alien Invasion** — **performing poorly in the data, and he defends it only conditionally.** Four
  mana, escalating attacking tokens. It needs a *small* board to matter, which means an
  interaction-heavy deck (Ninja of the Hand, removal) plus Undercover Skrull to deploy it a turn
  early. Not a card to jam into a normal midrange deck.
- **Blazing Crescendo** — ep 66: *"I would not be surprised if this card was like a rock solid C."*
  Combat tricks punish clunky expensive creatures, and the exiled card matters. Never revisited.

### Green

- **Undercover Skrull** — **the ep-68 namesake and his favorite common of the format.** Two-mana 1/1
  villain that taps for any color and becomes a 3/3 with all creature types once two creature cards
  are in your yard. *"Probably one of the strongest mana dorks we've gotten ever at common."* Careful
  with the ranking: **the best green common, not the best common in the set** — by ep 71 he takes it
  over every common except Hero in Training. Being a villain matters: it dodges Avengers: Under
  Siege. It is a mana dork you are happy to draw late because it is also a real creature.
- **Ant-Man's Army** — three-mana 3/2 with ETB food *or* treasure. One of his favorite commons; he
  plays it "all the time." The treasure is the key to double-splashing, and the Skrull-on-two →
  Army-on-three curve reaches six mana on turn four.
- **Go Nuts!** — one-mana teamwork-3 fight-and-counter. Ep 66 called it *"probably a sleeper for one of
  the better commons in the set"*; ep 68 confirms it as solid-rate removal. Best line: teamwork it off
  Ant-Man's Army on turn three after making the treasure, leaving a 4/3.
- **Pet Avengers** — four-mana 4/4 reach, power up 7 for a counter and a 3/2 vigilance token. *"Like
  the second best green common."* The reach comes up constantly in a flying format. Not a control
  card — just a good rate creature.
- **Punishing Punch** — very efficient green removal at uncommon; one of the three uncommons he treats
  as a signal to move into green.
- **She-Hulk, Jade Defender** — the green four-drop uncommon, same signal tier.
- **Ka-Zar of the Savage Land** — ep 66: *"This creature looks outrageous"* — five mana, and unusually
  **both** halves matter (a growing landfall token plus play-lands-off-the-top card advantage).
- **Rapid Rescue** — conditional, and he states the condition precisely: *"I really want to get to like
  18 non-land permanents to play this card… I'm usually playing the first copy when I hit that 18
  permanent threshold, almost never playing the second copy."* Easier than it sounds because your
  removal is often enchantments (Web Up, Frozen in Ice). Also finds Heroic Feast and triggers it at
  instant speed as a pseudo-trick.
- **Savage Land Dinosaur** — the green land cycler, six-mana 7/6 trample and **his favourite of the
  cycle** — with a caveat: it is a bad control card (it gets removed as your first threat and doesn't
  stabilize) and a great proactive-midrange card (it lands after the opponent has spent answers). It
  is the only double-pipped cycler, which makes it the hardest to splash. Best free-cast target for
  World War Hulk.
- **Wakandan Royal Guard** — he ran three in the five-drop slot and *"didn't love it."* Filler.
- **World War Hulk** — five-mana saga: free-cast a red or green creature, three counters, then double
  power/toughness with trample. Won him a Magic Online single-elim. Deckbuilding requirement stated
  plainly: **five to six real hit targets**, ideally a Savage Land Dinosaur (he'd take the second copy
  highly), four-drops acceptable, Ant-Man's Army as the floor. Wombo with **Doc Samson, Super
  Psychiatrist**, whose extra-counter trigger applies to lore counters — you get chapters 1 and 2
  immediately. *"I don't love Doc Samson, but I'm always looking for the first copy when I am playing
  a Hulk deck."*
- **Heroic Feast** — three-mana enchantment, food on ETB, counters on your creatures whenever you gain
  life. *"Pretty impressed with this in my green decks"*, good in multiples, and worth playing an
  off-color gain land or two purely for triggers.
- **Call Damage Control** — clunky by construction; you cannot cast it in your opening sequence and it
  needs things to have gone wrong first. Filed under open questions, never revisited.
- **Super Strength** — five-mana aura, +4/+4 trample ward 1. Ep 66's sleeper pick — *"I think this
  could be a card that we like the Lords of Limited clunky-looking overperformer category"* — argued
  from removal (especially instant-speed removal) being scarce. Never revisited.
- **Training Regimen** — *"I think this card's bad, but I wouldn't be surprised if it surprised me…
  a card I have my eye on as being a slight overperformer."* Never revisited.

### Multicolor and gold rares

- **Killmonger, Scourge of Wakanda** — *"by far the best of the bunch"* among signpost uncommons
  (ep 66) and still strong in ep 68: **treat it as a mono-green card.** In a base-green deck you have
  the fixing to splash it for free. Also a fine home for a spare Stark Industries Executive.
- **Captain America, Living Legend** — the other signpost that isn't a nephew: a three-mana 3/4
  vigilance that untaps your creatures, above rate and able to affect the turn it lands.
- **Iron Man, Master of Machines** — the archetypal nephew and ep 66's worked example. Enormous upside
  (a huge flyer that draws cards) and no ETB, no protection, no haste. Lenz still plays it with
  Vision Quest as his only two blue cards in a red deck.
- **The Super Hero Civil War** — top-tier gold rare. *"No one's really going to pass you Super Hero
  Civil War. The word is out on that one."*
- **Avengers: Under Siege** — four-mana BR saga (two menace villains → 2 damage to each non-villain
  and each opponent → a treasure per villain). **Performing in the A range on 17Lands despite the
  color pair**, and it still gets passed late. *"I would first pick this out of most packs… love this
  card, stop passing it."* Weakest in white-black (you're full of heroes); best in the junk space.
  Getting it down early off Undercover Skrull is a tempo play, not a mistake — it strands the
  opponent's two-toughness creatures in hand.
- **Wolverine, Fierce Fighter** — strong gold rare that tables because the many blue-white seats
  cannot cast it.
- **Vision Quest** — very splashable, needs only about two artifacts, comes surprisingly late.
- **Monica Rambeau** — named by Lenz among the top-performing red gold cards (flips into Photon).
- **The Coming of Galactus** — BG saga strong enough to double-splash off a heavy green base.
- **The Mighty Thor, Jane Foster** — very good UW rare; the pack-one-pick-one of the ep 68 draft log.
- **Black Panther, Vanguard** — the GW signpost.
- **Hulk, Gamma Goliath** — five-mana 6/5; the one signpost uncommon that survives Dark Deed.
- **The Ruinous Wrecking Crew** — BR rare that comes around but is *"really quite strong."*
- **U.S.Agent, John Walker** — WB hybrid 3/2 that makes and attaches its own +1/+2 equipment. *"That
  card also seems really good"* — preview take, never revisited.
- **Speedball, New Warrior** — effectively hexproof (redirect any spell targeting him, +2/+2). *"One
  of my favorite designs in the format… I have no idea how this is going to play."* He predicted the
  field would misread it on board and blow themselves out. Never revisited.
- **Iron Fist, Living Weapon** — three-mana 2/3 that pings for its power whenever you target your own
  creature. Undersized, real upside; the self-target theme it wants never got support.
- **Cloak and Dagger, Entwined** — his cube pick of the set; a powerful, hard-to-find WB card.

### Colorless, artifacts and lands

- **Baxter Building** — pay 4 and tap to draw if you control a toughness-4+ creature, or to make four
  mana of any colors. Card advantage out of your mana base has been *"very very valuable this
  format"*, and green is where the toughness requirement is free. **Do not count it as a green
  source** — it can't cast Undercover Skrull on two.
- **Villainous Hideout** — the one he plays most. Taps for any color for villains, and three mana to
  connive a villain. **It casts Undercover Skrull on turn two, so it does count toward your 9–10
  green sources** — that extra slot is what buys room for splash basics.
- **Avengers Tower** — the hero analog; play it with a lot of heroes. Activates at instant speed,
  which Lenz values in the treasure decks.
- **Surveillance Room** — the common surveil/filter land. Fine in two-color decks; **he cuts it from
  green splash decks**, because adding a mana to an already-expensive splashed bomb costs too much.
- **Gain lands (duals)** — good, and he wants **no more than three**; past that the tap-lands compound.
  Prefer ones touching green or your secondary color. Exception: play off-color ones for Heroic Feast.
- **Dependable Quinjet** — the Manalith. *"Which I don't usually play"* — but it made the cut in the
  heavy-splash green deck.
- **H.E.R.B.I.E. Scout Unit** — four-mana 2/1 flyer, ETB draw a card and put a land from hand into
  play tapped. Ep 66: *"probably will play a little worse than it reads, but still a rock-solid card
  you're happy to play in your artifact decks."* Never revisited.
- **Stolen Stark Tech** — a Battle Fan reprint; *"not like a really good equipment"*, roughly a C−.
- **HYDRA Assault Robot** — Lenz: *"too slow, and I don't want to put a bunch of clunky bad artifacts
  into my deck."* The artifact-matters plan is not supported below rare.
- **Armor Wars** — Lenz: *"I think it's not a good card"*, but reasonable in a very controlling
  treasure deck where chapter one draws four; an 11th-pick nobody wants.
- **The basic land cyclers, as a cycle** — colorless cycling cost, any basic, single-pipped except
  green. Ranked green > white > blue > red/black by Samp; Lenz takes the red one very highly for its
  five-mana 5/5 reach body, which walls the blue-white flyers and eats a Murdock's Crusade that would
  otherwise hit a bomb. **Don't trim lands to play them** in a green midrange deck — if you have to
  cycle them, they're worse than a land, and the whole point is that you want to cast the back half.

## Pitfalls

1. **Bending your pick order for a build-around.** The single clearest lesson of the retrospective.
   Ms. Marvel is the worked example: taking her early forces concessions that cost you more card
   quality than she gives back. Take the build-around *after* you already have the pieces.
2. **Chasing artifacts, equipment or self-targeting.** Half-supported themes with no mechanic behind
   them. They're worth doing only when they fall into your lap.
3. **Drafting red the way the red commons ask you to.** Aggressive red is why the color's data looks
   as bad as it does. The winning red deck is grindy, splashy and treasure-fuelled — the opposite.
4. **Running out of things to do.** The games are long. If you're activating Stark Industries
   Executive every turn and not advancing the board, you lose. Green ramp decks have the same failure
   mode: the mana from Undercover Skrull is not an advantage if you have nothing to spend it on.
5. **Trying to build a control deck.** There isn't enough removal in the set to support it. Base green
   in particular is a *proactive midrange* deck — *"you really want to be like this base green
   mid-range deck"*, explicitly not the soupy Secrets of Strixhaven converge pile.
6. **Mana-base errors in the splash decks:** counting Baxter Building as a green source; playing more
   than three gain lands; playing Surveillance Room alongside expensive splashes; trimming lands for
   land cyclers; double-splashing without a genuinely heavy base color.
7. **Playing nephews as your plan.** Four- and five-mana creatures with no ETB, no protection and no
   haste die to a two-mana removal spell for a game-losing tempo swing. You will have to play some;
   don't build around them.
8. **Assuming your black tokens matter.** Menace 2/1s look like a payoff and get blanked by clogged
   boards, 1/3 walls and Atlantis Attacks. Ep 67 names this as a top reason black underperformed.
