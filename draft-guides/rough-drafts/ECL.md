# ECL — Rough Drafts notes

> Source: **Rough Drafts** (Sam Pratt) — 7 Lorwyn Eclipsed episodes. Distilled 2026-08-20 from YouTube
> auto-caption transcripts; card names corrected where confident, uncertain readings marked `(?)`.
>
> This channel is structurally different from the other four in `draft-guides/`. Most episodes are a
> 30–60 minute deep dive on a **single card**, using that card as a lens onto a broader format
> concept — so its value here is depth and framing rather than card-by-card grades. The per-set
> **Hot Topics** (preview), mid-format episodes and **Retrospective** carry the format-level read.
> Sam is explicit that he sees his content as a complement to the full set reviews rather than a
> replacement, and points listeners at Lords of Limited and Limited Level-Ups for those.

## Supersessions (newer take wins)

- **The off-supported pairs as real decks** — Ep 44 (preview) treats UB fairies/flash, Mardu blight
  and Temur vivid as genuine archetypes because that is how the official archetype breakdown framed
  them → **Ep 50: that framing "led us astray."** Blight and Vivid are **orthogonal vectors layered
  onto a supported pair**, not decks. UB fairies specifically: *"a deck that I have not actually
  drafted or really seen an inroad into, and I don't think it's very good."* **Verdict: the five
  supported pairs are the format; blight and vivid are modifiers you bolt on.**
- **Brambleback Brute** — Ep 45 builds a whole episode around it as emblematic of the format's
  individually-powerful-card thesis → **Ep 47: "didn't really pan out to be a player in this
  format."** The *concept* survived; the card did not.
- **How hard to commit to a creature type** — early framing follows the supported-tribe map → **Ep 50
  settles on divorcing the colour pair from its tribe** (see Format read below).

## Format speed / meta read

- **The card-quality distribution is the format's defining feature: many A's, very few B's.** Sam's
  central mid-format realisation (Ep 47) is that ECL has an unusual tier spread — a lot of uncommons
  and rares that really matter, and unusually few cards in the "generically good, goes in most decks"
  middle band. **This changes how you draft.** In a normal format you can pack a deck with C+-to-B
  goodstuff and out-value the opponent; in ECL that plan loses. What matters is the **density of
  genuinely impactful cards** in your deck and how well you support them.
- **He frames ECL as the polar opposite of the preceding Avatar format** — where a good curve, board
  presence and tempo were the whole game and synergy was a backdrop. Curve, interaction and pressure
  still matter in ECL, but *"it is so much more about the strength of the individual cards in your
  deck."*
- **Divorce the colour pairs from their supported creature types.** The settled Ep 50 read, and the
  most useful single idea in this file. You do want to end up in a supported pair — but very few
  cards demand a high creature-type count. Most typal payoffs are **low-commitment**: the Eclipsed
  hybrid cycle and Thoughtweft Imbuer are fine with five to eight of a type rather than fifteen. Sam
  played Thoughtweft Imbuer happily in Merfolk decks, and ran Black-Green decks with goblin cards
  in them. *"There were relatively few cards in the format that really asked a lot of you for deck
  construction for your creature types."*
- **Blight and Vivid are orthogonal vectors, not archetypes.** A single Prismabasher in a BG deck
  subtly shifts which cards fill the back half of your deck (now you want Prideful Feastling, Gangly
  Stompling to buff it); a fully dedicated Vivid deck is a different, rarer thing. Same for blight —
  goblins can be built as a hard typal deck **or** as a blight-burn grind deck.
- **Creatures are generally small in this format**, which is why a four-mana 6/6 uncommon plays as a
  genuine threat and why removal that scales past two damage is at a premium.
- **Set structure (Ep 44):** five supported pairs — **WU Merfolk · UR Elementals · BR Goblins ·
  BG Elves · WG Kithkin** — each with a hybrid "Eclipsed" uncommon that digs for a land or a
  creature of the type, plus a gold uncommon that is usually an anthem. The other five pairs get a
  hybrid uncommon and a loose theme but **no gold card**: UB flash/fairies, the Mardu pairs (WB, WR)
  care about −1/−1 counters and blight, the Temur pairs (UG, RG) care about Vivid.
- **He compares the structure favourably to Ikoria and original Strixhaven** rather than to a
  paint-by-numbers Ravnica guild set, and rates the format highly overall — *"I would happily draft
  it again"* — while noting it rotated out before he had finished exploring it.
- **Green is the strongest colour, and Black-Green Elves is the strongest deck** (Ep 49). Green's
  commons and uncommons are generically good even outside a dedicated elf build, which is why
  starting green is defensible almost regardless of where you end up — including in **Red-Green**,
  which is not a supported pair and has no associated type, but is reachable precisely because green
  is that deep.

## Archetypes

- **BG Elves — the strongest deck.** Not because the elf payoffs are broken, but because green's
  card quality carries it and the high-commitment payoffs (Morcant's Eyes wants as many elves as
  possible) coexist with low-commitment ones you can run anywhere.
- **WU Merfolk — a convoke/tempo beatdown deck**, and the home Sam kept returning to for the
  push-damage tools below. Wants a genuinely low curve so the convoke spells are castable.
- **UR Elementals — the mana-rich, card-advantage deck.** The one archetype where a counterspell is
  live, because it makes a lot of mana, sees a lot of cards, and can afford to hold up interaction.
- **BR Goblins — two builds:** a hard goblin-typal version, or a blight-burn grind deck. The second
  is the one that overlaps with the Mardu blight cards.
- **WG Kithkin — go-wide convoke beatdown**, sharing most of its damage-pushing tools with Merfolk.
- **RG (unsupported) — a real deck anyway**, reachable off green's depth. Effectively a Temur Vivid
  base without the blue.
- **UB flash/fairies — avoid.** The one archetype Sam never found a way into and does not rate.

## Card tips

- **Cinder Strike** — *"the best common in the format."* One red mana, deals 2, or **4 if you blight
  1** as an additional cost. He compares it to Flame Slash and takes it over most uncommons.
  Killing a four-mana 4/4 for one mana is the specific line he calls out. ⚠ Note the data: it is a
  fine common but not the top one by win rate — the blight cost is a real build requirement, and the
  field's copies do less than his do.
- **Moon-Vigil Adherents** — 2GG 0/0 elf that grows for each creature you control **and each creature
  card in your graveyard** (not just elves). Seen as a 12/12 in a self-mill elf deck. No ETB and no
  protection, but effectively dodges every red removal spell once it is large. **Overperformer in both
  Elves and Kithkin.**
- **Omni-Changeling** — 3UU 0/0 clone with convoke and changeling; copies your most impactful
  creature or theirs. Especially strong in Merfolk where the token count makes convoke cheap. Flagged
  as broadly overperforming.
- **Gutsplitter Gang** — four-mana 6/6 goblin uncommon; blight 2 each first main or lose 3 life. Not
  generically good, but **as a single large threat in a black deck it impressed** — always a 6/6 on
  defence, since you don't pay until your own turn. A 6/6 is huge in this format.
- **The lord cycle (e.g. Deepchannel Duelist)** — beyond the anthem, they **tax your opponent's
  removal**: once you have played one, they must respect the next.
- **Bark of Doran** — two-mana white equipment, equip 1, +0/+2 and deals damage equal to toughness.
  Excellent on this format's high-toughness creatures; turns an unblockable 2/3 into a 4/4 that
  pushes real damage.
- **Thoughtweft Imbuer** — four-mana 0/5; a creature attacking alone gets +X/+X where X is your
  kithkin count. **You do not need a kithkin deck** — changelings and a few kithkin one-drops are
  enough. Called a way to win a game below rare.
- **Glister Bairn** — Simic hybrid 1/4 with vivid; pumps another creature by the number of colours
  among your permanents. In a two-colour deck that is reliably +3, which is a large boost on an
  evasive threat.
- **Wild Unraveling** — UU instant counterspell with an additional blight-2-or-pay-1 cost. **Not
  generically good** — bad in Merfolk — but strong specifically in Elementals, which has the mana and
  the card flow to hold it up.
- **Evershrike's Gift** — one-mana aura, +1/+0 and flying, rebuyable by blighting. Good with the big
  convoke bodies for pushing mid-to-late-game damage.
- **Goldmeadow Nomad** — one-mana 1/2 kithkin that exiles a creature from your graveyard to make a
  token. Sam played it **more in Merfolk than in Kithkin**, as convoke fodder.
- **Sun-Dappled Celebrant** — six-mana convoke 5/6 vigilance common; the payoff that makes a
  proactive convoke build work.
- **Flaring Cinder** / **Tanufel Rimespeaker** — the persistent card-advantage engines for the
  blue-side convoke and elemental decks, both keyed to casting spells of mana value 4+.
- **Stalactite Dagger** — *"not very good in this format."* Playable in a Merfolk deck that doesn't
  care about its creatures' stat lines, and no more than that.
- **Auntie's Sentence** — two-mana sorcery, choose Thoughtseize-style hand attack or −2/−2. He hadn't
  played it but flagged it as a card he might be **underrating**, with the caveat that it is a poor
  top-deck. Best as a sideboard answer in best-of-three.
- **Brambleback Brute** — the subject of Ep 45; by Ep 47 he reports it *"didn't really pan out."*

## Deep dives

### Ep 45 — Brambleback Brute: individually powerful cards
The thesis that would define his read on the format: ECL rewards packing your deck with cards that
individually matter, rather than assembling a smooth curve of interchangeable playables. The Brute
itself did not survive contact with the format, but the framing did.

### Ep 46 — Gravelgill Scoundrel: convoke, evasion and pushing damage
Uses an unblockable, vigilant blue common as the anchor for how the WU/WG convoke beatdown decks
actually win. Vigilance is load-bearing: attack with it, then still convoke your instant-speed spells
with it after combat. The episode is effectively a catalogue of damage-pushing tools — Bark of Doran,
Thoughtweft Imbuer, Glister Bairn, Evershrike's Gift — and the argument that these decks want a very
low curve so convoke is live every turn.

### Ep 47 — Eclipsed Boggart: the A-tier/B-tier distribution
The most valuable episode in the run. Contrasts ECL against Avatar to argue that ECL's tier spread is
unusually top-heavy — many A-level cards, few generically-good B's — so the "pile up mediocre value
and out-grind them" plan that works in most formats fails here. Draft for the density of impactful
cards and for supporting them.

### Ep 48 — Kulrath Mystic: reading a format as a metagame
Frames learning a limited format through a metagame lens — expected game length, whether early drops
matter, whether there are late-game mana sinks, average creature size — and argues 17Lands answers
these structural questions, not just "is this card good." A methodology episode rather than a card
episode.

### Ep 49 — Gangly Stompling: big creatures and why green is best
Argues green is the strongest colour on the strength of its commons and uncommons rather than the elf
payoffs, which is why Red-Green functions as a real deck despite having no support and no creature
type. Recorded after a 31st-place Spotlight Series finish.

### Ep 50 — Retrospective
Settles the format: the supported-pair structure is good design rather than a limitation; blight and
vivid are vectors, not decks; divorce the pairs from their tribes; the commons were outclassed by the
uncommons and rares and were relegated to role-player status. Overall verdict positive — a format he
would happily draft again and felt rotated out early.
