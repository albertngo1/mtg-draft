## Format speed / meta read

**Thesis: aggro is back. The developers seeded real aggressive tools (one-drops at common and
uncommon, a W/B artifact anthem, reach damage), so beatdown should be a present part of the format
rather than something the aggro deck has to break format norms to do. At the same time the fixing is
"kind of absurd", so mana stretching for top end and colourless utility lands is on the table.**

- **Spacecraft (Edge of Eternities lesson carries over).** Don't spend your life total too freely to
  keep a wide board for stationing. The common failure: you tap out to station, they kill the ship,
  and your tapped board eats a big attack. Stationing is mostly free with any creature-heavy deck; the
  skill is in sequencing. Common spacecraft look stronger than EOE's (three at common: colourless
  manalith, white, blue; five at uncommon). Don't play too many.
- **Power per card matters for stationing.** 1- and 2-power creatures station very inefficiently. Many
  signposts are high-toughness, low-power, so they station poorly.
- **Beam me up** (flashback variant; also return a creature you control to hand). Looks poor at first
  read. Best on **cheap value spells** you can cast whenever (Pattern Buffer Preservation, Open
  Communications, There's Coffee in that Nebula), worst on timing-sensitive ones (the
  Travel-Preparations-style counters card). Synergises with spacecraft: station with a creature, then
  beam it up for pseudo-vigilance. **Don't build hard around it**; prefer generically good ETB
  creatures (Fugitive Listener) over small-ball ones.
- **Value signposts.** Several gold uncommons are high-toughness engines (Seven of Nine; Vash,
  Freelance Archaeologist) whose value scales with game length. Play them in decks that want long
  games. He praises the stat lines: they block and survive.
- **Aggro.** W/B Borg artifact aggro "should build itself" around Locutus. Red gets good one-drops and
  reach (Transporter Malfunction). He is optimistic about the Mardu pairs (W/B, B/R, R/W) and maybe U/R.
  He calls R/B's nominal sacrifice theme "kind of fake". Caveat: if the format is well equipped to
  handle aggro, this fails.
- **Fixing.** Two rainbow fixers at common (Xenobotanist, Class-F Shuttlecraft). Common duals replace a
  basic in ~50% of packs (e.g. Andoria). Basic landcyclers again. All 10 shocklands at rare, so a
  couple per table. The rares are not especially worth splashing; expect to splash removal and synergy
  pieces, as in FRA. *"I am definitely on the lookout for really pushing my mana in this format."*
- **Colourless utility lands (uncommon anomaly cycle).** They cost you colour, especially next to WB
  two-drops like Locutus, but the fixing makes them more affordable than usual. They also count for
  the differently-named-lands mechanic (Strange New Worlds (?)). His hierarchy: **black and green
  clearly best; blue is overhyped.**
- **Overall vibe.** He disliked the previews ("a lot of dudes sitting in chairs") and expects gameplay
  similar to Marvel (MSH), but thinks TRK is a much more traditionally designed set with good colour
  balance at first read. *"Not as good as Reality Fracture, but this format will be fine."*

## Card tips

All pre-data predictions. Hedges are his.

- **Class-F Shuttlecraft** — colourless common manalith spacecraft; station 6 for a flying vigilant
  2/4 (?). *"I would not be surprised if the manalith in the set was one of the top commons."* Watch
  for one-mana removal to follow it on turn 3.
- **Pattern Buffer Preservation** — black common, 2 mana: look at two, one to hand, one to graveyard,
  beam me up for 2. Slots into your curve and can be beamed up late without much tempo loss. His
  favourite of the beam-me-up value spells.
- **Open Communications** — {U} draw a card, beam me up for 3. A flexible value beam-me-up card.
- **There's Coffee in that Nebula** — 2-mana sorcery, look at five for a land or nonland permanent MV
  ≤3, beam me up for 3. Likes it.
- **Fugitive Listener** — 3-mana 3/1, ETB each opponent discards. His preferred beam-me-up target;
  obvious synergy with Pattern Buffer Preservation.
- **Federation Probe** — blue common 1/1 flying artifact, ETB +1/+1 or surveil 1. A cheap
  beam-me-up re-trigger.
- **Spock, Always Your Friend** — U/R signpost 2/2, look at four for a noncreature spell. Good to beam
  up.
- **Emergency Medical Hologram** — {W} 0/1 artifact, ETB counter + gain 1 (Sunshower Druid
  comparison). *"I'm not into this card"* — too small ball. Only in a low-to-the-ground artifact aggro
  deck.
- **Boarding Action** — red common, 2-mana sorcery: 2/1 haste, beam me up for 3. Tension between
  aggressive start and re-enabling it, but *"I have a suspicion that this card is going to play better
  than it looks."*
- **Seven of Nine** — U/B 2/4 (?) artifact: draw when you face a dilemma. Value grows with game length.
- **Vash, Freelance Archaeologist** — B/G 3-mana 2/4: upkeep surveil 1, draw when a card leaves your
  graveyard (once per turn). Same long-game note.
- **Locutus, the One Who Speaks** — W/B 2-mana 1/1 artifact anthem for artifact creatures, discard
  ability to deal 2 and gain 2. The aggro deck's anchor; strictly {W}{B} on turn 2, so it punishes
  colourless lands.
- **Relentless Drednok** — black common one-drop 1/1 lifelink artifact, recursion from graveyard.
  One of the aggro one-drops.
- **Command Subadjunct** — white uncommon one-drop 2/1, artifact creatures have vigilance. "Really
  good for stationing, really good for fliers."
- **Starfleet Security** — {R} 1/1 haste, dies on your turn → Clue. Harried Spearguard comparison. Gets
  in more damage than it should because opponents must respect bigger attackers. "Good is to be seen",
  but he is optimistic.
- **Transporter Malfunction** — 2-mana sorcery, 3 to any target, beam me up for {R}{R}{R}. Eight
  mana for six is a bad rate, but once it's in the yard the opponent plays as if 3 life lower.
- **Xenobotanist** — green common 1/3 mana dork for any colour. Similar cards underperformed (in
  Bloomburrow, he thinks), but he is more optimistic given modern set design.
- **Andoria** — example common dual (U/R planet, enters tapped, gain 1).
- **Free Borg Revolutionaries** — white artifact landcycler, ETB team +1/+1. "Kind of good" in white
  aggro, especially artifacts.
- **Artificial Anomaly** — black colourless-land uncommon: 5, tap, sacrifice: drain 2. Looks bad on
  paper; compares to Ramunap Ruins. Worth stretching your mana for **in aggressive, near-mono decks
  only**, for the last 2 points of reach. Not for midrange or the differently-named-lands deck.
- **Temporal Anomaly** (?) — the green anomaly land (3+G, tap, sacrifice: mill four, take a permanent).
  With the black one, "clearly the best". He did not name it; the reading is from "crack the temporal
  anomaly, get one card".
- **Gaseous Anomaly** — the blue anomaly land (5+U: draw two, make a planet). Hyped, but *"spending
  your entire turn, seven mana, just to draw two cards is not really a good use of your mana in
  2026."* One of the weaker ones.
- **Suurok-Class Science Vessel** — blue common spacecraft, 3 mana ETB draw, station 5 for a 3/2
  flier. See open question 1. Leans this over Make It So, "not super strongly".
- **Make It So** — 4-mana sorcery: untap a permanent and draw two, or 1/1 Officer and draw two. Most
  decks will be happy with it.

## Pitfalls

- Tapping out to station a spacecraft right into removal, leaving your board tapped for a big swing
  back.
- Running too many spacecraft, or stationing with 1-power creatures.
- Building hard around beam me up, or casting the timing-sensitive beam-me-up cards at the wrong turn.
- Jamming colourless anomaly lands into a deck with double-pipped two-drops.
- Playing the long-game value signposts in a deck that doesn't go long.

## Open questions he set himself

1. **Suurok-Class Science Vessel vs Make It So.** Body plus fewer cards, or raw card count? Which wins
   will be "very telling" about pace of play and average game length. He'll watch the data "like a
   hawk".
2. Is aggro really back, or can the format's interaction handle it?
3. Is the fixing too much?
4. Does Boarding Action play better than it looks?
