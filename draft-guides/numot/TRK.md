## ⚠ Recency rule (read first)

- **This is all Early Access Sealed: four pools, about 25 games.** It's real gameplay, but a tiny sample, played before anyone knew the format ("day negative one of the format"). It shows how cards *played* in his games, not how good they are.
- **Sealed ≠ draft.** Sealed decks are slower, splash more and lean on rares. Don't carry a Sealed observation over to draft pick orders without checking 17Lands.
- **He is building for reps, not to win:** "I am NOT interested in building the perfect deck during this event, I'm more interested in just getting reps in with the cards." The fourth deck was explicitly four-colour "for funsies".
- **Newest video wins on conflict. 17Lands GIH WR supersedes all of it** once TRK has draft data.

## Format speed / meta read

- **Power level feels low.** After two pools: "Does feel like the power level has drastically dropped from previous sets." And: "I still haven't felt like it's been, 'Oh my gosh, my opponent played this insane card and I lost.' No, it's just like incremental advantages." By pool 4 he was seeing more bombs (Georgiou, Terran Emperor; U.S.S. Enterprise-D; Captain James T. Kirk), so treat this as an early read.
- **Grindy, value-driven games.** "Gritty grinding nonsense." Looting every turn put him "11 cards deeper" in one game. He also suggests "maybe the format's slow enough" for clunky value cards (Combadge, Sickbay).
- **Dilemma is everywhere.** "You face a dilemma" means you chose a mode for a spell or ability, so any "choose one" card counts, including the modal lands. "There are a lot of dilemmas in this format." The payoffs (Tell Me What You See, Elim Garak, Captain Benjamin Sisko, Jett Reno, Melkotian Adjudicator) are live in almost any deck.
- **Beam me up:** you cast the card from your graveyard for its beam me up cost, and you must also return a creature you control to your hand. It's good with cards that have enter-the-battlefield effects (Pattern Buffer Preservation, Tell Me What You See).
- **Strange new worlds** (costs X less, where X is the number of differently named lands you control) rewards nonbasics: duals, planets, the Anomaly lands. He was "so jealous of all these strange new worlds cards" when he played against a five-colour deck.
- **Spacecraft seemed scarce at first.** He opened zero in pool 1 and one in pool 2 ("maybe there aren't that many spacecraft in this format"). Pools 3–4 had more (Oberth-Class Starship, Enterprise NX-01, U.S.S. Enterprise-D). Cards that care about spacecraft (Sulu, Rutherford's second ability, Hemmer) get much worse without them.
- **Fixing:** the landcyclers are **basic** landcycling (any basic, not just their own colour). Each colour has one, a big common body: "Sweet sweet sweet land cyclers are back." The common duals enter tapped, gain 1 life and count as planets. **Shocklands show up at rare.** One opponent had three, and pool 4 had 12 rares.
- **The "8 power" mechanic** (whenever you attack while creatures you control have total power 8 or greater) reminded him of ferocious. The creature with the ability doesn't need to attack itself.
- **Joke verdicts swung** from "this is the worst format I've ever seen" (pool 1, game 2) to "you know what maybe this is a decent format" two games later.

## Archetypes

He never looked at the draft archetypes, so this is only what his four Sealed decks were:

- **Esper / W-B artifacts** (pool 1): artifact creatures with Primary Unimatrix Adjunct and Tactical Officer, plus removal and Tell Me What You See. A weak pool: "I'm pretty sure my sealed pool is terrible."
- **U-B splashing W, Borg Cube** (pool 2): 7 Island / 7 Swamp plus 4 landcyclers. Built on looters (Seven of Nine, Cloistered Telepath), dilemma payoffs (Elim Garak, Jett Reno, Subcommander T'Pol) and the Borg Cube splash. His best deck.
- **Rakdos aggro, Klingons** (pool 3): Gowron, Eager for Battle with Klingon sac fodder (Relentless Drednok, Storied Veteran), Savvy Profiteer, Red Alert!, Planetary Bombardment and Gray and Adira. He passed on Boros aggro ("double Kirk deck") because it had "very little actual removal".
- **Four-colour landfall soup** (pool 4): base G/B, splashing red and blue off 3 duals, Unexplored Territory and 3 landcyclers. He ran about 7 removal spells, Syndicate Liquidators, Vash, Hologram Stalker and Mirror Universe Intrusion.
- **Opponents' decks worth noting:** a heavy artifact deck (Antagonist Incorporator + Hive Mind Coprocessor) beat him twice. Five-colour "soup" decks with strange new worlds cards showed up more than once. A U/W-ish deck had Will Riker, Worf and Georgiou, and a lands deck had Daniels plus land-count payoffs.

## Sealed

**Four Early Access Sealed pools, Bo1.** Records are inferred from his running commentary unless quoted.

- **Pool 1: Esper artifacts, 3-3** (L W W W L L). The rares were weak ("I would not say we opened a single bomb"). He lost G1 to Pah-Wraith. He won G4 by "abusing" Tell Me What You See with Command Decision bouncing it. He lost G5 to Antagonist Incorporator + Hive Mind Coprocessor: "card quality differential". He lost G6 to a five-colour deck despite an Impossible Choice ×2 + Gul Dukat burn line.
- **Pool 2: U-B splashing Borg Cube, about 7-1.** He says "We are now six and one" before the last game, then won it. Seven of Nine and Data on turn 2 carried the games. The Borg Cube closed two of them. He lost one game to Kirk, Commanding Officer plus two Plasma Cascades, after a "greedy" line (Elim Garak before killing Kirk).
- **Pool 3: Rakdos aggro, about 4-2 (inferred).** Gowron won the games where it survived. He lost one to Pah-Wraith ("the biggest bomb so far I think I've seen in the format… kind of just like a Craterhoof, minus the trample") and one to flood.
- **Pool 4: four-colour soup, 3-3** ("two and one", then "three and two", then a loss). He lost two games to a very strong pool with Georgiou, Terran Emperor, and a final one to a lands/mill deck. He punted one win by forgetting his flyer had vigilance: "I've definitely missed at least nine damage… Reading the card explains the card."

**Sealed build lessons (his):**
1. **Count the landcyclers before choosing a land count.** With two landcyclers he ran 16 lands in pool 1. With four he ran 7/7 basics plus duals in pool 2 ("I could probably even go lower").
2. **Play the bomb colour pair even if you liked a different pair before.** "I went black white in my last pool but this Borg Cube is just too good, right?"
3. **Prefer the deck with removal over a clean aggro curve.** He rejected a 15-creature Boros deck: "That's a good looking curve though… I have very little actual removal."
4. **With a card that counts differently named lands (Seven of Nine), consider running off-colour nonbasics.** He considered it and decided his deck couldn't meet the threshold anyway.
5. **Cheap looters are worth the slot as discard fodder.** "The real reason we're running these Cloistered Telepaths is that they're nice to discard to Seven."

**Play lessons (his):**
- **Kill a mana dork on turn 2–3.** About an opponent who let his Savvy Profiteer live: "Killing a mana dork on turn two or three seems very solid."
- **Respond while the trigger is on the stack.** "With the surveil trigger on the stack, I could have killed it and then they wouldn't have been able to do this. It was a small punt."
- **Read your own permanents' keywords.** He missed vigilance on his own flyer for three turns.
- **Order removal so you don't feed the opponent.** Killing the enchantment first in the wrong order "might have given them an extra 1/1 of value for free."

## Card interactions & combos

- **Tell Me What You See + dilemma cards:** it returns to your hand whenever you face a dilemma, and it re-buys enter-the-battlefield value. Federation Outpost (a modal land) triggers it too. "Tell Me What You See is actually great in my deck, I have so many dilemma cards."
- **Command Decision on your own Tell Me What You See:** bounce it to replay it.
- **Impossible Choice + Gul Dukat, Colonial Prefect:** each player loses 2 from Impossible Choice, and Gul Dukat makes each opponent lose that much again at end step. "Losing four is actually losing eight here."
- **Deanna Troi, Psychic Counsel + token makers:** the white Anomaly land's two 1/1s come in as 2/2s.
- **Antagonist Incorporator + Hive Mind Coprocessor:** artifact creatures make Borg tokens and draw cards on combat damage. "Well that's terrifying for me."
- **Borg Cube + artifact creatures:** affinity for artifact creatures makes it cheaper, and losing an artifact creature makes it cost more again (his Data dying raised the cost). It assimilates on entering and on attacking, and gives your artifact creatures flying once its station reaches 12.
- **Seven of Nine, Unassimilated + Cloistered Telepath / landcyclers:** loot away the extra flash looters and landcyclers.
- **Make It So + Jett Reno:** untap Jett Reno to get another surveil/draw.
- **Gowron, Eager for Battle + any death:** it levels up whenever *any* creature dies (once each turn). Sacrificing your own creatures gives trample. Sacrificing a Klingon also draws a card. General Chang, Cold Warrior is "really good with Gowron synergy".
- **Gray and Adira, Forever Joined + Red Alert! / any noncreature spell:** copies Gowron (not legendary, gains haste). Cast Red Alert! before attacks to trigger it.
- **Beckett Mariner's first strike** resolves first, so it levels up Gowron before regular combat damage.
- **Vash, Freelance Archaeologist + Hologram Stalker + Mirror Universe Intrusion:** cards leaving the graveyard draw and make 3/3s. "Combo card leaving a graveyard, sick."
- **Pattern Buffer Preservation + beam me up:** "that kind of card is really good with beam me up."
- **Sickbay:** after it's on the battlefield, every creature you play gets counters. "Citadel Siege type thing… colorless."
- **Captain Benjamin Sisko:** makes a 1/1 for each dilemma you faced that turn, including the dual/modal lands.
- **Wesley Crusher (opponent's):** a modal ETB that can counter a noncreature spell, bounce a cheap creature, or draw-and-discard. "It's a lot of options."

## Pitfalls

- **Spacecraft payoffs with no spacecraft.** Sulu, Rutherford's second ability and Hemmer's protection are blank if your pool opened none. Check your count before playing them.
- **Ramp with nothing to sink the mana into.** Stable Wormhole is "not going to be a good card" without activated abilities to pay for.
- **Strange new worlds / differently-named-lands cards in a basics-heavy deck.** Seven of Nine's 7/9 mode needs five or seven distinct lands, and "my deck is not good at meeting the conditions for this."
- **Expensive top-ends that do little:** V'Ger at 7, Hoshi Sato's "look at a bunch, take one", and Tea, Earl Grey, Hot.
- **Misreading cards:** read the whole card. He misjudged 70,000 Light-Years (it sends the card to the opponent's hand, not a Path-style ramp) and Gowron (trample works on any sacrifice, not only Klingons) live, and missed vigilance on his own flyer.
- **Giving the opponent tokens for free:** Impossible Choice's destroy mode and removal order. Make sure the 1/1s you hand over don't matter.
- **Low power ≠ no bombs.** Pah-Wraith, Georgiou, Borg Cube, Gowron and Enterprise-D all decided games. Hold removal for them when you can.
