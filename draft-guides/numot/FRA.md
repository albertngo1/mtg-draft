## Format speed / meta read

- **Colour ranking (single colours, from the read-through):** **white first**, black second, green third, red fourth, **blue last**. On white: "every single card was like, yeah, I could see myself playing that at some point"; the worst white card (Ghalta the Immovable) is still "fine." Blue "looks horrendous in comparison," with several outright unplayables. **His own Sealed pool contradicted this:** "I need to play blue. I think I have too many good blue cards."
- **Empower Jace:** "most of the effects that have empowered Jace are going to be solid," but Empower 2 on its own is less than a card. He isn't sure yet whether attacking the opponent's Jace is worth it.
- **The Ways are good** ("all of the Ways of the Jace have seemed good"). The exception is the 5-mana Way of the Mind Sculptor. Because the enchantment stays on the battlefield, its ability comes back on the next Jace token after the first one dies.
- **Basic landcycling:** "if it's a basic land cycling card and it has any kind of relevant text, then it's probably good." His order: white (Hexhaven Battalion) far ahead, then black (Apex Witchstalker), green (Vinelasher Adept), red (Awaken the Inferno), and blue (Undulating Witness) last.
- **Themes:** lots of lifegain synergy, "so many ways to add +1/+1 counters", and "a million legendaries" (Ways count as legendary targets).
- **Duals:** the common Annex/Commons cycle enters tapped unless you control a planeswalker. The rare slow lands want to be played on turn 3 or later.
- **Hosers** are sideboard cards in Bo1, unless your deck has enough loot/rummage to cycle them away.
- **After the Sealed run:** joke verdicts swung from "worst format I've ever played" to "the best format ever" and back to "kind of horrendous." He likes that soup is possible: "when you can do soup in a format, usually that means I'm going to like it."

## Sealed

**One Early Access Sealed pool, played Bo1, finishing 3-3** (L W W W L L; inferred, not stated).

**Pool and build.** He opened six rares ("one of them unplayable"). The best was **Sphinx of False Conclusions** ("insane"). The final deck was **Grixis: U/B splashing Ajani's Anguish** on 3 red sources, with 9 blue and 9 black sources. Why:
- **Blue was the deepest colour in the pool:** Mindseeker Oculus, Theorist's Proxy, Semester Foreseer (?), Ruric Thar, Biomagus, and Unsummon (cut as "probably unnecessary").
- **Black brought the cheap interaction:** Mabel, Bitter Recluse, Last Gasp, Rank Rat.
- **He dropped green despite good cards,** because "I can't splash double green." Once the green landcyclers (Vinelasher Adept (?)) were out, Hapatra lost her fuel too: "if I'm not running the adepts, then Hapatra is a lot worse."
- **He forgot the red landcycler** (Awaken the Inferno (?)) and regretted it twice: "that's worth playing."

**Games.**
- **G1 (L).** A red-green deck with a menace threat and strong topdecks beat him ("the menace is so brutal here"). He also played his Jace-land before making a Jace.
- **G2 (W).** Mabel killed a Jace token, and Ruric Thar took over ("big daddy time").
- **G3 (W).** He killed the opponent's Chandra, then Ruric Thar won.
- **G4 (W).** Theorist's Proxy's 0/3 stabilised the board. Ruric Thar closed it; targeting it just gave him cards.
- **G5 (L).** Against Javier (Arena Championship 13): Heartwood tokens, Garruk making 4/4s that draw, and an unblockable + surveil engine (Kiora? (?)). Ajani's Anguish arrived but he had no red source. Self-diagnosed "1 million punts."
- **G6 (L).** The same opponent, it seems. He had them at 1 life and lost to a cheap removal spell.

**Sealed lessons (his, from the games):**
1. **Make the Jace before playing a Jace-land.** "I should have played Oculus and then played this." The land then enters untapped.
2. **Cast the creature first, then decide what to do with Jace.** "I should have played the creature and then decided to Jace or not."
3. **Jace tokens are targets both ways.** Mabel's counter removal kills one. Running a creature into Violent Echoes (?) hands the opponent a Jace from the excess damage ("I give them a Jace 5 potentially").
4. **Don't force a colour for double-pip cards you can't splash.** Take the deep colour.
5. **Play every landcycler.** They fix and they shuffle. Without a shuffle, a key card surveiled to the bottom is gone.
6. **A 3-source splash is fine for a late card but can strand it.** "Where are my red sources?"
7. **Keep removal up for early threats.** He regretted not having Last Gasp for G5's first threat, and lost G6 to one cheap removal spell.

**What played well:** Ruric Thar, Biomagus (won two games), Sphinx of False Conclusions (recurs), Theorist's Proxy ("the 0/3 is surprisingly good"), Mabel, Bitter Recluse. **Opposing cards that beat him:** Garruk, Curse Breaker, the unblockable-plus-surveil engine, and a menace attacker.

### Arena Direct Sealed, week two (2026-10-07 compilation, `JDZ3n8Uk-Jw`)

**Bo1 Sealed for an FRA Collector Booster box: 6,000 gems, 7 wins or 2 losses.** Kenji's running
tally across the event: run 21 started with "20 done, boxed in three". Later it was "26 events, four
boxes", about 10 scrub-outs, and no 6-win runs. He put his game win rate at about 65% and called that
low-rolling. This video shows four pools, and every one ended early (two 0-2 runs, plus runs of
about 1-2 and 2-2). Several losses came from flood ("10 lands in 15 cards") or from opponents' bombs.
He holds that the builds were right: **"Best of one sealed is already pretty crazy variance."**

**Pool texture:** pools have 6–9 rares (some are lands or unplayables). Of his pools, **landcyclers
were scarce**: often only one in his colours.

**Build lessons (his):**
1. **Base two colours with the removal, splash the best removal.** Pool 1: R/G main, splashing
   Extended Absence off one Swamp + one B/G dual + Loot + one landcycler ("fourish" black sources).
   Pool 2: Jund. "The power and the removal in Jund is too good" even though it meant leaving behind
   a Jace and other good blue cards. **Don't greed-splash blue just because you opened the Jace** if the
   deck doesn't need it.
2. **Murmuring Volume is good in pairs.** Two copies fix Jund and help against flood.
3. **A removal pile with few creatures can win.** Pool 3 was W/B (Esper fixing available) with about
   seven removal spells (Memory Trap, Break Under Pressure, Multiply by Zero, Extended Absence, Last
   Gasp …) and about 12 creatures, splashing only Way of the Mind Sculptor. "You don't need crazy
   bombs to win."
4. **16 lands is viable** in a low-curve G/W lifegain/counters pool (Titanbones, Edgar, two Wrecking
   Gecko, Kindred Judgment at the top).
5. **Five-colour soup is good if the pool supports it.** He lost to a five-colour deck with double
   Murmuring Volume. His own pool lacked the fixing.

**Play notes:**
- **Ignore a 1-loyalty Jace and go face** when your deck has plenty of its own empower. "One loyalty
  Jace does not concern me." This conflicts with the Lords of Limited default of always attacking
  Jace. The difference is context: his deck had its own engine.
- **Play out your lands when you hold Way of the Mind Sculptor**, so you can cast it plus another
  spell. He missed this once.
- **Cycle a landcycler early even if it's one of your best cards** (Hexhaven Battalion) when you
  need the land.
- Kill a Jace with removal that would otherwise be weak against tokens (Break Under Pressure).

**Card verdicts from these runs:**
- **Kindred Judgment** — "fantastic", much better than Face Yourself at seven mana. It also beat him:
  Kindred Judgment into Sphinx of False Conclusions.
- **Tether Technician** — chat called it mid-to-bad. He disagrees strongly: "there is no world where
  technician is mid to bad."
- **Edgar, Moonlit Sovereign** (?) — "Edgar's real good right now" in the G/W pool. Which Edgar is
  not certain; the green one fits that pool.
- **Gardenize** — "never worth playing unless you are literally mono green, and even then it's
  frequently not going to do anything."
- **Medic's Kitesail** — he considered it over Hunter's Axe: "Flying is just so good."
- **Lich's Relic** — an opponent beat him with Lich's Relic plus two Garruks (which Garruk is unclear
  from captions). "Maybe
  that's not beatable, period."
- **Big-picture verdict:** "lots of interesting decisions and deckbuilding", "but the power level of
  some of the bombs is certainly painful".

## Card interactions & combos
- **Campus Crier:** the graveyard exile is not sorcery speed. Block on the opponent's turn, then empower Jace at end of turn.
- **Enlightened Confidant:** gain any life (even 1) and surveil a land into the graveyard, and it is "just a free draw… Lands are always free if you've gained life."
- **Repurposed Enforcer:** turn-2 play, then an interaction or pump spell and attacks give "a Jace token with like six loyalty". The extra cards drawn keep the attacks going.
- **Return to the Light Realms + Hexhaven Battalion:** the white cycler is a sorcery, so you can't cycle three and reanimate them with Return.
- **Loyal Tutor + echo-pair planeswalkers:** he assumed you always open both halves. Chat corrected him ("you don't always get them").
- **Gideon's Memorial:** its discard mode (Gideon's Reproach) works with cadet tokens.
- **Thalia, the Survivor + +1/+1 counters:** turns into a huge lifelinker "that triggers all of your … lifelinks".
- **Yuriko, Blade of the Mighty:** attack the player with one creature (it gets double strike) and their planeswalker with the others.
- **Way of the Healer:** even if they kill the first Jace, the enchantment lets a later Jace keep making cadets.
- **Jace's Machinations:** cast at the opponent's end step, −3 to draw, then activate again on your own turn.
- **Sanctum Lurker:** turn 3, the +2 gives a Jace at 3 that drains 1 each turn. "This thing is insane."
- **Way of the Deathbringer:** a sacrifice outlet for ETB creatures, which you then turn into 4/4s.
- **Winter, Tormented Loner:** can sacrifice itself on turn 3 to make the opponent sacrifice.
- **Mabel, Bitter Recluse:** the ETB removes counters, so it can kill a Jace token.
- **Teyo, Diamondblade Mage:** give deathtouch to another blocker and trade up.
- **Chandra's Emberling + Artifist Acumen:** the spells deck.
- **Command the Stage:** better with more cadet makers and noncombat pings, which recur it.
- **Craterclaw Colossus + Heartwood tokens:** "not hard to just insta win". Draconic Visitor and Pia, Determined Rebuilder also scale with Heartwood tokens.
- **Hungering Puppetbeast:** its own Heartwood token protects it the moment it resolves.
- **Face Yourself:** if you control a Jace token, you keep the copied creatures.
- **Titanbones + Greenhouse Propagator:** Propagator on turn 3, Titanbones on turn 4, and Titanbones is a 6/5 immediately.
- **Way of the Pyromancer:** turn 2, then the Jace uptick for R means 4 mana on turn 3.
- **Pia, Aether Ascetic:** fetches Way enchantments. It swings from "unplayable" to "very very good" depending on your enchantment count.
- **Ferocity of the Hunt:** put it on a small creature to trade up and bring it back, or on a big one.
- **Hapatra, the Desert Fang:** X is the greatest mana value in your graveyard, so the land cyclers make it "kill something a lot of times".
- **Recursive Recruitment:** the counters apply only when cast from the graveyard. He misread it at first, then corrected himself: 4 mana for two 2/2s, and later 8 mana for "two five fives".
- **Tetsuko Umezawa, Pursuer:** punishes blocks by creatures with power or toughness 1 or less.
- **Eye of Jace:** fits the WU surveil decks.
- **Codie, Ravenous Codex:** "you have to build a prepared deck".
- **Emrakul, the Exigent Doom:** he will put it in Heartwood decks "and it's going to rot in my hand".

## Pitfalls


- **Loyal Tutor** assumes you have both echo-pair walkers, and you don't always get them. "Probably don't want to play this card."
- **Expensive sorceries** (Return to the Light Realms at 9, Kindred Judgment at 7, Face Yourself at 7) often won't just win on the spot. "nine mana is like turn 15 in limited."
- **Empower Jace 2 alone is less than a card.** Surveil/draw off a 2-loyalty token doesn't net a full card.
- **The 5-mana Way (Way of the Mind Sculptor)** is the one expensive exception in an otherwise strong cycle.
- **Build-arounds with no support:** Germinate Recruits needs real lifegain, Tomik needs many noncombat damage sources, Codie needs prepared cards, Pia, Aether Ascetic needs enchantments, and Kiora of Salt and Sand needs 8 loyalty.
- **"Cute" traps:** Sphinx's Approach's Sphinx clause will never trigger, and Identity Echo is "bad, but neat". Other traps: Flickering Hound, Puppet Crafting, Gardenize, Omnipresence, Entrust the Spark, Tam's Wooberg ability, Karn, Gilded Guardian, and both Yargles.
- **Blue's weak baseline:** Cruel Calculations is unplayable, Undulating Witness is the worst cycler, and Geist of Saint Thalia is not worth it.
- **Hosers** (Refute Destiny, Terminal Criticism, Essence Burn, Flourishing Grapple, Precise Redaction) belong in the sideboard unless you have lots of rummage/loot.
- **Common duals** enter tapped without a planeswalker. **Rare slow lands** want to be played on turn 3 or later.
- **Emrakul in Heartwood decks** "is going to rot in my hand."
