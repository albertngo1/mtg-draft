# HOB — The Hobbit (NumotTheNummy)

## ⚠ Recency rule (read first)

**Three Premier Draft VODs from the format's first week (2026-08-14 → 2026-08-16).** Kenji is
explicit that he is still learning: *"this is day two of the format being online, so nobody, not
even me, knows what's going on really."* On the first VOD he reports going **18-18 on his birthday
stream** and calls out *"a lot of punts with the new cards."* Weight these as **live gameplay
evidence, not settled evaluation** — the newest VOD wins on conflict, and 17Lands GIH WR supersedes
everything here.

This channel's signal is **what actually happened on board**, not grades. Where Kenji's stated
opinion conflicts with a card's real 17Lands number, that is worth noticing (several such splits are
flagged inline below) but the number wins.

## Supersessions

Only three days separate the first VOD from the last, so there are no hard reversals — but two
positions moved, and one stayed stubbornly against the data.

- **Gollum, Silent Slinker** — VOD 1 (Aug 14): *"I've had some really awkward experiences with this
  card just because I'm generally the defensive player, and so a [4/3 menace] that can't block is
  very awkward."* Later the same draft he takes it anyway, reasoning *"Gollum's really nice for the
  four-power-matters cards."* VOD 3 (Aug 16): *"I just don't like this Gollum. It has underperformed
  for me consistently."* **He never came around.** This is a genuine conflict with the data — Gollum
  sits near the top of black's commons on 17Lands and Limited Level-Ups calls it one of the most
  underrated cards in the set. The likely explanation is the one Kenji names himself: he drafts from
  a defensive posture, and this is a racing card. **Weight the number over the take.**
- **Troll Negotiations** — VOD 1: *"good removal, you need to have a creature, but if you do you're
  probably going to be able to kill one of their things."* VOD 1 later, on a second copy: *"this is
  not really the removal I was hoping for cuz it's four mana, sorcery speed, and you already have to
  have a creature."* Settled position: **playable filler, not the removal you want.** He wants
  Quarrel (the one-mana green instant punch) instead.
- **Troop of Ponies** — read as an auto-include on sight, then walked back after playing it: *"every
  time I've played the troop of ponies so far, it has felt too slow. On the surface of things this
  feels like a card that should be really good and it just slots into any deck... it just feels so
  slow."*

## Format speed / meta read

- **Mono-black is a real deck, and it broke his slump.** (Aug 17.) Seventeen drafts into the format
  Kenji was at *one trophy and a 54% win rate* — *"really low for me"* — and deliberately changed his
  approach: take the two-drop over the near-bomb, stay in black, and let the deck become
  **mono-black**. Result: **7 wins**. The build was four Crude Bent Blades, three Goblin Plate Mails,
  two Gnashing of Teeth, ten-plus two-drops, 16 lands. *"If I do not have a two-drop on turn two,
  something has gone wrong."* His own summary: *"What if I just started forcing mono-black every
  draft?"* This lines up with 17Lands, where Mono-Black posts the highest win rate on the board.
- **The play/draw gap is the format's headline stat.** *"This is the fastest format... whoever is on
  the play has the greatest advantage of any format besides cube."* Practical consequence he states:
  **play first, curve out, don't punt.**
- **He does not like the format.** Three small sets in a row, and this is the one he rates lowest —
  five supported archetypes, high repetition, and a lot of early losses. Read his card takes through
  that; the mono-black VOD is where he sounds like himself again.
- **Red-black curve-outs are the thing that beats you.** Kenji's single strongest first-week read,
  and he traces it back to sealed: *"we noticed this on the very first day of sealed, but red-black
  curve out is just very, very good."* His 18-18 birthday stream was, by his own account, mostly
  *"getting completely owned by red-black curve outs."*
- **Black is the best color, and that alone justifies pairing it with anything.** *"I'm going to
  really try to lock into green-black and playing this, which is convenient since black is basically
  the best color anyways."* In the third VOD he defaults to *"just continue to take good black
  cards as we see them"* as a way of staying open for six picks.
- **Low curve is a requirement, not a preference.** *"Something you really need to do in this format
  is have a low curve."* He repeatedly declines six-drops he likes on rate.
- **Unsupported pairs are real and he drafts them.** VOD 2 ends in **red-green fatties** —
  *"an unsupported color pair, you love to see it"* — and VOD 3 in **blue-black control splashing
  Thranduil's Company**. Both went fine. In VOD 2 his opponent was **four-color no black**:
  *"they are uber souping."* The five-archetype framing is not a cage.
- **Removal gets spent immediately.** Nothing in these VODs is held for value; he trades burn for
  two-drops on curve without hesitation, because the alternative is dying to a curve-out.

## Archetypes

Kenji drafted three decks across three VODs, and **none of them was one of the five signposted
pairs** — which is itself the takeaway.

- **B/G wolves ("four power matters")** — VOD 1. His stated target. The build wants
  **Chief Warg's Company** as the top end and cheap four-power enablers underneath it; Duskwatch
  Hunter is the key card because it *"can not only be a four-power creature on turn three, but if
  you play the two-drop, you can also just pump that up instead."* Green's removal is a weakness
  he names outright: *"what is green's removal? It's all punch and fight effects, and then like the
  random kill-a-flyer card."*
- **R/G fatties (unsupported)** — VOD 2. Fell into it after first-picking Beorn the Fierce and
  finding red open. Works because the curve stays low and Pinecone Strike is deep — he ended with
  **four copies** (*"man, infinite Pinecone Strikes. Is that bad or good? Don't think it's bad."*).
  Weakness: no way to answer a 5/5 flier.
- **U/B control splashing G (unsupported)** — VOD 3. Assembled by taking black commons for six
  picks with no plan, then splashing **Thranduil's Company** off Elven Passage + Old Thrush + a
  green-black dual. His verdict on the shell: *"kind of like a weird blue-black control deck
  splashing for Thranduil's Company."* It won.
- **Mono-black (Aug 17)** — the deck he broke his slump with. Enabled by black being *"very open"*
  at the table and by black's own equipment: with four Crude Bent Blades even **Old Thrush** becomes
  a three-power flier, so the deck needs no second colour for reach. Sixteen lands, ten-plus
  two-drops, no colour screw. *"An actual mono-color deck in The Hobbit."*
- **Blue-black control** — his only trophy before the mono-black run, and the archetype he keeps
  returning to: *"I've been doing incredibly well, better than my other color pairings, with
  blue-black control, just with all of these draw-multiple-card value cards."* Unsupported, and he
  drafts it anyway.
- **Blue-green elves** — never drafted, and late-wheeling Bilbos in VOD 1 make him say *"I'm
  guessing blue-green was the place I was supposed to be"* — meaning it was **open because nobody
  wanted it**, not because it was good.

## Card interactions & combos

- **Gathering of Darkness + discard outlets** — recruit/loot a fat creature away early, buy it back
  later. In VOD 3 he discards Gollum to a recruit trigger specifically because Gathering of Darkness
  is already in the deck.
- **Hobbit Hole → Gollum, Silent Slinker** — the halfling cycling finds Gollum, so a flooding hand
  converts into a threat. (Both LLU and Kenji hit this line; it is the main reason to run Hobbit
  Hole in a black deck.)
- **Giant's Boulder + artifact sacrifice** — *"Boulder is okay because of all the ways to sacrifice
  artifacts, and then it turns into a late-game removal spell."* Best in black/red, which have the
  sac outlets.
- **Rhovanion Rampager + a creature about to die** — sac the creature that is already trading, put
  its power in counters on the Rampager, and get the amass back when the Rampager dies. He does this
  mid-combat in VOD 3: *"we can sack the Rampager, get our value immediately."*
- **Mirkwood Nurturer + enters-the-battlefield permanents** — *"the nurturer is good with like the
  equipments that have enters effects."* He notes it is better in red/black (good ETBs, good sagas)
  than in the blue-green shell it's printed for.
- **Bothersome Noisemaker + a low creature count** — the amass trigger means a red-black deck can
  run 10–12 real creatures because equipment and amass sorceries fill the gap.

## Card tips

### Black

- **Crude Bent Blade** — *"this one's been a great performer."* His framing of the fail case is the
  same as LLU's: *"sure, you get to kill a creature of your opponent's choosing, but plus two plus
  one is also no laughing matter later on."* Takes it over most commons.
- **Gollum, Silent Slinker** — the channel's one sustained anti-consensus take. *"It has
  underperformed for me consistently."* He still takes it for the four-power-matters synergy. See
  Supersessions — the data disagrees with him.
- **Rage into the Valley** — *"probably better"* than Gollum, and he compares it to a known
  quantity: *"it's a Phyrexian Rager in essence."*
- **Down, Down to Goblin-town** — *"fantastic saga,"* and later *"one of my favorite sagas from the
  format."* Takes it very highly.
- **Gathering of Darkness** — a 3/3 gravedigger; *"seems fine."* Gets better once the deck has
  premium cheap creatures worth rebuying.
- **Snowslope Hunter** — *"amazing. Tons of equipment in the format that you can sacrifice, since
  you can sacrifice a creature *or* artifact with this. Also just good value with all the army
  cards."* Note this is a **more positive read than LLU's**, which calls it off-plan for red-black.
- **The Sackville-Bagginses** — *"that's just a good two-mana card"* (sac a creature or artifact on
  entry to draw + make a treasure).
- **Inside Information** — never seen it before opening it: *"whoa... this format has been really
  fast, so there are going to be times where you don't get to do this, but seems good."* The 17Lands
  numbers side against him — this is one of the format's marked traps.
- **Stir Up Trouble** — taken as removal when the pack is thin; wants sacrifice fodder to be live.

- **Gnashing of Teeth** — *"has been fantastic."* Five toughness is *"a pretty magical number"* in
  this format, and it is the answer to the white/blue recruit boards that blank point removal. Two
  copies made his mono-black deck.
- **Old Thrush** — normally unplayable, but with three or four Crude Bent Blades it is a two-mana
  flier that equips into a 3/3 in the air. The card that let mono-black skip a second colour for
  reach.
- **Ravenous Rats (Stony-Voiced Goblins)** — fine in an equipment-heavy black deck purely as another
  body to carry a blade.
### Red

- **Bothersome Noisemaker** — *"an incredible two-drop... you play this on turn two, and if they
  don't kill it and you have just any number of non-creature spells to cast, it's really good."*
  Called *"crazy good"* in a second VOD. One of his few unqualified raves.
- **Pinecone Strike** — the removal he actively wants. Took **four** in one draft without regret.
  Preferred over Quarrel in a straight comparison.
- **Burn, Burn, Tree and Fern** — *"burn tree and fern's been really good for me."*
- **Smaug, the Great Calamity** — *"is like okay."* Passable removal-plus-threat, never a high pick
  for him.
- **Goblin-town Flunkies** — *"all right"* — takeable, not wanted. (Matches LLU's post-play
  downgrade of the card.)
- **Smaug's Fury** — +3/+0, reach, first strike for two mana; a fine trick in a green deck.
- **Glóin the Mighty** — *"just okay."*
- **Tidings of War** — repeatedly passed in decks that couldn't use the flashback: *"another Tidings
  of War that we're not going to play."*

### Green

- **Desolation Prowler** — *"insane. It's two mana 4/4. Like, this is the two-drop that you want in
  this deck."* Takes it over rares. His highest-conviction uncommon.
  Aug 17: *"every time I have a Desolation Prowler, I feel like I cannot lose"* — taken over the
  near-bomb Tom, Bert and William on purpose.
- **Bejeweled Warg** — *"if you have this on turn two, it can sometimes just run away with the
  game."* But when compared directly: *"Desolation Prowler, amazing two-drop. Honestly, it's
  probably better than Bejeweled Warg."*
- **Duskwatch Hunter** — the B/G enabler that matters, because it can be the four-power creature
  *or* make one. See Archetypes.
- **Ordinary Bear** — *"ordinary bears are good on curve, that's for sure. And if you just go two-drop,
  three-drop, four-drop, it's a big boy."* Real playable, not filler.
- **Wargling** — *"the Worgling is fine"* / *"for us, Worgling's the pick"* over a Ravenous-Rats
  effect.
- **Attercop** — 2/1 reach deathtouch with landfall; taken as a fine two-drop, valued for deathtouch.
- **Troll Negotiations** — see Supersessions. Playable removal, four mana and conditional.

- **Wood Elves** — passed without comment. (LLU is explicit that a three-mana 1/1 is unplayable in

- **Great Fierce Bee** — *"I have not found the Great Fierce Bee particularly good. I've just never

- **Silvan Reveler** — *"is just so cool. Reminiscent of like Masked Admirers"* — but he passes it

- **Troop of Ponies** — see Supersessions. Looks auto-include, plays too slow.

- **Dáin's Company** — *"not very good, right?"* — his first-VOD read, notably at odds with LLU's

- **Moment of Glory** / **Rally the Rohirrim (?)** — *"it's like fine. The red-white equipment deck

- **Plunder the Trollshaws** — *"Plunder's also great for that one."*

- **The Queen of Dale** — no comment; unremarkable in play.

## Play patterns (HOB-specific)

Kenji's gameplay commentary is the bulk of these VODs, and most of it is about tempo decisions in a
format where the games are short.

- **The three-drop bomb question.** His stated plan with Chief Warg's Company: *"I am slamming the
  company on three and asking the question: hey, can you deal with this? And if the answer happens
  to be no, then I like my chances."* It worked repeatedly — *"I played a three-mana bomb and they
  never killed it and we won."* HOB's must-answer threats are cheap enough that holding them for a
  safe window is the wrong instinct.
- **Curving out is the whole game.** *"Curving out is overpowered."* He wins games on the strength of
  a two-drop into three-drop into four-drop with no bombs involved, and loses to the same from
  red-black. When a keep is marginal, the question is whether it curves, not whether it has action.
- **Equipment reassignment is the black decks' engine, and it's a targeting decision.** *"Now we're
  going to move the blade over to the 2/1, that way none of their 1/1s have a good block."* Where the
  equipment sits each turn changes which blocks are legal for them — treat it as a play, not upkeep.
- **Attack before you equip or activate.** He repeatedly attacks first and moves equipment
  post-combat, so the opponent commits blocks without full information.
- **Play around the counter-spell tax, not the counterspell.** He lost a lethal swing to a
  *"counter unless you pay four"* effect after declining to attack with everything the turn before —
  the error was the cautious turn, not the turn he got countered.
- **Fetches and cycling lands are held, not cracked on curve.** Hobbit Hole cycling to find Gollum,
  and Elven Passage held for a landfall payoff, both came up as real decisions. *"I used both my
  fetches before the company was drawn. It's too bad."*
- **Life is spendable until you're in burn range.** He declines a Desolation Prowler pump with
  *"I don't want to get randomly burnt out"* and is proven right by a Pinecone Strike the same turn.
  The Prowler's two-life activation is genuinely dangerous in a deck already paying life elsewhere:
  *"I have a lot of self-damage in this deck."*
- **Racing math beats blocking math.** *"Do I double block and lose both of my creatures here? Do I
  take eight? I think taking eight's the play."*

## Pitfalls

- **Amassing into open mana is a blowout.** The clearest tactical lesson in the run, and Kenji wins a
  game off it: *"if your opponent has open mana and you amass hoping to get in a big hit, it's almost
  like you're putting an aura on a creature... huge blowout on cards and tempo."* All the amass
  counters sit on one body, so one removal spell eats every investment.
- **Playing the defensive role is a losing default.** He names this about his own play — his dislike
  of Gollum traces to *"I'm generally the defensive player"* — in a format where the good cards are
  racing cards.
- **Don't hold removal for the perfect target.** *"This is a set where it's just like, yep, see a
  thing, kill a thing."* Being strategic with removal loses to curve-outs.
- **Don't first-pick fixing.** He talks himself out of a first-pick dual land twice.
- **Playing too many six-drops.** *"I love this card. I will probably not be playing this card very
  frequently. Just way too much mana."*
- **Playing scared with a winning board.** His one self-diagnosed punt of the run, and worth
  quoting in full: *"I think I punted this game by not attacking with everything. I had an easy win
  if I attack with everything... I got too scared of what they could have. Why wouldn't I just go in
  with 50 points of trample damage? Oh, that was so sloppy of me."* In a racing format the cautious
  line is usually the losing line.
- **Expecting your first-week record to mean anything.** He went **18-18** on release day and did not
  trophy in his **first eight drafts**, while making evaluations that mostly held up. Don't rewrite
  your pick order off a bad first weekend.
- **Splashing without enough sources.** He passes several splashable removal spells with *"I guess I
  would rather not splash if possible"* — splash only when the fixing showed up first.
