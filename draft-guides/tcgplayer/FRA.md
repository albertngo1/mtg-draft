## Format takeaways

**Colour verdicts (the reviewers' own words):**
- **Green: "looks very strong"** (Jůza). It has ramp, synergy pieces, mana sinks, removal and big late-game threats. The catch is "an unusually high amount of cards with a 0.0 rating."
- **White: "seems good"** (Jůza). It has four removal spells, each with a single W pip, so it will be popular in Sealed and easy to splash.
- **Blue: "doing quite well for itself"** (LSV). It combines card advantage with speed.
- **Black: "quite solid"** (LSV). It has "awesome commons", with the top three "a cut above", good removal and a good landcycler, but its creatures run small.
- **Red: mixed** (Jůza). Its removal is efficient, but many cards only work in one or two pairings. He expects the aggressive/prowess cards to underperform in Sealed.

**Mean grade per colour** (computed here, using the best case of any split grade): Red 3.24, Multicolor/Colorless/Lands 3.20, White 3.15, Black 3.06, Blue 3.01, Green 3.00. Green's mean is dragged down by its 0.0s, even though Jůza calls it the strongest colour.

**Top 5 commons per colour** (their lists):
- W: Memory Trap, Surgical Precision, Hexhaven Battalion, Campus Crier, Fateshaper Aspirant. In Sealed, Surgical Precision moves above Crier, and Aspirant and Battalion move up.
- U: Mindseeker Oculus, Infinite Coursework, Icy Reception, Semester Foreseer, Unsummon. Undulating Witness is also in the conversation.
- B: Last Gasp, Extended Absence, Apex Witchstalker, Rank Rat, Silence the Echo
- R: No Admittance, Wrath of the Bloodmane, Heartstring Puller, Skilled Battlecarver, Awaken the Inferno
- G: Compel Brutality, Arcane Amphisbaena, Greenhouse Propagator, Bestial Incursion, Budding Insurgent

**Archetype reads:**
- **Self-mill (UB / Omit Variables) is feast-or-famine.** Every Omit Variables card is `1.0 // 3.0`: great when you commit, unplayable otherwise. Paradox Shaper is "a build-around of the highest order."
- **BR "deal one damage" is real.** Stingerquill Voxmancer (3.5) is the enabler. Vicious Verse cards are filler that fits both BR and UR prowess.
- **RG Heartwood ramp is a strategy.** LSV lost to it with Karn, Gilded Guardian (`1.0 // 3.5`). Woodwork Prodigy (3.0) and Konstrari Improviser (3.0) are the glue. Aerid Konstrari is a 5.0.
- **GW lifegain** has payoffs: Titanbones (4.0 in lifegain decks) and Vigorbloom Vanguard (3.5, "a great reason to go Green-White").
- **Hybrid gold commons are safe early picks.** Fatehold Chronologist and Prudent Fateseer are both 3.5.
- **Colour hosers** (Refute Destiny, Flourishing Grapple, Essence Burn, Precise Redaction, Terminal Criticism) are sideboard cards. They are graded 0.5 mainboard / Bo1 and 3.0–3.5 from the sideboard (LSV writes it as `1.0 // 3.5`).
- **Legendary density:** 95 legendary cards, so "legendary matters" cards (Fateshaper Aspirant, Wrath of the Bloodmane) are live.
- **LSV's pick for best card in the set: Uldaros Theorix** (5.0).


## Card notes

Ratings verbatim from TCGplayer (0–5). `X // Y` = off-plan // on-plan (build-around). `A - B` = range depending on deck. Draft/Sealed and archetype splits kept as written. Reason is the reviewer's own first line, lightly trimmed. Articles list cards alphabetically; order kept.


### White (Martin Jůza, 39 cards)

- **Academic Ascent** — 2.0 — If your deck is in the market for a combat trick, Academic Ascent can fill the role just fine.
- **Ajani Resolute** — 3.5 — Ajani Resolute is a cheap planeswalker that takes a while to actually produce a creature, but it is a free lifegain engine which can be really powerful with cards like Unflinching Hortimancer, Bloombrute or Titanbones, Towering Heart.
- **Blossom-Blessed Angel // Seed Suture** — 2.5 — Solid filler which will be good at protecting your Jace token while attacking theirs. Seed Suture adds a little bit of flexibility and a cheap way to activate your lifegain payoffs.
- **Campus Crier** — 3.0 — This is a very solid curve-filler that leaves some value behind after it dies. Three power means it can trade up on mana if needed.
- **Danitha, Sword of Hope** — 1.5 - 3.5 — Danitha's stats are a bit underwhelming, but it can be a powerful value engine if you can pick up a lot of ways to target creatures you control, like Seed Suture or Predictive Preparations.
- **Enlightened Confidant** — 4.0 — I'm always happy to add a cheap lifelinker to my deck and Enlightened Confidant has strong synergy with the passive lifegain effects in this format.
- **Fateshaper Aspirant** — Draft: 2.0; Sealed: 2.5 — The stats are a bit underwhelming for a five-drop, but there are 95 cards with the legendary subtype in Reality Fracture , which makes Fateshaper Aspirant a reasonable filler card with some flexibility.
- **Flickering Hound** — 1.0 - 3.5 — Flickering Hound can be a powerful value engine, but investing four mana into a 2/2 creature with no immediate effect on the board is a significant loss in momentum.
- **Generous Revival** — 3.0 — Generous Revival can be a nice two-for-one in the right scenario if you are bringing back synergy pieces or against a deck full of removal.
- **Germinate Recruits** — 0.0 — This card does absolutely nothing without a way to gain life in the same turn, which is the lowest possible floor, a total blank.
- **Gideon's Memorial** — 4.0 — The discard ability is a cheap bit of interaction against an attacking or blocking creature and there will be times the main static ability will be relevant.
- **Ghalta the Immovable** — 2.5 — Ghalta is effectively a 7/7, though worth noting is that it works very poorly with any fight spells.
- **Graft Surgeon** — 2.0 — Graft Surgeon seems like a fine curve-filler that has nice synergy with Seed Suture and all the many ways in this format that let you add +1/+1 counters on your creatures like Predictive Preparations or Emergency Phytomedic.
- **Guiding Hydra** — 4.5 — Guiding Hydra being a repeatable way to buff your board with a flexible mana cost seems like a great deal, with an extra upside that even if you don't have any other creatures, you can play it as a big standalone threat.
- **Hexhaven Battalion** — Draft: 3.0; Sealed: 3.5 — Six power and six toughness worth of stats split across three bodies with an extra planeswalker on top can be a strong card in the right deck, or even in the right matchup where your opponent has a lot of one-for-one removal.
- **Kindred Judgment** — 2.0 - 4.0; Draft: 1.5 - 3.0; Sealed: 2.5 - 4.0 — Seven mana is a lot to pay even for a sweeper and Kindred Judgment is going to require some serious setting up because of overlapping creature types between your and your opponent's board.
- **Koth of the Homestead** — 4.0 — Koth is both a lifegain and a value engine combined, which is really strong for a cheap creature.
- **Liliana the Faultless** — 2.5 - 4.0 — Liliana the Faultless is an upgraded iteration of Soul Warden, which isn't typically a good Limited card because the stats are too poor for it to meaningfully matter in combat.
- **Loyal Tutor** — 0.0 - 1.5 — This set features eight planeswalkers and that is an unusually high number, though keep in mind that only one of them is white.
- **Lyra, Archangel of Dawn** — 4.0 — Lyra is a great card no matter how much lifegain or how many Angels you have, because the baseline stats of 3/3 flying for three mana is already well ahead of the curve.
- **Memory Trap** — 3.5 — Creatures keep getting better and better, especially by creating immediate value in some way when they enter the battlefield.
- **Predictive Preparations** — 3.0 — Travel Preparations was one of the best cards in Innistrad Limited. Although the flashback cost on Predictive Preparations has now doubled, and this is a big difference.
- **Prophesied End** — 4.0 — Two mana to destroy a creature is an excellent rate. Typically in Limited, you can only achieve this by answering an attacking creature, but Prophesied End lets you do this outside of combat but with a real drawback.
- **Refute Destiny** — Best of One: 0.5; Best of Three: 3.0 — Refute Destiny is an excellent sideboard card and I even remember some unbalanced formats where a card like Deathmark was a reasonable mainboard inclusion, especially if your deck had some draw and discard effects.
- **Repurposed Enforcer** — 4.0 — Two-drop with stats ahead of the curve and creates a planeswalker token when it attacks? Sign me up!
- **Rescue Girl, First Responder** — 2.0 - 4.0 — A combination of weak stats and not being able to use the ability on my opponent's turn makes Rescue Girl, First Responder useful in very specific decks with a bunch of high value and/or cheap creatures with good "enters the battlefield" abilities (which this set has plenty of).
- **Return to the Light Realms** — 0.5 — You are going to be surveilling often due to the Empower Jace mechanic, so the potential to set up a loaded graveyard is definitely there if the format is slow enough.
- **Saheeli, Consul of Oversight** — 4.0 — Good stats combined with a value engine makes Saheeli a strong card, especially in a format where having Jace tokens grants you consistent ways to surveil (and thus creating you Thopter tokens).
- **Shatterwing Pegasus** — 2.0 — Shatterwing Pegasus looks like a classic medium filler to me in a regular Limited format. Stats are below rate and the activation cost seems pricey, although having a mana sink adds extra value.
- **Surgical Precision** — 3.0 — As far as conditional removal spells go, Surgical Precision is amazing because it will never rot in your hand if your opponent doesn't have a target.
- **Teyo, Lightshield Expert** — 3.5 — Teyo can protect your permanent from a removal spell, help your creature win combat, and even add a loyalty counter on a planeswalker; that's some excellent utility for just two mana.
- **Thalia, the Survivor** — 3.0 — The stats on Thalia, the Survivor are unexciting for a four-drop, but lifelink can help you stabilize a racing situation or repeatedly enable lifegain payoffs.
- **Tomik, Orzhov Lawmage** — 3.5 — Tomik looks very strong to me. A flying two-drop that lets you pressure your opponent's Jace token while the attacking restriction helps you keep yours around.
- **Unflinching Hortimancer** — 3.0 — White seems to have a fair amount of lifegain in this format and especially Seed Suture synergizes nicely with Unflinching Hortimancer, potentially turning it into a much bigger threat for just one more mana.
- **Way of the Healer** — 4.25 — The ability to turn loyalty counters into creatures is very powerful and helps you protect your planeswalker.
- **Way of the Mentor** — 3.5 — Way of the Mentor always at least replaces itself, which is a great floor for Limited. White-Green lifegain decks should make really good use of it, turning into a potential Limited bomb.
- **Yoshimaru, Beloved Companion** — 2.5 — Yoshimaru, Beloved Companion is a slow synergy piece and an expensive mana sink, but the fact it's incredible with Predictive Preparations alone makes me believe he is going to be quite good in the correct deck.
- **Yuriko, Blade of the Mighty** — 2.0 - 3.5 — Yuriko, Blade of the Mighty is a very expensive way to repeatedly grant your attacker with double strike.
- **Your Fate Ends Here** — 3.5 — If I understand the wording correctly, the mana value restriction applies to both the creature and the planeswalker, which makes it a lot worse than if it could kill any creature.

### Blue (LSV, 39 cards)

- **Arni, Humble Scribe** — 3.5 — Arni rips through your deck with ease, and you don't even need to try and maximize the untap ability to get great value here.
- **Chandra, Chill of Compliance** — 3.5 — Chandra is cheap, can protect herself with stun counters, and gives you a little bit of extra cards and mana (with conditions attached).
- **Countersculpt** — 3.0 — I don't mind a Cancel with upside, and this can both cost less and give you some good value thanks to the Empower Jace ability.
- **Cruel Calculations** — 0.0 — This is way too situational to be worth running. It draw you zero or one card most of the time, which doesn't come close to making it worth it for the times you draw five.
- **Cryotheory Adept** — 2.5 — Cryotheory Adept is a nice way to apply pressure, and even gives you one last push from the graveyard.
- **Diviner of Victory // Unwind History** — 3.5 — Diviner of Victory isn't a Limited bomb, but it is going to contribute nicely to the predicted victories.
- **Divining Duelist** — 2.0 — Divining Duelist will always find something to do, but it will rarely be awesome. This is the definition of filler, and like some of these other cards, does better in faster games.
- **Fblthp, Impossibly Lost** — 1.0 // 3.0 — Fblthp is basically a payoff for hitting the opponent, and I had it do good work in a Blue-White fliers deck.
- **Geist of Saint Thalia** — 3.0 — If you have a good amount of non-creature spells, the Geist essentially pays for itself quickly.
- **Hapatra, the Desert Frost** — 3.0 — I'm not sure what Hapatra's untapping ability is about, but I don't mind a 4/3 for four that produces stun counters.
- **Icy Reception** — 3.0 — In exchange for your Essence Scatter not working as well in the late game, you gain the ability to counter legendary non-creatures and use it as a peculiar combat trick.
- **Infinite Coursework** — 3.5 — Blue really doesn't get much better removal than this, so I don't think you're going to pass on it all that often.
- **Jace's Machinations** — 3.5 — Making an eight loyalty Jace at the end of turn and immediately drawing a card is sweet, especially because the next thing you get to do is untap and activate Jace again.
- **Jace, Reality Sculptor** — 3.5 — Jace can't protect himself every turn, but he can basically make them skip combat with the -3 loyalty abulity.
- **Lyra, Tolarian Archangel** — 4.0 — Lyra is a massive threat just by herself, and sometimes you can even trigger her using other card draw (and hence you don't even need to attack).
- **Mindseeker Oculus** — 3.5 — This has to be in the running for one of the top commons in Reality Fracture — as you get a 2/1, a card, and a Jace on one loyalty all just for three mana (I suppose you don't have to use the Jace to draw, but I assume you will most of the time).
- **Perfected Theory** — 2.0 — This kind of combat trick often falls flat, though 4/5 is starting to get big enough that it's worth it.
- **Plan for All Outcomes** — 3.5 — I like the setup for the grindier games, where every turn you get a free surveil (or work towards drawing a card).
- **Precise Redaction** — 1.0 // 3.5 — I wouldn't mainboard any of the cards in this cycle (unless Sealed proves to be particularly lop-sided in color balance), but they add some good options for Traditional Draft.
- **Proft, Consulting Detective** — 3.0 — Proft is pricey enough to use that I wouldn't call it a must-kill threat, though it can accumulate a good amount of value over the game.
- **Protege's Awakening** — 2.5 — Protege's Awakening is a powerful card draw engine, netting you two cards and three life at a minimum.
- **Ruric Thar, Biomagus** — 4.0 — Ruric Thar hits hard and punishes them for targeting him — that's a nice combination of abilities.
- **Samut, Tyrant of Naktamun** — 2.0 — Samut is mostly just a way to fill out your curve, because people aren't responding to your spells often enough to spend a whole card on the ability.
- **Seasoned Cryomancer** — 4.0 — I don't think I've seen a card packed with this much value in quite some time. Nothing here is flashy, there is just a lot of it — two instances of drawing and discarding, two instances of stun counters, a 2/2 body, and two more cards.
- **Semester Foreseer // Peer Review** — 3.0 — Class is eternally in session here, as this gives you a lot to do with your mana. It smoothes out your draws, creates a token, and can surveil again.
- **Sphinx of False Conclusions** — 4.5 — Sphinx of False Conclusions is just flat-out incredible. Not only do you get a four-power flier with a decent attack trigger, it just comes back for free (albeit once).
- **Sphinx's Approach** — 1.0 — I love me a Divination (more than most, even), but double-blue and single blue are not in the same league for this kind of spell.
- **Surveillance Phantasm** — 3.0 — Surveillance Phantasm basically justifies itself without any help, and becomes even better when it's in the right deck.
- **Tetsuko Umezawa, Fugitive** — 3.0 — Tetsuko applies a lot more pressure than you might think, and works especially well with X/1's.
- **The Theorist, Jace Beleren** — 4.5 — Every mode on this is a threat, and there are a lot of modes. The Theorist, Jace Beleren gives you a steady stream of cards and plenty of interaction to keep that stream going, making it a fantastic card for Limited.
- **Theorist's Proxy** — 3.5 — I'd pretty happily play an 0/3 that Empowers Jace for three without any other text, much less flash and the anti-counter ability.
- **Traxos, Academy Guardian** — 3.5 — Traxos is massive and only costs two mana a lot of the time (assuming you've slotted it into a good spells deck).
- **Undulating Witness** — 3.0 — I love when the landcyclers are good cards to play, and Undulating Witness hits a great note.
- **Unsummon** — 3.0 — Time is a flat circle in the Echoverse and Unsummon is good once more, many years after it first saw the light of day.
- **Variable Chaser // Arc of Fortune** — 3.5 — I'd play a 2/3 flying prowess in most blue decks, so getting the Arc of Fortune option is a nice bonus.
- **Way of the Cryomancer** — 1.5 — I've been the Way of the Mind Sculptor deck multiple times by now, and never did I consider Way of the Cryomancer.
- **Way of the Mind Sculptor** — 3.5 — This, on the other hand, is a great way to get value from Jace. This makes all your Jace tokens to -3 to draw two cards, which is a huge boost.
- **Yargle, Goliath of Otaria** — 1.5 — If you need the stats, this is passable. This obviously not exciting.
- **Yuriko, Hope from the Shadows** — 3.0 — Yuriko does more than enough to be worth one mana, and I like this in any assertive deck. You want the -x/-0 ability to work well regardless, and that means having a lot of creatures in play.

### Black (LSV, 39 cards)

- **Apex Witchstalker** — 3.5 — I absolutely love Apex Witchstalker. It has a huge impact on the board when you do cast it, and is easy enough to cycle away when you don't.
- **Bloodline Recollector // Ancestral Craving** — 2.5 — It's pretty hard to get Bloodline Recollector going, though it's quite powerful if you do.
- **Break Under Pressure** — 3.5 — This is a great removal spell that even gains you a bit of life. It's going to kill the card you want most of the time, making it efficient enough as well.

- **Danitha, Spear of Agony** — 3.5 — Danitha starts out fairly solid and grows easily, making her a strong addition to any aggressive deck.

- **Mabel, Bitter Recluse** — 3.0 — I'm always in for a nice deathtouch creature, and this even eats a Jace token if there's one in play.
- **Massacre Girl, Most Wanted** — 3.5 — Massacre Girl gets out of control quickly, and has a huge effect even when she's not involved in combat.
- **Multiply by Zero** — 4.0 — The only thing Multiply by Zero misses on are creatures with +1/+1 counters, making it one of the most efficient removal spells in the set.
- **Overwrite the Multiverse** — 4.5 — A black sweeper that permanently answers the board is good to start, and this leaves behind a potentially massive Jace token.
- **Proft, Sinister Mastermind** — 3.5 — The split of good and cheap removal spell along with solid late-game creature is a nice one, and I've been impressed by Proft so far.
- **Rampart Hunter** — 1.5 — The stats on Rampart Hunter aren't bad, but this doesn't really do much that most decks want.
- **Rank Rat** — 3.0 — There are enough sacrifice cards to make this a solid playable, and the floor on a two mana creature that forces a discard is pretty high to place to start.
- **Rewrite Regrets** — 3.0 — As long as you have some good targets, this gives you enough value that it's worth running.
- **Rise of the Deathbringer** — 3.5 — Rise of the Deathbringer combines two drastically different modes into one card, which is usually a nice feature.

- **Screeching Soulbreaker** — 2.5 — Screeching Soulbreaker may not always find a home, since it's a vaguely-aggressive flier. It does have a lot of stats though, and gaining a life can make some decks pop off.
- **Silence the Echo** — 3.0 — Silence the Echo is a fine card, and becomes even better if you have good fodder to sacrifice.
- **Solve for Disappointment** — 1.5 — I've solved for disappointment, and I regret to inform you that it is this card. When you hit, it's about even — you spent two mana, they spent zero, but you took a (presumably) good card and a surveil.
- **Terminal Criticism** — 1.0 // 3.5 — This is a great sideboard card and a horrible mainboard one — that's my main criticism.

- **Tinybones, Pocket Nuisance** — 3.0 — Tinybones chips away at their hand and offers some recurring damage — that's a lot for three mana.
- **Void Extrapolator // Omit Variables** — 1.0 // 3.0 — All the Omit Variables cards are pretty feast or famine to me. When your deck is doing the thing and wants to self-mill, they are all great and compliment each other.
- **Vraska's Final Mercy** — 3.5 — I had this in a deck and it was solid. It's not incredible but good enough, and it's well worth two mana.
- **Way of the Deathbringer** — 1.0 // 3.0 — You have to have ample sacrifice support, and ideally a good amount of empower, before Way of the Deathbringer becomes good.

- **Winter, Tormented Loner** — 3.0 — Winter never is all that bad, assuming you put it in creature-heavy decks. It's usually solid once you hit the mid-game, though most of the time you are trading fodder for fodder by that point.
- **Yargle, Glutton of Urborg** — 1.0 — These stats are horrible and it has no other abilities — I'm good.

### Red (Martin Jůza, 40 cards)

- **Ajani's Anguish** — 4.5 — Fireball has always been one of the best Limited cards for any deck regardless of format because of the versatility it brings.
